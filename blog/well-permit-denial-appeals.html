Well Permit Denial in California: Appeals and Next Steps

Common Reasons for Well Permit Denials

Well permits are commonly denied when setback requirements from septic or contamination sources cannot be met, when the property is in a prohibited drilling zone or flood area, when the groundwater basin is under moratorium for new wells, when parcel size is inadequate for well and septic placement, or due to environmental concerns or protected species habitat.

Understanding Your Denial Notice

Review the specific code sections or regulations cited in your denial. Note any suggested alternatives mentioned by the reviewer. Check the deadline for appeals, typically 10-30 days. Request a meeting with the reviewing official for clarification, and get denial reasons in writing if not already documented.

The Appeals Process

Most counties have formal appeals to the planning commission or a hearing officer. Appeals typically cost $200-$1,000 in filing fees. You may present new information or engineered solutions. Professional representation from an attorney or consultant can help. Appeal decisions can take 30-90 days, with further appeals to the county board of supervisors possible.

Alternatives If Your Appeal Fails

If you cannot obtain a well permit, consider connecting to a public water system if available, rainwater harvesting for non-potable uses, water hauling and storage tanks, purchasing water rights in regulated basins, working with neighbors on a shared well agreement, or reconsidering your property use plans.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I reapply for a well permit after being denied in California?

Yes, you can typically reapply if circumstances change. This might include relocating your septic system to allow proper setbacks, modifying the proposed well location, or demonstrating that the original denial reason no longer applies. Reapplication fees usually apply, and you should address all concerns from the original denial.

How much does it cost to appeal a well permit denial?

Appeal filing fees range from $200-$1,000 depending on your county. If you hire a consultant or attorney to assist with your appeal, expect additional costs of $1,000-$5,000+. Consider whether the appeal cost is justified compared to alternative water solutions before proceeding.

What are my options if I can't get a well permit due to groundwater basin restrictions?

In basins with well moratoria or restrictions, options include purchasing existing water rights or allocations, connecting to municipal water if available, water hauling and storage for household use, or working with the Groundwater Sustainability Agency on potential exemptions. Some basins allow replacement wells but not new wells.
