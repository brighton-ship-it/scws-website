Well Rehabilitation Services

Updated February 2026 • By SCWS

Is your well producing less water than it used to? Before assuming you need a new well, consider rehabilitation. Professional well rehabilitation can often restore production at 20-40% the cost of drilling new.

When to Consider Well Rehabilitation

  • Gradual decline in water production over years
  • Increased sediment in water
  • Pump running longer to maintain pressure
  • Well is less than 30 years old with good casing
  • No major structural damage to well

Rehabilitation Methods

Hydrofracturing (Hydrofracking)

High-pressure water injection opens new fractures in rock formations, creating pathways for water to flow into your well. Most effective in crystalline bedrock (granite, similar formations common in San Diego backcountry).

  • Cost: $2,000-$5,000
  • Success rate: 70-80% see improvement
  • Best for: Wells in fractured rock with declining yield

Chemical Treatment

Acid solutions dissolve mineral deposits (calcium, iron, manganese) that clog well screens and reduce flow. Often combined with mechanical cleaning.

  • Cost: $1,500-$4,000
  • Best for: Wells with mineral encrustation, hard water areas

Mechanical Cleaning

Brushing, surging, and airlifting remove sediment, bacterial buildup, and loose deposits from the well and screen.

  • Cost: $1,000-$3,000
  • Best for: Sediment accumulation, biofouling

Shock Chlorination

Heavy chlorine treatment kills iron bacteria and other biological growth clogging wells and pumps.

  • Cost: $200-$500
  • Best for: Iron bacteria, sulfur bacteria

What to Expect

  1. Well assessment: Camera inspection, flow test, water level measurement
  2. Diagnosis: Identify cause of declining production
  3. Treatment plan: Recommend appropriate rehabilitation method(s)
  4. Rehabilitation: Perform treatment
  5. Testing: Measure improvement in yield
  6. Documentation: Provide before/after flow data
