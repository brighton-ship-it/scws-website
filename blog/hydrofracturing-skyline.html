What is Hydrofracturing?

Hydrofracturing is a well rehabilitation technique that uses high-pressure water to clean and expand existing fractures in the bedrock surrounding your well. This process:

  • Clears mineral deposits and sediment blocking water flow
  • Opens new pathways for groundwater to reach your well
  • Can increase well yield by 2-10 times in many cases
  • Extends the life of your existing well

Unlike oil and gas fracking, well hydrofracturing uses only clean water—no chemicals are involved.

Signs Your Skyline Well Needs Hydrofracturing

  • Declining water production over time
  • Well runs dry during peak usage
  • Slow recovery rate after pumping
  • Pump cycling on and off frequently
  • Lower water pressure than previous years

The Hydrofracturing Process

  1. Initial Assessment: We evaluate your well's current yield and condition
  2. Equipment Setup: Specialized high-pressure equipment is positioned at your well
  3. Isolation: Packers seal off sections of the well for targeted treatment
  4. Pressurization: Water is pumped at 500-3,000 PSI to fracture bedrock
  5. Development: Well is cleaned and tested for improved yield

Many Skyline wells that appear to be "going dry" can be successfully rehabilitated through hydrofracturing, saving homeowners tens of thousands of dollars.

Hydrofracturing in Skyline: An Honest Look for a City Neighborhood

Skyline is a residential neighborhood in the southeastern part of the City of San Diego, in San Diego County, sitting on the hills and canyons east of National City and west of the Encanto and Paradise Hills communities. It is a hillside neighborhood of single-family homes that, like the rest of urban San Diego, is served by municipal water. Because of that, the candid starting point for anyone in Skyline researching hydrofracturing is this: for a typical Skyline home on city water, hydrofracturing has no role to play, because there is no private well to treat. We think the most valuable thing we can offer is a clear explanation of when this technique matters and when it does not, so you do not spend money chasing the wrong fix.

Southern California Well Service is a licensed C-57 well contractor with more than 30 years of experience. A big part of being useful is telling people when a service does not apply. This page lays out what hydrofracturing actually does, why it rarely fits an urban San Diego neighborhood like Skyline, and where it genuinely delivers value across the county.

What Hydrofracturing Is

Hydrofracturing, also called hydrofracking in the well trade, is a rehabilitation method that boosts the yield of a low-producing well drilled into hard, fractured crystalline bedrock, the decomposed granite and granitic rock common in inland San Diego County. Such a well takes in water only through natural cracks in the rock. The technique injects clean water at high pressure, usually 1,000 to 3,000 PSI, into an isolated portion of the borehole, scouring sediment and mineral scale from the existing fractures and opening new ones so the well connects to more water-bearing rock. It uses water alone, no chemicals, and the job is finished in a single day inside the existing casing.

Why It Rarely Fits Skyline

Two realities make hydrofracturing a poor match for Skyline proper:

  • Municipal water. Skyline homes are connected to the City of San Diego's water system, so private wells are essentially absent. With no borehole, there is nothing for hydrofracturing to act on.
  • Urban setting. Even setting aside the supply, the dense residential layout of Skyline is not where private bedrock wells are drilled or maintained.

If you live in Skyline and your water pressure has fallen or your bill has spiked, the productive first steps have nothing to do with wells. Check your home's pressure regulator, look for leaks, and contact the City if the issue appears to be on the supply side. A licensed plumber will resolve the vast majority of Skyline water complaints faster and cheaper than any well contractor could.

Where Hydrofracturing Truly Helps

Move inland from the city, into the granitic foothills and backcountry of San Diego County, toward Jamul, Alpine, Dehesa, and Ramona, and hydrofracturing becomes a powerful, economical option. Out there, many homes rely on bedrock wells, and the technique can revive a tired one for a fraction of the cost of drilling new. It is worth a serious look when:

  • The well is drilled into granite or other hard crystalline rock, not sand or alluvium.
  • Yield has declined from the figure on the original driller's report.
  • A newly drilled well came in disappointingly low.
  • The well recovers slowly after pumping or runs short during dry months.
  • You want a lower-cost rehabilitation before committing to a new well.

Comparing Your Options

For a rural property owner with a fading bedrock well, the real choices are deepening, drilling new, or hydrofracturing. A new well in San Diego County typically runs $18,000 to $42,000 turnkey. Deepening is unpredictable and priced by the foot. Hydrofracturing, at $3,000 to $8,000, is the natural first experiment, because when it succeeds it often doubles or triples flow at a fraction of the new-well price, and when it does not, you have risked relatively little. For an urban Skyline reader on city water, by contrast, the meaningful comparison is between accepting your municipal supply and adding a water treatment system for taste or hardness, not between well techniques.

How a Bedrock Well Slows Down

To see why hydrofracturing matters where it does, it helps to know how a healthy granite well declines. When the borehole is first drilled, the fractures it crosses are open and productive. Over the years, fine silt drifts into those cracks, dissolved minerals such as iron and calcium harden into scale on the rock face, and slow biological growth adds a film. The well does not fail overnight; it just stops keeping up during a long shower or a hot irrigation season. By the time the owner calls, the well may be delivering a small fraction of its original gallons per minute. Hydrofracturing reverses much of that buildup by driving water through the fracture network under pressure, flushing out the sediment and scale and reconnecting the borehole to the surrounding rock. That mechanism is exactly why the technique needs solid, cracked rock to work on, and why it does nothing in the absence of a bedrock well.

The Right Help for an Urban Home

For a Skyline homeowner, the genuinely useful improvements are different in kind from well work. If your water tastes off or leaves scale, a treatment system targets that. If your pressure is weak, a plumber can inspect the regulator, supply lines, and fixtures, and frequently finds a simple, inexpensive cause. And if the concern is the broader supply, the City of San Diego is the authority to consult. Pointing you to the right professional is part of doing this job honestly, even when that professional is not us.

The Process, Step by Step

  1. Diagnostic and flow test. We verify the well is in suitable bedrock and record baseline yield. The $125 diagnostic is credited toward the work if you proceed.
  2. Pump removal. The pump and piping are pulled to open the borehole.
  3. Camera inspection. A downhole video checks casing and locates fracture zones.
  4. Packer isolation. An inflatable packer concentrates pressure where it helps.
  5. High-pressure injection. Clean water at 1,000 to 3,000 PSI clears and opens fractures, zone by zone.
  6. Redevelopment and re-test. The well is flushed, the pump reset, and a fresh flow test documents the gain.

On-site work usually takes four to eight hours.

Realistic Expectations

In suitable San Diego County bedrock wells, hydrofracturing improves yield in roughly 70 to 80 percent of cases, commonly two to five times the prior flow. It cannot manufacture water in dry rock, and a straight-dealing contractor will say so before quoting. Outcomes depend on the fracture network your borehole intersects.

What to Check Before You Call

  • Are you on a well or city water? In Skyline, almost certainly city water, in which case a plumber or the utility is your contact.
  • What does the well log say? Depth, casing, and formation tell us whether hydrofrac applies.
  • Has the pump been ruled out? A failing pump or pressure switch imitates low yield cheaply.
  • Is the casing sound? Cracked casing must be addressed before pressure work.

When to Call a Professional

Reach out when you own a genuine bedrock well that is underperforming, when a new well disappoints, or when you simply want an honest opinion on whether the issue is the aquifer, the pump, or the plumbing. Hydrofracturing is high-pressure work requiring proper packers and casing expertise, and is not a DIY task.
