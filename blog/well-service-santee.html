Santee's Groundwater Geology

Properties near the San Diego River benefit from alluvial aquifers—sand and gravel deposits that can yield good water production at moderate depths. The eastern hills tap into fractured granite aquifers, which require finding productive fracture zones. You can find detailed hydrogeological information for this area on the San Diego County groundwater resource page.

Common Well Problems in Santee

Aging Well Systems

Many Santee wells were drilled 30-50 years ago when the area was more rural. These older systems may need pump replacement, casing repairs, or complete rehabilitation. If your well was installed before modern standards, a professional inspection can identify potential issues.

Increased Water Demand

Property improvements—ADUs, expanded landscaping, pools—can strain older well systems designed for more modest demands. We can evaluate your system's capacity and recommend upgrades if needed.

Water Quality Changes

Santee wells may experience changes in water quality over time, including increased mineral content or hardness. Regular testing helps track these changes and determine if treatment is needed.

Seasonal Fluctuations

Like much of East County, Santee wells can experience seasonal water level variations. Proper pump placement accounts for these fluctuations to ensure year-round reliability.
