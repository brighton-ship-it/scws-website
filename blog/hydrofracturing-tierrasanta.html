What is Hydrofracturing?

Hydrofracturing is a well rehabilitation technique that uses high-pressure water to clean and expand existing fractures in the bedrock surrounding your well. This process:

  • Clears mineral deposits and sediment blocking water flow
  • Opens new pathways for groundwater to reach your well
  • Can increase well yield by 2-10 times in many cases
  • Extends the life of your existing well

Unlike oil and gas fracking, well hydrofracturing uses only clean water—no chemicals are involved.

Signs Your Tierrasanta Well Needs Hydrofracturing

  • Declining water production over time
  • Well runs dry during peak usage
  • Slow recovery rate after pumping
  • Pump cycling on and off frequently
  • Lower water pressure than previous years

The Hydrofracturing Process

  1. Initial Assessment: We evaluate your well's current yield and condition
  2. Equipment Setup: Specialized high-pressure equipment is positioned at your well
  3. Isolation: Packers seal off sections of the well for targeted treatment
  4. Pressurization: Water is pumped at 500-3,000 PSI to fracture bedrock
  5. Development: Well is cleaned and tested for improved yield

Many Tierrasanta wells that appear to be "going dry" can be successfully rehabilitated through hydrofracturing, saving homeowners tens of thousands of dollars.

Hydrofracturing in Tierrasanta: A Candid Look

Tierrasanta is a master-planned residential community in the City of San Diego, San Diego County, set on the mesas and canyons between Mission Trails Regional Park, Mission Gorge, and Marine Corps Air Station Miramar. Known as "The Island in the Hills," it was developed largely from the 1970s onward as a self-contained suburb of single-family homes, townhomes, and parks, all served by the City of San Diego's municipal water supply. Because Tierrasanta is a modern, fully developed neighborhood on city water, hydrofracturing is a weak fit for almost every property here — and we think it is more useful to explain that honestly than to pretend otherwise.

What Hydrofracturing Is For

Hydrofracturing (hydrofracking) is a technique for improving the yield of private water wells drilled into hard, fractured bedrock. By injecting clean water at high pressure, it cleans and widens the rock fractures that feed a rural well, often increasing output substantially. It is a backcountry and rural tool. Tierrasanta, by contrast, was planned and built as a connected urban community: homes receive treated municipal water, and there are essentially no producing private wells on residential lots. With no well to treat, hydrofracturing has nothing to act on for the typical Tierrasanta resident.

Why Tierrasanta Homes Are on Municipal Water

When Tierrasanta was master-planned, water and sewer infrastructure were engineered into the development from the start. That means every home connects to the city distribution network rather than drawing from individual wells. This is the opposite of the rural East County situation, where homes sit on large parcels far from any water main and depend on private wells in granite bedrock. The practical consequence: a water complaint in Tierrasanta is almost always a plumbing or municipal-pressure matter, not a well-yield matter.

Common Water Concerns in Tierrasanta — and Their Real Causes

  • Low household pressure. Often a failing pressure-reducing valve at the home's service connection, or buildup in older fixtures and valves.
  • Hard water and scale. San Diego's imported supply is naturally hard; this is a treatment question (a softener), not a well question.
  • Hot-side flow problems. Sediment in the water heater can restrict hot-water flow specifically.
  • Hillside elevation effects. Homes on the higher mesas may notice pressure differences; the city manages distribution pressure, and a booster or regulator adjustment is the fix where needed.

For each of these, a licensed plumber — or for hardness, a water-treatment specialist — is the appropriate professional. None are addressed by hydrofracturing.

The Uncommon Case Where a Well Exists

A handful of older or larger parcels at the edges of San Diego communities retain legacy wells, often used for irrigation or long since capped. If your Tierrasanta property genuinely has a private well and it is underperforming, the geology is on your side: San Diego County's bedrock is predominantly granitic and crystalline, the exact formation in which hydrofracturing performs well. In that rare situation we would assess the well, run a flow test, and recommend treatment only if the data supports it. For nearly all Tierrasanta households, though, there simply is no well in play.

How the Process Works, If You Do Have a Well

For a true hard-rock well, we lower inflatable packers to isolate a target zone in the cased borehole and inject clean water at 1,500-3,000+ PSI. That pressure flushes silt and mineral scale from the fractures feeding the well and can extend those fractures to reach new water-bearing cracks. Successful treatment in good San Diego County granite commonly improves yield two to five times. No chemicals are used, and the job typically takes four to eight hours with documented before-and-after flow tests. This is relevant only to genuine private wells, which are rare in Tierrasanta.

Tierrasanta's Setting and What It Means for Groundwater

Tierrasanta occupies a series of mesas cut by steep canyons that drain toward Mission Gorge and the San Diego River, with Mission Trails Regional Park wrapping much of its eastern and southern edge. The underlying rock in this part of the county includes older marine sedimentary formations and granitic basement, but none of that is being tapped by individual homeowners, because the community was never built to rely on groundwater. When it was planned, the developers tied the entire neighborhood into the regional water system that imports the great majority of San Diego's supply from outside the region. So while the geology beneath your feet is real, it is not part of your household water equation. This is worth understanding because it explains why a technique aimed at coaxing more water out of bedrock fractures has no role to play for the typical Tierrasanta resident, no matter how appealing the promise of more water sounds. The honest path to better flow or quality here runs through your home plumbing and, where relevant, water treatment.

What to Check Before You Call

  • Verify your water source. A City of San Diego water bill means you are on municipal water and have no producing well.
  • Check pressure with a gauge at an outdoor spigot to learn whether the issue is whole-house or localized.
  • Inspect the pressure-reducing valve near your main; replacing a worn one is a routine, affordable repair.
  • Consider water hardness if your complaint is scale or spotting rather than flow — that points to treatment, not a well.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can hydrofracturing raise my water pressure in Tierrasanta? No. Tierrasanta homes are on City of San Diego water, so pressure problems are plumbing or municipal in origin and hydrofracturing does not apply.

How can I tell whether I have a private well? If you pay a city water bill, you are on municipal water. A well would have its own wellhead, pump, and pressure tank and no metered city service.

My water is hard and leaves spots — is that a well issue? No. San Diego's imported water is naturally hard; a water softener ($1,500-$3,500) is the appropriate solution.

I have an old well on my property — is it worth reviving? Possibly. If it reaches granitic bedrock, hydrofracturing may help an underperforming well. We can evaluate it, though wells are uncommon in Tierrasanta.

Why is hydrofracturing better suited to the backcountry? Because rural homes rely on private bedrock wells, while planned urban communities like Tierrasanta are served by municipal water with no wells to treat.

Does the process use chemicals? No. Well hydrofracturing uses only clean water under pressure and is unrelated to oil-and-gas fracking.
