El Cajon's Geology & Well Depths

El Cajon sits in a unique geological setting. The valley floor is surrounded by the Cuyamaca Mountains to the east and foothills on all sides. This geography creates distinct drilling conditions depending on where your property is located:

Valley Floor & Bostonia Area

  • • Typical depths: 250-400 feet
  • • Decomposed granite and alluvial deposits
  • • Generally easier drilling conditions
  • • Variable water quality

Foothills (Granite Hills, Dehesa, Crest)

  • • Typical depths: 400-600 feet
  • • Hard granite and metamorphic rock
  • • Air rotary drilling required
  • • Water found in fracture zones

The deeper wells common in El Cajon's foothill areas require more robust pump systems—typically 1/2 to 1 HP submersible pumps capable of lifting water from significant depths. We size every system appropriately for your specific well characteristics and household demand.

Water Quality in El Cajon Wells

El Cajon well water quality varies by location but has some common characteristics:

  • Hard water — Very common throughout the area; water softeners are standard
  • Iron/manganese — Some foothill areas have elevated levels causing staining
  • TDS (Total Dissolved Solids) — Often higher than municipal water
  • Nitrates — Possible in agricultural areas (we test for this)

We provide comprehensive water testing with every new well and can recommend appropriate treatment systems to ensure your water meets your needs.

Frequently Asked Questions

How deep are wells in El Cajon?

Most El Cajon wells range from 300-600 feet. Valley floor properties may hit water at 250-400 feet, while foothill locations (Granite Hills, Dehesa, Crest, Flinn Springs) typically require 400-600 feet. We analyze neighboring well data and local geology to estimate depth before drilling.

Why are El Cajon wells deeper than coastal areas?

El Cajon's foothill geology features hard granite and metamorphic rock formations. Water is typically found in fracture zones within these rock formations, which tend to be deeper than the sandstone and alluvial aquifers found in coastal and valley areas. The trade-off is that foothill wells often have excellent water quality and good long-term reliability.

How often should I service my El Cajon well?

We recommend annual inspections to check pump performance, pressure tank function, water quality, and overall system health. El Cajon's hard water and the demands of deeper well systems make proactive maintenance especially important to avoid costly emergency repairs.
