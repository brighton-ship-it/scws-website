Seeing rusty, orange, or red-tinted water from your El Cajon well? This common issue is usually caused by iron in your groundwater or corroding well components. The good news: it's fixable.

What Causes Rusty Well Water?

  • Dissolved Iron: Naturally occurring iron in San Diego County groundwater
  • Iron Bacteria: Bacteria that feed on iron, creating rusty slime
  • Corroding Casing: Old steel well casing breaking down
  • Corroding Pipes: Galvanized or iron plumbing in your home
  • Failing Water Heater: Rusting anode rod or tank

Is Rusty Water Safe to Drink?

Iron itself isn't a health hazard at typical well water concentrations. However, rusty water:

  • Tastes metallic and unpleasant
  • Stains sinks, toilets, and laundry
  • Can clog pipes and fixtures over time
  • May indicate other water quality issues

We recommend water testing to identify the specific cause and concentration of iron in your El Cajon well.

Casing Replacement

Severely corroded steel casing may need to be lined or replaced to stop rust contamination.

Plumbing Updates

Replacing old galvanized pipes with PEX or copper eliminates household sources of rust.

East County's geology, built on decomposed granite and fractured crystalline rock, naturally introduces iron and minerals into well water. If your East County well is producing rusty water, this guide explains why, how it is treated, and what it costs to fix.

A well in Crest can behave very differently from one in Rancho San Diego just a few miles away. How Iron and Rust Get Into East County Wells

Rust is oxidized iron, and on a well it comes from a few distinct sources. Identifying the right one determines whether you need treatment equipment, a well rehabilitation, or a casing repair.

Dissolved Iron From the Granite

As groundwater moves through the decomposed granite that underlies El Cajon and the surrounding hills, it dissolves naturally occurring iron. This ferrous iron comes up clear and turns rusty orange the moment it meets air or chlorine and oxidizes. It is the single most common cause of discolored water on East County wells, and it is handled with an iron-removal system.

Iron Bacteria

Iron bacteria are harmless microbes that feed on iron and leave a slimy, reddish-brown buildup behind. They colonize the well casing, pump, and pressure tank, and you can usually spot them as a gelatinous orange film in the toilet tank. They are common in the region's wells and require shock chlorination to clear.

Corroding Steel Casing

Older East County wells were often completed with steel casing. After many years underground, that steel corrodes and sheds rust directly into the water column. A well that has gradually produced more and more rust over the years may have a casing nearing the end of its life.

Household Plumbing

In older El Cajon homes, galvanized supply lines and aging water heaters can also produce rust — the giveaway being rust that clears after the tap runs, or rust on the hot side only.

Common East County Scenarios

  • Seasonal iron swings: After heavy winter rains recharge the aquifer, or during late-summer drawdown, iron levels in a well can change noticeably.
  • Low-yield wells pulling sediment: During drought, a dropping water table can cause a pump to draw in sediment and rust from near the bottom of the well.
  • Long-idle wells: Vacation properties or wells left off for months often produce a burst of rust and iron bacteria when restarted.
  • Aging steel casing: Many decades-old East County wells are now at the point where casing corrosion becomes the dominant rust source.

What to Check Before You Call

  1. Is your home on a well or on Helix water? If you have a well, rust is most likely a groundwater or casing issue. On city water, suspect plumbing or the water heater.
  2. Hot, cold, or both? Hot-only points to the water heater. Both sides points to the well source or the casing.
  3. Does it clear after running? Rust that clears is in your pipes; rust that never clears is coming continuously from the well.
  4. Check the toilet tank: Orange slime indicates iron bacteria; gritty orange sediment indicates dissolved iron or casing rust.
  5. The glass test: Uniform tint that stays put is dissolved iron; cloudiness that settles out is particulate rust and sediment.

Solutions for Rusty Well Water

Iron-Removal Systems

For clear-water iron, an oxidizing media filter — manganese greensand, catalytic carbon, or an air-injection system — converts dissolved iron into a filterable solid and removes it. Higher iron or a sulfur smell may call for chlorine or peroxide injection followed by filtration. Where iron is low and hardness is the bigger issue, a properly sized water softener can address both.

Well Rehabilitation

When iron bacteria has taken hold, shock chlorination plus mechanical cleaning of the casing and pump clears the colony. This is the essential first step; installing a filter over an infected well only fouls the filter.

Casing Repair or Lining

A badly corroded steel casing can be lined or, in severe cases, the well may need to be re-cased or replaced. We assess casing condition with a downhole inspection before recommending anything that significant.

Plumbing Fixes

For household-side rust, re-piping galvanized lines with PEX or copper and replacing a failing water heater solves the problem at its source.

When to Call a Professional

Reach out when rust will not clear after flushing, when it appears continuously rather than just on the first draw, when you also notice a drop in pressure or yield, or when the water develops a sulfur smell. A sudden change in a well's water is worth a prompt inspection, because it can signal a failing pump, a dropping water table, or casing deterioration — problems that get more expensive the longer they wait.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are there really private wells in the El Cajon area?

Yes. While the city center is largely on the Helix Water District, the surrounding hills and unincorporated parcels — Crest, Granite Hills, parts of Lakeside and Rancho San Diego — have many private wells. Rusty water on those wells is usually a groundwater or casing issue.

Why does my East County well water turn orange after it sits?

That is classic dissolved iron. It comes up clear, then oxidizes and turns orange when exposed to air. An iron-removal system corrects it.

Is rusty well water safe to drink?

Iron itself is not considered a health hazard at typical levels, but it tastes metallic and stains fixtures and laundry. Sudden or persistent discoloration, especially with an odor, should be tested before you drink it.

My well has gotten rustier over the years. Is the casing failing?

A gradual, long-term increase in rust often points to a corroding steel casing. A downhole inspection confirms it, after which the casing can be lined or the well re-cased.

What is hydrofracturing and would it help my well?

Hydrofracturing uses water pressure to open existing fractures in the granite around the well, improving yield. It is most useful for low-producing wells in fractured rock, common in East County, and runs roughly $3,000 to $8,000.
