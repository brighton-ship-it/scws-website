Well Drilling in Glen Avon

Glen Avon property owners rely on private wells for clean, reliable water. As a licensed C-57 well drilling contractor, Southern California Well Service provides professional well drilling throughout Riverside County.

Why Drill a New Well?

  • Water independence — No monthly water bills
  • Property value — Wells add significant value
  • Agricultural needs — Irrigation for crops and livestock
  • Backup supply — Secondary water source
  • Development — New construction requirements

Our Drilling Process

Professional well drilling requires expertise and proper equipment:

  1. Site evaluation — We assess your property and review well logs from nearby wells
  2. Permitting — We handle all Riverside County permits
  3. Drilling — Air rotary or mud rotary depending on geology
  4. Casing installation — SDR 17 PVC for longevity
  5. Well development — Maximize water production
  6. Pump installation — Complete turnkey system

Riverside County Geology

Glen Avon sits in a region with varied geology. Well depths typically range from 200-500 feet depending on location. Our experience with local conditions helps us drill efficiently and find productive water zones.

What Affects Well Depth?

  • Proximity to aquifers and water-bearing formations
  • Elevation and terrain
  • Historical drought conditions
  • Neighboring well data

Well Drilling Costs

Well drilling in Glen Avon typically costs $25-$50 per foot for drilling, plus casing, development, and pump system. Total projects range from $15,000-$50,000 depending on depth and equipment needed.

We provide detailed written quotes before any work begins. No hidden fees, no surprises.

FAQs: Well Drilling in Glen Avon

How deep will my well need to be?

Most wells in the Glen Avon area range from 200-500 feet. We review nearby well logs and local geology to estimate depth before drilling.

How long does drilling take?

Most residential wells are completed in 2-5 days. Deeper wells or challenging geology may take longer.

Do I need a permit?

Yes. Riverside County requires permits for new wells. We handle all permitting as part of our service.

What about dry holes?

While rare in this area, we discuss all scenarios upfront. Our knowledge of local geology minimizes this risk.
