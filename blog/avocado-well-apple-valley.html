Avocado Water Demands

Avocados are thirsty trees:

  • Mature tree: 40-70 gallons per day in summer
  • Per acre: 4-6 acre-feet per year
  • Critical periods: Fruit set and sizing

A reliable well is essential for profitable avocado production in San Bernardino County.

Chloride Sensitivity

Avocados are highly sensitive to chloride in irrigation water. If your Apple Valley well has elevated chloride:

  • Blending with lower-chloride water source
  • Leaching irrigation to flush salts
  • Rootstock selection for salt tolerance
  • Regular soil and leaf testing

Honest Well and Irrigation Guidance for Apple Valley Property Owners

Apple Valley is an incorporated high-desert town in San Bernardino County, spread across about 78 square miles of the Victor Valley at roughly 2,900 feet, just north of the San Bernardino Mountains. It is Mojave Desert country, with big skies, sagebrush, horse properties, and a climate of scorching summers and frosty winter nights. We want to be straight with anyone searching for avocado well service here: Apple Valley is not avocado-growing territory. Hard winter frosts and extreme summer heat make commercial avocado production impractical, since avocado trees are damaged by the freezes that Apple Valley regularly experiences. The genuine avocado belt lies in the milder, coastal-influenced country to the south and west, not in the High Desert.

What wells in Apple Valley genuinely do is supply rural households, water horses and livestock, and irrigate hardy desert-adapted landscaping. That is the work that matters here, and we have done it across San Bernardino County and the Victor Valley for more than 30 years.

How a Desert Well System Works in Apple Valley

Apple Valley primarily overlies the Alto subarea of the Mojave Basin, which is comparatively well-recharged by Mojave River flows and mountain runoff. Wells here are often shallower than in outlying desert towns: roughly 150-250 feet near the river and 300-400 feet or more on the valley margins, set in alluvial deposits over granitic basement rock. A submersible pump lifts water to a pressure tank and switch that maintain household pressure, and many properties add a storage tank so a modest well can fill overnight and meet daytime demand for animals or landscaping. Because desert wells work hard through long, hot summers, correct pump sizing for the depth and flow is the most important decision you will make.

Common Local Scenarios in Apple Valley

  • Hard, salty water: Sodium-chloride chemistry with total dissolved solids over 1,000 mg/L in places drives scale, staining, and salty taste.
  • Declining water levels as regional groundwater drops, leaving older, shallower wells short.
  • Pumps short-cycling or pulling air when the water column falls near the intake.
  • Sediment and sand wearing out pumps and clogging fixtures.
  • Heavy summer demand for livestock, horses, and desert landscaping during extreme heat.

Frost-protection irrigation for tender trees is not the realistic concern here; the practical Apple Valley challenge is reliably lifting and treating desert groundwater for the home and property.

What to Check Before You Call

  • Pump behavior: Does it run continuously, short-cycle, or sputter? Sputtering can mean the water level has dropped near the pump.
  • Pressure: Note weak pressure or a waterlogged tank.
  • Water quality: Watch for salty taste, white scale, cloudiness, or sand.
  • Well records: The original well log and pump depth tell us how much room there is to lower the pump.
  • Power: Rural desert circuits can swing and damage pump motors.

When to Call a Professional

Call us if you lose water, if the pump pulls air, if pressure collapses, or if water quality changes. Desert wells are deep, the equipment is heavy, and the wiring is high-voltage, so this is not a homeowner repair. As a licensed C-57 well drilling contractor, we can lower a pump, hydrofracture a declining well, install treatment, or recommend deepening, and we will tell you honestly which fits your aquifer and budget.
