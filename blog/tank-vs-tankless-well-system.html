Tank vs Tankless Well Systems: Pressure System Comparison

Compare traditional pressure tank systems and modern constant pressure (tankless) well systems. Learn the pros, cons, and costs to choose the right system for your water delivery needs.

How Pressure Tank Systems Work

Traditional pressure tank systems store 20-80+ gallons of pressurized water. The pump cycles on when pressure drops (typically around 30 PSI) and off when it reaches the high setting (50 PSI). This creates pressure fluctuations you may notice in the shower. The tank reduces pump cycling but takes up significant space. Systems cost $1,500-$3,000 installed.

How Constant Pressure Systems Work

Constant pressure (tankless) systems use variable frequency drive (VFD) technology to adjust pump speed in real-time. The pump speeds up when you use more water and slows when demand drops—maintaining steady pressure without a large tank. Only a small expansion tank is needed. Systems cost $2,500-$5,000 installed.

Performance Comparison

Pressure consistency: Tankless wins with steady 50-60 PSI regardless of demand. Multiple fixtures: Tankless handles simultaneous showers, irrigation, and appliances without pressure drops. Energy efficiency: Variable speed pumps use less power than on/off cycling. Pump longevity: Soft starts reduce motor stress. Space: Tankless requires minimal space compared to 50-gallon pressure tanks.

Choosing the Right System

Pressure tanks work well for simple, budget-conscious installations with moderate water demands. Constant pressure systems excel in larger homes, properties with irrigation needs, or anywhere pressure consistency matters. Consider upgrading to constant pressure when replacing an aging pump system.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do tankless well systems save energy?

Yes. Variable speed pumps typically use 30-50% less electricity than traditional on/off pump cycling. The soft-start feature also reduces power surge demands and extends motor life.

How long do pressure tanks last?

Quality pressure tanks last 10-20 years depending on water quality and bladder type. Steel tanks may develop leaks sooner. Bladder failure is the most common issue, causing frequent pump cycling.

Can I upgrade from a tank to constant pressure system?

Yes. Upgrading requires replacing the pump motor and controller with VFD-compatible components. The existing well casing and piping typically work fine. A small expansion tank replaces the large pressure tank.
