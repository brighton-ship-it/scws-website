The Most Common Well Emergencies in Sage

Deep wells, low yields, and rugged granite terrain shape the kind of emergencies we see in Sage. With many wells reaching well past 300 feet and some far deeper, the failures here tend to be serious when they come.

  • Submersible pump failure deep in the well. Pumps set hundreds of feet down work hard against the lift, and when one fails it leaves a home or ranch with nothing.
  • Wells drawing down low. In fractured bedrock with limited yield, a well can be pumped down faster than it recovers, leaving the pump pulling air, especially in dry years.
  • Pressure tank and storage system failures. Many Sage properties run storage tanks to buffer low production, and a failed tank, float, or booster pump can cut off the whole supply.
  • Electrical and control box failure. Rural power, summer surges, and rodents in pump houses take a toll on control boxes, capacitors, and pressure switches.
  • Sudden total loss of water. The most common urgent call, and a real hardship on a ranch with livestock to water.
  • Power outage restart failures. Pumps often fail to come back cleanly after an outage and trip protection.

What to Check First

Before assuming you are facing a full pump replacement, run through these safe checks. Many emergencies turn out to be simpler than expected, and telling us what you find on the phone saves valuable time.

  1. Check your breaker. Locate the well or pump breaker in your panel. If it has tripped, switch it fully off and then back on once. If it trips again immediately, leave it off and call us rather than resetting it again and again.
  2. Read the pressure gauge. The gauge on your pressure tank or pump assembly should normally read 40 to 60 psi. A reading near zero points to a supply or pump issue; a stuck high reading points to a switch problem.
  3. Tap the pressure tank. Knock on the tank up high and down low. Hollow at the top and solid at the bottom is healthy. A tank that sounds heavy and full all the way up is likely waterlogged.
  4. Do not run a dry pump. If you suspect the well has drawn down or the pump is pulling air, shut it off. Running a pump dry can burn out the motor within minutes and turn a small problem into a costly one.
  5. Confirm the grid. Verify the well circuit has power and that there is not a wider neighborhood or area outage in progress.

What to Do While You Wait

Once you have called and a technician is on the way, a few steps speed up the repair. If the breaker keeps tripping, leave it off so the motor is not straining against a fault, and do not keep resetting it. Conserve the water already in your lines and any storage for essential use, and hold off on irrigation. If you have livestock, prioritize stored or hauled water for the animals and let us know the scale of your property when you call. Clear access to the well head, pressure tank, and control panel so our crew can begin right away, and keep your phone handy in case we need help finding the property.
