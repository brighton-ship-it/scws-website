Temecula Valley's Unique Groundwater Conditions

Temecula Valley sits in a structural trough bounded by the Santa Rosa Plateau to the west and the Agua Tibia Mountains to the east. This geography creates favorable conditions for groundwater storage, with several distinct aquifer systems that vary across the valley.

  • Alluvial Basin Aquifers: The valley floor features productive alluvial deposits along Murrieta Creek and Temecula Creek, often providing excellent water yields.
  • Pauba Formation: This sedimentary formation underlies much of wine country and can produce reliable water supplies when properly developed.
  • Fractured Bedrock: Properties in the surrounding hills access water from fractures in crystalline basement rock, requiring specialized drilling techniques.
  • Variable Depth Requirements: Well depths in Temecula range from 150 feet in favorable valley locations to over 500 feet in hillside areas.

Vineyard and Winery Well Solutions

Temecula Valley's wine industry depends on reliable water for irrigation, frost protection, and winery operations. SCWS has drilled wells for numerous vineyards throughout the region, understanding the specific demands that grape growing places on water systems.

Vineyard irrigation requires consistent water availability during the growing season, with the flexibility to adjust application rates based on weather and growth stage. We design wells and pumping systems with variable frequency drives that optimize efficiency while providing the precise control winemakers need.

Frost protection systems require high flow rates for short periods during critical spring nights. We can design systems that meet these peak demands while maintaining normal irrigation capacity throughout the season. For wineries, we ensure adequate water for processing, cleaning, and hospitality operations.

Equestrian Property Water Systems

Temecula has long been horse country, with countless equestrian properties throughout the valley and surrounding hills. These properties have unique water requirements that go beyond typical residential needs.

Horses consume 5-10 gallons of water per day under normal conditions, and significantly more during hot weather or heavy work. A boarding facility or training operation may need to supply dozens of horses plus water for arena maintenance, facility washing, and pasture irrigation.

We design well and pump systems specifically for equestrian operations, ensuring adequate pressure and flow for automatic waterers, wash racks, and irrigation systems. Our systems include appropriate storage capacity to handle peak demand periods without overworking the pump.

Well Rehabilitation and Deepening

Many Temecula properties have existing wells that may be underperforming or showing signs of age. Before drilling a new well, it's often worth exploring rehabilitation options that can restore production at a fraction of the cost.

  • Well Cleaning: Removing mineral deposits and biological growth that restrict water flow.
  • Hydrofracturing: Using high-pressure water to open new fractures and improve production.
  • Well Deepening: Drilling deeper to access additional water-bearing zones.
  • Pump Upgrades: Installing more efficient modern pumping equipment.
