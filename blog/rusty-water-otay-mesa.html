What Causes Rusty Well Water?

  • Dissolved Iron: Naturally occurring iron in San Diego County groundwater
  • Iron Bacteria: Bacteria that feed on iron, creating rusty slime
  • Corroding Casing: Old steel well casing breaking down
  • Corroding Pipes: Galvanized or iron plumbing in your home
  • Failing Water Heater: Rusting anode rod or tank

Is Rusty Water Safe to Drink?

Iron itself isn't a health hazard at typical well water concentrations. However, rusty water:

  • Tastes metallic and unpleasant
  • Stains sinks, toilets, and laundry
  • Can clog pipes and fixtures over time
  • May indicate other water quality issues

We recommend water testing to identify the specific cause and concentration of iron in your Otay Mesa well.

Why Otay Mesa Wells Run Rusty

Iron is abundant in the sedimentary and crystalline formations beneath Otay Mesa, and groundwater dissolves it in a clear, ferrous form as it moves through the ground. That iron stays invisible until it reaches the oxygen at your faucet, where it oxidizes into the orange particles we call rust, which is why clear-looking well water can turn orange after sitting in a glass. In the Otay Mesa area, the rusty water we diagnose usually arises from dissolved ferrous iron coming directly from the aquifer; ferric iron that has already oxidized inside the well or pipes; manganese, which causes darker black or purple staining and frequently accompanies iron; iron bacteria, the source of the reddish slime in toilet tanks and on fixtures; and corrosion of steel casings, drop pipes, and galvanized household plumbing.

On Otay Mesa's rural and agricultural parcels, we often find older wells whose steel casing has corroded over years of service and now sheds rust into the water. The semi-arid South County climate means dry stretches can lower the water table and draw iron-rich sediment into the well, changing the water's color noticeably. And as everywhere, when only the hot water runs rusty, the water heater is typically to blame rather than the well.

Is the Water Safe to Use?

Under EPA classification, iron and manganese are secondary, aesthetic contaminants, evaluated on taste, color, and odor rather than acute toxicity. Iron at the levels common in San Diego County wells will not make a healthy person sick, but it stains laundry and porcelain, tastes metallic, scales up pipes and appliances, and feeds the iron bacteria that clog plumbing. Manganese deserves closer scrutiny because elevated concentrations raise health questions. The only way to know your exact levels and choose the correct treatment is a proper water test.

What to Check Before You Call

A few quick observations speed up the diagnosis. Note whether the rust shows up in hot water only, which usually points to the water heater rather than the well. Check whether it is whole-house or limited to one fixture, since a single rusty tap typically signals a local pipe. Look for reddish slime in the toilet tank, the telltale sign of iron bacteria. Pay attention to timing, because rust that appears only on first draw often means sediment in the pressure tank, and consider any recent changes such as a dry spell or recent pump work that could explain a sudden shift in water quality.

Solutions That Restore Clear Water

Iron and Manganese Filtration

For dissolved iron and manganese from the aquifer, a backwashing oxidizing filter is the reliable solution. We size it to your measured load so it keeps up without wasting water, and where iron is lower but hardness is present, a properly sized softener may also handle it.

Shock Chlorination and Well Cleaning

When iron bacteria are producing slime, disinfecting the entire well and plumbing system eliminates the colony, after which we set a maintenance schedule to keep it from coming back.

Casing and Plumbing Repairs

Where corroded steel casing is the rust source, lining or replacing it stops contamination at the source, and replacing galvanized household pipe with PEX or copper removes the in-home source of rust.

Sediment Pre-Filtration

A sediment filter ahead of the main system traps particulate rust and protects the treatment equipment, an inexpensive and worthwhile first line of defense.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does my Otay Mesa well water turn orange after sitting?

That is dissolved ferrous iron, invisible underground until air oxidizes it into orange rust. An oxidizing iron filter removes it before it reaches your fixtures.

Is rusty well water safe to drink?

Iron is an aesthetic contaminant and not an acute health risk at typical levels, though it stains and tastes metallic. We test manganese at the same time, since that one is worth watching more closely.

Why is only my hot water rusty?

That usually means the water heater rather than the well. A corroding anode rod or rusting tank discolors hot water only; servicing or replacing it normally clears it up.

My South County well water changes color during dry spells. Why?

When the water table drops in the semi-arid climate, the pump can draw iron-rich sediment from near the bottom of the well, intensifying the rust. Diagnosis identifies whether it is the well or the aquifer.

What is the slimy red residue in my toilet tank?

Iron bacteria, which feed on iron and clog plumbing and pump screens. Shock chlorination kills the colony and clears the slime.
