Kern County Well Overview

68,000 Total registered water wells in Kern County

Source: California DWR Well Completion Reports, Kern County Environmental Health

Kern County is California's second-largest agricultural producer and has one of the state's largest well populations. The county sits primarily within the critically overdrafted Kern County Subbasin, making groundwater management a critical issue under SGMA (Sustainable Groundwater Management Act).

Key Kern County Well Facts

  • 68,000+ registered wells - second only to Fresno County
  • 235,000+ residents rely on private wells for drinking water
  • Critically overdrafted basin under SGMA designation
  • 1,089 new well permits issued in 2025
  • Agriculture uses 85%+ of groundwater pumped
  • Land subsidence of 1-2 feet per decade in some areas
  • Average well age: 28 years
  • Deepening trend: Avg depth increased 95 feet since 2015

Frequently Asked Questions

How many wells are in Kern County?

Kern County has approximately 68,000 registered water wells, second only to Fresno County in California.

What is the average well depth in Kern County?

Average residential depth is 350-500 feet. Agricultural wells average 600-1,200 feet, with some exceeding 1,400 feet in western Kern.

How much does it cost to drill a well in Kern County?

A complete residential well costs $35,000-$55,000. Costs are higher than other regions due to increased depth requirements.

What is SGMA and how does it affect Kern County?

SGMA (Sustainable Groundwater Management Act) designates the Kern County Subbasin as critically overdrafted. GSAs are implementing pumping allocations and sustainability plans by 2040.

Do I need a permit for a well in Kern County?

Yes, all wells require permits from Kern County Environmental Health. SGMA may require additional GSA registration.
