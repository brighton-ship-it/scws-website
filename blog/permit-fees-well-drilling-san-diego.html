Current Permit Fee Structure (2026)

San Diego County Department of Environmental Health (DEH) administers well permits. Fees vary by well type and complexity:

Standard Domestic Well Permit

Fee Type Amount Notes
Application/Plan Review $800-$1,200 Initial processing and site review
Permit Issuance $200-$400 Once approved
Inspections (included) $0-$200 Usually included, may have re-inspection fees
Total Typical Range $1,200-$1,800 Standard domestic well

Factors That Increase Permit Costs

Situation Additional Cost Reason
CEQA review required $500-$1,500+ Environmental compliance review
Special review zones $300-$800 Groundwater protection areas, watersheds
Agricultural/commercial well $500-$1,000+ Higher capacity requires additional review
Well deepening $800-$1,500 Similar to new well permit
Well destruction (sealing) $400-$800 Abandonment permit
Expedited processing 50-100% premium Faster turnaround if available

Note: Fees change periodically. Contact DEH or your contractor for current rates.

What the Permit Process Includes

Application Requirements

  • Property information: APN, address, ownership documentation
  • Site plan: Proposed well location showing setbacks from septic, property lines, structures
  • Well specifications: Proposed depth, casing size, drilling method
  • Contractor information: C-57 license number, insurance
  • Owner authorization: Signature required

Review Process

  1. Completeness check: DEH verifies all documents submitted
  2. Site review: Verifies setback requirements can be met
  3. Zoning verification: Confirms well is allowed on property
  4. Environmental screening: Checks for special review requirements
  5. Approval and permit issuance: Permit card issued for posting

Inspections Included

  • Pre-drilling inspection: May be required in some areas
  • Post-drilling inspection: Verifies well construction meets standards
  • Water quality testing: May be required before domestic use approval

Permit Timeline

Permit Type Typical Processing Time Notes
Standard domestic well 4-8 weeks Most common scenario
Simple well, rural area 3-4 weeks If all requirements easily met
CEQA review required 2-4 months Environmental assessment takes time
Special review zones 6-12 weeks Additional agency coordination
Agricultural/high-capacity 6-12 weeks More detailed review required

What Can Delay Permits

  • Incomplete applications: Missing documents restart the clock
  • Setback violations: Proposed location doesn't meet requirements
  • Property issues: Unclear ownership, easements, deed restrictions
  • Environmental concerns: Sensitive habitat, watershed issues
  • High application volume: Busy periods extend processing

Setback Requirements

San Diego County requires minimum distances between wells and potential contamination sources:

From Well To: Minimum Distance
Septic tank 50 feet
Septic leach field 100 feet
Property lines 5-10 feet (varies)
Buildings 5 feet minimum
Sewer lines 50 feet
Animal enclosures 100 feet

Note: Actual requirements may vary. Site-specific conditions may require increased setbacks.

What Requires a Permit vs. What Doesn't

Permit Required

  • New well drilling
  • Well deepening
  • Well destruction/abandonment (sealing old wells)
  • Modifications to well casing or seal
  • Converting monitoring well to production well

No Permit Required

  • Pump repair or replacement
  • Pressure tank replacement
  • Water treatment system installation
  • Electrical repairs to well system
  • Routine maintenance and service

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a well drilling permit cost in San Diego County?

San Diego County well permit fees typically range from $1,200 to $2,500+ depending on well type and location. Basic domestic well permits start around $1,200-$1,500, while wells requiring CEQA review or in special zones can cost $2,000-$3,500+. Fees include application, plan review, and inspections.

Who handles the well permit process?

Licensed well drilling contractors typically handle the permit process for their customers. At SCWS, permit processing is included in our drilling quotes—we prepare applications, submit to the County, coordinate inspections, and ensure compliance. You sign as the property owner, but we do the work.

How long does it take to get a well permit in San Diego County?

Standard permit processing takes 4-8 weeks from complete application to approval. Simple domestic wells in unincorporated areas may process faster (3-4 weeks). Wells requiring CEQA review or in sensitive areas can take 2-4 months. Expedited processing is available for additional fees.

Do I need a permit to repair or replace a well pump?

No. Pump repairs and replacements don't require permits in San Diego County—only new well drilling, well deepening, well destruction (sealing abandoned wells), and modifications to the well casing/seal require permits.

Can I pull my own well permit?

Technically yes, but it's not recommended. The drilling itself must be done by a licensed C-57 contractor anyway, and permit applications require technical specifications that contractors are better equipped to provide. Most contractors include permit processing in their quotes.

What if my property can't meet setback requirements?

Variance requests are possible but add time and cost. Sometimes well placement can be adjusted to meet requirements. In rare cases, alternative water sources may need to be explored. We evaluate these issues during site assessment before quoting.
