Private Well Drilling: Complete Guide for San Diego & Riverside Homeowners

Published February 1, 2025 | 18 min read

Private well drilling offers Southern California homeowners the ultimate in water independence. This comprehensive guide covers everything you need to know about private well drilling—from initial planning through long-term maintenance.

What Is Private Well Drilling?

Private well drilling is the process of boring a hole deep into the earth to access groundwater for residential, agricultural, or commercial use. Unlike municipal water that travels through public infrastructure, a private well gives you direct access to water beneath your property.

The process involves specialized drilling equipment that penetrates through soil and rock layers until reaching a water-bearing formation called an aquifer. Steel or PVC casing is then installed to keep the well open and prevent contamination, followed by a pump system to bring water to the surface.

Types of Private Wells

There are several types of private wells, though drilled wells are standard for most San Diego and Riverside County properties:

  • Drilled Wells – The most common type in Southern California. Created using rotary or cable tool rigs, typically 200-600 feet deep. Most reliable and protected from contamination.
  • Bored Wells – Shallow wells (less than 100 feet) created with auger equipment. Rare in our area due to deep water tables.
  • Driven Wells – Small-diameter wells driven into the ground. Not suitable for San Diego's geology.
  • Dug Wells – Historical wells excavated by hand. Not permitted for new construction.

For San Diego and Riverside County properties, drilled wells are virtually always the right choice. Our geology—decomposed granite, fractured bedrock, and alluvial deposits—requires professional drilling equipment to reach adequate water-producing zones.

Benefits of Private Well Drilling

Why are so many Southern California homeowners investing in private wells? The benefits are substantial:

No Monthly Water Bills

The most immediate benefit of a private well is eliminating monthly water bills. Rural San Diego County residents often pay $150-$400 per month for municipal water. Over 20 years, that's $36,000-$96,000 in water costs—often more than the well itself.

Your only ongoing costs with a private well are electricity to run the pump (typically $20-$50/month) and periodic maintenance. Most homeowners see a complete return on investment within 8-15 years.

Water Independence During Droughts

California's recurring droughts bring mandatory water restrictions—limited irrigation days, fines for overuse, and pressure to reduce consumption. With a private well:

  • No water rationing or mandatory restrictions
  • Water your landscape when and how you want
  • Maintain your property without worrying about penalties
  • Security against future water shortages

This is especially valuable for properties with orchards, vineyards, horses, or extensive landscaping.

Better Water Quality

Many private well owners report significantly better-tasting water compared to municipal supplies. Well water:

  • Contains no chlorine or chloramine (added to municipal water)
  • Often has beneficial minerals
  • Typically tastes fresher and cleaner
  • Requires no treatment for taste (though filtration may be recommended)

Increased Property Value

A producing well adds substantial value to rural property. In areas where municipal water isn't available, a well is essential for development. Even where city water exists, a well represents:

  • Built-in infrastructure worth $20,000-$40,000+
  • Marketing advantage when selling
  • Proof of viable water supply
  • Reduced utility burden for buyers

Sustainability and Self-Sufficiency

For homeowners prioritizing sustainability and self-reliance, a private well is a cornerstone of independent living. Combined with solar power and proper land management, you control your essential resources.

The Private Well Drilling Process

Here's what to expect when drilling a private well on your property:

Step 1: Site Assessment

Before any drilling begins, a licensed contractor evaluates your property:

  • Review of neighboring well records for depth and production data
  • Geological assessment of your specific location
  • Identification of potential drilling sites
  • Verification of setback requirements (distance from septic, property lines, etc.)
  • Access evaluation for drilling equipment

Step 2: Permitting

All private wells in California require permits. Your contractor handles:

  • Well permit application submission
  • Site plans showing well location and setbacks
  • Permit fee payment (typically $400-$800 in San Diego County)
  • Coordination with county inspectors

Permits typically take 1-2 weeks for approval. Learn more about California well drilling permits.

Step 3: Drilling

Once permitted, drilling begins:

  • Drilling rig is positioned at the approved location
  • Drilling progresses at 50-200 feet per day depending on rock
  • Driller monitors for water-producing zones
  • Drilling continues until adequate water is found
  • Steel or PVC casing is installed
  • Sanitary seal (grouting) prevents surface contamination

Actual drilling typically takes 1-3 days for most residential wells.

Step 4: Development & Testing

After drilling, the well is developed and tested:

  • Well is purged to remove drilling debris
  • Flow rate is measured (gallons per minute)
  • Recovery rate is tested
  • Water sample may be collected for quality testing

Step 5: Pump Installation

The pump system brings water from your well to your home:

  • Submersible pump is lowered into the well
  • Pressure tank is installed
  • Electrical connections and controls are set up
  • System is tested and adjusted for proper pressure

Learn about the best well pump brands for your private well.

Step 6: Connection & Completion

Final steps include:

  • Connection to your home's plumbing system
  • Final county inspection
  • Homeowner training on system operation
  • Warranty documentation

Private Well Requirements & Regulations

California has specific requirements for private wells to protect groundwater quality and ensure safe construction:

Setback Requirements

  • 100 feet minimum from septic tanks and leach fields
  • 50 feet from sewer lines
  • 50 feet from animal enclosures
  • 5 feet from property lines
  • Clear access for drilling equipment

Construction Standards

California well standards (enforced by DWR and county health departments) require:

  • Minimum 6-inch diameter casing for residential wells
  • Steel or PVC casing extending above ground level
  • Sanitary seal (grout) from surface to minimum 50 feet
  • Proper wellhead protection
  • Watertight well cap

Contractor Requirements

Only licensed C-57 contractors may drill private wells in California. This ensures:

  • Proper training and experience
  • Insurance and bonding protection
  • Knowledge of current regulations
  • Accountability to the Contractors State License Board

⚠️ Important: Never hire an unlicensed contractor for private well drilling. Unlicensed work violates California law, may not pass inspection, and leaves you without warranty protection or legal recourse.

Maintaining Your Private Well

Once your private well is drilled, proper maintenance ensures decades of reliable service:

Annual Maintenance

  • Visual inspection of wellhead and cap
  • Check pressure tank operation
  • Monitor pump performance
  • Water quality testing (recommended annually)

Signs Your Well Needs Attention

  • Reduced water pressure or flow
  • Pump running constantly or cycling frequently
  • Sandy, murky, or discolored water
  • Air sputtering from faucets
  • Unusual noises from pump or pressure tank
  • Higher than normal electric bills

If you notice any of these signs, contact a well service professional promptly. Early attention to problems prevents costly repairs.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does private well drilling cost?

Private well drilling in San Diego County typically costs $15,000-$50,000 for complete installation. Most residential wells fall in the $20,000-$35,000 range depending on depth and geology.

Is a private well worth it?

Yes, for most rural properties. A private well eliminates monthly water bills ($100-300+), provides water independence, and adds property value. Most wells pay for themselves in 8-15 years.

Do I need a permit for a private well?

Yes, all private wells in California require permits. Your licensed contractor handles the entire permit process through the county health department.

How long does a private well last?

A properly constructed well can last 30-50+ years. The well itself often lasts indefinitely with maintenance. Pumps typically last 10-25 years.

How deep will my private well be?

Depth varies by location: Valley areas 200-350 feet, foothill communities 300-450 feet, mountain areas 400-600 feet. Your contractor will research neighboring wells for accurate estimates.

Can I drill a well anywhere on my property?

Wells must meet setback requirements: 100+ feet from septic systems, 50 feet from property lines, and accessible to drilling equipment. Your contractor identifies the best location.
