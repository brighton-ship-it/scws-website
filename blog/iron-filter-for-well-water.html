Iron Filters for Well Water: Types, Costs & How They Work

Updated February 2026 | By Southern California Well Service

Quick Answer: Iron filters oxidize dissolved iron so it can be filtered out. Main types: greensand filters ($1,000-$2,500), air injection systems ($1,500-$3,500), and chemical injection ($1,000-$2,000). For low iron (under 3 ppm), a water softener may work. For high iron or iron bacteria, air injection or chemical oxidation is most effective.

Types of Iron in Well Water

Ferrous Iron (Clear Water Iron)

  • Dissolved—water looks clear from tap
  • Turns orange/red when exposed to air
  • Most common form in well water
  • Requires oxidation before filtering

Ferric Iron (Red Water Iron)

  • Already oxidized—water looks rusty
  • Visible orange particles
  • Can be caught by sediment filter

Iron Bacteria

  • Bacteria that feed on iron
  • Creates slimy, reddish-brown deposits
  • Harder to treat—needs oxidation + disinfection
  • May require well treatment too

Organic Iron

  • Iron bound to organic matter (tannins)
  • Often tea-colored water
  • Resistant to standard oxidation
  • May need chemical oxidation

Iron Filter Systems

Greensand Filters

Uses manganese greensand media that oxidizes and captures iron.

  • How it works: Media oxidizes iron on contact, filter traps it
  • Regeneration: Periodic potassium permanganate treatment
  • Capacity: Up to 8-10 ppm iron
  • Cost: $1,000-$2,500
  • Maintenance: Add permanganate solution monthly

Birm Filters

Catalytic media that accelerates iron oxidation using dissolved oxygen.

  • How it works: Speeds natural oxidation, traps particles
  • Requirements: pH above 6.8, dissolved oxygen present
  • Capacity: Up to 3-4 ppm iron
  • Cost: $800-$1,800
  • Maintenance: Periodic backwash only

Air Injection (AIO) Systems

Injects air to oxidize iron, then filters particles.

  • How it works: Air pocket oxidizes iron, media filters it
  • Advantages: Chemical-free, handles high iron
  • Capacity: Up to 15-20 ppm iron
  • Cost: $1,500-$3,500
  • Maintenance: Backwash only—no chemicals

Chemical Oxidation (Chlorine/Peroxide)

Injects oxidizing chemical followed by filtration.

  • How it works: Chemical oxidizes iron, carbon filter removes it
  • Advantages: Most effective for iron bacteria
  • Capacity: Very high iron levels
  • Cost: $1,000-$2,500
  • Maintenance: Refill chemical solution monthly

Water Softeners

Can remove low levels of ferrous iron during softening process.

  • Capacity: Up to 2-3 ppm iron only
  • Caution: High iron fouls softener resin
  • Cost: $800-$2,000 (plus iron removal benefit)
  • Not recommended as primary iron treatment

How to Choose the Right System

Based on Iron Level

Iron Level Recommended System
0.3-2 ppm Birm filter or water softener
2-5 ppm Air injection or greensand
5-10 ppm Air injection or chemical
10+ ppm Chemical injection + filtration

Other Factors

  • Iron bacteria present: Use chemical oxidation
  • Sulfur also present: Air injection handles both
  • Low pH: May need neutralizer before Birm
  • Want chemical-free: Air injection system
  • Limited maintenance: Air injection backwash-only

Water Test First

Always test before selecting a system:

  • Total iron (ppm)
  • pH level
  • Hydrogen sulfide
  • Manganese
  • Hardness

Maintenance Requirements

Birm Filters

  • Automatic backwash (no action needed)
  • Media lasts 5-10 years
  • Lowest maintenance option

Greensand Filters

  • Add potassium permanganate solution every 2-4 weeks
  • Monitor solution tank level
  • Media lasts 5-10 years

Air Injection Systems

  • Automatic backwash cycle
  • Occasional air valve check
  • Media lasts 8-10+ years

Chemical Injection

  • Refill chlorine/peroxide monthly
  • Replace carbon filter annually
  • Most involved maintenance

Frequently Asked Questions

What removes iron from well water?

Oxidation filters (greensand, Birm), air injection systems, chemical oxidation, or water softeners (low levels). The best choice depends on your iron type and level.

How much iron is too much in well water?

EPA secondary standard is 0.3 ppm. Above this causes staining and taste issues. Treatment is recommended above 0.5 ppm.

Will a water softener remove iron?

Low levels only (under 2-3 ppm). Higher iron fouls softener resin. For significant iron, use dedicated iron filtration.

Is iron in well water harmful?

Not a health hazard at typical levels. It's a quality issue—causes staining, metallic taste, clogged fixtures, and laundry problems.

Why is my water still rusty after the iron filter?

Possible causes: iron bacteria (needs chemical treatment), media exhausted, wrong system for your iron type, or very high iron level.
