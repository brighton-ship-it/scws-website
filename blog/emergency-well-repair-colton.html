The Most Common Well Emergencies We See in Colton

The wells that serve Colton's river-bottom and agricultural parcels often draw from the shallow alluvial deposits along the Santa Ana River, and many of these systems have been pumping for decades. Age, mineral-laden groundwater, and steady demand on working properties produce a familiar list of breakdowns:

  • Sudden, complete loss of water. When every fixture goes dry at once, the fault lies with the well system rather than the home's interior plumbing.
  • Submersible pump failure or motor burnout. A pump that has served a property for fifteen or twenty years eventually wears its bearings or burns out, especially under heavy agricultural demand.
  • Pressure tank failure. A waterlogged or ruptured tank makes the pump cycle rapidly, and that short-cycling will quickly ruin the motor if it is not addressed.
  • A well producing sand or running low. Shallow bottomland wells can draw in sand and silt or lose level during dry stretches, leaving the pump pulling grit or air.
  • Electrical and control box failures. Capacitors, relays, and control boxes fail without notice and are a common reason a pump will not start at all.
  • Burst pipe or leak. A cracked drop pipe, failed fitting, or leaking pitless adapter drains pressure and can waste hundreds of gallons.
  • No water after a power outage. When the grid flickers, control boxes and pressure switches do not always reset cleanly, and a tripped breaker or damaged relay can leave you with nothing.

What to Check First Before You Call

A few simple, safe checks can occasionally restore water on the spot, and when they cannot, they help our technician arrive ready to work. Please do not open the well cap, touch wiring while standing in water, or attempt to pull the pump yourself.

  1. Check the breaker. In your main panel, find the breaker marked for the well or pump, switch it all the way off, then back on. If it trips again immediately, leave it off and call us, because that points to an electrical problem.
  2. Read the pressure gauge. The gauge near your pressure tank should normally read between 40 and 60 psi. A reading of zero means the pump is not building any pressure.
  3. Tap the pressure tank. Knock on it from top to bottom; a hollow upper section over a solid lower section is normal, while a tank that sounds solid all the way up is waterlogged.
  4. Listen at the pressure switch. The small box on the pipe by the tank should click when water is called for. No click can indicate a failed switch or no power reaching the pump.
  5. Confirm the grid. Make sure power is on across the property and that any sub-panel or disconnect serving the well is energized, which matters on larger parcels where the well sits far from the house.

What to Do While You Wait for Us

Once you have called, a little preparation keeps your home running and protects your equipment. If the well breaker keeps tripping, leave it off so the motor is not harmed by repeated start attempts. Use the water still stored in your pressure tank and water heater sparingly for drinking and essentials. If your property includes animals or irrigated crops, draw what water you can from stored supplies or a neighbor, since the Inland Empire heat is hard on livestock and plantings alike. Clear safe access to the wellhead, pressure tank, and electrical panel so our technician can begin the moment we arrive.

Frequently Asked Questions

How fast can you reach my property in Colton?

We offer same-day emergency service seven days a week throughout Colton and the surrounding San Bernardino County communities. Call (760) 440-8520 early and we will do everything possible to reach you the same day, often within hours.

The power came back but I still have no water. Why?

A power interruption can trip the well breaker, upset the pressure switch, or damage a control box component. Check the breaker and pressure gauge first, and if water does not return, call us. These post-outage failures are common on Colton's older river-bottom well systems.

My well water suddenly looks sandy or dirty. What should I do?

Shallow wells along the Santa Ana River bottomlands can begin drawing sand or silt as the pump or screen wears or the water level drops. Stop using the water and call us; we can assess the well, the pump's depth, and whether the screen or pump needs attention.

How much does the emergency diagnostic cost?

The emergency diagnostic visit is $125, and that amount is credited toward your repair if you decide to proceed with us. You always receive a clear quote before any work begins.

Can you handle an agricultural or higher-demand well?

Yes. Many Colton-area parcels run higher-capacity pumps for irrigation or animals. We service and replace pumps across the full range of residential and agricultural horsepower and understand how urgent water is for crops and livestock in the heat.

Should I try to pull the pump myself to save money?

No. Pulling a submersible pump from a deep well takes proper rigging and is dangerous without it, and a slip can drop equipment down the well. Let our licensed crew handle it safely; in many cases the underlying problem is an inexpensive electrical part anyway.
