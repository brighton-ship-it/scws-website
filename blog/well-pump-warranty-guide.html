Well Pump Warranty Guide: What's Covered & How to Protect Your Investment

SC

By SCWS Team

February 1, 2026 · 14 min read

Your $3,000 well pump fails after 18 months. The manufacturer says it's not covered. This nightmare scenario plays out more often than you'd think—and it's almost always preventable. Understanding your warranty before something goes wrong could save you thousands of dollars.

A new well pump represents a significant investment—typically $1,500 to $4,500 including installation. Yet many homeowners don't fully understand what their submersible pump warranty covers, what can void it, or how to file a claim when something goes wrong. This comprehensive guide explains everything you need to know about well pump warranties from major manufacturers like Franklin Electric, Grundfos, and Goulds.

Why Well Pump Warranties Matter

Unlike appliances you can easily access and replace, a submersible well pump sits hundreds of feet underground. When a pump fails, you're not just paying for a new pump—you're paying for specialized labor to pull the old pump from deep in your well and install the replacement. This labor can cost $500 to $2,000 or more depending on well depth.

A good well pump warranty can save you thousands of dollars if your pump fails prematurely due to a manufacturing defect. However, warranties vary significantly between manufacturers and product lines. Understanding the details before purchase helps you make informed decisions and avoid costly surprises.

Key Warranty Insight

Most well pump warranties cover parts only, not labor. Since labor is often the largest cost in pump replacement, this means even with a warranty, you may still pay $500-$2,000+ for a covered failure. Understanding this upfront helps set realistic expectations.

Warranty Periods by Manufacturer

The three major submersible pump manufacturers—Franklin Electric, Grundfos, and Goulds (Xylem)—offer varying warranty periods depending on the product line and application. Here's what you can expect from each:

Franklin Electric Pump Warranty

Franklin Electric is one of the most common brands in residential wells, known for reliable motors and wide availability. Their warranty structure includes:

  • Standard Residential Pumps: 1-year limited warranty on motors and pumps
  • Premium Residential (SubDrive): 3-year limited warranty
  • Commercial/Industrial: 1-2 years depending on model
  • Franklin Electric Motors: Separate 1-5 year warranty depending on series

Franklin pump warranty coverage applies to defects in materials and workmanship under normal use. The warranty requires professional installation and proper operating conditions. Franklin's extensive dealer network makes warranty claims relatively straightforward—your installer can often handle the process directly.

Grundfos Pump Warranty

Grundfos, a Danish manufacturer, is known for premium quality and efficiency. Their submersible pump warranty reflects this positioning:

  • SQ/SQE Series (Residential): 2-year standard warranty
  • SP Series (High-capacity): 2-year standard warranty
  • Extended Warranty Program: Available for up to 5 years total
  • Commercial Applications: 1-2 years depending on installation

Grundfos warranties are known for being honored reliably, though the company's premium pricing means you're paying more upfront. Their pumps often outlast warranty periods significantly—many Grundfos units run 15-20+ years—which partially justifies the higher cost.

Goulds (Xylem) Pump Warranty

Goulds, now part of Xylem, has been manufacturing pumps for over 170 years. Their residential submersible warranty includes:

  • Residential Submersible Pumps: 5-year limited warranty (industry-leading)
  • Commercial/Industrial: 1-3 years depending on application
  • Jet Pumps: 2-5 years depending on model
  • Control Boxes: Often warranted separately, typically 1 year

Goulds' 5-year residential warranty is notably longer than competitors, making it an attractive option for homeowners prioritizing warranty protection. However, as with all warranties, coverage depends on proper installation and operating conditions.

What's Covered vs. Not Covered

Understanding exactly what your well pump warranty covers—and doesn't cover—prevents unpleasant surprises when you need to file a claim.

What's Typically Covered

Standard well pump guarantee coverage includes:

  • Manufacturing defects: Flaws in materials or assembly that cause premature failure
  • Motor failure: Electrical or mechanical motor problems under normal operation
  • Impeller defects: Problems with pump stages or impeller assembly
  • Seal failures: Mechanical seal issues that allow water intrusion (if not caused by external factors)
  • Electrical components: Wiring and connections within the pump/motor assembly

What's Typically NOT Covered

Most submersible pump warranty exclusions include:

  • Labor costs: The cost to remove and reinstall the pump
  • Damage from running dry: Pumping without adequate water supply
  • Lightning or power surge damage: Electrical damage from storms or grid problems
  • Sand or sediment damage: Wear from pumping abrasive materials
  • Corrosive water damage: Failure due to aggressive water chemistry
  • Improper installation: Failures traced to installation errors
  • Lack of proper protection devices: Running without required overload protection
  • Normal wear and tear: Expected degradation over time
  • Consequential damages: Water damage, lost crops, or other secondary losses

Common Warranty Pitfall

Many warranty claims are denied because the pump was damaged by conditions outside the pump itself—voltage problems, running dry, or corrosive water. Having your well and electrical system properly evaluated before pump installation helps ensure your warranty remains valid.

How to Maintain Warranty Validity

Your well pump warranty is only as good as your ability to claim it. Taking these steps ensures your warranty remains enforceable:

1. Professional Installation

Nearly all manufacturers require professional installation by a qualified contractor. In California, this means a licensed C-57 (Well Drilling) contractor. DIY installation or installation by unlicensed individuals typically voids the warranty entirely.

2. Proper Sizing and Application

Installing a pump in conditions beyond its design specifications voids the warranty. This includes:

  • Using a pump rated for less depth than your well
  • Exceeding flow rate or pressure specifications
  • Installing in water temperatures beyond rated limits
  • Using standard pumps in high-sand or corrosive conditions

Professional installers ensure proper pump sizing that keeps your warranty intact.

3. Required Protection Devices

Manufacturers require specific protection devices for warranty coverage:

  • Overload protection: Motor overload relays in control box (required)
  • Lightning/surge protection: Highly recommended, sometimes required
  • Low-water cutoff: Required in some applications to prevent dry running
  • Check valve: Prevents backflow that can damage pump

4. Keep Documentation

Maintain these records to support any future warranty claim:

  • Original purchase receipt or invoice
  • Pump model and serial numbers
  • Installation date and contractor information
  • Warranty registration confirmation (if applicable)
  • Maintenance records

5. Register Your Warranty

Some manufacturers offer or require warranty registration. While registration isn't always mandatory, it can:

  • Extend warranty period beyond standard coverage
  • Streamline the claims process
  • Provide proof of purchase date if receipt is lost
  • Enable manufacturer notifications about recalls or updates

Labor vs. Parts Coverage: The Hidden Cost

Here's the reality that catches many homeowners off guard: manufacturer warranties almost never cover labor costs. Let's break down what this means financially:

Typical Warranty Claim Cost Breakdown

Scenario: Pump fails under warranty at 300 feet deep

  • Replacement pump: $0 (covered by warranty)
  • Labor to pull old pump: $600-$1,000
  • Labor to install new pump: $400-$800
  • Trip charge/diagnosis: $100-$200
  • Your out-of-pocket cost: $1,100-$2,000

This is why extended warranties or contractor labor warranties that include labor coverage can be valuable—they cover the cost that typically isn't included in manufacturer warranties.

Extended Warranty Options

Given the significant labor costs involved in any pump replacement, extended warranty programs deserve serious consideration:

Manufacturer Extended Programs

Some manufacturers offer extended warranty programs:

  • Franklin Electric: Extended programs through authorized dealers
  • Grundfos: Extended warranty options at purchase
  • Various: Third-party warranty companies partner with manufacturers

Cost typically runs $100-$400 for an additional 2-3 years of parts coverage.

Contractor Warranty Programs

Many well service contractors offer their own warranty programs that may include:

  • Labor warranty: Coverage for installation labor if pump fails
  • Extended parts coverage: Beyond manufacturer warranty
  • Annual maintenance plans: That include pump coverage
  • Package deals: Combining parts and labor protection

Home Warranty Companies

Some home warranty programs cover well pumps, but read the fine print carefully:

  • Coverage limits may be lower than actual replacement cost
  • Depth limitations (some won't cover pumps below certain depths)
  • Pre-existing condition exclusions
  • Requirement to use their contractors (who may lack well expertise)

Common Warranty Claim Issues

Understanding why warranty claims get denied helps you avoid these pitfalls:

1. Improper Installation Documentation

Claims are denied when the manufacturer determines installation was improper—wrong wire gauge, incorrect voltage, missing protection devices, or depth set incorrectly. Always ensure your installer provides documentation of proper installation procedures.

2. Environmental Damage

Pump failure caused by well conditions—not the pump itself—isn't covered:

  • Sand damage: Abrasive wear on impellers and seals
  • Corrosive water: Acidic or high-mineral water attacking components
  • Scale buildup: Mineral deposits reducing performance
  • Dry running: Operating without sufficient water

A water quality test before installation can identify potentially warranty-voiding conditions.

3. Electrical Problems

Power-related failures are typically excluded:

  • Lightning strikes: Direct or nearby strikes damaging motor windings
  • Power surges: Grid problems sending high voltage
  • Low voltage: Brownouts causing motor overheating
  • Phase loss: (Three-phase systems) Single-phasing damage

Installing surge protection and ensuring proper electrical supply protects both your pump and your warranty.

4. Missing Proof of Purchase

Without documentation proving when and where the pump was purchased, claims become extremely difficult. Keep copies of invoices in multiple places.

Questions to Ask Before Purchase

Before agreeing to a pump installation, ask these warranty-related questions:

Essential Warranty Questions

  1. What is the manufacturer's warranty period? Get specifics for the exact model being installed.
  2. Does the warranty cover just parts, or labor too? Most are parts-only—know what you're getting.
  3. Do you offer any labor warranty? Ask if the contractor warranties their installation work.
  4. What documentation will I receive? Ensure you'll get serial numbers, model info, and installation date.
  5. Is warranty registration required? If so, will the contractor handle it?
  6. What protection devices are required? Ensure all warranty-required devices are included.
  7. Are extended warranties available? Get pricing and terms before deciding.
  8. Who handles warranty claims? Will the contractor process claims or are you on your own?
  9. Are there conditions that could void the warranty? Understand specific exclusions for your situation.
  10. What's my cost if the pump fails under warranty? Get a realistic estimate of labor-only costs.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long is a typical well pump warranty?

Most residential well pump warranties range from 1 to 5 years depending on the manufacturer and product line. Franklin Electric offers 1-5 year warranties, Grundfos provides 2-5 years, and Goulds offers 1-5 years. Premium and commercial-grade pumps typically have longer warranty periods than economy models.

Does a well pump warranty cover labor costs?

Most manufacturer warranties cover parts only—not labor. Since submersible pump replacement can cost $500-$2,000+ in labor alone, this is a significant consideration. Some contractors offer labor warranties separately, and extended warranty programs may include labor coverage. Always ask about both parts and labor coverage before installation.

What voids a well pump warranty?

Common warranty-voiding conditions include improper installation, running the pump dry (no water), voltage problems or lightning damage, using the pump in unsuitable water conditions (high sand, corrosive water), operating without proper overload protection, and unauthorized modifications. Professional installation by a licensed contractor helps ensure warranty validity.

Can I get an extended warranty on my well pump?

Yes, extended warranties are available through several channels. Some manufacturers offer extended warranty programs for an additional cost. Many well service contractors provide their own extended coverage plans. Third-party home warranty companies sometimes cover well pumps, though with limitations. Extended warranties typically add 2-5 years beyond the standard warranty period.

How do I file a well pump warranty claim?

To file a warranty claim, you'll typically need proof of purchase (receipt or invoice), the pump's serial number and model, documentation of the failure, and in many cases, a diagnosis from a licensed contractor. Contact your installer first—they often handle warranty claims directly with the manufacturer. Keep all installation paperwork and register your pump if the manufacturer offers registration.
