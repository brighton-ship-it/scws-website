Well Permits in San Diego County: Complete Guide

Updated February 2026 | By Southern California Well Service

Quick Answer: All new wells in San Diego County require permits from the Department of Environmental Health (DEH). Permit fees: $500-$1,200. Processing time: 2-4 weeks. Key setbacks: 100 ft from septic fields, 50 ft from septic tanks. Licensed C-57 contractors handle the permit process.

When Is a Well Permit Required?

Permits Required For:

  • New water well construction—domestic, irrigation, or agricultural
  • Monitoring wells—for groundwater studies
  • Geothermal wells—closed and open loop systems
  • Well modification—deepening, redrilling, casing changes
  • Well destruction—sealing abandoned wells
  • Pump test wells—temporary water extraction

Permits NOT Required For:

  • Pump replacement (same well, no structural changes)
  • Pressure tank replacement
  • Above-ground equipment repair
  • Water treatment system installation

What the Application Requires

  • Completed permit application form
  • Site plan showing proposed well location
  • Proof of property ownership or authorization
  • Licensed C-57 contractor information
  • Setback compliance documentation
  • Parcel map or assessor's parcel number
  • Permit fee payment

Setback Requirements

San Diego County requires minimum distances between wells and potential contamination sources:

Feature Minimum Distance Notes
Septic leach field 100 feet Most restrictive
Septic tank 50 feet From tank only
Sewer line 50 feet Public or private
Animal enclosure 100 feet Horses, livestock, etc.
Property line 5 feet May vary by area
Building foundation 5 feet Any structure
Surface water 50-100 feet Streams, ponds
Existing well Varies Depends on use

Important Notes

  • Measurements are to the center of the proposed well
  • Some areas have additional local requirements
  • Variances may be possible in limited cases
  • We verify all setbacks before submitting applications

Variance Process

If your property can't meet standard setbacks, a variance may be possible:

  • Requires additional engineering review
  • May require deeper well construction
  • Adds time and cost to the permit process
  • Not guaranteed—depends on specific circumstances

Application Process

Step 1: Site Evaluation

  • Contractor visits property
  • Verifies setback compliance
  • Identifies best well location
  • Assesses access for drilling equipment

Step 2: Application Preparation

  • Complete permit application forms
  • Prepare site plan with measurements
  • Gather required documentation
  • Contractor signs as responsible party

Step 3: Submission

  • Submit to DEH with fee payment
  • Can be done online or in person
  • Receipt provided for tracking

Step 4: County Review

  • DEH reviews application for completeness
  • Verifies setback compliance
  • May request additional information
  • Approves or denies permit

Step 5: Permit Issuance

  • Permit valid for specified period (usually 1 year)
  • Must be posted at job site during drilling
  • Work must follow approved plans

Step 6: Construction & Inspection

  • Well drilled according to permit specifications
  • County inspector verifies construction
  • Sanitary seal installation verified

Step 7: Testing & Completion

  • Water quality testing (bacteria required)
  • Well completion report filed
  • Final approval from DEH

Common Reasons for Permit Denial

Setback Violations

  • Too close to septic system
  • Property too small for required distances
  • Neighboring septic within setback zone

Documentation Issues

  • Missing site plan or measurements
  • Incorrect property information
  • Unlicensed or expired contractor license

Environmental Concerns

  • Protected habitat areas
  • Groundwater contamination zones
  • Special management areas

How to Avoid Denial

  • Work with experienced contractor who knows local requirements
  • Get site evaluation before committing to property purchase
  • Verify all setbacks before application
  • Submit complete, accurate applications

Well Destruction Permits

Unused or abandoned wells must be properly destroyed to prevent groundwater contamination.

When Required

  • Well is no longer in use
  • Property is being developed
  • Well is damaged beyond repair
  • Required by county or during property sale

Destruction Process

  1. Obtain destruction permit from DEH
  2. Remove pump and equipment
  3. Fill well with approved materials (cement grout)
  4. County inspection
  5. File completion report

Destruction Costs

  • Permit fee: $400-$600
  • Destruction work: $1,500-$5,000 (depends on depth)
  • Total: $2,000-$5,500 typically

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a well permit cost in San Diego County?

$500-$1,200 depending on well type. New water wells: $800-$1,000. Modifications: $400-$600. Destruction: $400-$600. Fees paid to DEH with application.

How long does it take to get a well permit?

Standard processing: 2-4 weeks. Complex applications: 4-8 weeks. Working with an experienced contractor who submits complete applications speeds the process.

Can I drill a well without a permit?

No. All new wells require permits in San Diego County. Unpermitted wells can result in fines, required destruction, and problems selling the property.

What are the setback requirements?

Key setbacks: 100 ft from septic leach fields, 50 ft from septic tanks, 50 ft from sewer lines, 100 ft from animal enclosures, 5 ft from property lines.

Can I get a variance if I can't meet setbacks?

Sometimes. Variances require additional engineering review and aren't guaranteed. Deeper well construction may be required. Discuss options with your contractor.

Do I need a permit to replace my well pump?

No. Pump replacement doesn't require a permit if you're not modifying the well structure. Same for pressure tanks and above-ground equipment.

How long is a well permit valid?

Typically 1 year from issuance. If work isn't completed, you may need to apply for an extension or new permit.

Does my contractor handle the permit?

Yes. Licensed C-57 contractors typically handle all permit applications, inspections, and completion reports as part of the project.
