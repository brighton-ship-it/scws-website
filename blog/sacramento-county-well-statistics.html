Sacramento County Well Overview

Sacramento County sits atop two major groundwater basins - the North American Subbasin (north of American River) and South American Subbasin (south of American River). These alluvial basins provide excellent groundwater resources but face management challenges from population growth and historical overdraft.

Key Sacramento County Facts

  • 185,000+ residents rely on private wells or small water systems using groundwater
  • 12% of Sacramento County population depends on domestic wells
  • 994 square miles of county area
  • Two major SGMA-regulated basins: North American and South American Subbasins
  • Sacramento Groundwater Authority (SGA) manages basin north of American River
  • Sacramento Central Groundwater Authority (SCGA) manages south basin
  • Average well age: 32 years
  • 1.5 million acre-feet estimated basin storage capacity

South American Subbasin (South of American River)

  • Basin area: 218 square miles
  • Priority: Medium (SGMA)
  • Estimated storage: 800,000+ acre-feet
  • Communities: Elk Grove, Wilton, Galt, Herald, Rancho Cordova (south)
  • Sustainability deadline: 2042

North American Subbasin (North of American River)

  • Basin area: 341 square miles
  • Priority: Medium (SGMA)
  • Estimated storage: 700,000+ acre-feet
  • Communities: Fair Oaks, Carmichael, Citrus Heights, Rio Linda
  • Managing agency: Sacramento Groundwater Authority

Frequently Asked Questions

How many wells are in Sacramento County?

Sacramento County has approximately 42,300 registered wells as of 2026, including about 28,500 active domestic wells.

What is the average well depth?

The average residential well depth is 285 feet, ranging from 195 feet in Rio Linda to 320 feet in Folsom.

Is Sacramento groundwater sustainable?

Both Sacramento County basins are Medium Priority under SGMA with Groundwater Sustainability Plans targeting sustainability by 2042.

How much does a well cost?

Average complete well system cost is $38,500 in 2026, ranging from $22,000 to $85,000.
