Why Norco Horse Properties Need Wells

Municipal water can technically serve a horse property, but it often doesn't make economic or practical sense. Here's why most serious Norco equestrians choose private wells:

The True Cost of Watering Horses on City Water

A single horse drinks 5-10 gallons of water daily—more in Norco's hot summers. Add bathing, stall cleaning, arena watering, and pasture irrigation, and a modest 4-horse property easily uses 500+ gallons daily for equestrian purposes alone. At current municipal rates, that's thousands of dollars annually just for horse water. A properly designed well provides essentially unlimited water at minimal ongoing cost once installed.

Freedom from Water Restrictions

California's recurring droughts mean municipal water restrictions that can devastate horse properties. You can't tell a horse to drink less, and dust control in arenas isn't optional for horse health. Well owners in Norco irrigated their pastures and watered their arenas throughout recent droughts while neighbors on city water watched their properties turn to dust.

Property Value and Marketability

When selling a Norco horse property, a producing well is a major selling point. Buyers understand the value of water independence, and properties with wells command premium prices. The investment in drilling often returns 100% or more when you sell.

Understanding Norco's Groundwater

Norco's location along the Santa Ana River provides generally favorable conditions for well drilling, though depth and production vary significantly across town.

The Santa Ana River Aquifer

The Santa Ana River—California's largest river system south of the Tehachapi Mountains—has deposited extensive alluvial sediments that form productive aquifers beneath Norco. Properties closer to the river corridor typically enjoy shallower wells and better production, while those in the northern hills require deeper drilling through more challenging geology.

  • Central Norco / Sixth Street corridor: 200-300 feet
  • Norco Hills / Northern areas: 300-400 feet
  • Hidden Valley area: 250-350 feet
  • Western Norco near Corona: 200-300 feet
  • Eastern Norco near Riverside: 200-350 feet

Water Quality in Norco

Norco groundwater is generally good quality, suitable for both household use and livestock without treatment. Some areas experience moderate hardness that may warrant softening for household fixtures, but horses actually benefit from the minerals in hard water. Our comprehensive water testing verifies your water is safe and identifies any treatment recommendations.

Designing Wells for Horse Properties

A well for a Norco horse property requires different planning than a simple residential well. We work with you to design a system that meets your actual needs.

Calculating Your Water Needs

Every horse property is different. We help you calculate total water demand based on:

  • Number of horses – 5-10 gallons/day drinking, plus bathing and stall cleaning
  • Arena size and use – Dust control typically requires 50-100 gallons per watering
  • Pasture irrigation – Varies widely based on acreage and grass type
  • Household use – Standard residential demands
  • Future expansion – Planning to add horses or amenities?

Pump Sizing for Equestrian Use

Horse properties need more than minimum residential flow rates. While a 3-5 GPM pump might technically serve a household, a property with horses typically needs 10-20 GPM to handle simultaneous demands—filling water troughs while running household fixtures, for example. We size pumps for your actual usage patterns, not just minimum requirements.

Separate Irrigation Systems

Many Norco horse properties benefit from separate irrigation lines running directly from the well to arenas, pastures, and wash racks. This allows high-volume irrigation without affecting household water pressure and lets you use untreated well water for outdoor purposes while softening household water if desired.

Frequently Asked Questions

How deep are wells in Norco, CA?

Wells in Norco typically range from 150 to 400 feet deep. Properties along the Santa Ana River corridor and lower elevations often find water at 150-250 feet, while those in the hillier northern areas or near the Cleveland National Forest may require 300-400 feet or more.

How much does it cost to drill a well in Norco?

Well drilling in Norco typically costs between $15,000 and $35,000 for a complete installation including pump and pressure system. Horse properties with higher water demands may require larger systems costing $25,000-$45,000. Our free site assessments provide accurate estimates for your specific property.

How much water do I need for a horse property in Norco?

Horses require 5-10 gallons of water per day per horse, plus water for washing, arena dust control, and pasture irrigation. A typical Norco horse property with 2-4 horses needs a well producing at least 5-10 gallons per minute. Larger operations require proportionally more capacity.

Can I use well water for my horse arena in Norco?

Absolutely. Well water is ideal for arena dust control and can significantly reduce water costs for horse property owners. We can design systems with dedicated irrigation lines and appropriate pumps to handle arena watering needs without affecting household water pressure.

What areas near Norco does SCWS serve?

We serve all of Norco plus Corona, Riverside, Eastvale, Jurupa Valley, Mira Loma, Pedley, Glen Avon, and throughout western Riverside County.
