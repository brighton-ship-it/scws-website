California Well Statistics Overview (2026)

California relies heavily on groundwater, with 85% of Californians depending on groundwater for some portion of their water needs and groundwater supplying up to 60% of the state's water during drought years.

~2M Total Water Wells

Key Statewide Statistics

  • Total water wells: Approximately 2 million water wells tap California's groundwater (CA DWR)
  • Annual new construction: 7,000 to 15,000 new wells constructed each year (CA DWR)
  • Monitored wells: Over 35,000 wells in the Water Data Library with continuous monitoring (CA DWR)

Permit Requirements

All water well construction in California requires:

  • Permit from Local Enforcement Agency (LEA): Typically county environmental health or water districts
  • Licensed C-57 Well Drilling Contractor: Required for all well construction, modification, or destruction
  • Well Completion Report: Must be filed within 60 days of completion
  • Compliance with California Well Standards: Bulletin 74-81 and 74-90 requirements
3.2% — Statewide permit denial rate for new well applications (2026-2026)

Success Rates & Well Completion Statistics

Not all well drilling attempts result in successful, productive wells. Success rates vary significantly by region and geology.

85-95% Success Rate (Alluvial Basins)
65-85% Success Rate (Hard Rock)
3.2% Permit Denial Rate
15-25 gpm Typical Residential Yield

Factors Affecting Success Rate

  • Geology: Alluvial basins (sand/gravel) have highest success; fractured rock is unpredictable
  • Well Siting: Professional hydrogeological surveys improve success by 15-25%
  • Depth: Deeper wells generally more successful but significantly more expensive
  • Seasonal Timing: Wells drilled during wet season may show higher initial yields
  • Proximity to Other Wells: High well density can affect yields

Minimum Yield Requirements

  • Single-family residential: 1-5 gpm typically sufficient with storage
  • Small farms/homesteads: 10-25 gpm recommended
  • Agricultural irrigation: 200-2,000+ gpm for large operations
  • Municipal wells: 500-5,000+ gpm for public supply

Historical Trends (2015-2026)

California well drilling has evolved significantly over the past decade, driven by drought cycles, regulatory changes under SGMA, and shifting water economics.

Key Trends

  • Increasing Depths: Average residential well depth increased 47 feet from 238 ft (2015) to 285 ft (2026)
  • Rising Costs: Total installation costs up approximately 45% in 10 years, outpacing inflation
  • SGMA Implementation: Since 2026, critically overdrafted basins face pumping restrictions
  • Drought Spikes: 2026-2026 saw 30-50% increase in permit applications
  • Well Deepening: Growing trend to deepen existing wells rather than drill new
  • Monitoring Growth: 400+ new monitoring wells installed countywide in San Diego alone

Frequently Asked Questions

How many water wells are in California?

California has approximately 2 million water wells according to the California Department of Water Resources. This includes domestic, agricultural, municipal, and industrial wells across the state. The actual number may be higher as some historical wells are undocumented.

How many new wells are drilled in California each year?

Between 7,000 and 15,000 new wells are constructed in California annually. The number varies based on drought conditions, economic factors, and agricultural demand. During the 2026-2026 drought, the number exceeded 13,000 annually.

What is the average depth of a water well in California?

The average residential well depth in California is 285 feet as of 2026, according to DWR Well Completion Reports. This represents an increase of 47 feet over the past decade. Agricultural wells average 400-600+ feet, and some exceed 1,000 feet.

How much does it cost to drill a well in California?

Well drilling costs in California range from $15,000 to $50,000+ for residential wells and $50,000 to $250,000 for agricultural wells. Costs depend on depth, geology, location, and required equipment. The statewide average for a complete residential installation is approximately $25,000-$35,000.

Which California counties have the most wells?

The Central Valley counties lead in well density: Fresno (~95,000), Kern (~88,000), Tulare (~82,000), Merced (~55,000), and Kings (~48,000) have the highest concentrations of wells, primarily for agricultural use. These five counties account for over 25% of all California wells.

What percentage of Californians rely on groundwater?

85% of Californians depend on groundwater for some portion of their water needs. During drought years, groundwater supplies up to 60% of the state's total water supply.

What is SGMA and how does it affect well drilling?

The Sustainable Groundwater Management Act (SGMA), passed in 2014, requires medium- and high-priority groundwater basins to achieve sustainability by 2040-2042. In critically overdrafted basins, new well permits may be restricted, and some existing wells may face pumping limitations.
