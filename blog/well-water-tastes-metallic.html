A metallic taste in your well water is more than unpleasant—it can indicate mineral content that affects your plumbing, appliances, and possibly your health. The most common culprits are iron and manganese, naturally occurring minerals that are especially common in Southern California groundwater.

Common Causes

  • High Iron Content: Iron is the most common cause of metallic taste. Even at levels safe for consumption, it creates a distinctive taste.
  • Manganese: Often found alongside iron, manganese adds a metallic or bitter taste and can darken water.
  • Copper from Plumbing: Copper pipes can leach into water, especially if it sits in pipes overnight or if water is acidic.
  • Low pH (Acidic Water): Acidic water dissolves metals from pipes and well components, adding metallic taste.
  • Zinc or Lead: Older plumbing with galvanized pipes or lead solder can contribute metallic tastes.
  • Iron Bacteria: These bacteria feed on iron and can create off-tastes along with discoloration.

Warning Signs to Watch For

  • Metallic or penny-like taste
  • Taste worse after water sits in pipes
  • Staining on fixtures (red/orange for iron, black for manganese)
  • Water has slight color or cloudiness
  • Tea or coffee tastes off
  • White clothing develops rust stains

How to Fix This Problem

1. Get a Water Test

A comprehensive water test identifies exactly which minerals are present and at what levels. This guides treatment decisions.

2. Test pH Level

If water is acidic (pH below 7), it may be dissolving metals from plumbing. A neutralizer can correct this.

3. Install Iron/Manganese Filter

Oxidizing filters, greensand filters, or air injection systems remove these minerals effectively.

4. Consider Water Softener

For moderate iron levels (under 3 ppm), a water softener may be sufficient. Higher levels need dedicated iron removal.

5. Check Plumbing Age

If copper or metallic taste started recently, investigate plumbing. Corroding pipes may need replacement.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is metallic tasting well water safe to drink?

Usually yes, if the cause is iron or manganese at typical levels. However, water should be tested to rule out harmful contaminants like lead.

How do I remove metallic taste from well water?

Treatment depends on the cause. Iron filters, water softeners, or pH neutralizers can all address metallic taste depending on what's causing it.

Why does my water taste metallic in the morning?

Water sitting in pipes overnight has more contact time with metal plumbing. Running water for 30 seconds before use often reduces the taste.

Can iron in well water make you sick?

Iron at normal levels isn't harmful and is actually a dietary nutrient. However, very high levels (usually indicated by visible rust color) may cause digestive issues.
