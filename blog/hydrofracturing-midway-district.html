What is Hydrofracturing?

Hydrofracturing is a well rehabilitation technique that uses high-pressure water to clean and expand existing fractures in the bedrock surrounding your well. This process:

  • Clears mineral deposits and sediment blocking water flow
  • Opens new pathways for groundwater to reach your well
  • Can increase well yield by 2-10 times in many cases
  • Extends the life of your existing well

Unlike oil and gas fracking, well hydrofracturing uses only clean water—no chemicals are involved.

Signs Your Midway District Well Needs Hydrofracturing

  • Declining water production over time
  • Well runs dry during peak usage
  • Slow recovery rate after pumping
  • Pump cycling on and off frequently
  • Lower water pressure than previous years

The Hydrofracturing Process

  1. Initial Assessment: We evaluate your well's current yield and condition
  2. Equipment Setup: Specialized high-pressure equipment is positioned at your well
  3. Isolation: Packers seal off sections of the well for targeted treatment
  4. Pressurization: Water is pumped at 500-3,000 PSI to fracture bedrock
  5. Development: Well is cleaned and tested for improved yield

Is Hydrofracturing Right for a Midway District Property?

For a parcel inside the Midway District or the adjacent flatland neighborhoods (the area around the Midway-Pacific Highway community plan, Loma Portal, and the Sports Arena redevelopment zone), the honest answer is no. How Hydrofracturing Actually Works

Hydrofracturing (often called hydrofracking) is a well-rehabilitation technique used on wells drilled into bedrock. The crew lowers an inflatable packer into the borehole and seals off a target section. High-pressure water — clean water, no chemicals — is then injected into that isolated zone at roughly 1,000 to 3,000 PSI. The pressure flushes out the silt, clay, and mineral scale that has plugged the rock's natural fractures over the years, and it widens those existing seams so groundwater can travel into the well more freely.

The result, on a good candidate well, is a meaningful jump in yield — commonly two to five times the pre-treatment flow. A well that trickled at one gallon per minute can sometimes recover to three, five, or more. The process is non-destructive to the casing when done by a licensed crew, takes most of a working day, and costs a fraction of drilling new.

Why This Matters for Rural San Diego County, Not the Midway District

San Diego County's backcountry sits on a basement of granitic and metamorphic rock — the same batholith that forms the Cuyamaca and Laguna mountains. Wells out there are drilled into that hard, fractured granite, and their yield depends entirely on intercepting water-bearing seams. When those seams clog or when a well was drilled in a tight spot to begin with, hydrofracturing is the proven fix. That geology is completely different from the river-valley fill under the Midway District.

So if you own a home in Ramona, Julian, Santa Ysabel, or out toward Descanso and your well is fading, you are a far better hydrofracturing candidate than any address inside the city core. The technique was built for exactly your situation.

Common Causes of Low Yield on Rural San Diego Wells

  • Mineral and biofilm fouling — iron and manganese deposits, plus bacterial slime, slowly seal the fractures that feed the well.
  • Drought drawdown — after dry winters, the regional water table drops and shallow fractures go dry, leaving only the deeper seams producing.
  • A well drilled into tight rock — not every borehole hit a generous fracture network; some new wells underperform from day one.
  • Sediment infill — decomposed granite (locally called “DG”) sloughs into the borehole and chokes the lower screen or open hole.

What to Check Before You Call

  • Pull your original well completion report, if you have it — total depth, static water level, and the driller's reported yield tell us whether the well is in bedrock and a candidate.
  • Note whether the problem is gradual (declining yield, a hydrofracturing question) or sudden (likely a pump or pressure-switch failure instead).
  • Check your pressure tank's air charge and your pressure switch — many “low water” complaints are really a $150–$350 switch or a waterlogged tank, not a dry well.
  • Listen for the pump short-cycling, and time how long the well takes to recover after heavy use.

When to Call a Professional

Call us when yield has declined steadily, when the well runs dry under normal household demand and is slow to recover, or before you commit to the expense of drilling a replacement well. Do homes in the Midway District have water wells?

Virtually none. The Midway District is fully served by City of San Diego municipal water. Private wells in our service area are on rural parcels in the county backcountry, not in this urban corridor.

Then why would I look into hydrofracturing here?

Most people researching “hydrofracturing San Diego” actually own a well outside the city. If that is you, hydrofracturing can restore yield to a fractured-rock well for a fraction of the cost of a new one. If you truly are inside the Midway District, your better path is plumbing or treatment, not well work.

How much does hydrofracturing cost?

Typically $3,000 to $8,000, depending on well depth and how many zones we isolate and treat. A new well, by contrast, runs $18,000 to $42,000.

How long does the treatment take and how soon do I see results?

The injection itself takes about four to eight hours. We run a flow test before and after, and improved yield is usually measurable within 24 to 48 hours once the well settles.

Is hydrofracturing safe for my well and my water?

Yes. We use only clean water under pressure — no chemicals. Performed by a licensed C-57 contractor with proper packers, it does not harm the casing, and it is a routine, well-established rehabilitation method.

What if hydrofracturing will not help my well?

We tell you. Our $125 diagnostic determines candidacy first. If your well is in loose sediment or simply tapped out, we will recommend a more honest fix — a deeper pump, a new well, or treatment — rather than a treatment that will not pay off.
