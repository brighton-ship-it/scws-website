Geology and Groundwater in Johnson Valley

Johnson Valley sits in an area with deep bedrock, typically requiring wells to penetrate granite or metamorphic formations. The average depth of 406 feet reflects the need to drill through hard rock layers to reach productive aquifers. These deeper wells often require air rotary or DTH (down-the-hole) hammer drilling methods and tend to have lower yields but more stable water quality. Well depths in the area vary significantly—from 91 to 840 feet—reflecting diverse geological conditions across the region. Shallower wells often indicate alluvial areas, while deeper wells suggest locations over bedrock or in zones with lower water tables.

The average static water level (depth to water when the pump is off) in Johnson Valley wells is approximately 258 feet below ground surface. This indicates the natural resting level of the water table in the area.
