What is Hydrofracturing?

Hydrofracturing is a well rehabilitation technique that uses high-pressure water to clean and expand existing fractures in the bedrock surrounding your well. This process:

  • Clears mineral deposits and sediment blocking water flow
  • Opens new pathways for groundwater to reach your well
  • Can increase well yield by 2-10 times in many cases
  • Extends the life of your existing well

Unlike oil and gas fracking, well hydrofracturing uses only clean water—no chemicals are involved.

Signs Your Lake Gregory Well Needs Hydrofracturing

  • Declining water production over time
  • Well runs dry during peak usage
  • Slow recovery rate after pumping
  • Pump cycling on and off frequently
  • Lower water pressure than previous years

The Hydrofracturing Process

  1. Initial Assessment: We evaluate your well's current yield and condition
  2. Equipment Setup: Specialized high-pressure equipment is positioned at your well
  3. Isolation: Packers seal off sections of the well for targeted treatment
  4. Pressurization: Water is pumped at 500-3,000 PSI to fracture bedrock
  5. Development: Well is cleaned and tested for improved yield

Hydrofracturing for Low-Yield Wells in Lake Gregory

Lake Gregory sits high in the San Bernardino Mountains near Crestline, where private water wells are drilled directly into hard granitic and fractured crystalline bedrock. That geology is exactly the kind of formation where hydrofracturing tends to deliver its best results. If your Lake Gregory well has slowed to a trickle during the dry summer months, or it simply never produced the gallons-per-minute you hoped for, high-pressure hydrofracturing may restore and even increase your flow without the cost and disruption of drilling a brand-new well.

Mountain wells around Crestline draw their water from cracks and seams in the rock rather than from a loose sand-and-gravel aquifer. Over the years those fractures clog with fine sediment, mineral scale, and drilling residue, and the natural water pathways narrow. Hydrofracturing reopens and extends them, and in a strong bedrock setting like Lake Gregory it is one of the most cost-effective tools a well owner has.

How Hydrofracturing Works on a Crestline Mountain Well

The technique is straightforward but requires specialized equipment. Our crew lowers an inflatable packer into your well casing and seals off a target zone below the water-bearing fractures. We then inject clean water at pressures that typically run from 1,500 to over 3,000 PSI. That pressure pushes into the existing cracks in the granite, flushing out the silt and scale that have accumulated and physically widening the seams so groundwater can move more freely toward the borehole. We often treat several zones in a single well, moving the packer up the casing to open fractures at different depths. The entire process usually takes four to eight hours, and no chemicals are ever used—only water.

Why Lake Gregory Wells Are Strong Candidates

Not every well benefits equally from hydrofracturing, and we are upfront about that. The ideal candidate is a well completed in hard, fractured rock—precisely the decomposed granite and crystalline bedrock that underlies the Crestline and Lake Gregory area. Wells drilled into deep loose sand or gravel valley fill are far weaker candidates, because there are no rock fractures to open. Lake Gregory's mountain setting puts most local wells squarely in the favorable category. When a well here is "going dry," the problem is frequently restricted fractures rather than a vanished water table, and that is a problem hydrofracturing is built to solve.

Signs Your Lake Gregory Well May Need Treatment

  • Steadily declining yield — the well produced more water a few years ago than it does today
  • Runs dry under normal household demand — you have to ration showers, laundry, and irrigation
  • Very slow recovery — after the pump runs, the water level takes hours to come back
  • A disappointing new well — a recently drilled mountain well came in with low flow even though it hit rock
  • Seasonal shortfalls — the well struggles every late summer and fall as mountain groundwater drops

Realistic Results in San Bernardino County Bedrock

In our experience treating fractured granite wells across the San Bernardino Mountains, hydrofracturing improves yield in roughly 70 to 80 percent of wells, with typical gains of two to five times the pre-treatment flow rate. Some wells jump even more; a few barely respond. We never promise a specific number, because every fracture network is different. What we do promise is an honest assessment and documented before-and-after flow testing so you can see exactly what the treatment accomplished. Wells in genuinely hard fractured rock—the norm at Lake Gregory—respond best.

What to Expect During Your Service

  1. Diagnostic and pre-treatment flow test — we measure your current yield as a baseline. The $125 diagnostic fee is credited toward the work if you proceed.
  2. Video inspection — a downhole camera survey checks your casing condition and helps us locate the fracture zones worth targeting
  3. Hydrofracturing — high-pressure injection at the target zones, generally four to eight hours on site
  4. Post-treatment flow test — we verify the improvement, usually within 24 to 48 hours as the well settles
  5. Results report — a clear before-and-after comparison of your well's performance

Hydrofracturing Cost vs. Drilling a New Well

For most Lake Gregory homeowners, the math strongly favors trying hydrofracturing first. The treatment generally runs $3,000 to $8,000. A new turnkey well in the mountains—drilling, casing, pump, and hookup—typically lands between $18,000 and $42,000, and mountain bedrock drilling can push toward the high end because of the hard rock and steep, narrow access common around Crestline. If you also need pump work, a replacement pump usually runs $2,500 to $5,500. Because hydrofracturing costs a fraction of a new well, it is the logical first step whenever the existing borehole and casing are sound.

When We Recommend Deepening or Redrilling Instead

Hydrofracturing is not always the answer, and we will tell you when it is not. If your casing is cracked or collapsed, if a downhole camera shows the well has filled with collapsed rock, or if the well was simply drilled too shallow to reach reliable water, then deepening the existing borehole or drilling a new well may be the smarter long-term investment. Sometimes the best plan is hydrofracturing combined with a new pump sized correctly for the restored yield. We give you the trade-offs honestly rather than selling you the most expensive option.

Protecting Your Mountain Water Supply Long Term

A successful hydrofracturing treatment can add many productive years to a Lake Gregory well, but pairing it with good habits protects that investment. We recommend an annual flow check, periodic monitoring of your static water level, and a properly sized pump and pressure tank matched to the restored yield. Mountain wells around Crestline also face seasonal swings as winter snowmelt recharges the fractures and late-summer levels drop, so a well that has been opened up by hydrofracturing gives you a wider safety margin heading into the dry season. If your water shows heavy mineral content, light treatment can also slow the scale buildup that narrows fractures over time. Caught early, a declining mountain well is almost always cheaper to rehabilitate than to replace.

Frequently Asked Questions About Hydrofracturing in Lake Gregory

Will hydrofracturing work on my granite well at Lake Gregory?

Wells drilled into the hard granitic and fractured crystalline bedrock common around Lake Gregory and Crestline are strong candidates. Hydrofracturing opens the existing rock fractures that feed these wells, and in our experience roughly 70 to 80 percent of fractured-rock wells in San Bernardino County see improved yield. We confirm suitability with a diagnostic and video inspection before recommending the work.

How much does hydrofracturing cost in the Lake Gregory area?

Hydrofracturing typically costs between $3,000 and $8,000, depending on well depth and the number of zones treated. That is a fraction of the $18,000 to $42,000 a new turnkey well usually costs in the mountains, which is why we recommend it as a first option for most sound wells.

How much can I expect my well yield to improve?

Typical results are a two to five times increase over the pre-treatment flow rate, though every fracture network is different. We measure your yield before and after and give you a documented comparison. Some wells improve dramatically and a small percentage respond little, so we never guarantee a specific gallons-per-minute figure.

Does hydrofracturing use chemicals that could contaminate my water?

No. Well hydrofracturing uses only clean water under high pressure. Unlike oil and gas fracking, no chemicals, sand, or additives are injected, so there is no contamination risk to your drinking water.

How long does the hydrofracturing process take?

Most Lake Gregory wells are treated in a single visit lasting four to eight hours. We then perform a post-treatment flow test, usually within 24 to 48 hours, once the well has settled, before delivering your results report.

When should I drill a new well instead of hydrofracturing?

If your casing is damaged or collapsed, the borehole has filled in, or the well was drilled too shallow to reach reliable water, deepening or redrilling may be the better investment. We inspect the well first and give you an honest recommendation rather than defaulting to the most expensive solution.
