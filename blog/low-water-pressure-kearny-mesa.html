The Mesa Geology Under Kearny Mesa

Kearny Mesa is a textbook example of San Diego's marine terrace geology. The flat mesa surface is capped by the Linda Vista Formation — a reddish-brown, sandy-to-cobbly marine terrace deposit, only a few meters thick — sitting on older sedimentary bedrock. The mesa stands at roughly 300 to 500 feet of elevation and is cut by steep canyons, and the broader area is laced by the Rose Canyon fault zone. For well owners, this means water is generally found below the thin terrace cap, in the underlying formations and the alluvium of the canyon bottoms, rather than in a deep, generous aquifer. Yields and depths vary with where a parcel sits relative to the canyons and the older bedrock beneath. The water tends to be hard, leaving scale in equipment, and canyon-edge wells can draw fine sediment.

Why Wells Around Kearny Mesa Lose Pressure

For the well-served properties on the Kearny Mesa fringe, low pressure usually comes from:

  • Hard-water scale: San Diego mesa groundwater is hard, and calcium and magnesium scale narrows pump passages, coats tank interiors, and clogs the pressure-switch port.
  • Aging pumps and tanks: Many fringe wells run older equipment that no longer holds pressure under modern household demand.
  • Sediment from canyon-edge wells: Wells near the canyon alluvium can pull fine sand that packs filters and wears the pump.
  • Pressure tank failure: A waterlogged tank or torn bladder makes the pump short-cycle and the pressure swing.
  • Elevation differences: On the mesa's broken, canyon-cut topography, pumping up to a house above the well costs pressure at the top.
  • Worn pressure switches: A scaled or pitted switch trips at the wrong points, leaving pressure low.

What You Can Check First

Locate your pressure gauge and watch a full pump cycle — a healthy system swings about 20 PSI somewhere between 40 and 60 PSI and shuts off cleanly. Knock on the pressure tank; a uniform dull thud means it is waterlogged. If only one fixture is weak, clean its aerator before suspecting the well. Replace a dirty sediment filter cartridge. Where the lot steps up a canyon rim, compare pressure near the well to pressure at the highest fixture to gauge elevation loss. Do not open the wellhead, control box, or pressure-switch enclosure yourself — those carry live voltage and the risk of damaging a deep-set pump.

Maintaining a Private Well in an Urban Setting

Owning one of the last private wells in a mostly city-water area like Kearny Mesa comes with a particular kind of responsibility: there is rarely a neighbor nearby who knows well systems, so proactive maintenance falls squarely on you. We recommend checking the pressure tank's air charge a couple of times a year and keeping it about 2 PSI below the pump's cut-in pressure, since a waterlogged tank quietly cycles a pump to failure. Replace sediment filter cartridges on a schedule rather than waiting for pressure to drop. Because San Diego mesa water is hard, a softener or scale-control system keeps your pump impellers and pressure switch clear and noticeably extends equipment life.

An annual inspection with a flow test is the smartest single thing a Kearny Mesa well owner can do. It confirms the pump is drawing the right amperage, the tank is holding its charge, and the water level is stable, and it catches small problems while they are still inexpensive. If you are weighing a switch to city water, that same visit lets us advise on properly decommissioning the old well to county code. Frequently Asked Questions

Are there really private wells in Kearny Mesa?

Most of Kearny Mesa is on city water, but estate properties, older parcels, and rural-edge lots on the canyon rims and toward Serra Mesa and Tierrasanta still use private wells. Those owners face the same pressure issues as any well owner, and they benefit from a contractor who specializes in wells rather than a general plumber.

What kind of ground are Kearny Mesa wells drilled into?

Kearny Mesa is a marine terrace capped by the thin, sandy Linda Vista Formation over older bedrock, at roughly 300 to 500 feet of elevation and cut by canyons. Water is generally found below the terrace cap in the underlying formations or in canyon-bottom alluvium, so depth and yield depend on where the parcel sits.

Why does my well water leave white scale everywhere?

San Diego mesa groundwater is hard, so calcium and magnesium leave scale on fixtures and inside your pressure tank, pump, and pressure switch. That scale gradually restricts flow and can drag down pressure. A water softener or scale-control system protects both your pressure and your equipment.

I'm connecting to city water. Do I need to do anything with my old well?

Yes. An unused well should be properly decommissioned to code to protect groundwater and meet county requirements. We handle decommissioning for $1,500 to $5,000 depending on the well, and we can advise on the right approach for your property.

Can a general plumber fix my low well pressure?

Sometimes for a simple fixture issue, but most well pressure problems involve the pump, pressure tank, switch, or water table — work that needs well-specific tools and a C-57 license for deeper repairs. We diagnose the whole system rather than guessing, which avoids unnecessary replacements.
