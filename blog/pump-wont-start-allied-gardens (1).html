Well Pump Won't Start in Allied Gardens?

Turn on the faucet and nothing happens? A well pump that won't start is one of the most common—and stressful—well problems Allied Gardens homeowners face. Let's troubleshoot.

Common Causes

  • Tripped Breaker: Check your electrical panel first
  • Pressure Switch Failure: Switch that tells pump to turn on
  • Control Box Issues: Capacitor or relay failure (submersible pumps)
  • Motor Burnout: Pump motor has failed
  • Wiring Problems: Damaged wires or loose connections
  • Low Water: Pump may have protection that shuts it off

Quick Checks You Can Do

  1. Check if circuit breaker is tripped (reset once only)
  2. Listen at pressure tank for clicking from pressure switch
  3. Check pressure gauge reading on tank
  4. Look for any visible wire damage
  5. Check if other electrical items on same circuit work

Caution: Do not repeatedly reset a tripping breaker—this can cause electrical fire or further damage.

When to Call a Professional

Well pump electrical systems use 240 volts—dangerous for DIY repairs. Call us if:

  • Breaker trips repeatedly
  • You smell burning or see scorch marks
  • Quick checks don't reveal the problem
  • You're not comfortable with electrical diagnosis

Understanding the System Before You Blame the Pump

The single most useful thing a well owner can know is that the pump only runs when something tells it to. That something is a sequence of parts, and a fault anywhere in the sequence produces the same dead-faucet symptom.

It starts at the electrical panel, where a dedicated 240-volt breaker powers the well. The power feeds a pressure switch mounted near the pressure tank. The switch monitors water pressure and snaps closed when it drops to the cut-in point — typically around 40 psi — sending power on to the pump, then opens again at the cut-out point near 60 psi.

The pump on most modern San Diego County wells is a submersible set deep in the casing. Three-wire submersibles depend on an above-ground control box housing the start capacitor and relay; two-wire models build that into the motor. The motor turns an impeller assembly that pushes water up the drop pipe to the pressure tank, whose internal air charge stores pressurized water and keeps the pump from cycling on every single tap.

Power supply

Check the breaker first. A tripped 240-volt double-pole breaker is both a symptom and a clue. Reset it once. If it holds, the trip was likely a one-off surge. If it trips again at once, there is a fault drawing too much current — do not keep resetting it.

Pressure switch

The pressure switch is the most replaced part on any well, and for good reason. Its contacts pit and burn from arcing, and its sensing tube can clog with the fine mineral sediment common in San Diego County groundwater. A switch that can't close never tells the pump to run.

Control box and capacitor

When a three-wire pump hums but produces no water, the start capacitor in the control box has usually weakened. Without that starting boost the motor can't break free and spin up. It is one of the more affordable repairs in well service.

Pressure tank

Tanks lose their air charge over years of service and become waterlogged. The pump then short-cycles, hammering the switch and motor until one fails. A heavy, fully-saturated tank usually has a ruptured bladder.

Motor and wiring

The drop cable and its splice run the full depth of the well, and corrosion or a nicked conductor can mimic a dead pump. We confirm an actual motor burnout with insulation-resistance and winding tests rather than assuming the worst.

Water level in the well

If the well's water level has fallen — during drought or simply from heavy use — a low-water protection feature may shut the pump down to keep it from running dry. That self-protection can look exactly like a pump that refuses to start.

Patterns We See Across San Diego County

Certain failures cluster. After a regional power event, switch contacts pit and breakers trip in batches. Older properties that have run the same equipment fifteen or twenty years tend to lose a pressure tank or a motor around the same time. And sun-exposed control boxes give up their capacitors in the first hot stretch of summer. Spotting which pattern fits your situation usually narrows the diagnosis fast.

Safe Checks Before You Call

  • Breaker: verify it is on; reset once, then stop if it re-trips.
  • Gauge: note tank pressure — zero with the pump idle means a drained system.
  • Switch: open a faucet and listen for the click that signals the switch trying to call the pump.
  • Tank: tap from top to bottom; a hollow ring up high and a solid thud down low is normal.
  • Wiring: visually inspect the wellhead for rodent damage or scorching — no contact.

When to Bring In a Professional

Past the breaker reset, you're dealing with lethal 240-volt circuits, and pulling a deep submersible is a job for proper equipment. What does it mean if my pump clicks but won't run?

A click with no run usually comes from the pressure switch closing its contacts while the pump fails to respond. That points downstream — to a failed start capacitor in the control box, a wiring fault, or a stalled motor. It is the opposite of a silent switch, which points at the switch itself. Either way it needs a meter to confirm.

Is a pump that won't start an emergency?

If it leaves you with no water, yes — and we treat it that way with same-day service. It is also worth acting fast because some causes, like a breaker that keeps tripping or a motor stuck humming, can worsen quickly. Shut the breaker off after one reset and call rather than letting it run in a fault.

How much does it cost to find out what's wrong?

Our diagnostic is a flat $125, and we credit it toward any repair. You get a definitive answer from a licensed C-57 contractor who arrives with the test gear to check voltage, the pressure switch, and the control box — no guessing, no return trips just to learn the cause.

Can a bad pressure tank stop the pump from starting?

Indirectly, yes. A waterlogged tank causes rapid short-cycling that wears out the pressure switch and the motor. Once those components fail from the abuse, the pump won't start. Replacing only the pump without fixing the tank just sets you up to fail again, which is why we check the tank's air charge every visit.
