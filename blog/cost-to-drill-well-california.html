How Much Does It Cost to Drill a Well in California?

Planning to drill a well in California? You're looking at one of the best long-term investments for rural and semi-rural properties—but also one with significant variation in cost. Well drilling in California typically runs $15,000 to $75,000 or more, with most residential projects landing in the $25,000-$50,000 range. The wide spread comes down to your location, how deep you need to drill, and what kind of rock the drilling rig encounters. This guide breaks down costs across California regions so you know what to expect.

Southern California (San Diego, Riverside, Imperial)

Southern California offers some of the more moderate well drilling costs in the state, with typical residential wells running $18,000-$50,000. The region features mixed geology—decomposed granite, alluvial basins, and some hard rock formations in mountain areas.

For detailed local pricing, see our guides for San Diego well drilling costs and Riverside County well drilling costs.

Central Valley (Fresno, Sacramento, Bakersfield)

The Central Valley presents a wide cost range. Residential wells in suburban areas may cost $25,000-$45,000 for 300-500 foot depths. However, agricultural irrigation wells often require drilling 800-1,200+ feet due to aquifer depletion, pushing costs to $75,000-$150,000 or more.

The good news: Central Valley's predominantly soft alluvial soils allow faster, less expensive per-foot drilling compared to hard rock regions. The bad news: you often need to drill much deeper to reach reliable water.

Sierra Foothills (Auburn, Grass Valley, Sonora)

The Gold Country foothills feature fractured rock aquifers that can be unpredictable. Typical costs run $25,000-$60,000 for residential wells. The hard rock requires air rotary drilling at higher per-foot costs, but depths are usually moderate (300-600 feet) where fractures contain water.

Coastal California

Coastal areas from Santa Barbara to Mendocino generally have shallower water tables and moderate drilling costs of $20,000-$55,000. However, some coastal areas face saltwater intrusion concerns that require careful well siting and potentially deeper drilling to access fresh water.

What Affects California Well Drilling Costs?

1. Drilling Depth

Depth is the single biggest cost factor. Every additional foot of drilling adds to your total bill. California's water tables vary dramatically:

  • Coastal valleys: Often 100-300 feet to water
  • Inland valleys: Typically 200-400 feet
  • Foothills and mountains: 300-700 feet depending on fractures
  • Central Valley agricultural: 500-1,500+ feet in many areas
  • Desert regions: 300-800+ feet

Learn more about how deep wells need to be and the factors that affect drilling depth.

2. Geology and Rock Type

What's underground dramatically affects drilling speed and cost:

⛏️ California Drilling Costs by Rock Type

  • • Alluvial soils (Central Valley): $35-$55/ft
  • • Decomposed granite (Southern CA): $40-$60/ft
  • • Sedimentary rock: $50-$70/ft
  • • Hard granite/metamorphic: $65-$100/ft

3. Drilling Method Required

Different geological conditions require different drilling techniques:

  • Mud rotary: Most economical, used for softer formations
  • Air rotary: Required for hard rock, faster but more expensive equipment
  • Cable tool: Traditional method, slower but effective for certain conditions
  • Reverse circulation: Used for large-diameter agricultural wells

4. Casing Requirements

California regulations and local conditions affect casing specifications:

  • Standard steel casing: $15-$25/ft installed
  • PVC casing: $10-$18/ft (where permitted)
  • Larger diameter (8"+): Higher costs for high-capacity wells
  • Deeper grouting requirements: Adds $2-$5/ft in some areas

5. Site Accessibility

Drilling rigs need access to your well site. Difficult access adds cost:

  • Narrow or winding roads
  • Steep terrain
  • Limited work area
  • Overhead obstacles (power lines, trees)
  • Remote locations requiring longer mobilization

6. Permit and Regulatory Costs

California counties have varying permit requirements and fees:

  • Well permit: $200-$800 depending on county
  • Environmental review: Required in some areas
  • Setback compliance: May affect well placement options
  • Well completion report: Required statewide

What's Included in California Well Drilling Cost?

When comparing quotes, make sure you're getting complete pricing. A turnkey installation should include:

Drilling and Well Construction

  • Site preparation and rig mobilization
  • Drilling to required depth
  • Steel or PVC casing
  • Well screen in production zone
  • Gravel pack and filter material
  • Sanitary seal and grouting
  • Well development (cleaning and testing)

Pump and Pressure System

  • Submersible pump sized for your depth and demand
  • Drop pipe and pump cable
  • Pitless adapter
  • Pressure tank (40-119 gallon typical)
  • Pressure switch and gauge
  • Check valve
  • Electrical wiring to panel

Permits and Testing

  • County well permit
  • Well completion report
  • Basic water quality test
  • Flow rate documentation

⚠️ Watch for Incomplete Quotes

Some contractors quote drilling only, excluding pump equipment, electrical, and other essentials. A $15,000 "drilling" quote can easily become $30,000+ once you add everything needed for a working well. Always get itemized, turnkey quotes.

California Well Permit Requirements

All California counties require well permits. Here's what to expect:

Standard Requirements

  • Setback from septic: Minimum 100 feet in most counties
  • Property line setback: Usually 5-10 feet minimum
  • Licensed contractor: C-57 Well Drilling Contractor license required
  • Well completion report: Must be filed with California DWR within 60 days

Special Restrictions

Some California areas have additional regulations:

  • Groundwater Sustainability Agencies: May limit new wells in critically overdrafted basins
  • Moratoriums: Some counties have temporary drilling restrictions
  • Agricultural vs. domestic: Different rules may apply
  • Environmental review: Required in sensitive areas

Your well drilling contractor handles permit applications and knows local requirements. Learn more in our San Diego well permit guide.

Is Drilling a Well Worth It in California?

Despite the significant upfront cost, a private well often makes excellent financial sense for California properties:

Monthly Savings

California municipal water rates are among the highest in the nation and rising 5-8% annually in many areas. Typical savings:

  • Average household: $100-$200/month savings
  • With irrigation: $200-$400/month savings
  • Agricultural use: $500-$2,000+/month savings

Property Value Increase

A producing well adds significant value to California property:

  • Typical value increase: $15,000-$50,000+
  • Higher increase for agricultural properties
  • Essential for properties without municipal water access
  • Attractive to buyers seeking water independence

Water Independence

Beyond financial savings, a well provides:

  • Freedom from drought restrictions and rationing
  • No tiered rate penalties for irrigation
  • Reliable water during infrastructure failures
  • Control over your water supply

Compare the full picture in our well water vs. city water guide.

Payback Calculation

A typical California payback scenario:

  • Well cost: $35,000
  • Annual water bill savings: $2,400
  • Annual operating cost (electricity): $300
  • Net annual savings: $2,100
  • Simple payback: ~17 years (less accounting for rising water rates)

Financing Options for California Well Drilling

Home Equity Loans/HELOCs

Often the lowest interest rate option for homeowners with equity. The well increases property value, supporting the loan.

Personal Loans

Banks and credit unions offer unsecured loans for well projects. Typical terms: 5-10 years, 8-15% APR depending on credit.

USDA Rural Development

Properties in qualifying rural areas may be eligible for USDA loans with favorable terms. Much of California outside urban cores qualifies.

County Emergency Programs

Some California counties offer assistance for properties with failed wells or contaminated water. Programs vary—check with your county health department.

Contractor Financing

Many well drilling companies work with financing partners to offer payment plans. Ask about options when getting your estimate.

How to Get an Accurate California Well Estimate

Online estimates can only take you so far. For accurate pricing specific to your property:

  • Request a site visit: Good contractors will visit your property before quoting
  • Research neighbor wells: Well logs from nearby properties indicate likely depth
  • Get multiple quotes: Compare at least 2-3 licensed contractors
  • Verify licensing: Confirm C-57 license on California CSLB website
  • Ask for itemized quotes: Understand exactly what's included
  • Check references: Talk to recent customers

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to drill a well in California?

Well drilling in California typically costs $15,000 to $75,000 or more for a complete installation. Most residential wells fall in the $25,000-$50,000 range. Costs vary significantly by region—Southern California averages $20,000-$45,000 while Central Valley agricultural wells can exceed $100,000.

What is the cost per foot to drill a well in California?

California well drilling costs $35-$100 per foot for drilling alone, depending on geology and drilling method. Soft alluvial soils cost $35-$55/ft, while hard granite formations requiring air rotary drilling cost $65-$100/ft. This doesn't include casing, pump, or other equipment.

Why is well drilling so expensive in California?

California well drilling costs more due to deeper water tables in many areas, hard rock geology requiring specialized equipment, strict permitting requirements, higher labor costs, and drought-depleted aquifers requiring deeper drilling. The state's diverse geology also means costs vary dramatically by location.

How deep are wells in California?

California residential wells typically range from 100-700 feet deep depending on location. Coastal areas may be 100-300 feet, foothill regions 300-500 feet, and mountain areas 400-700+ feet. Agricultural wells in the Central Valley often exceed 1,000 feet due to aquifer depletion.

Do I need a permit to drill a well in California?

Yes, California requires well permits in most counties. Permits typically cost $200-$800 and require setback compliance from septic systems, property lines, and contamination sources. Some areas have drilling moratoriums or restrictions. Your well contractor handles permit applications as part of the project.

Is drilling a well worth it in California?

For many California properties, a well provides significant long-term value: no monthly water bills (saving $1,200-$3,000+ annually), water independence during restrictions, increased property value ($15,000-$50,000+), and agricultural viability. Payback period is typically 7-15 years depending on usage and municipal water costs.

What's included in a complete well drilling quote?

A complete turnkey quote should include permits, drilling to required depth, steel or PVC casing, well screen, grout seal, well development, submersible pump, drop pipe, pressure tank, electrical connections, and water quality testing. Get itemized quotes to compare accurately—some contractors quote drilling only.

Are there financing options for well drilling in California?

Yes, financing options include home equity loans, personal loans, USDA rural development loans for qualifying properties, and contractor financing programs. Some California counties also have emergency well programs for properties with failed wells. Typical financing terms range from 5-15 years.
