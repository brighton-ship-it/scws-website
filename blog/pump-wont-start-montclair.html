Common Causes

  • Tripped Breaker: Check your electrical panel first
  • Pressure Switch Failure: Switch that tells pump to turn on
  • Control Box Issues: Capacitor or relay failure (submersible pumps)
  • Motor Burnout: Pump motor has failed
  • Wiring Problems: Damaged wires or loose connections
  • Low Water: Pump may have protection that shuts it off

Quick Checks You Can Do

  1. Check if circuit breaker is tripped (reset once only)
  2. Listen at pressure tank for clicking from pressure switch
  3. Check pressure gauge reading on tank
  4. Look for any visible wire damage
  5. Check if other electrical items on same circuit work

Caution: Do not repeatedly reset a tripping breaker—this can cause electrical fire or further damage.

Common Reasons a Well Pump Won't Start

When a Montclair pump goes quiet, expect one of these:

  • Tripped breaker or blown fuse — no power reaching the pump. By far the most common cause. A surge, a loose lug, or a struggling motor can drop the dedicated pump circuit.
  • Failed pressure switch or clogged sensing port. The pressure switch tells the pump when to run. Inland Empire groundwater is mineral-rich and hard, and that scale can plug the switch's small sensing port so the pump is never commanded on.
  • Burned-out capacitor or control box fault. Start capacitors and relays in the surface control box weaken with age and Inland Empire summer heat, and are a leading no-start cause.
  • Failed or seized motor or pump. Years of cycling plus hard-water mineral buildup can wear bearings or short a winding until the motor won't turn.
  • Wiring and corrosion faults. Loose connections, corroded terminals, and rodent-chewed wire in junction boxes interrupt the circuit on older valley-floor systems.
  • Stuck check valve. A check valve frozen closed can stall the pump or trip its overload protection.
  • Waterlogged or air-bound pressure tank. A tank that has lost its air charge short-cycles the pump and can trip controls until it looks dead.
  • Power problems. An aging panel, undersized service, or a utility surge can leave the pump without the steady voltage it needs to start.

Safety-First Steps Before You Call

Warning: well pumps run on 240 volts. That is lethal voltage. Never open a control box or touch wiring while the power is live. Work through these in order:

  1. Turn the pump breaker OFF at the main panel first. Always cut the power before you inspect anything.
  2. Look at the breaker. If it is in the tripped middle position, reset it ONCE — fully off, then on.
  3. If it trips again immediately, stop. Leave it off and call us; repeated tripping signals a short or failing motor and a fire hazard.
  4. Check the pressure tank gauge. A reading stuck at zero, or far above the normal cut-out, is worth reporting.
  5. Listen at the pressure switch for a click when water should start the pump. No click usually means a switch or control issue.
  6. Scan visible wiring and conduit for burns, melted insulation, corrosion, or a burnt smell — keep the power off and call if you find any.
  7. Confirm the rest of the house has power, separating a pump-specific fault from a property-wide outage.

Electrical Causes Worth Understanding

Most Montclair wells are relatively shallow compared to desert or mountain wells, which is good news for repair cost — but the electrical components fail just the same. A three-wire submersible pump keeps its start capacitor and relay in a surface control box, the easiest and cheapest place to diagnose and fix a fault. A two-wire pump has those parts in the motor downhole, so a failure means a pull. A motor that hums but won't spin most often points to a weak capacitor or low voltage rather than a dead motor. Hard Inland Empire water also leaves mineral scale that can stress motors and bind moving parts over time. We meter the incoming voltage, test the motor windings' insulation resistance, and check amperage draw on every no-start call, because correctly separating a $250 capacitor fix from a full pump replacement is what protects your wallet.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why won't my pump start if my house still has power?

The pump is on its own dedicated circuit. A tripped pump breaker, a failed pressure switch, or a bad capacitor can keep the pump silent while the rest of your Montclair home runs normally. Always check the dedicated pump breaker first.

Can I reset the pump breaker on my own?

You may reset it once. If it trips again right away, stop and call us. Repeated tripping points to a short or failing motor, and forcing it risks an electrical fire.

Does the Inland Empire's hard water affect my pump?

Yes. Mineral-rich, hard groundwater leaves scale that can clog the pressure switch's sensing port and, over time, stress the motor and bind moving parts — both of which can stop a pump from starting.

Are Montclair wells expensive to repair compared to desert wells?

Generally less so. Valley-floor wells here are typically shallower, so when a full replacement is needed the labor to pull the pump is lower, keeping costs toward the bottom of the range.
