What is Hydrofracturing?

Hydrofracturing is a well rehabilitation technique that uses high-pressure water to clean and expand existing fractures in the bedrock surrounding your well. This process:

  • Clears mineral deposits and sediment blocking water flow
  • Opens new pathways for groundwater to reach your well
  • Can increase well yield by 2-10 times in many cases
  • Extends the life of your existing well

Unlike oil and gas fracking, well hydrofracturing uses only clean water—no chemicals are involved.

Signs Your Highgrove Well Needs Hydrofracturing

  • Declining water production over time
  • Well runs dry during peak usage
  • Slow recovery rate after pumping
  • Pump cycling on and off frequently
  • Lower water pressure than previous years

The Hydrofracturing Process

  1. Initial Assessment: We evaluate your well's current yield and condition
  2. Equipment Setup: Specialized high-pressure equipment is positioned at your well
  3. Isolation: Packers seal off sections of the well for targeted treatment
  4. Pressurization: Water is pumped at 500-3,000 PSI to fracture bedrock
  5. Development: Well is cleaned and tested for improved yield

Hydrofracturing for Low-Yield Wells in Highgrove

Highgrove is an unincorporated community in Riverside County, nestled between the city of Riverside and Grand Terrace, north of the UC Riverside campus and within the Santa Ana River watershed. Its history as a community of citrus groves and small ranches lives on in the older homesteads and agricultural parcels along Iowa Avenue, Main Street, and the areas south of Center Street, many of which still depend on private wells drilled decades ago. The ground beneath Highgrove is made of alluvial deposits, sand, gravel, silt, and clay that form part of the Bunker Hill Basin aquifer system. These formations generally hold good water, but aging wells and clogged formations leave many Highgrove owners with disappointing yield and pressure. Hydrofracturing (also called hydrofracking) is often the most economical way to bring an underperforming Highgrove well back to life. For the older homesteads that have watered families and small groves here for generations, restoring an existing well is far less disruptive than starting over.

How Hydrofracturing Works

Hydrofracturing uses high-pressure clean water to clean and reopen the water-bearing zones around your well. We seal off a target section of the casing with inflatable packers, then inject water at roughly 1,500 to 3,000-plus PSI. That pressure flushes out the silt, fine sand, and mineral scale that gradually pack into the formation and the well perforations, and it reopens the pathways that carry groundwater into the well. In Highgrove's alluvial deposits, where decades of fine sediment can choke an older well, this cleaning and re-development is frequently exactly what is needed. The water is usually there in the Bunker Hill Basin formations; the challenge is simply getting it to flow into the casing efficiently again. The process involves no chemicals and is usually finished in four to eight hours.

Why Highgrove Wells Lose Yield

Most Highgrove well problems trace back to the same handful of causes:

  • Formation clogging. The valley's sand, silt, and clay produce abundant fines that, over decades, seal the perforations and the surrounding formation, restricting flow even where water remains plentiful.
  • Aging boreholes. Many wells here date to the citrus era. An old well loses efficiency as its screen and the formation around it gradually plug.
  • Shallow depths. Local wells are commonly 100 to 300 feet deep. Shallower wells are more sensitive to seasonal water-table fluctuations and to any loss of inflow efficiency.
  • Hard-water scaling. Mineral-rich Santa Ana watershed groundwater deposits scale that compounds the clogging problem.

Because these are flow-restriction and development problems rather than a lack of regional groundwater, they respond well to hydrofracturing.

What to Check Before You Call

  • Time your recovery. Note how long the well takes to refill after heavy use; a slow recovery indicates a clogged or under-developed formation.
  • Rule out the simple parts. A waterlogged pressure tank ($600-$1,500) or a worn pressure switch ($150-$350) often masquerades as a weak well and is inexpensive to fix.
  • Observe the pump. Short-cycling or loss of prime points to the well drawing down faster than it recovers.
  • Find your driller's log. It confirms your depth and the alluvial formations, which guides the treatment.
  • Note sediment. Sand or cloudiness in the water signals clogging or screen wear and shapes the plan.

Frequently Asked Questions

My Highgrove well has good water but weak pressure. Will hydrofracturing help?

Often, yes, if the weak pressure stems from restricted inflow into the well. We first confirm the problem is the well and not the pump, tank, or pressure switch, then hydrofracturing can clean the clogged formation and restore flow. The diagnostic pinpoints the true cause.

Will it work on a shallow alluvial well?

If the well is clogged with fines or scale, hydrofracturing can clear those pathways and restore yield. Wells finished entirely in loose, fracture-free sand are less ideal candidates, which our diagnostic determines before any work begins.

Is my old well casing strong enough for the treatment?

We inspect the casing with a downhole camera first. On older Highgrove wells this step is especially important, and it lets us apply pressure safely within the well's limits.

How much improvement is realistic?

For suitable wells, yield commonly improves two to five times, with success in roughly 70 to 80 percent of good candidates. We document your flow before and after.

How long does the job take?

The injection usually takes four to eight hours in a single visit. We run a post-treatment flow test, and most owners see improved, stabilized flow within 24 to 48 hours.

What if the treatment does not solve the problem?

If a treated well still underperforms, the diagnostic data guides the next step, whether deeper rehabilitation or a new well. The investigation is never wasted, and we present every option with honest pricing.
