Types of Well Inspections in California

California requires several types of well inspections. Pre-construction inspection verifies the approved well location. During-construction inspection checks casing and grouting. Final inspection confirms proper completion and sanitary seal. Some counties require water quality testing as part of final approval. Domestic wells generally have stricter inspection requirements than agricultural wells.

What Inspectors Check During Construction

Inspectors verify that casing material meets California standards (steel or approved plastic), proper grout mixture and application (typically neat cement), annular seal extends minimum 50 feet or to the first impermeable layer, casing extends 12 inches minimum above ground surface, and no cross-connections or potential contamination sources exist nearby.

Sanitary Seal and Wellhead Inspection

The concrete surface seal must slope away from the well casing with a minimum 2-foot radius concrete pad around the wellhead. A proper well cap or sanitary seal must be installed with no gaps or cracks that could allow surface water entry. Adequate drainage must prevent ponding around the well.

Common Inspection Failures and How to Avoid Them

Common failures include insufficient setbacks from septic systems or property lines, improper grouting depth or mixture, missing or damaged well caps, casing that doesn't extend far enough above grade, and incomplete well completion reports or missing documentation. Working with an experienced well driller helps avoid these issues.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I schedule a well inspection in California?

Contact your local county environmental health or building department to schedule inspections. Most counties require 24-48 hours notice. Your well driller typically coordinates inspections, as they must be present during construction inspections. Have your permit number ready when calling.

What happens if my well fails inspection?

You'll receive a correction notice listing required fixes. Common corrections include extending casing height, improving the concrete seal, or addressing drainage issues. Once corrections are made, schedule a re-inspection. Additional fees may apply for re-inspections. Significant failures may require well modification or destruction.

Are there different inspection requirements for agricultural wells vs. domestic wells?

Yes, domestic wells typically have stricter inspection requirements since the water is used for drinking. Agricultural wells may have fewer inspection points but must still meet construction standards. Both require proper sanitary seals, but domestic wells also require water quality testing for bacteria and nitrates before final approval.
