When Your Well Needs Chlorination

Well chlorination (shock chlorination) is the process of disinfecting your entire well system with chlorine to kill bacteria and other microorganisms. Julian well owners may need chlorination when:

  • Positive bacteria test — Coliform or E. coli detected
  • New well — Disinfect before first use
  • After repairs — Pump replacement or well work
  • After flooding — Surface water contamination
  • Rotten egg smell — Sulfur bacteria
  • Slime buildup — Iron or sulfur bacteria

Our Chlorination Process

  1. Calculate chlorine dose — Based on well depth and diameter
  2. Add chlorine solution — Food-grade calcium hypochlorite
  3. Circulate through system — Run to all fixtures until chlorine smell
  4. Contact time — Let stand 12-24 hours
  5. Flush system — Remove all chlorinated water
  6. Re-test — Verify bacteria eliminated

Chlorination Cost

Professional well chlorination in Julian typically costs $200-$400 depending on well depth and system complexity. This includes proper chlorine dosing, circulation, and follow-up testing recommendations.

DIY vs Professional

While homeowners can attempt chlorination, professional service ensures:

  • Correct chlorine concentration (too little won't work, too much damages equipment)
  • Proper circulation through entire system
  • Safe disposal of chlorinated water
  • Follow-up testing to confirm success

Ongoing Protection

If bacteria returns repeatedly, you may need continuous disinfection:

  • UV disinfection — Kills bacteria without chemicals
  • Chlorine injection — Continuous low-dose treatment
  • Well rehabilitation — Address the source of contamination

Well Chlorination Service in Julian

If your Julian well has tested positive for coliform bacteria, or you're doing annual maintenance, shock chlorination is the standard treatment. We provide professional well chlorination that eliminates bacteria and disinfects the entire system — from the well casing to your household plumbing.

When to Chlorinate Your Well

  • Positive bacteria test — coliform or E. coli detected in water sample
  • After well work — any time the well is opened for pump replacement, repair, or inspection
  • After flooding — surface water may have entered the well during heavy rains
  • Annual maintenance — preventive chlorination as part of routine well care
  • Slime or biofilm — iron bacteria or sulfur bacteria creating buildup in the well

Our Chlorination Process

  1. Calculate dosage — based on well depth, diameter, and water volume, we determine the correct chlorine concentration (typically 200+ ppm)
  2. Introduce chlorine — food-grade sodium hypochlorite is poured directly into the well
  3. Circulate — we run water through the system, opening each faucet until chlorine smell is detected, ensuring the entire distribution system is treated
  4. Contact time — chlorine remains in the system for 12-24 hours to kill all bacteria
  5. Flush — we flush the system until chlorine levels drop to safe levels
  6. Re-test — a follow-up bacteria test confirms the treatment was successful

Cost

Professional well chlorination in Julian typically costs $200-$500 depending on well depth and system complexity. DIY chlorination is possible but risks under-dosing (ineffective) or over-dosing (pipe damage). We ensure proper concentration and complete system coverage.

Why does my well water smell like rotten eggs?

The sulfur smell is caused by hydrogen sulfide gas, usually from bacteria in the well or naturally occurring sulfur in groundwater. Treatment options include well chlorination ($200-$500), aeration systems, or activated carbon filtration.

How often should I chlorinate my well?

We recommend shock chlorination annually as preventive maintenance, or immediately if you detect bacteria, notice odor changes, or after any well work. The process typically costs $200-$500 depending on well depth.
