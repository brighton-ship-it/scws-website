Buying Property with a Well: Complete Inspection Checklist

Updated February 2026 | By Southern California Well Service

Quick Answer: Before buying, get: water quality test (bacteria, nitrates minimum), flow/yield test, well inspection, and equipment assessment. Request well log and maintenance records. Budget $500-$1,000 for comprehensive testing—cheap insurance against $15,000-$50,000 replacement costs.

Why This Matters

Well Issues Are Expensive

  • New well: $15,000-$50,000+
  • New pump: $1,500-$4,000
  • Water treatment system: $1,000-$5,000
  • Well rehabilitation: $3,000-$10,000

Inspection Costs Are Not

  • Comprehensive inspection: $500-$1,000
  • That's 2-7% of potential replacement cost
  • Findings can be negotiated before closing

No Disclosure Required

Sellers may not know (or reveal) well problems. You're responsible for due diligence.

Essential Tests

1. Water Quality Test

Cost: $100-$500

Minimum Panel

  • Coliform bacteria
  • E. coli
  • Nitrates

Recommended Full Panel

  • pH
  • Hardness
  • Iron, manganese
  • TDS (total dissolved solids)
  • Lead, arsenic (if area is prone)

2. Flow Rate / Yield Test

Cost: $200-$400

  • Measures gallons per minute (GPM)
  • Tests well recovery after pumping
  • Determines if well can meet household needs
  • Should be 5+ GPM for typical home

3. Well Inspection

Cost: $100-$300

  • Visual inspection of wellhead and cap
  • Check equipment condition (pump, tank, wiring)
  • Verify proper installation and sealing
  • Assess pump age and type

4. Camera Inspection (Optional but Valuable)

Cost: $300-$600

  • Video of well interior
  • Shows casing condition
  • Reveals problems not visible from surface
  • Recommended for wells over 20 years old

Documents to Request

Well Log / Driller's Report

  • When well was drilled
  • Total depth
  • Casing material and depth
  • Static water level
  • Initial yield
  • Geology encountered

Permits

  • Original drilling permit
  • Any modification permits
  • Confirms well is legal

Maintenance Records

  • Pump installation/replacement dates
  • Water treatment system info
  • Past water tests
  • Any repairs done

If No Records Exist

  • Check county records for well log
  • Not a deal-breaker, but less information
  • More reason for thorough inspection

Red Flags

Serious Concerns

  • Bacteria present in water test
  • Low flow rate (under 3-5 GPM)
  • Unknown well age or no documentation
  • Wellhead below ground level
  • Well located near septic, fuel tanks, animal areas

Moderate Concerns (Negotiation Points)

  • Older pump (15+ years)
  • High iron or hardness (needs treatment)
  • Shallow well (more vulnerable to contamination)
  • No sanitary seal or damaged cap
  • Outdated pressure tank

Deal Breakers?

Context matters:

  • Low yield might be okay with storage tank
  • Water quality issues often treatable
  • Old equipment can be replaced (factor into offer)
  • Structural well issues are more serious

Questions to Ask

Ask the Seller

  1. When was the well drilled?
  2. Has it ever run dry or low?
  3. When was the pump last replaced?
  4. Are there any water treatment systems?
  5. Have you had water tested? When?
  6. Any known issues with water quality or quantity?
  7. Is there a well log available?

Ask the Inspector

  1. What's the overall condition?
  2. Estimated remaining life of pump?
  3. Any immediate concerns?
  4. What would you recommend addressing?
  5. Estimated costs for any needed work?

Negotiation Tips

Using Findings

  • Get written estimates for any needed work
  • Request price reduction or seller credit
  • Ask seller to fix issues before closing
  • Negotiate home warranty that covers well

Common Negotiation Items

Issue Typical Cost Negotiation Request
Old pump (15+ years) $1,500-$4,000 Credit or escrow holdback
Failed water test Varies Seller remediate before closing
Low yield $3,000-$10,000 Storage tank credit
Water treatment needed $1,000-$5,000 Seller credit at closing

Walk Away If...

  • Structural well failure and seller won't negotiate
  • Contamination source can't be eliminated
  • No reasonable water supply alternative
  • Costs exceed property value adjustment

Printable Checklist

Before Making Offer

  • ☐ Ask seller basic well questions
  • ☐ Request any available documentation
  • ☐ Include well inspection contingency in offer

During Due Diligence

  • ☐ Water quality test (bacteria, nitrates minimum)
  • ☐ Flow rate / yield test
  • ☐ Well system inspection
  • ☐ Camera inspection (if well over 20 years)
  • ☐ Review well log if available
  • ☐ Check county records for permits

Review Results

  • ☐ Water test passes or issues identified
  • ☐ Flow rate meets needs (5+ GPM typical)
  • ☐ Equipment condition acceptable
  • ☐ No structural well issues
  • ☐ Get repair estimates if needed

Before Closing

  • ☐ Negotiate credits for any issues
  • ☐ Confirm any seller repairs completed
  • ☐ Obtain all well documentation
  • ☐ Know location of pump controls/shutoffs

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I check when buying a house with a well?

Water quality test, flow/yield test, well inspection, and equipment assessment. Request well documentation. Budget $500-$1,000 for comprehensive testing.

How much does a well inspection cost?

Basic: $100-$300. Water testing: $100-$500. Flow test: $200-$400. Camera: $300-$600. Comprehensive package: $500-$1,000.

Who pays for the well inspection?

Typically the buyer, like a home inspection. Sometimes negotiated as part of purchase agreement.

What if the water test fails?

Depends on the issue. Bacteria may just need shock chlorination. Other issues may need treatment systems. Get costs and negotiate with seller.

How long do wells last?

Wells can last 25-50+ years with proper maintenance. Pumps typically last 10-20 years. Well condition matters more than age alone.
