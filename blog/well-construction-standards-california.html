Proper well construction isn't just about getting water—it's about protecting groundwater from contamination and ensuring your well lasts for decades. California has specific standards that all wells must meet.

California Well Standards Overview

Well construction in California is governed by:

  • Bulletin 74-90: California Water Well Standards (state guidance)
  • County ordinances: Local requirements that may exceed state minimums
  • AWWA standards: American Water Works Association technical specifications
  • Permit conditions: Site-specific requirements

Key Construction Components

Casing

  • Material: Steel or PVC meeting specifications
  • Depth: Must penetrate to competent material, typically minimum 50 feet
  • Thickness: Based on diameter and depth requirements
  • Height: Extend at least 12 inches above finished grade
  • Purpose: Prevents collapse, keeps out surface water and contamination

Annular Seal

  • Material: Neat cement grout or bentonite
  • Depth: Minimum as specified (often 50 feet or to competent material)
  • Purpose: Prevents surface contamination from migrating to aquifer
  • Method: Placed from bottom up, displacing water

Sanitary Seal

  • Location: Where pipes and wires enter the casing
  • Material: Rubber or neoprene compression seal
  • Purpose: Prevents contamination entry at surface
  • Requirement: Watertight and vermin-proof

Well Cap

  • Type: Vermin-proof, ventilated cap
  • Attachment: Bolted or screwed (not just placed)
  • Purpose: Keeps out debris, insects, animals

Licensing Requirements

California requires a C-57 Water Well Drilling Contractor license to:

  • Drill new water wells
  • Deepen or modify existing wells
  • Destroy (abandon) wells
  • Install well pumps (also requires C-61/D-21 for some work)

Verify Your Contractor

Always verify C-57 license with the California Contractors State License Board (CSLB). Unlicensed drilling is illegal and can result in non-compliant wells, permit issues, and contaminated water.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are California well construction standards?

Standards are governed by Bulletin 74-90 and county ordinances. They specify casing, annular seal, sanitary seal, and surface construction requirements to protect groundwater.

What is an annular seal?

Grouted material (cement or bentonite) filling space between casing and surrounding soil. It prevents surface contaminants from reaching groundwater.

Who can drill wells in California?

Only C-57 licensed contractors. Verify licenses with the Contractors State License Board.
