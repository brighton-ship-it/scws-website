Common Well Problems in San Diego County

San Diego's unique climate and geology create specific challenges for well owners. Understanding these common issues helps you recognize problems early and know when to call for service.

Hard Water Issues

San Diego is notorious for extremely hard water. Our mineral-rich geology means most well water contains high levels of calcium and magnesium. Signs include:

  • White scale buildup on fixtures, showerheads, and appliances
  • Soap that doesn't lather well
  • Stiff, dingy laundry
  • Spots on dishes and glassware
  • Dry, itchy skin and hair
  • Reduced water heater efficiency
  • Shortened appliance lifespan

Solution: Water softener installation. See our hard water solutions guide.

Drought & Low Water Yield

California's periodic droughts affect groundwater levels, and San Diego wells can experience:

  • Decreased water production (lower GPM)
  • Air spurting from faucets as water level drops
  • Well running dry during peak usage
  • Need to deepen wells or drill new ones
  • Pump damage from running without sufficient water

Solutions: Storage tank installation, well deepening, or drilling a new well. See our well running dry guide.

Pump Failures

Well pumps in San Diego often fail due to:

  • Sediment and sand: Abrasive particles wear impellers faster
  • Hard water scale: Mineral buildup reduces efficiency and causes overheating
  • Age: Most pumps last 8-15 years with proper conditions
  • Electrical issues: Power surges, lightning, and voltage problems
  • Running dry: Low water conditions damage pumps quickly

Solution: Professional diagnosis and pump repair or replacement.

Water Quality Problems

Common water quality issues in San Diego wells include:

  • Iron/manganese staining: Red, brown, or black stains on fixtures
  • Sulfur odor: "Rotten egg" smell in some areas
  • Bacteria contamination: Often after flooding or septic issues
  • Nitrates: Common in agricultural areas
  • Arsenic: Naturally occurring in some East County areas

Solution: Water testing followed by appropriate treatment.

Pressure Problems

Pressure issues affecting San Diego well systems:

  • Low pressure: Failing pump, tank problems, or pipe restrictions
  • Fluctuating pressure: Waterlogged pressure tank or check valve issues
  • Pressure tank cycling: Tank losing pre-charge or bladder failure
  • Pump short-cycling: Rapid on-off cycling that damages equipment

Solution: Diagnosis of pressure tank and pump system.

How often should I service my well in San Diego?

We recommend annual inspections for San Diego wells, including checking the pump operation, pressure tank function, and overall system health. Water testing for bacteria and nitrates should be done annually. Full pump system service every 3-5 years helps prevent failures. More frequent service may be needed for older systems, wells in harsh conditions, or high-use applications like agricultural wells.
