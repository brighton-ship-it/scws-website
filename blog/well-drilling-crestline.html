Crestline Geology & Groundwater

Understanding Crestline's geology is essential for successful well drilling:

The Granite Foundation

Crestline sits on the San Bernardino Mountains batholith — crystalline granite and related igneous rocks. While solid granite might seem impermeable, water actually accumulates in:

  • Fractured zones — Cracks and joints in the granite
  • Weathered granite — Decomposed rock near surface
  • Fault zones — Major fracture systems
  • Contact zones — Where different rock types meet

Water Production

Granite wells differ from valley wells:

  • Water comes from fractures, not aquifers
  • Production can vary by location
  • Deeper drilling often finds more productive zones
  • Water quality is typically excellent

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does drilling cost in Crestline?

Crestline wells typically cost $35,000-$75,000+ total. Granite drilling runs $75-175/foot. Costs depend on depth needed and site accessibility.

How deep are Crestline wells?

Most wells range from 300-600+ feet. Properties near Lake Gregory may be shallower. We assess each site individually.

Can you drill through granite?

Yes, we specialize in granite drilling using air rotary methods. While slower than softer formations, granite wells often produce excellent quality water.

What if you don't find water?

Our site evaluations help minimize this risk. If a well is unproductive, we discuss options. We only drill where we believe there's a reasonable chance of success.

How long does the project take?

From start to water flowing: typically 4-8 weeks. This includes 2-4 weeks for permits and 1-2 weeks for drilling and completion.
