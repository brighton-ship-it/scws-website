California Disclosure Requirements

Sellers must complete the Transfer Disclosure Statement (TDS) honestly, including questions about water supply. Many counties have additional well disclosure forms covering flow rate, water quality, and shared well agreements. Disclose any known issues: contamination history, low flow periods, equipment problems, or neighbor disputes. When in doubt, disclose—it's far cheaper than defending a nondisclosure lawsuit.

Pre-Sale Well Assessment

Schedule a professional well inspection 1-2 months before listing. Have water tested for bacteria, nitrates, and regional contaminants. The inspector should evaluate pump condition, pressure tank function, flow rate, and wellhead seal. Getting a clean inspection report to share with buyers builds confidence and can justify a higher asking price compared to properties with unknown well conditions.

Repairs and Improvements That Pay Off

Address any safety or code issues before listing—a failing septic near the well, improper wellhead seal, or contaminated water will halt most sales. Consider replacing an aging pump (15+ years) proactively; buyers discount heavily for needed repairs. A new pressure tank, water treatment system, or recent flow rate documentation can be selling points that reduce negotiation friction.

Working with Buyers and Their Inspections

Be prepared for buyer inspections to find items you missed—this is normal. Decide in advance how you'll handle repair requests: fix issues, offer credits, or negotiate price? Having your own inspection report helps counter inflated buyer demands. For FHA/VA buyers, know that loans require specific water test results and flow rates; help facilitate testing to keep sales on track.

Frequently Asked Questions

What must I disclose about my well when selling?

California requires sellers to disclose all known material facts, including well age, any known problems, water quality issues, past repairs, and whether the well has ever run dry. Use the Transfer Disclosure Statement (TDS) and any local well disclosure forms. Nondisclosure can result in lawsuits after sale.

Should I get a well inspection before listing my home?

Yes. A pre-listing inspection ($300-500) identifies issues you can fix before buyers find them. Surprises during buyer inspections often kill deals or lead to last-minute price reductions. Being proactive shows good faith and gives you negotiating strength.

Do I need to provide water test results to buyers?

While not always legally required, providing recent water tests (within 6 months) builds buyer confidence and speeds transactions. Lenders require tests for FHA/VA loans anyway. Having clean test results ready demonstrates a well-maintained property and reduces buyer concerns.
