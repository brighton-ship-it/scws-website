Well Pump Control Box: What It Does & Signs of Failure

Updated February 2026 | By Southern California Well Service

Quick Answer: The control box contains capacitors, relay, and overload protection for your submersible pump. Signs of failure: pump won't start (but hums), clicking sounds from box, burning smell, or visible damage. Control boxes cost $100-$350 and are accessible above ground—no pump pulling needed for replacement.

What Is a Control Box?

Purpose

The control box provides the electrical components needed to start and run a submersible well pump. Because the pump motor is down in the well (under water), the starting components are kept above ground where they can be serviced.

Location

  • Wall-mounted near pressure tank
  • Connected to pump by wires running down well
  • May be gray/green metal box or plastic enclosure
  • Size varies by pump horsepower

Not All Pumps Have One

  • 2-wire pumps: No control box (electronics in motor)
  • 3-wire pumps: Require control box (most common for larger pumps)

Components Inside

Start Capacitor

  • Provides extra power for motor startup
  • Black cylindrical component
  • Most common failure point

Run Capacitor

  • Keeps motor running efficiently
  • May be silver/gray metal can
  • Not present in all systems

Relay (Contactor)

  • Switches power to pump motor
  • Controlled by pressure switch signal
  • May click when engaging

Overload Protection

  • Thermal or electronic overload
  • Protects motor from burnout
  • May need reset after tripping

Terminal Block

  • Wire connections to pump
  • Labeled for proper wiring

Signs of Control Box Failure

Pump Won't Start

  • Pressure switch clicks but no pump action
  • Humming sound but motor doesn't run
  • Often capacitor failure

Clicking from Box

  • Relay clicking repeatedly
  • Trying to start but failing
  • May cycle rapidly

Burning Smell

  • Overheated component
  • Burned wiring
  • Turn off power immediately

Visible Damage

  • Bulging or leaking capacitor
  • Burn marks on components
  • Melted plastic
  • Corroded terminals

Breaker Trips

  • Immediately when pump tries to start
  • Could be control box or motor
  • Requires diagnosis

Intermittent Operation

  • Sometimes works, sometimes doesn't
  • Loose connection
  • Failing component

Troubleshooting

⚠️ Safety First: Control boxes contain capacitors that can store dangerous charge even with power off. If not experienced with electrical work, call a professional.

Basic Checks

  1. Check breaker (is power getting to box?)
  2. Listen for relay click when pressure switch activates
  3. Look for obvious damage (burns, swelling)

Capacitor Test

  1. Turn off power
  2. Discharge capacitor (carefully)
  3. Remove and test with multimeter
  4. Compare reading to label value

When to Call Professional

  • Not comfortable with electrical
  • Breaker trips repeatedly
  • Burning smell or smoke
  • Basic checks don't find problem

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a well pump control box do?

Houses capacitors and relay needed to start and run a 3-wire submersible pump. Located above ground for easy service access.

Where is my well pump control box?

Usually mounted on wall near pressure tank. Metal or plastic box, typically gray or green. May be labeled with pump specs.

How long does a control box last?

10-20 years typically. Capacitors often fail first. Heat and power surges shorten life.

Can I replace just the capacitor?

Yes—often more economical than replacing entire box. Make sure to match specs exactly.

Does a 2-wire pump have a control box?

No—2-wire pumps have starting components built into the motor. Only 3-wire pumps use external control boxes.
