Filling Your Pool with Well Water

If you have a private well, using it to fill your swimming pool can save hundreds of dollars compared to municipal water costs or commercial water delivery. However, there are important considerations to ensure your well can handle the demand and your pool water quality is acceptable. Here's what Southern California well owners need to know.

Can Your Well Handle Filling a Pool?

The key question is whether your well can produce enough water fast enough without running dry.

How Much Water Do You Need?

Pool volume estimates:

  • Small pool (12x24 ft, 4-5 ft avg depth): 10,000-12,000 gallons
  • Medium pool (16x32 ft, 4-5 ft avg depth): 18,000-20,000 gallons
  • Large pool (20x40 ft, 4-6 ft avg depth): 30,000-40,000 gallons

Well Flow Rate Requirements

Your well's flow rate (measured in gallons per minute, GPM) determines how long it takes to fill the pool:

Well Flow Rate 15,000 Gallon Pool 25,000 Gallon Pool
5 GPM 50 hours 83 hours
10 GPM 25 hours 42 hours
15 GPM 17 hours 28 hours
20 GPM 12.5 hours 21 hours

Well Recovery Rate

More important than instantaneous flow is your well's recovery rate – how fast it refills after pumping:

  • Good well: Can sustain continuous pumping for pool filling
  • Marginal well: May need to fill pool in stages with recovery breaks
  • Low-yield well: Fill slowly over several days, pumping only during low household demand times

Important: If your well struggles to keep up with normal household use, filling a pool may deplete it dangerously. Consider commercial water delivery instead.

Best Practices for Filling a Pool from a Well

1. Check Your Well Capacity First

  • Know your well's GPM rating (check your well log or have us test it)
  • Verify static water level is adequate
  • If unsure, have a professional evaluate before attempting

2. Fill Gradually

Recommended approach:

  • Fill during overnight hours when household water use is minimal
  • Use a garden hose, not multiple hoses (slower fill rate is safer for wells)
  • Monitor well recovery – if pump starts cycling rapidly or pressure drops significantly, stop and let well recover
  • Plan for 2-4 days of gradual filling rather than trying to fill in one day

3. Protect Your Pump

  • Don't let pump run continuously for 24+ hours – give it breaks
  • Monitor pump cycle frequency – excessive cycling indicates pressure tank or well recovery issues
  • Watch for overheating (breaker trips, burning smell, erratic operation)

4. Time It Right

Best time to fill a pool from a well:

  • Spring (March-May): Water tables are typically highest after winter rains
  • Avoid late summer/fall: Water tables are lowest after summer demand
  • Not during drought: If wells in your area are struggling, wait or use delivered water

Well Water Quality for Pools

Common Well Water Issues in Pools

Southern California well water often contains minerals that affect pool water:

Iron

Problem: Water may initially look clear but turn brown or rust-colored when chlorine is added.

Solution: Add a metal sequestrant before chlorinating. Consider pre-filtering if iron content is very high (>0.3 ppm).

Hardness (Calcium/Magnesium)

Problem: Very common in SoCal well water. Causes scaling on pool surfaces and equipment.

Solution: Usually manageable with proper chemical balance. Calcium hardness of 200-400 ppm is acceptable for most pools.

Manganese

Problem: Causes black or purple staining.

Solution: Metal sequestrant and maintaining proper chlorine levels.

Total Dissolved Solids (TDS)

Problem: High TDS makes water feel harsh, can cloud water.

Solution: If TDS is extremely high (>2000 ppm), partial drain and refill periodically.

Should You Pre-Filter Well Water?

For most Southern California wells, pre-filtering isn't necessary. However, consider it if:

  • Iron or manganese levels are very high (>0.5 ppm)
  • Water is visibly cloudy or discolored
  • You have sediment issues

Pre-fill water test ($75-$150): Have your well water tested before filling to identify potential issues. Pool supply stores can test for metals, pH, alkalinity, and hardness.

Cost Comparison: Well Water vs. Alternatives

Option 20,000 Gallon Pool Cost
Your well water $0 (only electricity: ~$15-$30)
Municipal water (if connected) $100-$250
Water delivery service $400-$800+

Using your well saves significant money, making it worth the effort if your well can handle it.

When NOT to Use Well Water for Pool Filling

Use commercial water delivery instead if:

  • ✗ Your well barely keeps up with household use now
  • ✗ You're in a severe drought with declining water levels
  • ✗ Well has very high iron/manganese that requires extensive treatment
  • ✗ Your well produces less than 5 GPM sustained
  • ✗ Neighbors' wells have recently failed or gone dry
  • ✗ You need the pool filled immediately (well filling takes days)

Pool Filling Checklist

  1. Verify well flow rate and recovery capacity
  2. Test water for iron, manganese, pH, hardness, TDS
  3. Purchase metal sequestrant if needed (before filling)
  4. Fill gradually, preferably overnight over several days
  5. Monitor pump operation and well recovery
  6. Keep household water use minimal during filling
  7. Balance pool chemistry after filling (may take extra chemicals due to mineral content)
  8. Document fill time and any issues for future reference
