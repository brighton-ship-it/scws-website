What Causes Rusty Water?

  • Dissolved Iron: Naturally occurring iron in San Diego groundwater
  • Iron Bacteria: Bacteria that feed on iron, creating rusty slime
  • Corroding Casing: Old steel well casing breaking down
  • Corroding Pipes: Galvanized or iron plumbing in your home
  • Failing Water Heater: Rusting anode rod or tank

Is Rusty Water Safe to Drink?

Iron itself isn't a health hazard at typical concentrations. However, rusty water:

  • Tastes metallic and unpleasant
  • Stains sinks, toilets, and laundry
  • Can clog pipes and fixtures over time
  • May indicate other water quality issues

We recommend water testing to identify the specific cause and concentration of iron in your Emerald Hills water.

Severely corroded steel casing may need to be lined or replaced to stop rust contamination.

Rusty Water in Emerald Hills: An Older-Home Issue

Emerald Hills is a hillside neighborhood in southeastern San Diego, part of the City of San Diego and tucked among the Encanto communities with sweeping views from its namesake ridge. It is an established, largely built-out area with many homes dating to the mid-twentieth century, and that age is the key to understanding rusty water here. Emerald Hills is not well country; homes are served by the City of San Diego Public Utilities Department through municipal mains. So when residents see orange, brown, or yellow water, the cause is virtually always corrosion inside the home, particularly older galvanized plumbing and aging water heaters, or a temporary disturbance in the city's distribution system.

This matters because the solution looks completely different from a rural well repair. There is no aquifer or casing to inspect. Instead, the rust is coming from the steel inside your own pipes and tank, or from sediment stirred up in the public mains. Both are common in older San Diego neighborhoods, and both are very fixable once you identify which is at play. A few minutes of simple observation usually narrows it down before any work begins.

What Causes the Discoloration

  • Galvanized steel plumbing. Many Emerald Hills homes were built or plumbed in an era when galvanized pipe was standard. After decades, the protective zinc coating wears away and the steel beneath corrodes, shedding rust into the water, often worst after the water has sat overnight.
  • Water heater corrosion. A water heater's sacrificial anode rod corrodes on purpose to protect the tank. Once it is depleted, the tank itself rusts and discolors the hot water at every faucet.
  • City main flushing or repairs. When the City of San Diego flushes hydrants, repairs a main, or a break occurs, settled iron and mineral sediment gets stirred up and can briefly reach your taps before clearing.
  • Old service lines and fittings. Corroded fittings or an aging service connection can contribute rust independent of your main interior piping.

A simple test points the way: hot-only rust suggests the water heater; cold water that clears after a few minutes suggests city mains or your service line; cold water that is constantly rusty suggests your interior galvanized plumbing.

Is It Safe to Use?

The iron in rusty water is a secondary, aesthetic contaminant, so the discoloration itself is generally not an acute health hazard for healthy adults. It is still worth acting on, because rust signals deteriorating pipes or a failing water heater, it stains laundry and fixtures, and it carries a metallic taste. In older homes there is one extra reason to take discolored water seriously: where galvanized pipe is present, decades-old systems can also harbor legacy concerns, so if you have very old plumbing it is wise to have the water tested rather than assume. If discoloration is widespread on your block, contacting the City of San Diego helps confirm whether it is a system-side issue.

How We Fix Rusty Water in Emerald Hills

Galvanized Pipe Replacement and Repipe

The lasting fix for corroding galvanized plumbing is to replace it with PEX or copper. We can target a single problem run or, for a home with widespread old pipe, scope and perform a full repipe that ends the rust at its source and restores flow that decades of internal corrosion have choked down.

Water Heater Service or Replacement

If the rust is on the hot side only, the water heater is usually the culprit. An anode rod replacement and flush can extend a tank caught early, while a tank already rusting through needs replacement, typically $600 to $1,500 depending on size and type.

Whole-House and Sediment Filtration

To guard against sediment from the municipal system and capture rust particles before they reach fixtures, a sediment or whole-house filter ($300 to $900 for sediment-focused systems) is an effective, affordable step. If hardness is also a concern, a softener or combination system runs $1,500 to $3,500.

Pressure Improvements

Older galvanized lines often reduce flow as they corrode shut. Replacing them frequently restores pressure on its own, and for hillside homes with pressure quirks a booster or constant-pressure system ($2,000 to $4,500) can help.

How Galvanized Pipe Fails Over Time

Galvanized steel pipe was the standard residential plumbing material for much of the twentieth century, and it works well at first because a layer of zinc shields the steel from water. Over the decades that zinc gradually erodes, and once it is gone the exposed steel begins to rust from the inside. Rust scale builds up on the pipe walls, breaking loose in flakes that discolor your water and slowly narrowing the interior diameter until flow and pressure suffer. In a neighborhood like Emerald Hills, where many homes share that mid-century construction era, this is the most common reason cold water runs rusty around the clock rather than just after the system has been disturbed. Replacing the old pipe is the only permanent cure, because no filter can stop a pipe from corroding once the process has started; a filter can capture the loose particles, but it cannot rebuild the pipe wall itself.

What to Check Before You Call

  1. Run hot and cold separately and note which is discolored.
  2. Let the cold tap run two to three minutes to see if it clears.
  3. Ask whether neighbors have the same issue, pointing to the mains.
  4. Check the age and condition of your water heater.
  5. Note whether the rust is worst first thing in the morning.
  6. Fill a clear glass and let particles settle.

When to Call a Professional

Call us if the rust persists after flushing, if it is isolated to the hot water, if your home still has galvanized pipe, or if discoloration keeps coming back. Corroded galvanized lines only get worse, and a rusting water heater can fail suddenly and cause water damage. Frequently Asked Questions

Why is my Emerald Hills water rusty in the morning but clear later?

Overnight, water sits in your pipes and picks up rust from corroding galvanized walls. It clears once fresh water flushes through. Persistent morning rust is a strong sign your interior plumbing should be replaced.

Does my older home have galvanized pipe?

Many mid-century Emerald Hills homes do. We can inspect your plumbing and confirm. If galvanized pipe is corroding, a repipe in PEX or copper is the permanent fix.

Only my hot water is rusty. What does that mean?

That points to the water heater. The anode rod is spent and the tank is corroding. We can advise whether a flush and new anode will help or whether the tank needs replacing.

Is the rusty water dangerous?

Iron is an aesthetic contaminant and generally not an acute hazard at these levels, but in very old plumbing it is wise to test the water, since aging systems can carry other legacy concerns.

What will it cost to fix?

A water heater is about $600 to $1,500, sediment filtration $300 to $900, and a softener or combination system $1,500 to $3,500. A repipe is quoted after inspection. A $125 diagnostic confirms the cause and is credited toward your repair.

Can a well company really help with a city-water home?

Yes. We are a full-water-systems contractor, so we diagnose and fix plumbing, water heaters, filtration, and pressure, not only wells.
