California State Minimum Setback Requirements

California Well Standards require a minimum of 50 feet from septic tanks, 100 feet from septic leach fields or seepage pits, 50 feet from sewer lines, and 100 feet from any source of contamination. Counties may impose stricter requirements than state minimums, so always check local regulations.

Property Line and Structure Setbacks

Most counties require 5-10 feet from property lines as a minimum, while some require 50 feet unless the neighbor provides written consent. Wells must be accessible for maintenance and testing. They cannot be located under structures or in areas with vehicle traffic. Easements and rights-of-way also affect allowable well locations.

Special Setback Situations

Animal enclosures require a 100-foot minimum setback. Chemical storage areas need increased distance. Flood zones may prohibit wells entirely. Abandoned wells must be 50 feet away. Some counties allow reduced setbacks with engineered solutions for unique situations.

Measuring and Documenting Setbacks

Measurements are taken from the well centerline to the nearest edge of each feature. A professional survey may be required for properties with unclear boundaries. Your site plan must show all setback distances clearly. GPS coordinates are increasingly required for well location documentation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I get a variance for well setback requirements in California?

Variances are rare and handled case-by-case by your county environmental health department. You'll need to demonstrate that meeting setbacks is impossible and propose engineered alternatives (like deeper casing or additional sealing). Variances typically require a formal hearing and additional fees. Many counties simply deny variance requests.

What if my neighbor's septic system is within 100 feet of my proposed well location?

California setback requirements apply regardless of property ownership. If your neighbor's septic system is within 100 feet of your only viable well location, you may need to work with your county on alternatives. Options might include a deeper well with enhanced casing, a different location, or connecting to a public water system if available.

Do setback requirements differ for shallow vs. deep wells?

The same minimum setback distances apply regardless of well depth. However, some counties may allow reduced horizontal setbacks for deeper wells with properly sealed casings that extend below potential contamination zones. This requires engineering justification and county approval.
