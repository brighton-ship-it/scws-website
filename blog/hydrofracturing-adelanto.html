What is Hydrofracturing?

Hydrofracturing is a well rehabilitation technique that uses high-pressure water to clean and expand existing fractures in the bedrock surrounding your well. This process:

  • Clears mineral deposits and sediment blocking water flow
  • Opens new pathways for groundwater to reach your well
  • Can increase well yield by 2-10 times in many cases
  • Extends the life of your existing well

Unlike oil and gas fracking, well hydrofracturing uses only clean water—no chemicals are involved.

Signs Your Adelanto Well Needs Hydrofracturing

  • Declining water production over time
  • Well runs dry during peak usage
  • Slow recovery rate after pumping
  • Pump cycling on and off frequently
  • Lower water pressure than previous years

The Hydrofracturing Process

  1. Initial Assessment: We evaluate your well's current yield and condition
  2. Equipment Setup: Specialized high-pressure equipment is positioned at your well
  3. Isolation: Packers seal off sections of the well for targeted treatment
  4. Pressurization: Water is pumped at 500-3,000 PSI to fracture bedrock
  5. Development: Well is cleaned and tested for improved yield

Hydrofracturing for Adelanto-Area Wells

Adelanto sits in the High Desert of San Bernardino County, on the western edge of the Victor Valley near Victorville, Hesperia, and Apple Valley. This is part of the Upper Mojave River Valley groundwater basin, and it is a region where groundwater does most of the heavy lifting, the Mojave River runs only intermittently, so private and municipal users depend on what the aquifer holds. Years of pumping for the fast-growing Victor Valley communities have drawn the regional water table down significantly, and a well-known cone of depression has formed near Adelanto itself. For property owners whose wells are slipping, hydrofracturing can be a valuable rehabilitation tool, though candidacy in this area depends heavily on the geology a given well taps.

It is important to be honest about the rock. Much of the Adelanto basin is filled with alluvium, layers of sand, gravel, and silt deposited over geologic time, and wells screened entirely in loose sand and gravel are generally not hydrofracturing candidates, because there are no rigid fractures to open. Where hydrofracturing earns its keep around Adelanto is in wells that reach the fractured bedrock at the basin margins and along the foothills, or wells finished in the consolidated, partly cemented older deposits of the regional aquifer. In those settings, high-pressure treatment can reopen tight fractures and clear plugging, restoring flow that drilling alone could not.

How Hydrofracturing Works

The process uses pressure and clean water rather than chemicals. Our crew lowers an inflatable packer into the well to seal off a chosen section of the borehole, then injects clean water at high pressure, commonly 1,500 to 3,000 PSI and sometimes higher for deep, tight rock. In a well that bottoms in fractured bedrock or consolidated material, that pressure pushes into the existing cracks, flushing out fine sediment and mineral scale and opening hairline fractures so they connect to a wider water-bearing network. The packers let us isolate and target the most promising zone rather than wasting pressure on solid or unsuitable material.

For Adelanto-area owners, the two key points are these: the treatment uses only clean water, with no chemicals or additives that could affect water quality, which matters in a basin already managing arsenic and mineral concerns; and a proper evaluation up front determines whether your specific well, given what it is screened in, will actually respond. A treatment generally takes four to eight hours.

Steps in a Treatment

  1. Baseline flow test to record current yield and water level.
  2. Downhole camera inspection to see what the well is finished in and check casing condition.
  3. Packer isolation and pressurization of the bedrock or consolidated zones worth targeting.
  4. Re-development and re-test to clear the well and verify any improvement.

Common Causes of Declining Yield Around Adelanto

In the Victor Valley, several patterns drive low-yield calls:

  • Regional water-table decline — sustained basin-wide pumping has lowered groundwater levels and deepened the cone of depression near Adelanto, leaving some wells reaching for water.
  • Fracture and screen plugging — fine sediment and mineral scale clog the productive zones in bedrock-margin wells and around well screens over time.
  • Wells that need to chase the water table — older, shallower wells may no longer reach the saturated zone reliably.
  • Seasonal and drought stress — High Desert summers and dry years sharpen demand just as supply tightens.

For wells whose decline comes from a fixable issue, plugged fractures in bedrock, a tight bedrock-margin completion, hydrofracturing can help. For wells simply outpaced by a dropping water table in loose alluvium, deepening or a new well may be the better answer, and we will tell you which situation you are in.

What to Check and Signs You May Need It

Watch for these indicators that a well is struggling:

  • Air sputtering from faucets when demand is high.
  • A pump running much longer than it used to, or short-cycling.
  • Falling pressure when several fixtures run together.
  • Sediment or cloudiness after heavy use that settles with rest.
  • A markedly longer recovery time after a big draw.

Confirm the basics first, a faulty pressure switch, waterlogged tank, or worn pump can imitate low yield. A diagnostic separates a true supply problem from a mechanical one and clarifies whether hydrofracturing is even the right tool.

When to Call a Professional

Because hydrofracturing involves high-pressure equipment and an informed read of the local geology, it belongs in the hands of a licensed C-57 contractor. In a mixed basin like Adelanto's, that expertise matters even more, the difference between a well that will respond and one that will not often comes down to what it is screened in. Call when your yield is clearly declining, when a new well disappointed, or when you are weighing rehabilitation against a deeper or new well.

How Hydrofracturing Compares to Deepening or Drilling New

In a basin like Adelanto's, where the water table has dropped and the geology is mixed, choosing the right response matters. Hydrofracturing is the least invasive and least expensive option, and for a well that taps fractured or consolidated rock it can restore strong flow at a fraction of the cost of alternatives. Deepening pushes an existing borehole farther down to chase the water table or reach new water-bearing material; it can be the correct call when the table has fallen below an older well, but it costs more and offers no guarantee. Drilling an entirely new well is the largest investment and is sometimes unavoidable, yet it should rarely be the first step when a candidate well can be rehabilitated for far less. The honest answer depends on your specific well, which is exactly what our diagnostic is designed to determine before you spend a dollar on the work itself.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will hydrofracturing work on my Adelanto well?

It depends on what your well is finished in. Wells reaching fractured bedrock or consolidated older deposits are good candidates; wells screened entirely in loose sand and gravel generally are not. We confirm with a camera inspection before recommending it.

Does the process use chemicals?

No. Only clean water is used. There are no chemicals or additives, which is important in a basin already managing arsenic and mineral content.

Can hydrofracturing fix a well affected by the falling water table?

If the decline is from plugged fractures or a tight bedrock completion, yes. If the regional water table has simply dropped below an older well in loose alluvium, deepening or a new well may serve you better, and we will tell you which applies.

How long does the treatment take?

The treatment itself runs four to eight hours, with re-testing within 24 to 48 hours. Most wells are back in service quickly.

Will it damage my well casing?

No. We inspect the casing first and isolate the zone with packers, directing pressure into the open rock below the casing rather than against the pipe.

How much could my yield improve?

For a good candidate in fractured or consolidated rock, improvements of two to five times the pre-treatment flow are common. We document the before-and-after so the gain is measurable.
