What is Hydrofracturing?

Hydrofracturing is a well rehabilitation technique that uses high-pressure water to clean and expand existing fractures in the bedrock surrounding your well. This process:

  • Clears mineral deposits and sediment blocking water flow
  • Opens new pathways for groundwater to reach your well
  • Can increase well yield by 2-10 times in many cases
  • Extends the life of your existing well

Unlike oil and gas fracking, well hydrofracturing uses only clean water—no chemicals are involved.

Signs Your Ocean Beach Well Needs Hydrofracturing

  • Declining water production over time
  • Well runs dry during peak usage
  • Slow recovery rate after pumping
  • Pump cycling on and off frequently
  • Lower water pressure than previous years

The Hydrofracturing Process

  1. Initial Assessment: We evaluate your well's current yield and condition
  2. Equipment Setup: Specialized high-pressure equipment is positioned at your well
  3. Isolation: Packers seal off sections of the well for targeted treatment
  4. Pressurization: Water is pumped at 500-3,000 PSI to fracture bedrock
  5. Development: Well is cleaned and tested for improved yield

Cost vs. New Well Drilling in Ocean Beach

In Ocean Beach, drilling a new well can cost $15,000-$50,000+ depending on depth. Hydrofracturing typically costs a fraction of that—often $3,000-$8,000—making it an excellent first option before considering a new well.

Many Ocean Beach wells that appear to be "going dry" can be successfully rehabilitated through hydrofracturing, saving homeowners tens of thousands of dollars.

Hydrofracturing in Ocean Beach: An Honest Overview

Ocean Beach — OB to locals — is a laid-back coastal neighborhood at the mouth of the San Diego River, part of the City of San Diego in San Diego County. It is also entirely on municipal water, with no private bedrock wells in the neighborhood. If hydrofracturing brought you here, the honest answer is that the technique does not apply to property within Ocean Beach itself. It becomes relevant only if you own a well on an inland or mountain property elsewhere in the county. Below, we explain the why and how, and how we can help.

Why Ocean Beach Is Not Hydrofracturing Country

Hydrofracturing rehabilitates bedrock wells by opening the natural fractures in solid crystalline rock. Ocean Beach offers none of what the method requires:

  • Coastal sediment and soft marine rock. The area sits on beach sand, terrace deposits, and the soft sedimentary rock of the Point Loma peninsula — not the hard, fractured granite that fractures usefully.
  • Brackish shallow groundwater. Near the coast and the river mouth, shallow water is salty and not a freshwater source you would develop.
  • City water everywhere. Properties are served by the municipal system; private wells are not part of the picture.

We tell OB owners this directly rather than chase a treatment that cannot work.

How Hydrofracturing Works on a Real Candidate

Where there is genuine fractured bedrock, the process is straightforward and chemical-free. We seal off a section of the well's borehole with an inflatable packer, then inject clean water at high pressure — commonly 1,500 to 3,000 PSI. The pressurized water forces open the rock's natural hairline fractures, flushes out the silt and scale clogging them, and widens the connected network so groundwater flows to the well more freely. A typical job takes four to eight hours, and on a good candidate it can raise yield several times over.

Where San Diego County's Good Candidates Live

The wells worth treating are inland and uphill, in the foothill and mountain communities where homes and ranches draw from decomposed granite and fractured crystalline bedrock — areas such as Ramona, Julian, Alpine, Jamul, and the wider backcountry. If you split time between Ocean Beach and a rural property like that, the rural well may be a strong hydrofracturing candidate. That is where our well crews spend their days.

Signs a Bedrock Well Could Benefit

  • A once-productive well whose yield has slipped over the years.
  • A new bedrock well that came in lower than hoped.
  • Slow recovery after heavy pumping.
  • Running short in late summer and fall.

What to Check Before You Call (For an Inland Well)

  • Confirm it is a private well rather than a shared or municipal connection.
  • Locate the well completion report. Granite or fractured rock near the bottom is a good sign; all sand and gravel is not.
  • Read the symptoms. Air spitting at the tap and slow recovery suggest the well; steady low pressure throughout the house suggests the pump or pressure tank.
  • Check the equipment. A failing pump, waterlogged tank, or worn pressure switch can mimic a dying well far more cheaply.

Ocean Beach sits where the San Diego River meets the Pacific, beside the rocky Point Loma peninsula.
