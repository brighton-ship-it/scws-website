Pedley Geology & Groundwater

Pedley sits within the Riverside groundwater sub-basin of the upper Santa Ana River watershed. The ground beneath most Pedley lots is dominated by alluvial deposits — layered sand, gravel, silt, and clay washed down over millennia from the surrounding Jurupa Mountains and the Santa Ana River floodplain. These water-bearing alluvial sands are generally productive and relatively forgiving to drill, which is good news for cost and schedule. Beneath the alluvium lies the crystalline basement rock of the Peninsular Ranges: hard granitic and metamorphic rock that some deeper wells must partially penetrate to reach a stable, reliable aquifer.

Because Pedley is close to the Santa Ana River, the water table here is often shallower than in the higher, rockier communities farther east in Riverside County. That means many residential wells can be completed at moderate depths — frequently in the 150-to-400-foot range — though a proper site assessment is always the first step. Groundwater levels in the basin fluctuate with rainfall, upstream recharge, and pumping across the valley, so we evaluate neighboring well logs and California Department of Water Resources completion records before we ever spot a rig on your property.

How a New Well Is Drilled

A turnkey new well in Pedley moves through a predictable sequence of stages. Understanding each one helps you know exactly what you're paying for and why every step matters.

1. Site Assessment & Well Siting

We start by studying the parcel, reviewing nearby well records, and identifying the best drilling location — a spot with good aquifer access that also satisfies setback rules from septic systems, property lines, and any potential contamination sources. Correct siting protects both your water quality and your permit approval.

2. Permitting

Riverside County well permits are issued through the county's Department of Environmental Health. Turnaround is typically two to three weeks. We handle the entire application, inspection scheduling, and required well completion report for Pedley properties, so you never have to navigate county paperwork on your own.

3. Drilling the Borehole

Our fleet includes a Gefco rotary drill rig capable of reaching 1,000+ feet. In Pedley's alluvial ground, the rig advances steadily through sand and gravel, circulating drilling fluid to carry cuttings to the surface and stabilize the hole. If we reach the harder Peninsular Ranges basement rock, drilling slows but the rig is built to handle it. Most residential wells here reach target depth in one to three days.

4. Casing & Well Completion

Once the borehole is complete, we install casing — PVC or steel depending on depth and geology — to keep the hole from collapsing and to seal out shallow surface contamination. A gravel pack is placed around the perforated screen zone to filter fine sediment while letting clean water flow in, and a sanitary surface seal (grout) locks out runoff at the top of the well.

5. Development & Pump Installation

We then develop the well — surging and pumping it to clear fines and maximize yield — and test the water. Finally we install a properly sized submersible pump, pressure tank, pressure switch, and controls, then tie the system into your plumbing. The complete process, from first cut to running water, typically takes three to five business days.

Permits, Depth & Cost in Pedley

Because Pedley's aquifer is comparatively shallow and its alluvial ground drills efficiently, a new residential well here often lands toward the friendlier end of the cost range. A full turnkey well — drilling, casing, gravel pack, development, pump, tank, and controls — typically runs $18,000 to $42,000 depending on final depth, casing requirements, and the pump system your household needs. Every well is quoted individually after a site assessment; we never bury surprises in the fine print.

Individual components, whether for a new build or a later repair, fall in these ranges:

  • Submersible pump: $2,500–$5,500
  • Pressure tank: $600–$1,500
  • Pressure switch: $150–$350
  • Control box / capacitor: $400–$900
  • Constant-pressure / booster system: $2,000–$4,500
  • Well inspection: $150–$400
  • Diagnostic visit: $125 (credited toward your repair)

Common Pedley Scenarios

Many of the calls we take in Pedley fall into a few familiar patterns. Owners of older equestrian and ranch parcels often want a new well to replace an aging shallow well that no longer keeps up with irrigation and livestock demand. New buyers on unincorporated Jurupa Valley lots need a first well before they can build or occupy. And homeowners on existing wells frequently call for water quality upgrades — sediment filtration ($300–$900), a water softener ($1,500–$3,500) for the mineral-rich groundwater common in the basin, UV treatment ($800–$1,800), or a reverse-osmosis drinking system ($300–$1,200). Whatever the situation, we start with an honest assessment of what your property actually needs.

What to Check & When to Call a Pro

If you already have a well and notice dropping pressure, sputtering faucets, cloudy or gritty water, rising electric bills from a pump that runs constantly, or a sudden loss of water, those are all signals to call before a small problem becomes an emergency. New-well drilling, pump work below the water line, and any electrical control work should always be handled by a licensed C-57 contractor — both for safety and to keep your well in compliance with Riverside County regulations. Frequently Asked Questions

How deep do wells need to be in Pedley?

Because Pedley sits in the shallow alluvial ground near the Santa Ana River, many residential wells reach reliable water in the 150-to-400-foot range. Local conditions vary, so we base final depth on a site assessment and nearby well records before drilling.

How much does a new well cost in Pedley?

A complete turnkey well in Pedley typically runs $18,000 to $42,000, covering drilling, casing, gravel pack, development, pump, tank, and controls. Pedley's moderate depths often keep projects toward the lower end. We provide a free written estimate first.

Do I need a permit to drill a well in Pedley?

Yes. Riverside County requires a well permit through its Department of Environmental Health, typically issued within two to three weeks. We handle the entire permit and completion-report process for you.

How long does the drilling process take?

Most residential wells in Pedley are drilled in one to three days. The full turnkey process — including casing, development, pump installation, and plumbing tie-in — usually takes three to five business days.

What kind of casing and pump will my Pedley well use?

We use PVC or steel casing depending on depth and geology, with a gravel pack for optimal water production. Pumps are submersibles sized to your household demand and well depth, paired with a matched pressure tank and controls.

Is the groundwater in Pedley hard?

Groundwater across the Santa Ana basin is often mineral-rich. Many Pedley wells benefit from a water softener ($1,500–$3,500) and sediment filtration ($300–$900). We test your water and recommend only the treatment your supply actually needs.
