What to Look for in a Local Well Company

Not all well companies are created equal. Here's what separates reliable contractors from those who'll leave you with problems:

Proper Licensing

In California, well work requires specific licenses from the Contractors State License Board (CSLB):

  • C-57 Well Drilling Contractor: Required for drilling new wells
  • C-61/D21 Machinery & Pumps: May perform pump work

Always verify licensing at cslb.ca.gov before hiring. Unlicensed work is illegal, potentially dangerous, and can create serious problems when you try to sell your property.

Local Experience

San Diego County's geology varies dramatically—from decomposed granite in Ramona to fractured rock in Julian to alluvial deposits in the valleys. A well company that knows local conditions will:

  • Accurately estimate drilling depth and costs
  • Know which aquifers are reliable in your area
  • Understand local water quality issues
  • Navigate county permitting efficiently
  • Have relationships with local inspectors

Insurance & Bonding

Legitimate well contractors carry:

  • General liability insurance: Protects your property during work
  • Workers' compensation: Covers employees injured on your property
  • Contractor's bond: Required by CSLB, provides recourse if work is substandard

Ask for certificates of insurance and verify they're current. A company that hesitates to provide this documentation is a red flag.

Questions to Ask Before Hiring a Well Company

Before signing a contract, ask these questions:

📋 Essential Questions Checklist

  1. "What's your license number?" Verify at cslb.ca.gov
  2. "How long have you worked in this area?" Local experience matters
  3. "Can you provide references from projects near me?" Recent, local references are best
  4. "Do you handle the permits?" A good contractor manages permitting
  5. "What's included in your written estimate?" Get detailed, itemized quotes
  6. "What warranties do you offer?" Quality contractors stand behind their work
  7. "What's your response time for emergencies?" Know before you need help
  8. "Who will be doing the actual work?" Some companies subcontract
