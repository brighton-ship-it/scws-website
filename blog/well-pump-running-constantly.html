Well Pump Running Constantly: 8 Causes & How to Fix

  • Pump Running Non-Stop?
Quick Answer: A pump that won't shut off usually means it can't reach the cut-off pressure (typically 50-60 PSI). Most common causes: water leak in system, waterlogged pressure tank, failed pressure switch, or pump can't deliver enough pressure. Turn off the pump to prevent motor damage and diagnose the issue.

Why This Is Urgent

A constantly running pump is an emergency. Here's why:

  • Motor overheating: Submersible pumps are cooled by water flow, designed for cycles, not continuous operation
  • Shortened lifespan: Continuous running can destroy a pump in weeks or months
  • Higher electric bills: Pump uses electricity the entire time
  • Water waste: If there's a leak, you're losing water constantly

Immediate Action

  1. Turn off power to the pump at the breaker
  2. Check for obvious leaks
  3. Call for service if you can't identify the cause

8 Common Causes

1. Water Leak in System (Most Common)

Any leak prevents pressure from building to the shut-off point.

  • Check: Look for wet spots, dripping faucets, running toilets, irrigation leaks
  • Fix: Find and repair the leak

2. Waterlogged Pressure Tank

When the tank loses its air charge, it fills completely with water. Pressure fluctuates rapidly, and the pump can't maintain steady pressure.

  • Check: Tank feels heavy, sounds solid when tapped
  • Fix: Recharge air or replace tank ($500-$1,200)

3. Failed Pressure Switch

The switch should turn off the pump at 50-60 PSI. If contacts are welded or stuck, it won't shut off.

  • Check: Look at pressure gauge while pump runs
  • Fix: Replace switch ($100-$250)

4. Worn Pump (Can't Build Pressure)

Worn impellers can't generate enough pressure to reach shut-off.

  • Check: Pressure gauge never reaches cut-off (e.g., maxes at 40 when cut-off is 50)
  • Fix: Replace pump ($1,500-$4,000)

5. Leaking Check Valve

If the check valve leaks, water drains back down the well. The pump has to keep replacing it.

  • Check: Pressure drops quickly when pump stops briefly
  • Fix: Replace check valve ($150-$400)

6. Low Water Level in Well

If the well can't keep up, the pump pulls air mixed with water, affecting pressure.

  • Check: Sputtering water, air in lines
  • Fix: Reduce usage, lower pump, or address well production

7. Pressure Switch Set Too High

If cut-off pressure is set higher than the pump can deliver, it runs forever.

  • Check: Verify switch settings match pump capacity
  • Fix: Adjust settings or replace with appropriate switch

8. Broken Drop Pipe

If the pipe from pump to surface is cracked, water leaks back into the well.

  • Check: Pump sounds normal, pressure never builds
  • Fix: Pull pump and replace pipe ($500-$2,000)

How to Diagnose

Step 1: Check Pressure Gauge

  • Pressure rising slowly → leak or weak pump
  • Pressure won't rise past certain point → pump can't deliver or major leak
  • Pressure reaches cut-off but pump continues → switch stuck

Step 2: Look for Leaks

  1. Turn off all fixtures in house
  2. Check all faucets, toilets, hose bibs
  3. Listen for running water
  4. Check irrigation system
  5. Look for wet spots in yard

Step 3: Test Pressure Tank

  1. Turn off pump
  2. Tap tank from top to bottom
  3. Should be hollow at top, solid at bottom
  4. If solid throughout = waterlogged

Step 4: Test Shut-Off

  1. Close valve between tank and house
  2. Turn pump on
  3. Watch if it shuts off at cut-off pressure
  4. If yes, leak is in house plumbing
  5. If no, problem is pump, tank, or switch

Fixes by Cause

Leak Repair

  • Indoor plumbing: Plumber repair
  • Irrigation: Repair or cap broken heads/lines
  • Underground: May need leak detection

Pressure Tank

  • Recharge air if bladder is intact ($50-$150)
  • Replace tank if bladder failed ($500-$1,200)

Pressure Switch

  • Clean contacts (temporary fix)
  • Replace switch ($100-$250 installed)

Worn Pump

  • Replace pump ($1,500-$4,000)
  • No repair option for worn impellers

Frequently Asked Questions

Why won't my well pump shut off?

Usually because it can't reach the cut-off pressure due to: a leak, waterlogged tank, failed switch, or worn pump. Turn off power and diagnose.

Is it bad if my well pump runs constantly?

Yes—very bad. Continuous running overheats the motor and can destroy the pump in weeks. It's an emergency situation.

How long can a well pump run continuously?

Pumps are designed for cycles, not continuous operation. Running more than 30-60 minutes continuously is concerning. Non-stop running causes rapid wear.

What if I can't find the leak?

Leaks can be underground or inside walls. You may need professional leak detection. Meanwhile, use water minimally and turn off pump when not needed.

Can I just let it run until I fix it?

No—you'll burn out the pump motor. Turn it off between uses. Better to have no water temporarily than to destroy a $3,000 pump.
