Well Digging vs. Well Drilling: What's the Difference?

When people search for well diggers near me, they're usually looking for well drilling services. Here's why the distinction matters:

Traditional Well Digging (Rarely Used Today)

Historically, wells were literally dug by hand or with excavating equipment. These "dug wells" had characteristics that made them problematic:

  • Shallow depth: Typically only 10-30 feet deep
  • Large diameter: 3-6 feet wide
  • Contamination risk: Shallow depth means exposure to surface contamination
  • Unreliable supply: Easily affected by drought and seasonal changes
  • Safety hazards: Large openings pose fall risks

Modern Well Drilling (Industry Standard)

Today's water wells are created using rotary or percussion drilling rigs that offer significant advantages:

  • Greater depth: Typically 200-600+ feet in San Diego County
  • Small diameter: 4-8 inch casing protects the water supply
  • Protected from contamination: Sanitary seal prevents surface water infiltration
  • Reliable supply: Deep aquifers provide consistent year-round water
  • Safe and sanitary: Sealed wellhead meets health codes

ℹ️ Note: In San Diego and Riverside Counties, all new water wells are drilled rather than dug. County health departments don't permit traditional dug wells because they don't meet modern sanitary standards. When you're looking for "well diggers," you're actually looking for licensed well drilling contractors.

How to Find Well Diggers Near You

Finding reliable well diggers near me (well drilling services) requires some research. Here's how to find qualified professionals:

Step 1: Search for Licensed Contractors

In California, well drilling requires a C-57 Water Well Contractor license. Search for:

  • "Well drilling" + your city name
  • "Water well contractor" + your area
  • "C-57 contractor" + your county

Step 2: Verify Credentials

Before contacting any contractor, verify their license at cslb.ca.gov. Check that:

  • License is active and in good standing
  • License includes C-57 classification
  • No serious complaints or disciplinary actions
  • Workers' compensation insurance is current (if they have employees)

Step 3: Check Reviews and References

Look for consistent positive feedback:

  • Google Reviews: Look for detailed reviews from local customers
  • Yelp: Check both positive and negative reviews for patterns
  • Ask for references: Request 3-5 recent customers in your area
  • BBB rating: Check for accreditation and complaint history

Step 4: Get Multiple Estimates

Contact at least three contractors for written estimates. A good estimate should include:

  • Estimated depth range and per-foot costs
  • Casing specifications (type, diameter)
  • Pump brand, size, and specifications
  • Pressure tank size and type
  • Permit fees and who handles permitting
  • Timeline from start to completion
  • Warranty terms on equipment and workmanship
  • Policy if deeper drilling is required

What to Expect: The Well Drilling Process

When you hire well diggers (drillers), here's what the process looks like:

Phase 1: Consultation and Estimate

The contractor visits your property to:

  • Evaluate potential drilling locations
  • Check setback requirements from septic and property lines
  • Assess access for drilling equipment
  • Research neighboring well records
  • Provide detailed written estimate

Phase 2: Permitting (1-2 Weeks)

Your contractor handles all permit requirements with the county Department of Environmental Health, including site plans and fee payment.

Phase 3: Drilling (1-3 Days)

The drilling rig arrives and work begins:

  • Rig setup at the designated location
  • Drilling progresses at 50-200 feet per day (depending on rock)
  • Driller monitors for water-producing zones
  • Casing installed to protect the wellbore
  • Grouting creates sanitary seal

Phase 4: Inspection

County inspector verifies proper construction before pump installation can proceed.

Phase 5: Pump Installation (1-2 Days)

  • Submersible pump lowered into well
  • Electrical connections made
  • Pressure tank installed and configured
  • System tested and adjusted

Phase 6: Final Connection and Handoff

The well is connected to your plumbing system, final inspection is completed, and you receive documentation and training on your new well system.
