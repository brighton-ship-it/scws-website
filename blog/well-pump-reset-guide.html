Quick Answer: Reset breaker first (off, then on). Some pressure switches have a reset lever/button—turn off power, reset, turn power back on. If pump trips again immediately, don't keep resetting—there's a problem. Call a professional.

When to Try a Reset

Good Candidates for Reset

  • Power outage just ended
  • Breaker tripped once
  • System was working fine before
  • No other symptoms

Don't Keep Resetting If

  • Breaker trips again immediately
  • Pump hums but doesn't run
  • Burning smell
  • Multiple recent trips
  • Already reset several times

Common Reset Scenarios

  • After power outage: Reset usually works
  • Lightning nearby: May need reset, check surge protector
  • Random trip: One reset okay, repeat trips = problem

Reset Steps

Step 1: Check the Breaker

  1. Locate electrical panel
  2. Find well pump breaker (usually labeled)
  3. Check position:
    • ON = normal
    • OFF = turned off
    • Middle = tripped
  4. If tripped: Turn fully OFF first, then ON

Step 2: Listen for Pump

  • After resetting breaker, pump should start
  • May hear it running (jet pump) or see pressure rise
  • Submersible pumps are quiet—watch pressure gauge

Step 3: Check Pressure Switch (If Needed)

  • Located near pressure tank
  • Gray box with wires
  • Some have reset lever or button

To Reset Pressure Switch:

  1. Turn off breaker first
  2. Remove cover (if applicable)
  3. Find reset lever (small metal lever) or button
  4. Push/flip to reset position
  5. Replace cover
  6. Turn breaker back on

Step 4: Monitor System

  • Watch pressure gauge
  • Should build to cut-off pressure (typically 40-60 PSI)
  • Pump should shut off
  • Run water—pump should restart when pressure drops

Safety Warnings

  • Always turn off breaker before touching switch
  • Water and electricity don't mix
  • If unsure, call a professional
  • Don't force anything

If Reset Doesn't Work

Breaker Trips Again Immediately

  • Electrical short somewhere
  • Could be pump, wiring, or control box
  • Stop resetting—call professional

Pump Hums But Won't Run

  • Motor trying but stuck
  • Bad capacitor (common)
  • Seized pump
  • Turn off immediately—motor will burn out

Nothing Happens

  • No power to pump
  • Check breaker is actually on
  • Check for tripped GFCI (rare)
  • Pressure switch or control box issue

Pump Runs But No Water

  • Water level too low
  • Pump not primed (jet pump)
  • Leak in system
  • Pump failure

When to Call Professional

  • Multiple reset attempts failed
  • Burning smell
  • Pump hums but won't start
  • Breaker trips repeatedly
  • You're not comfortable with electrical

Preventing Future Trips

Install Surge Protection

  • Lightning/surge suppressor on pump circuit
  • Protects from power spikes
  • Cost: $50-$200

Maintain Pressure Tank

  • Check air charge annually
  • Waterlogged tank causes rapid cycling
  • Rapid cycling stresses system

Proper Sizing

  • Pump sized for well
  • Correct wire gauge for distance
  • Appropriate breaker size

Low Water Protection

  • Install low-water cutoff
  • Prevents running dry
  • Protects pump from damage

Frequently Asked Questions

Where is the reset button on my well pump?

Most don't have a reset button—you reset by cycling the breaker. Some pressure switches have a reset lever inside the cover.

Why does my well pump keep tripping?

Could be: overloaded circuit, short in wiring, bad pump motor, or ground fault. Don't keep resetting—diagnose the cause.

Can I damage my pump by resetting too much?

Yes—if there's a short or motor problem, repeated starts can cause further damage. One or two resets is okay; more needs diagnosis.

How do I know if my pressure switch needs resetting?

If breaker is on but pump won't start, switch may need reset. Turn off power, reset lever if present, restore power.

My pump runs continuously—is that a reset issue?

No—continuous running indicates other problems: leak, low water, bad pressure switch, or pressure tank issue.
