Common Well Pump Problems in Winchester

No Water from the Faucets

Complete water loss is the most urgent situation. Common causes in Winchester include:

  • Electrical failure: Tripped breaker, failed capacitor, burnt control box, or lightning damage
  • Pump motor burnout: Common in older pumps, especially after hot summers
  • Pressure switch failure: Switch not activating the pump
  • Dropped water table: During drought, pump intake may be above water level
  • Broken drop pipe: Pipe or fitting failure inside the well

Low Water Pressure

Gradually declining pressure often signals developing problems:

  • Waterlogged pressure tank: Lost air charge, needs service or replacement
  • Worn pump impellers: Sand or normal wear reducing pump output
  • Clogged filters: Sediment buildup restricting flow
  • Failing pump: Motor losing power, producing less flow

🐴 Horse Property Tip

Winchester's many horse properties have higher water demands than typical homes. If you're experiencing pressure drops during peak usage—filling troughs, running sprinklers, and household use simultaneously—your pump or pressure tank may be undersized. We can evaluate your system and recommend upgrades to meet your actual water needs.

Pump Cycling or Short-Cycling

Rapid on-off cycling damages pumps and indicates:

  • Waterlogged pressure tank: Most common cause of short-cycling
  • Pressure switch issues: Incorrect settings or failing switch
  • System leaks: Water escaping prevents pressure buildup
  • Check valve failure: Water flowing back down the well

Sand or Sediment in Water

If you're suddenly seeing sand or sediment, address it immediately—this damages pump impellers, clogs fixtures, and worsens rapidly. Causes include pump set too deep, well screen failure, or dropping water table exposing sandy zones.
