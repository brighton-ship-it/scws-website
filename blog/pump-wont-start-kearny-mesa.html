Common Causes

  • Tripped Breaker: Check your electrical panel first
  • Pressure Switch Failure: Switch that tells pump to turn on
  • Control Box Issues: Capacitor or relay failure (submersible pumps)
  • Motor Burnout: Pump motor has failed
  • Wiring Problems: Damaged wires or loose connections
  • Low Water: Pump may have protection that shuts it off

Quick Checks You Can Do

  1. Check if circuit breaker is tripped (reset once only)
  2. Listen at pressure tank for clicking from pressure switch
  3. Check pressure gauge reading on tank
  4. Look for any visible wire damage
  5. Check if other electrical items on same circuit work

Caution: Do not repeatedly reset a tripping breaker—this can cause electrical fire or further damage.

Common Reasons a Well Pump Won't Start in Kearny Mesa

On urban and commercial mesa properties, the usual no-start causes mix with the effects of age and intermittent irrigation use. Here is what we see most often around Kearny Mesa:

  • Tripped breaker or blown fuse: Always the first thing to rule out. On a commercial or older residential panel, a surge or an overloaded circuit can cut power to the 240-volt pump.
  • Failed pressure switch or clogged sensing port: The pressure switch tells the pump when to run. Pitted contacts or a sediment-clogged sensing port—common on irrigation wells that cycle seasonally—leave the pump with no start signal.
  • Burned-out capacitor or control box: Capacitors weaken with age and duty cycle. A failed capacitor usually leaves the motor humming but not turning.
  • Failed or seized motor or pump: Irrigation pumps that sit between watering seasons can seize, and older motors lose their windings over time.
  • Wiring and corrosion faults: Aging conduit, weathered junction boxes, and corroded splices are common on long-established mesa properties.
  • Stuck check valve: A check valve seized open or shut disrupts normal cycling, especially after idle periods.
  • Waterlogged or air-bound pressure tank: A tank that has lost its air charge short-cycles and can keep the pump from starting cleanly.
  • Legacy and private well factors in an urban setting: Mesa wells are often older irrigation systems with original or modified controls. Deferred maintenance and undocumented wiring frequently turn a simple no-start into a quick diagnostic puzzle.

Safety-First Step-by-Step Checks

Well pumps—residential or commercial—run on 240 volts, which can seriously injure or kill. On commercial sites in particular, treat the equipment with respect and never open a control box or pressure switch while the power is live.

  1. Turn the power OFF at the breaker first. Before inspecting the pressure switch, control box, or wellhead, switch off the pump's double-pole breaker. 240V is dangerous—this step comes first, every time.
  2. With power off, check the panel for a tripped or scorched breaker and reset it once only.
  3. Restore power and listen at the pressure tank for the pressure switch to click, then watch the gauge for movement.
  4. Confirm other 240V circuits or equipment are working to separate a panel-side problem from a pump fault.
  5. Inspect visible wiring, conduit, and the control box for corrosion or scorching—never while energized.
  6. If the breaker trips again immediately, stop and call a professional. Repeated resets can cause a fire.

Electrical Causes Worth Understanding

The majority of Kearny Mesa no-start calls are electrical. Aging pressure switches, tired capacitors, corroded connections, and panel issues interrupt the start signal or the current without the pump itself being at fault. On irrigation wells that only run part of the year, components corrode and degrade during the off-season and then fail to restart when watering resumes. Because mesa wells frequently use original or modified controls, a careful electrical check pays off. A technician measures incoming voltage, tests the capacitor's microfarads, and checks the motor windings to pinpoint whether the panel, the controls, the wiring, or the pump is the real cause before any parts are replaced.

Frequently Asked Questions

My irrigation well won't start after the off-season. Why?

Wells that sit idle between watering seasons commonly develop seized pumps, stuck check valves, pitted pressure-switch contacts, and weakened capacitors. The system worked at the end of last season but won't restart now because components corroded or degraded while idle. A technician can identify and fix the affected part.

Do you service commercial wells in Kearny Mesa?

Yes. We service both residential and commercial wells, including the irrigation and secondary-supply systems on office parks, landscaped sites, and older parcels across the mesa. We work to minimize disruption to your operation.

Is the old wiring on my mesa well safe?

Legacy and commercial wells sometimes carry outdated or modified wiring. If you notice corrosion, brittle insulation, or non-standard connections, have it evaluated. We can bring the control wiring up to a safe, code-conscious standard.

Is it safe to reset my pump breaker myself?

Resetting a tripped breaker once is fine. If it trips again immediately, stop and call a professional. Repeated resets on a faulting circuit risk fire and further motor damage.

My pump hums but won't turn. What does that indicate?

A humming motor that won't turn typically means a failed start capacitor or control box. Turn off the power and call a professional; running it that way can overheat and destroy the motor.
