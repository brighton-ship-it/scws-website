Annual Well Maintenance Checklist: Your Complete Yearly Inspection Guide

Updated February 2026 • Southern California Well Service

A well-maintained water well can last 25-50 years, but neglected systems often fail within 10-15 years. This annual maintenance checklist will help you catch problems early, extend equipment life, and ensure your family has safe, reliable water year-round.

Why Annual Inspections Matter

Preventive maintenance is the most cost-effective approach to well ownership. Studies show that well-maintained systems last 50% longer than neglected ones. More importantly, catching small problems early—before they cascade into system failures—saves thousands of dollars in emergency repairs.

Consider this: a worn pump capacitor costs $30-50 to replace during routine maintenance. But if that capacitor fails completely, it can burn out your pump motor—turning a $50 fix into a $2,000-3,000 pump replacement. Annual inspections identify these warning signs before they become emergencies.

Beyond cost savings, regular maintenance protects your family's health. Well water quality can change gradually due to aquifer conditions, nearby land use, or developing contamination sources. Annual testing catches these changes before they become health hazards.

Visual Inspection Points

Start your annual inspection with a thorough visual check of all accessible components. You don't need specialized tools for this—just your eyes, a flashlight, and about 30 minutes.

Well Cap and Seal: Examine your well cap for cracks, corrosion, or damage. The cap should sit flush and secure—if it wobbles or has gaps, contaminants can enter your well. Check the vent screen for holes or debris that could allow insects or rodents inside. Look for any signs of tampering or damage from equipment or vehicles.

Pressure Tank: Inspect your pressure tank for rust spots, moisture, or bulging. Feel around the base and fittings for any dampness that indicates leaks. Listen for unusual sounds when the pump cycles—sloshing or rumbling often indicates a waterlogged tank. Note the tank's age if you know it; most bladder tanks last 7-12 years.

Electrical Components: Check the control box or pump starter for burn marks, corrosion, or signs of overheating. Look for any damaged wiring, loose conduit, or evidence of rodent activity. The pressure switch should operate quietly without chattering or buzzing.

Performance Testing

Beyond visual inspection, test your system's actual performance. These simple tests help establish baselines so you can detect gradual changes over time.

Pressure Check: Note your pressure switch settings (typically 30/50 or 40/60 psi). Turn on a faucet and watch the pressure gauge drop until the pump kicks on (cut-in pressure). Let it run until it shuts off (cut-out pressure). Both numbers should match your switch settings within 2 psi. If they don't, the switch may need adjustment or replacement.

Recovery Time: With the pump off and pressure tank at cut-in pressure, time how long it takes to reach cut-out. Compare this to previous years—significant changes indicate either pump wear or changing well conditions. A healthy system should have consistent recovery times year over year.

Tank Air Charge: With the pump off and tank drained (open a faucet until the pump would normally kick on, then turn off the breaker), check the air pressure at the tank's air valve with a tire gauge. It should be 2 psi below your cut-in pressure. Low air charge causes rapid cycling and premature pump wear.

Water Quality Check

Water testing is perhaps the most important part of annual maintenance, yet it's often overlooked. Your well water can look, smell, and taste perfectly fine while harboring invisible contaminants.

Minimum Annual Tests: At minimum, test for coliform bacteria and nitrates every year. These are the most common well water contaminants and pose immediate health risks. Bacteria testing checks for the presence of potentially harmful microorganisms, while nitrate testing is especially important if you have infants, pregnant women, or livestock using the water.

Additional Testing: Consider testing for pH, hardness, iron, and manganese every 2-3 years—these affect water quality and can damage plumbing and appliances. If you're near agricultural areas, test for pesticides. Near industrial sites? Test for volatile organic compounds. Homes built before 1990 should test for lead.

When to Test More Often: Test immediately if you notice any change in taste, odor, or appearance. Also test after any work on the well, after flooding or earthquakes, or if neighbors report water problems. A sudden change often indicates a contamination event that needs immediate attention.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I have my well professionally inspected?

We recommend a professional well inspection annually, or at minimum every 2-3 years. Professional inspections include pump amp draw testing, flow rate measurements, and checks that require specialized equipment. If you notice any changes in water quality, pressure, or system behavior, schedule an inspection immediately regardless of when your last one was.

What does a professional well inspection include that I can't do myself?

Professional inspections include amp draw testing on the pump motor (requires a clamp meter and knowledge of motor specifications), flow rate testing with calibrated equipment, water level measurements with electronic sensors, video inspection of the well casing if needed, and comprehensive water quality testing sent to certified labs. We also evaluate overall system efficiency and can spot subtle signs of wear that untrained eyes miss.

Should I test my well water every year even if it looks and tastes fine?

Yes, absolutely. Many dangerous contaminants like bacteria, nitrates, and arsenic have no taste, odor, or color. The EPA and California Department of Public Health recommend annual testing for coliform bacteria and nitrates at minimum. If you're near agricultural land, septic systems, or have had previous contamination, more frequent testing is wise. Testing costs $50-150 and provides peace of mind that your family is drinking safe water.
