What Does Video Inspection Reveal?

A downhole camera inspection is the only way to actually see inside your well. Here's what we look for:

Casing Condition

  • Cracks or holes: Can allow contamination to enter
  • Corrosion: Steel casing deterioration over time
  • Joint separations: Where casing sections have pulled apart
  • Offset or misalignment: Can affect pump operation
  • Mineral deposits: Scale buildup reducing effective diameter

Well Screen Condition

  • Plugged slots: Reduced water entry
  • Physical damage: Crushed or collapsed screen
  • Encrustation: Mineral or biological buildup
  • Sand intrusion: Evidence of failed screen

Water Entry Points

  • Fracture locations: Where water enters from rock
  • Flow patterns: Active vs. inactive zones
  • Water clarity: Turbidity at different depths

Other Findings

  • Obstructions: Debris, dropped objects, collapsed material
  • Actual depth: Verify true well depth vs. records
  • Pump condition: If visible, check for problems
  • Sediment level: Buildup at well bottom

When Do You Need a Video Inspection?

Video inspection provides valuable information in many situations:

Real Estate Transactions

Buying a property with a well? A video inspection reveals problems that surface-level inspections miss. This is especially important for:

  • Older wells (20+ years) with unknown history
  • Properties that have been vacant
  • Wells with no documentation or records
  • When negotiating repair credits

Troubleshooting Problems

When your well isn't performing right, video inspection can identify the cause:

  • Reduced flow: See if casing or screen is blocked
  • Sand in water: Identify screen failure location
  • Water quality changes: Check for casing breaches
  • Pump problems: Visually inspect pump condition

Before Major Work

Video inspection should precede expensive well work:

  • Before deepening: Verify casing can support the work
  • Before hydrofracturing: Identify best treatment zones
  • Before rehabilitation: Diagnose what needs fixing
  • Before pump replacement: Check for obstructions

Routine Maintenance

Even if nothing seems wrong, periodic video inspection catches problems early:

  • Every 5-10 years for wells over 20 years old
  • After significant changes in water level or quality
  • When planning long-term property improvements

💡 Pro Tip: Combine Services

If you're already having pump work done (replacement, pulling for repair), add a video inspection at the same time. With the pump already out, inspection cost is minimal and provides valuable documentation of your well's condition.

The Video Inspection Process

Here's what to expect during a well camera inspection:

Step 1: Setup (15-30 minutes)

  • Technician arrives with camera equipment
  • Wellhead is opened and prepared
  • Camera system is tested and calibrated
  • Recording equipment is set up

Step 2: Camera Descent (30-60 minutes)

  • Camera is slowly lowered into well
  • Real-time viewing on monitor
  • Depth is tracked and recorded
  • Notable features are marked

Step 3: Inspection (15-30 minutes)

  • Camera reaches bottom of well
  • Slow withdrawal with detailed viewing
  • Problem areas are examined closely
  • Multiple passes if needed

Step 4: Completion (15-30 minutes)

  • Camera is removed
  • Well is secured
  • Video is transferred to your media
  • Findings are discussed on-site

Total time: 1.5-3 hours depending on well depth and complexity.

Do You Need Pump Removal?

Whether the pump needs to come out depends on several factors:

Camera Alongside Pump

In many wells, especially those with 6" or larger casing, a slim camera can be lowered alongside the pump and drop pipe. This is the least expensive option but has limitations:

  • Pros: Lower cost, faster, no pump disturbance
  • Cons: Can't see full casing, may not reach bottom
  • Best for: Initial evaluation, known issues above pump

With Pump Removal

For comprehensive inspection, removing the pump first provides complete access:

  • Pros: Full well visibility, can inspect pump, accurate depth
  • Cons: Additional cost, more time, pump handling risk
  • Best for: Pre-purchase inspection, troubleshooting, before major work

Factors That Affect Video Inspection Cost

1. Well Depth

Deeper wells require more time and longer cable. Wells over 400-500 feet may require specialized equipment with extended reach.

2. Well Diameter

Narrow wells (4" casing) may require slim-line cameras and always need pump removal. Standard 6" wells offer more flexibility.

3. Pump Removal Needs

If pump removal is required, the cost depends on well depth (more pipe to pull) and pump condition (potential for complications).

4. Location and Access

Remote locations may have travel charges. Difficult access to the wellhead can add time and complexity.

5. Condition of Well

Very dirty water, heavy sediment, or obstructions may require extra time or multiple inspection attempts.

What Problems Can Video Inspection Find?

Real examples of issues we've discovered through video inspection:

🔴 Casing Failure

Corroded steel casing with holes allowing surface water to enter—explaining bacterial contamination.

🔴 Dropped Objects

Tools, pipe sections, or debris from previous work blocking the well or damaging the pump.

🔴 Screen Collapse

Crushed well screen explaining sand in water and reduced flow rate.

🔴 Encrustation

Heavy mineral or bacterial buildup reducing effective well diameter by 50%+.

🟢 Good Fractures

Strong water-producing fractures identified for hydrofracturing treatment.

🟢 Casing in Good Condition

Confirming the well is suitable for deepening—saving thousands vs. drilling new.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a well camera inspection cost?

A well video inspection costs $200-$500 in San Diego County, depending on well depth. Shallow wells (under 200 ft) run $200-$300, while deep wells (400+ ft) cost $350-$500. Prices may be higher if pump removal is required.

What can a well camera inspection reveal?

Video inspection shows casing condition (cracks, holes, corrosion), obstructions, pump location and condition, water entry points, screen condition, sediment buildup, and actual well depth. It's the only way to see what's happening inside your well.

Does the pump need to be removed for video inspection?

It depends on your well setup. Some wells have enough space to lower the camera alongside the pump. For most accurate results, we recommend pump removal, which adds $300-$800 to the total cost but provides a complete view.

When should I get a well camera inspection?

Get a video inspection before buying property with a well, when experiencing reduced water flow, if you notice sand or sediment, before deciding on well deepening or rehabilitation, or if your well is over 20 years old and has never been inspected.

Do I get to keep the video?

Yes, we provide a copy of the video recording on USB drive or via digital download. This documentation is valuable for your records and for any future work on the well.
