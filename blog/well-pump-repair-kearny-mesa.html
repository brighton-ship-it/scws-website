Common Pump Problems We Fix

Most Kearny Mesa pump calls fall into a handful of patterns, and each points to a different fix. Learning to tell them apart is what keeps a $250 repair from being misdiagnosed as a $4,000 one.

  • No water from the well — Pump failure, an electrical fault, or a fracture-fed well drawn below the pump intake
  • Pump runs constantly — A failing pressure switch or a waterlogged pressure tank
  • Low water pressure — Worn impellers that spin but no longer move volume, or a tank losing its air charge
  • Pump cycles on and off — Almost always a waterlogged tank or a small system leak, not the pump itself
  • Strange noises — Worn bearings, cavitation from a low water level, or loose components
  • High electric bills — An inefficient or constantly running pump straining the motor

Motor Burnout, Worn Impellers, and Waterlogged Tanks

When a submersible motor overheats and the windings fail, the pump has to come up and be replaced — we confirm this with a resistance and insulation test at the wellhead before pulling. Worn impellers are subtler: sediment slowly erodes the impeller stack until pressure fades over months even though the motor is fine. A waterlogged pressure tank, where the bladder has ruptured, makes the pump short-cycle rapidly and can destroy the motor over time. We separate these three by watching the pressure gauge, testing the switch, and checking the tank's air pre-charge.

The Pull-and-Inspect Process and Well Recovery

When testing points downhole, we bring in a pump hoist and pull the pump, drop pipe, and wire. Kearny Mesa and the surrounding San Diego County wells vary widely in depth, from shallow completions to deeper fracture-seeking wells, so we bring equipment matched to the job. With everything laid out, the diagnosis becomes visual: we inspect the impellers, check the motor for heat damage and bearing play, test the drop wire foot by foot, and examine the check valve. A pump that dropped from a corroded joint or bad splice can sometimes be reset without a new motor. A full submersible replacement typically runs $2,500 to $5,500 depending on depth and horsepower, and because the pull is the labor cost, we replace worn wire and a tired check valve while the well is open. On fracture-fed hard-rock wells, correct pump sizing matters — an oversized pump just draws the level down to the intake and burns up, so we match the pump to the well's real recovery rate and add low-water protection where needed.

What You Can Safely Check First — and When to Call a Pro

Before you call, reset the well breaker once, read the pressure gauge (zero with the pump running points downhole; normal pressure with dry taps points to plumbing), and tap the pressure tank to hear whether it is waterlogged. Beyond that, stop — the wellhead is high-current and wet, and lifting a pump without the right equipment can drop hardware down the casing. Anything involving opening the wellhead or pulling the pump should be handled by a licensed contractor to avoid damaging the well casing.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if my Kearny Mesa well pump needs repair?

Common signs are no water or low pressure, a pump that runs constantly, rapid on-off cycling, a breaker that trips on start-up, air at the faucets, sandy water, or a rising electric bill. Any of these is worth a professional diagnosis before it becomes a full failure.

How much does well pump repair cost in Kearny Mesa?

Minor repairs like a pressure switch run $150 to $350, a control box or capacitor $400 to $900, and a full submersible replacement $2,500 to $5,500 depending on depth. Our diagnostic is a flat $125, credited toward the repair, and estimates are free.

Why does my pump run but produce little water?

Usually worn impellers, a water level drawn below the intake, a failed check valve, or a broken drop pipe. We verify with electrical and pressure testing before pulling the pump so you don't pay to lift pipe unnecessarily.

Can I repair my well pump myself?

Surface tasks like resetting the breaker or checking the pressure switch are safe. Anything that involves pulling the pump from the well requires specialized equipment and should be handled by a licensed contractor to avoid damaging the well casing.
