What Causes Rusty Well Water?

  • Dissolved Iron: Naturally occurring iron in San Diego County groundwater
  • Iron Bacteria: Bacteria that feed on iron, creating rusty slime
  • Corroding Casing: Old steel well casing breaking down
  • Corroding Pipes: Galvanized or iron plumbing in your home
  • Failing Water Heater: Rusting anode rod or tank

Is Rusty Water Safe to Drink?

Iron itself isn't a health hazard at typical well water concentrations. However, rusty water:

  • Tastes metallic and unpleasant
  • Stains sinks, toilets, and laundry
  • Can clog pipes and fixtures over time
  • May indicate other water quality issues

We recommend water testing to identify the specific cause and concentration of iron in your Oak Park well.

Plumbing Updates

Why Your Water Turns Rusty

Iron sits in the rock and soil beneath nearly all of San Diego County. As water filters down into the aquifer, it dissolves iron and manganese in a form that stays invisible until it reaches the open air at your tap. There, oxygen converts the dissolved iron into solid rust particles, which is why a glass of clear-looking well water can turn cloudy and orange after sitting on the counter. The most common forms we encounter in the Oak Park area are:

  • Ferrous iron: Clear at the tap, orange after exposure to air, this is groundwater iron.
  • Ferric iron: Already rust-colored when it leaves the faucet, indicating oxidation inside the well or pipes.
  • Manganese: Brown-black staining that frequently rides alongside iron in local groundwater.
  • Iron bacteria: A reddish, slimy film in toilet tanks and on screens, produced by organisms that consume iron.
  • Corroded metal: Rust shed by an old steel casing, a galvanized drop pipe, or aging household plumbing.

Causes That Show Up in Southeast San Diego

Many properties in and around Oak Park were built mid-century, which means the plumbing and any older wells have had decades to corrode. We routinely trace rusty hot water to a failing water heater anode rod, and whole-house rust to a galvanized service line that has rusted from the inside. On hillside parcels, a dropping water table during dry San Diego winters can pull the pump intake into the iron-rich silt near the bottom of the well, turning previously clean water cloudy almost overnight.

Should You Worry About Drinking It?

The EPA lists iron and manganese as secondary contaminants, meaning they are judged on taste, odor, and appearance rather than acute toxicity. Iron at typical well concentrations will not make a healthy adult sick, but it ruins the taste of coffee, permanently stains laundry and porcelain, and feeds the iron bacteria that gum up plumbing. Manganese deserves a closer look, since elevated levels carry their own health questions. A simple test settles the matter and tells us how to size any treatment.

Quick Checks You Can Do First

  • Hot, cold, or both? Rust in hot water only usually means the water heater, not the well.
  • Whole house or one tap? A single rusty fixture points to a localized pipe.
  • Slime present? Reddish slime in the toilet tank signals iron bacteria.
  • Timing: Rust only at first draw suggests sediment in the pressure tank.
  • Recent events: New staining after a dry spell or recent pump work helps narrow the cause.

How We Fix It

Oxidizing Iron Filtration

For dissolved iron and manganese, a backwashing oxidizing filter is the most reliable solution. We size it to your measured iron load so it keeps up without wasting water on excessive backwashing.

Shock Chlorination

If iron bacteria are producing slime, we disinfect the entire well and plumbing system to wipe out the colony, then set a prevention schedule.

Casing, Pipe, and Heater Repairs

Where corrosion is the source, we line or replace failing steel casing, swap galvanized household pipe for PEX or copper, and service or replace rust-shedding water heaters.

Sediment Pre-Filtration

An inexpensive sediment filter ahead of the main system catches particulate rust and protects your investment.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does my Oak Park well water look clear but turn orange in the bathtub?

That is dissolved ferrous iron, which is invisible underground and only forms orange rust once it meets air. An oxidizing iron filter removes it before it reaches your fixtures.

Is the iron in my water safe?

Iron is an aesthetic contaminant and not an acute health hazard at normal well concentrations, but it stains and tastes metallic. We test for manganese at the same time, since that one is worth watching more closely.

Why is my hot water rusty but my cold water fine?

The water heater is the likely culprit. A corroding anode rod or rusting tank discolors hot water only; servicing or replacing the heater usually clears it up.

What causes the slimy red film in my toilet tank?

Iron bacteria. These organisms feed on iron and form a slimy buildup that clogs plumbing. Shock chlorination eliminates the colony.

Do you charge for a water test?

Our diagnostic visit is $125, and we credit it toward any system or repair we install. It measures iron and manganese so treatment is sized correctly.

How long does an iron filter last before it needs service?

With proper backwashing, the media in a quality iron filter typically lasts several years before replacement. We set you up with a simple maintenance schedule for your Oak Park well.
