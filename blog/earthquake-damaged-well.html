Immediate Post-Earthquake Well Safety

Before approaching your wellhead, check for gas leaks, downed power lines, and structural hazards. If you smell gas or see sparking near the well, stay away and call 911. Once safe, visually inspect the wellhead for visible cracks, shifting, or damage. Don't drink the water until you've completed basic checks and preferably tested.

Signs of Earthquake Well Damage

Watch for these indicators: sudden decrease in water flow or pressure, water turning muddy, sandy, or discolored, unusual sounds from the pump, air spurting from faucets, visible cracks or shifting at the wellhead, or new sediment in toilet tanks. Any of these warrant professional inspection.

Common Types of Earthquake Damage

Casing damage: Cracks in the steel or PVC casing allow contaminants to enter. Drop pipe damage: The pipe connecting pump to surface can crack or disconnect. Pump displacement: The pump can shift or fall. Aquifer changes: Ground shifting can alter water flow or introduce sand. Electrical damage: Conduit and wiring can be severed. Severe damage may require well rehabilitation or replacement.

When to Call a Professional

Call immediately if: water flow stopped or significantly decreased, persistent sediment or discoloration, visible damage to wellhead or equipment, electrical problems with the pump, or if the earthquake was magnitude 5.0+ within 20 miles. Well cameras can inspect casing integrity, and professionals can assess whether damage is repairable or if deeper intervention is needed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can an earthquake damage my well even if my house is fine?

Yes. Wells extend deep underground where seismic forces are different than at the surface. Earthquakes can crack well casings, shift aquifers, cause sand infiltration, damage drop pipes, and dislodge pumps—all while your home shows no damage.

Should I test my well water after an earthquake?

Yes, always test after any significant earthquake. Ground shifting can introduce surface contaminants, disturb sediment, or crack casings that allow contamination. Get a basic bacteria test at minimum, or a full panel if you notice any changes in water quality.

My well water turned muddy after the earthquake—is it ruined?

Not necessarily. Earthquakes often disturb sediment in aquifers, causing temporary turbidity. Run the well for several hours (water plants, not drain) and see if it clears. If turbidity persists beyond 24-48 hours, or if sand appears, you need professional evaluation for casing damage.
