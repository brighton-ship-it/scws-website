Well Water Treatment in Lenwood

Lenwood is a Mojave Desert community on the western edge of Barstow in San Bernardino County, strung along old Route 66 and the I-15 corridor on the high desert floor. With its mix of homes, small ranches, and roadside businesses, Lenwood depends on groundwater from the Mojave River basin, and many properties run on private wells. Desert groundwater here tends to be mineral-heavy, so understanding your water and treating it correctly is an essential part of life on a Lenwood well.

Mojave Desert Groundwater Around Lenwood

Lenwood draws from aquifers associated with the Mojave River basin, where water sitting in desert alluvium becomes very hard and high in total dissolved solids. As in much of the high desert, naturally occurring arsenic and fluoride can be present, and alkalinity often runs high, giving water a flat or mineral taste. The Barstow area also has a documented history of groundwater contamination concerns, which is one more reason a full lab panel is the smart starting point for any Lenwood well rather than relying on taste or appearance.

Common Well Water Problems in Lenwood

For Lenwood and Barstow-area well owners, the problems we address most often include:

  • Very hard water that scales pipes, heaters, and coolers quickly.
  • High total dissolved solids and alkalinity affecting taste.
  • Naturally occurring arsenic, invisible and revealed only by testing.
  • Elevated fluoride typical of high-desert aquifers.
  • Sediment from desert sands and older casings.

Sizing and Maintaining Mojave Treatment Equipment

Lenwood's very hard, high-TDS desert water demands equipment sized to the load. We match softener capacity to your hardness and flow so it regenerates efficiently rather than constantly, and we add the right pre-filtration to keep desert sand out of the system. For arsenic, fluoride, or high dissolved solids, a properly sized reverse osmosis unit delivers safe drinking water without overtaxing a small cartridge. Desert systems need regular care: filters load quickly in sandy, high-mineral water, brine tanks must be checked, and RO membranes wear faster under high mineral loads. We build a simple maintenance plan so your Lenwood equipment keeps performing through the Mojave heat year after year.

Testing Your Lenwood Well Before You Buy Anything

No two wells are alike, and the only way to choose the right equipment is to know exactly what is in your water. A complete test measures hardness, iron, manganese, pH, hydrogen sulfide, total dissolved solids, nitrates, and bacteria. We never recommend a system before we see those numbers. Our diagnostic visit costs $125, and that fee is credited toward any treatment system you install with us, so testing first saves you money by making sure you only pay for the treatment your Lenwood water actually needs.

Treatment Options Matched to Your Water

Once the lab results are in, we match equipment to the specific problems in your sample rather than selling a generic package:

  • Water softeners for hard water. An ion-exchange softener protects plumbing, water heaters, and appliances and ends scale and soap-scum problems. Installed softeners typically run $1,500 to $3,500.
  • Iron, manganese, and specialty filtration. Oxidizing or catalytic media filters remove staining metals and sulfur odor. These systems generally run $1,500 to $3,500.
  • Sediment filtration to capture sand and silt before they reach the rest of the system, usually $300 to $900.
  • UV disinfection for bacteria. An ultraviolet system inactivates pathogens without chemicals, typically $800 to $1,800.
  • Reverse osmosis for drinking water. A point-of-use RO unit at the kitchen sink removes nitrates, dissolved solids, and many contaminants, usually $300 to $1,200.

What to Check on Your Own System

Between professional visits, watch for new staining on fixtures or laundry, note any change in taste or smell, look for cloudiness after heavy rain, and make sure your pressure tank holds steady pressure rather than rapidly cycling the pump. If a softener stops producing soft water or a filter cartridge is overdue, schedule service. Keep a simple log of each annual water test so you can spot trends before they turn into expensive repairs.

When to Call a Professional

Call right away if you notice a sudden change in water clarity, a new odor, falling pressure, or any positive bacteria result, since these can signal a cracked casing, a failing pump, or surface intrusion. Treatment equipment also has to be sized, plumbed, and wired correctly to perform safely and to protect its warranty, which is why DIY installs so often disappoint. As a licensed C-57 well-drilling contractor, we handle everything from the well head to the kitchen faucet so the whole system works together.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is Lenwood well water so hard?

Mojave desert alluvium makes groundwater very hard. A test gives exact grains per gallon, and a softener ($1,500 to $3,500 installed) stops the fast scaling that hard desert water causes on heaters and coolers.

Should I test my Lenwood well for arsenic?

Yes. Naturally occurring arsenic is common in the high desert and is invisible and tasteless. Only a lab test reveals it, and reverse osmosis or specialty media removes it effectively.

What improves the flat, mineral taste of my water?

High dissolved solids and alkalinity cause that taste. A reverse osmosis unit removes dissolved solids and many contaminants for crisp drinking water.

Can fluoride be removed from desert well water?

Yes. Elevated fluoride is removed by reverse osmosis or specialty media. A water test confirms whether your Lenwood well needs it.

How often should a Barstow-area well be tested?

Annually for bacteria and nitrates, plus a full chemical panel including arsenic every couple of years given the area's history. A test runs about $150 to $400.

Yes. Lenwood and the Barstow area are part of our service area, and we provide same-day emergency response when you lose water.
