Well Services in Point Loma

Point Loma is one of San Diego's most iconic coastal neighborhoods — a peninsula community with stunning views, historic properties, and proximity to the Naval Base. Like most urban San Diego, Point Loma is served primarily by City of San Diego municipal water.

However, Southern California Well Service provides well services in Point Loma for commercial properties, institutional facilities, and maintenance of existing wells on older properties. Our focus in urban coastal areas is typically on existing well maintenance and specialized commercial applications.

Urban Coastal Context

Point Loma's coastal peninsula location creates unique considerations:

  • Dense development — Limited space for new well sites
  • Saltwater intrusion risk — Coastal aquifer concerns
  • Historic properties — Some older estates have existing wells
  • Commercial opportunities — Specific applications may be viable

Existing Well Services

Our primary services in Point Loma focus on maintaining, repairing, and managing existing wells.

Well Maintenance

  • Annual inspections — Pump, electrical, water quality checks
  • Water testing — Quality monitoring (especially important near coast)
  • Pump service — Maintenance to extend lifespan
  • Pressure system checks — Tank and controls

Well Repairs

  • Pump replacement — When pumps fail or lose efficiency
  • Pressure tank replacement — Failed or waterlogged tanks
  • Electrical repairs — Control boxes, wiring, breakers
  • Piping repairs — Drop pipe, discharge line issues

Well Rehabilitation

Declining wells can often be restored:

  • Chemical treatment — Dissolve mineral buildup
  • Mechanical cleaning — Remove sediment, debris
  • Redevelopment — Surge and pump to restore flow
  • Video inspection — Assess casing condition

Well Abandonment

Unused wells must be properly destroyed per San Diego County regulations:

  • Prevents groundwater contamination
  • Required before property sale in many cases
  • Eliminates safety hazards
  • Proper permitting and documentation

Coastal Drilling Considerations

Point Loma's peninsula location creates unique hydrogeological challenges:

Saltwater Intrusion

Coastal aquifers can be affected by seawater:

  • Pumping too heavily can draw saltwater into fresh aquifer
  • Closer to coast = higher risk
  • Some areas have restricted pumping
  • Water quality monitoring essential

Point Loma Formation

The peninsula consists of:

  • Point Loma Formation — Cretaceous sandstone and shale
  • Limited freshwater storage capacity
  • Variable permeability
  • Some areas have perched water tables

Regulatory Oversight

  • San Diego County DEH permitting
  • City of San Diego coordination
  • Possible California Coastal Commission involvement
  • Military base proximity considerations

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I drill a residential well in Point Loma?

New residential wells in Point Loma are uncommon and challenging. Most properties use City of San Diego water. Space constraints, saltwater intrusion risk, and permitting make residential well drilling impractical in most cases.

Do you service old wells on Point Loma properties?

Yes, we provide maintenance, repair, rehabilitation, and abandonment services for existing wells on Point Loma properties. Many older estates have wells that need professional attention.

Is the water quality good near the coast?

Coastal water quality can be affected by saltwater intrusion. Wells farther from the shore generally have better quality, but testing is essential for any coastal well.

What about commercial wells in the Midway District?

The Midway District, being slightly inland, may have better opportunities for commercial wells. We can evaluate specific properties for feasibility.

How much does well service cost?

Service calls typically run $150-350 for diagnosis. Pump replacements range from $2,500-8,000 depending on depth and system type. Well abandonment costs $2,000-5,000.
