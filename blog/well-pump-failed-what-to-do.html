Confirming Pump Failure vs Other Issues

Before assuming pump failure, eliminate other possibilities: Check the pressure switch by tapping it gently (stuck contacts are common). Test the control box capacitors if you have a submersible pump. Verify voltage at the well with a multimeter. About 30% of suspected pump failures are actually electrical component issues that cost $100-300 to fix versus $2,000+ for pump replacement.

Emergency Steps When Pump Fails

Turn off the breaker to the well immediately—a failing pump left running causes additional damage. Document any sounds, smells, or behaviors you noticed. Check your pressure tank gauge and note the reading. Don't attempt to restart the pump repeatedly; each attempt can worsen motor damage. Call a well service company and describe exactly what happened.

What Happens During Emergency Pump Replacement

For submersible pumps, the well technician will pull the pump using specialized equipment—this can take 1-4 hours depending on well depth (some wells are 400+ feet deep). They'll inspect the pump to confirm failure, check the well condition, and install the new pump. Most emergency replacements are completed the same day if parts are available.

Preventing Future Pump Failures

Install a pump saver or cycle sensor to protect against dry running. Consider a backup generator for power outages. Have your well system inspected annually. Address low-yield issues before they cause pump cycling damage. Most pumps last 10-15 years with proper care—sudden failures often indicate an underlying issue that should be identified.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if my well pump has actually failed?

Signs of pump failure: no water despite power, pump running but not building pressure, breaker tripping immediately when pump tries to start, or burning smell from the well cap area. If the pump hums but doesn't run, the motor may be seized.

How much does emergency well pump replacement cost?

Emergency pump replacement in Southern California typically costs $1,500-$4,500 depending on well depth, pump type, and accessibility. Submersible pumps in deep wells cost more due to extraction requirements. Same-day service may add $200-400 to standard rates.

Can a failed well pump be repaired or must it be replaced?

Most failed pumps need replacement—internal motor failures, burned windings, and seized bearings aren't economically repairable. However, what seems like pump failure is sometimes a failed capacitor, pressure switch, or control box, which are much cheaper to replace.
