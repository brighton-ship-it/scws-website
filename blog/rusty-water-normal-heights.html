What Causes Rusty Well Water?

  • Dissolved Iron: Naturally occurring iron in San Diego County groundwater
  • Iron Bacteria: Bacteria that feed on iron, creating rusty slime
  • Corroding Casing: Old steel well casing breaking down
  • Corroding Pipes: Galvanized or iron plumbing in your home
  • Failing Water Heater: Rusting anode rod or tank

Is Rusty Water Safe to Drink?

Iron itself isn't a health hazard at typical well water concentrations. However, rusty water:

  • Tastes metallic and unpleasant
  • Stains sinks, toilets, and laundry
  • Can clog pipes and fixtures over time
  • May indicate other water quality issues

We recommend water testing to identify the specific cause and concentration of iron in your Normal Heights well.

How Iron Turns Normal Heights Water Rusty

Rust is iron that has met oxygen and changed color. San Diego County's granite aquifers naturally carry iron and manganese, and in a historic neighborhood the home's own pipes add a second potential source. Here are the four main pathways.

Dissolved Iron and Manganese

Underground, where oxygen is limited, iron dissolves invisibly as ferrous iron. As soon as the water hits air in your tank or glass, it oxidizes into rust-colored ferric particles. Manganese does the same but stains black or purple-brown. Wells around central San Diego drilled into decomposed granite often carry enough of both to leave steady staining.

Iron Bacteria

Iron bacteria feed on iron and produce a rusty slime and a marshy odor. They are not the kind of bacteria that make you sick the way coliform does, but they clog screens and pipe interiors and frequently cause water to turn orange intermittently or grow gel-like deposits in toilet tanks.

Corroding Steel Casing

Older wells around the canyon rims were often built with steel casing that corrodes over decades and sheds rust flakes into the water. Rust that appears mainly when the pump first starts points toward an aging casing.

Vintage Galvanized Plumbing and the Water Heater

This is the big one in Normal Heights. Many historic bungalows still have their original galvanized steel supply lines, which rust from the inside out after 50 to 80 years and turn water brown, especially after the house has sat unused for a few hours. A water heater with a spent anode rod or rusting tank discolors only the hot water. Because so much of Normal Heights' housing stock is old, household plumbing is often the true culprit, and a simple test confirms it.

What to Check Before You Call

  • Hot vs. cold: Rusty hot water with clear cold water points to the water heater. Both rusty points to the well, the main line, or whole-house galvanized pipes.
  • First-draw test: If the first water each morning is brown and then clears, suspect corroding household pipes.
  • The jar test: Cloudy-orange immediately is oxidized iron; clear-then-orange as it sits is dissolved iron from the aquifer.
  • Odor and feel: A swampy smell or slime points to iron bacteria.
  • Stain color: Reddish-brown is iron; black specks are manganese.

Recording these observations helps us pinpoint the source faster.

The Hidden Cost of Old Pipes Plus Iron-Rich Water

In a neighborhood as historic as Normal Heights, rusty water often comes from two sources working together: iron in the groundwater and aging galvanized plumbing that the iron has corroded from within. When both are present, treating only one leaves the other to keep staining your fixtures. Iron deposits also build up inside water heaters and appliances, shortening their life and raising energy costs as scale insulates heating elements. The most durable fix usually combines source-water treatment with addressing any failing pipe sections, and we evaluate the whole system so you are not paying to solve half the problem. Catching it early protects your historic home's plumbing and your budget alike.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is rusty well water in Normal Heights safe to drink?

Iron and manganese at common concentrations are aesthetic concerns under the EPA's secondary standards, not acute health hazards, but rusty water can carry iron bacteria and mask other issues. We always recommend a full test before assuming it is safe.

My old bungalow's water is brown only in the morning. Why?

That classic pattern usually means corroding galvanized pipes. Rust settles in the lines overnight and flushes out with the first heavy draw. It often points to repiping rather than a well problem.

Why is only my hot water rusty?

Clear cold and rusty hot water almost always means the water heater, typically a corroded anode rod or rusting tank.

Will a water softener remove rust?

Softeners handle only light dissolved iron and foul quickly with iron bacteria. Most wells with real iron problems need a dedicated oxidizing filter.

How do I know if it is my well or my pipes?

The hot/cold and first-draw tests are the quickest clues, and our diagnostic visit confirms it. We test the water at the source and at the tap to isolate where the rust enters.
