Well Drilling Cost Per Foot: Complete 2026 Pricing Guide

How much does well drilling really cost per foot? It's the first question every property owner asks—and the answer determines whether your well project costs $20,000 or $60,000. Well drilling in California typically runs $35-$65 per foot, but that's just the beginning of the story. Understanding what's included (and what's NOT) in per-foot pricing helps you budget accurately and evaluate contractor quotes intelligently.

💰 Quick Cost Summary

  • • Drilling & casing: $35 - $65 per foot
  • • San Diego County average: $45 - $55 per foot
  • • Typical well depth: 200 - 600 feet
  • • Drilling portion of total: 60-75% of project cost
  • • Total project cost: $15,000 - $50,000+

What's Included in Cost Per Foot?

The "cost per foot" you see in quotes isn't as simple as it sounds. Different contractors include different items, so you need to know exactly what you're paying for.

Typically INCLUDED in Per-Foot Pricing

  • Drilling the borehole: Creating the actual hole in the ground
  • Casing installation: Steel or PVC pipe that lines the well
  • Grout/cement seal: Sealing the annular space between casing and borehole
  • Basic well development: Cleaning and flushing to remove drilling debris
  • Screen installation: Perforated section that allows water entry

Typically NOT INCLUDED in Per-Foot Pricing

These critical costs are separate from the per-foot drilling price:

  • Pump and motor: $1,500 - $5,000+ depending on depth and capacity
  • Pressure tank: $400 - $1,200
  • Electrical work: $1,000 - $3,000
  • Control box and pressure switch: $300 - $800
  • Permits and fees: $1,500 - $3,000
  • Site preparation: $500 - $2,000
  • Hydrogeological assessment: $1,000 - $3,000 (if needed)
  • Water quality testing: $300 - $1,000
  • Water treatment (if needed): $2,000 - $15,000+

⚠️ Critical Pricing Alert

Never compare quotes based solely on per-foot pricing. A contractor quoting "$40/foot" may charge separately for items another includes at "$55/foot." Always request itemized quotes showing EXACTLY what's included to make accurate comparisons.

Breaking Down the Per-Foot Cost

Understanding what drives per-foot pricing helps you evaluate quotes and understand variations between contractors:

Drilling Labor and Equipment ($15-$30 per foot)

This is the actual cost to drill the borehole:

  • Rig operation: $5,000-$15,000 per day including crew
  • Drilling rate: 50-150 feet per day typical (varies with geology)
  • Fuel and consumables: $500-$1,500 per day
  • Mobilization: $2,000-$5,000 (amortized across depth)

Harder geology means slower drilling and higher costs per foot. Granite bedrock might only allow 30-50 feet per day, while soft sedimentary formations could yield 150+ feet daily.

Casing Materials ($8-$20 per foot)

Casing is the permanent pipe that lines your well:

Casing Type Cost Per Foot Best For
6" PVC Schedule 40 $8 - $12 Shallow wells, non-corrosive water
6" Steel (standard) $12 - $18 Most residential wells
8" Steel $18 - $28 High-capacity or commercial wells
Stainless Steel $25 - $40 Corrosive water conditions

Grout and Sealing ($5-$10 per foot)

California requires grouting the annular space between the borehole and casing to prevent contamination:

  • Cement/bentonite mixture
  • Pumped from surface to required depth
  • Creates sanitary seal
  • Required by county health departments

Development and Testing ($5-$8 per foot)

After drilling, the well must be developed:

  • High-pressure air/water cleaning
  • Removal of drilling mud and debris
  • Pump testing to determine yield
  • Development ensures clean water and maximum flow

Factors That Increase Cost Per Foot

Several factors can push your per-foot cost above the averages:

1. Difficult Geology

Impact: +20-50% per foot

  • Hard rock (granite, basalt): Requires slower drilling and more expensive bits
  • Fractured rock: Casing complications and circulation loss
  • Boulders: May require special drilling techniques
  • Artesian conditions: High-pressure water requiring blowout prevention

2. Larger Diameter Wells

Impact: +30-60% per foot

Commercial wells with 8-12 inch diameter cost significantly more:

  • More material removed (exponentially more volume)
  • More expensive casing
  • Heavier-duty drilling equipment
  • Longer drilling time per foot

3. Difficult Access

Impact: +15-40% per foot

  • No road access: Smaller rigs or hand equipment needed
  • Steep slopes: Equipment stabilization and safety measures
  • Tight spaces: May require compact drilling rigs
  • Long hauls: Distance from pavement to drill site

4. Extreme Depth

Impact: +10-25% per foot (beyond 500 feet)

Wells deeper than 500 feet face additional challenges:

  • Heavier, more expensive casing required
  • Additional time for hoisting pipe
  • More complex grouting operations
  • Higher-capacity pumps and motors needed

5. Remote Location

Impact: +$5-15 per foot

  • Long mobilization distance
  • Equipment and supply hauling costs
  • Worker travel time
  • Lack of local competition

Why Per-Foot Pricing Varies Between Contractors

Getting three quotes for the same well might show $42, $50, and $58 per foot. Understanding why helps you make informed decisions:

What's Included in the Quote

Some contractors bundle more into their per-foot price:

  • Contractor A ($42/ft): Drilling and basic casing only
  • Contractor B ($50/ft): Drilling, casing, grouting, development
  • Contractor C ($58/ft): Complete turnkey including pump installation

Without itemization, you can't tell if Contractor A is actually cheaper—they may charge $8,000 extra for items Contractor C includes.

Equipment and Methods

  • Modern rotary rigs: Faster drilling, better accuracy, higher upfront cost
  • Older equipment: Slower but lower hourly rates
  • Specialized rigs: Premium pricing but better results in difficult conditions

Experience and Expertise

  • Established contractors: Higher rates but proven track record
  • Specialists: Premium for difficult geology or commercial work
  • Newer operations: Competitive pricing to build business

Risk Assessment

Contractors price in risk differently:

  • Unknown geology = higher per-foot quotes
  • Difficult access = premium pricing
  • Remote location = travel cost markup
  • Tight timeline = premium for priority scheduling

✅ Smart Quote Evaluation

  • • Get 3-4 quotes for comparison
  • • Request itemized breakdowns
  • • Verify licensing and insurance
  • • Check references and well logs
  • • Don't automatically choose lowest bid

How Well Depth is Determined

Since depth is the primary cost driver, understanding how it's determined helps you budget realistically:

Pre-Drilling Assessment Methods

  • County well log review: Check depths of nearby wells (most common method)
  • Geological surveys: Regional aquifer maps and studies
  • Hydrogeological assessment: Professional analysis of your specific site
  • Geophysical surveys: Ground-penetrating radar or seismic (expensive, rarely needed)
  • Neighbor interviews: Informal but useful data on local well performance

Most contractors review county well logs within 1-2 miles of your property to estimate required depth. San Diego County maintains excellent well log records you can access online.

Actual Drilling Depth

The final depth is determined during drilling:

  • Drill until adequate water-bearing formation is found
  • Test flow rate and water quality
  • May drill deeper if initial zones inadequate
  • Seal off non-productive or poor-quality zones
  • Set pump below static water level with adequate submergence

Ways to Reduce Your Per-Foot Cost

While you can't change geology, several strategies can reduce your total project cost:

1. Optimize Well Location

  • Choose location with easy rig access
  • Minimize distance from driveway to drill site
  • Avoid steep slopes or obstacles
  • Consider proximity to house (shorter piping runs)

2. Time Your Project

  • Schedule during off-peak season (winter in most areas)
  • Allow flexible timing for contractor scheduling efficiency
  • Avoid rush fees for urgent completion

3. Bundle Multiple Wells

  • If developing multiple properties, drill together
  • Mobilization costs spread across multiple wells
  • Volume discounts on materials

4. Do Your Homework

  • Research local well depths before getting quotes
  • Understand typical geology in your area
  • Complete permit applications yourself (if capable)
  • Prepare site access in advance

Questions to Ask About Per-Foot Pricing

Before signing a drilling contract, get clear answers:

  • "What exactly is included in your per-foot price?" Get itemized list
  • "What's NOT included that I'll need to pay for separately?" No surprises
  • "Do you charge more for drilling through hard rock?" Know if price can increase
  • "What happens if we don't hit water at the estimated depth?" Understand risk allocation
  • "Is there a maximum depth or cost cap?" Protect yourself from runaway costs
  • "What diameter casing is included?" Confirm adequate size
  • "Does your price include grouting and well development?" These are essential
  • "What warranties do you provide?" Know your protections

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to drill a well per foot?

Well drilling costs $35-$65 per foot on average in California, depending on geology, location, and well diameter. San Diego County residential wells typically cost $45-$55 per foot for drilling and casing combined. This includes the borehole, casing installation, and grouting but not the pump or pressure system.

What is included in the cost per foot?

Cost per foot typically includes drilling the borehole, steel or PVC casing, grout/cement seal, and basic well development. It does NOT include the pump system ($2,000-$5,000), pressure tank, electrical work, permits ($1,500-$3,000), or water testing. These add $8,000-$15,000 to the per-foot drilling cost.

How deep do you need to drill for water in California?

Well depth varies by location. San Diego County wells typically need 200-600 feet. Coastal areas may find water at 150-300 feet, while inland mountain areas often require 400-800+ feet. Riverside County averages 300-500 feet. A hydrogeologist reviews local well data to estimate your specific depth requirements.

Why do some wells cost more per foot than others?

Per-foot costs increase with: harder rock geology requiring slower drilling; larger diameter casing (8-12 inch vs standard 6 inch); difficult access requiring smaller rigs or hand equipment; deeper depths requiring heavier casing; and remote locations with long mobilization distances. Expect 20-40% higher costs in challenging conditions.

Can you estimate my well cost if I know the required depth?

Yes, use this formula: (Depth in feet × $45-55) + $8,000-15,000 for equipment and services. Example: 400-foot well = (400 × $50) + $12,000 = $32,000 average total cost. This is an estimate—exact pricing requires site assessment and contractor quotes.
