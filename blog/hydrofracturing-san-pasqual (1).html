What is Hydrofracturing?

Hydrofracturing is a well rehabilitation technique that uses high-pressure water to clean and expand existing fractures in the bedrock surrounding your well. This process:

  • Clears mineral deposits and sediment blocking water flow
  • Opens new pathways for groundwater to reach your well
  • Can increase well yield by 2-10 times in many cases
  • Extends the life of your existing well

Unlike oil and gas fracking, well hydrofracturing uses only clean water—no chemicals are involved.

Signs Your San Pasqual Well Needs Hydrofracturing

  • Declining water production over time
  • Well runs dry during peak usage
  • Slow recovery rate after pumping
  • Pump cycling on and off frequently
  • Lower water pressure than previous years

The Hydrofracturing Process

  1. Initial Assessment: We evaluate your well's current yield and condition
  2. Equipment Setup: Specialized high-pressure equipment is positioned at your well
  3. Isolation: Packers seal off sections of the well for targeted treatment
  4. Pressurization: Water is pumped at 500-3,000 PSI to fracture bedrock
  5. Development: Well is cleaned and tested for improved yield

Why San Pasqual's Geology Favors Hydrofracturing

Groundwater in the hills around San Pasqual does not sit in a deep, sandy aquifer the way it does under a river-bottom farm town in the Central Valley. Instead, water moves through cracks, joints, and fracture planes in granite and metamorphic rock. A well drilled into this kind of formation only produces as much water as the fractures it happens to intersect. Two homes on the same ridge can have wildly different yields depending on whether the bit crossed a productive seam. Over years of pumping, those seams accumulate fine sediment, iron and manganese precipitate, and mineral scale builds on the rock face, slowly choking off the flow paths. Because the rock itself is sound and brittle, it is an ideal candidate for re-opening with water pressure. That is the same reason the broader backcountry from San Pasqual to Ramona, Valley Center, and the San Marcos hills sees strong hydrofracturing success rates.

How the High-Pressure Treatment Works

Hydrofracturing on a domestic water well has nothing to do with the chemical-and-sand oil-and-gas process people read about in the news. We use clean water only. The technician lowers a pair of inflatable rubber packers into the cased or open borehole and inflates them to seal off a target zone, isolating it from the rest of the well. A surface pump then forces water into that isolated interval at pressures that typically run between 1,000 and 3,000 PSI. The pressure surge widens existing hairline fractures, flushes out the packed sediment and scale, and connects the borehole to water-bearing seams that were previously too tight or too clogged to contribute. The packers are repositioned and the process repeated at multiple depths so the whole productive section of the well gets treated. A full job usually runs four to eight hours.

Signs a San Pasqual Well Is a Good Candidate

  • Gradual yield decline — the well used to keep up with the house and the irrigation but now struggles, especially in late summer.
  • Slow recovery — after a long pumping cycle, the static water level takes hours rather than minutes to rebound.
  • Short-cycling pump — the pump kicks on and off rapidly because the well cannot deliver a steady column of water.
  • A newer well that disappointed — drilled into solid granite but never produced the gallons-per-minute the driller hoped for.
  • You are weighing a new well — at $18,000-$42,000 turnkey for a new well versus $3,000-$8,000 for hydrofracturing, treating the existing borehole first is almost always the smart financial move.

What to Check Before You Call

A little homework helps us help you. Pull your original well completion report or driller's log if you have it; total depth, casing depth, and the lithology notes tell us whether you have the hard fractured rock that responds to treatment. Note your symptoms: when the decline started, whether it tracks the dry season, and how the pump behaves. Have a rough sense of your current yield in gallons per minute if you can; even a bucket-and-stopwatch estimate at the wellhead is useful. Check whether anything mechanical might be masquerading as a yield problem. A failing pressure switch ($150-$350), a waterlogged pressure tank ($600-$1,500), or a tired submersible pump ($2,500-$5,500) can all mimic low water without the aquifer being at fault. We sort that out during the diagnostic so you do not pay to frac a well that only needed a $200 switch.

When to Bring in a Professional

Hydrofracturing is not a DIY project. It requires high-pressure equipment, packers rated for borehole conditions, and the judgment to read a well before pressurizing it. A cracked or corroded casing, a collapsing borehole wall, or a well sited in loose sand rather than competent rock are all reasons NOT to frac, because pressurizing those wells can do real damage. That is why every job we do starts with a $125 diagnostic (credited toward the work if you proceed) and, when warranted, a downhole video inspection to confirm the casing is sound and to identify the productive fracture zones we should target. If the camera shows a failed casing or the rock is wrong for the technique, we will tell you honestly and steer you toward the option that actually fixes the problem, whether that is a liner repair, a deeper well, or improved storage and pressure equipment.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will hydrofracturing contaminate my San Pasqual well?

No. Domestic water-well hydrofracturing uses only clean water under pressure. No chemicals, sand, or proppants are introduced. After treatment we develop and flush the well, and you should disinfect and, if you wish, test the water before returning it to service.

How much will my yield improve?

In San Diego County's fractured granite, roughly 70-80% of treated wells see improvement, with typical gains of two to five times the pre-treatment flow. Results vary by site, and we measure your yield before and after so the improvement is documented, not guessed.

How long do the results last?

Most wells hold their improved yield for many years. Because the technique reopens and extends the natural fracture network, it is not a temporary patch like simply cleaning the screen. Some wells are eventually re-treated after a decade or more.

Is my property too far out for service?

San Pasqual is well within our Ramona service radius. We routinely work the surrounding canyons and ridgelines, including parcels with rough access.

What if hydrofracturing does not work on my well?

If the diagnostic shows your well is not a candidate, we will say so before any pressure work begins. If a treated well underperforms, we discuss next steps honestly, which may include a deeper or new well. We would rather lose a frac job than sell you one that cannot succeed.

Can you also fix my pump and pressure problems at the same time?

Yes. As a full-service well contractor we handle pumps, pressure tanks, constant-pressure systems, and water treatment, so a single visit can address both the well and the equipment.
