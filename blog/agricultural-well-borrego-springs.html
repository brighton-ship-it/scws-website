Agricultural Well Service in Borrego Springs

Borrego Springs is a desert oasis in northeastern San Diego County, completely surrounded by Anza-Borrego Desert State Park. Despite searing summers and only a few inches of rain a year, Borrego has long been farm country — citrus and grapefruit groves, table grapes, date palms, and specialty crops thrive in the warm winters and long growing season. There is no imported water here; the entire valley, agriculture and town alike, draws from a single groundwater basin. That makes well performance and groundwater stewardship unusually important in Borrego, and it makes a knowledgeable C-57 contractor essential. Southern California Well Service has worked desert wells for more than 30 years.

The Borrego Valley sits on a deep alluvial basin filled with sediments washed down from the surrounding mountains. Agricultural wells here are often deep and high-capacity, drawing from an aquifer that has been heavily pumped for decades. Borrego is one of California's basins formally working through the Sustainable Groundwater Management Act because the aquifer has been in long-term overdraft, with water levels declining and pumping lifts increasing over time. Practically, that means deeper static levels, bigger lift requirements, and a real premium on efficient, correctly sized equipment.

How a Desert Agricultural Well Works in Borrego

A Borrego grove or vineyard well typically uses a large submersible pump set deep in the basin aquifer, delivering substantial flow to irrigate citrus, grapes, or dates through the brutal summer when daily water demand is at its highest anywhere in the county. Because lift is significant and energy costs are real, efficiency is paramount — an oversized or poorly matched pump wastes money on every acre-foot. Most operations run through storage and pressurized drip or microsprinkler systems engineered for the heat and the salinity of desert groundwater.

In a declining basin, sizing the pump and setting it at the right depth is critical. As static levels drop, a pump set too shallow loses suction and a pump sized for old water levels can no longer deliver. We evaluate current water levels, size and set pumps for where the aquifer actually is today, and design for efficiency so growers aren't pumping more energy than the crop requires. We also manage the salinity and mineral content typical of desert basins so irrigation water doesn't damage sensitive crops.

Common Agricultural Well Problems in Borrego Springs

Declining water levels and increasing lift

The basin's long-term overdraft means static levels fall over the years. Wells that produced well a decade ago may now need the pump lowered or replaced with equipment sized for greater lift.

Salinity and high mineral content

Desert groundwater often carries elevated salts, total dissolved solids, and sometimes arsenic or other minerals. For citrus, grapes, and dates, water chemistry must be tested and managed.

Heat-driven peak demand

Summer irrigation demand in Borrego is extreme, and any weakness in the well or pump shows up exactly when failure is most costly. Proper sizing and storage protect against mid-heatwave shortfalls.

What to Check Before You Call

  • Confirm power at the well and that no breaker or motor overload has tripped.
  • Check the pressure gauge and storage-tank level.
  • Note whether yield has been gradually declining (a basin/level issue) or dropped suddenly (a pump issue).
  • Verify the pressure tank's air charge.
  • Note recent outages or extreme heat events.

If the pump runs without producing water, shut it down to avoid dry-running damage and call us.

When to Call a Professional

Call a licensed C-57 contractor when water stops, when yield declines, when you suspect the water level has dropped below your pump, when salinity rises, or before you plant in the desert heat. Frequently Asked Questions

Why is my Borrego well producing less than it used to?

The Borrego Valley aquifer has been in long-term overdraft, so static water levels have been declining for years. As the level drops toward or below your pump, yield falls. Lowering the pump or replacing it with equipment sized for greater lift usually restores production.

Is desert groundwater safe for citrus, grapes, and dates?

It can be, but Borrego groundwater often carries elevated salts and minerals that affect sensitive crops. We test your water and recommend management strategies so irrigation water doesn't slowly damage your trees and vines.

How deep are agricultural wells in Borrego Springs?

Borrego ag wells are typically deep, drawing from a thick alluvial basin, and the productive depth has effectively increased as water levels decline. A current water-level measurement and yield test tell us how to size and set your pump today.

How do I keep pumping costs down in the desert?

Efficiency is everything when lift is high. We size the pump precisely to the well and the crop, set it at the right depth, and avoid the over-pumping that wastes energy — so you're not paying to lift more water than your operation needs.

Do you offer emergency well service in Borrego?

Yes — same-day emergency response is available. In Borrego's summer heat, a lost pump threatens an entire grove or vineyard fast, so call (760) 440-8520 or text (760) 219-5877.

Should I worry about groundwater rules in Borrego?

Borrego is actively managing its basin under state sustainable-groundwater requirements, which is why efficient water use matters more here than almost anywhere. We focus on making every well as efficient and reliable as possible within those realities.
