Well Pump Repair & Replacement

Well Rehabilitation & Deepening

Older Lakeside wells sometimes struggle to meet modern household demands or have suffered from declining water tables during extended droughts. Before committing to a completely new well, consider professional rehabilitation services that can restore or improve performance at significantly lower cost:

  • Hydrofracturing to enhance water production from fractured rock
  • Well deepening to reach additional aquifer zones
  • Chemical treatment to remove iron bacteria and mineral deposits
  • Video camera inspection to assess well integrity
  • Screen replacement or addition for better water infiltration
  • Surge development to clean fine sediment from fractures

Rehabilitation works best for wells in good structural condition that simply need enhanced productivity or access to deeper water zones. Our evaluation determines whether your well is a good candidate for rehabilitation.

Water Quality Testing & Treatment

Lakeside groundwater is generally excellent quality, but some properties encounter specific issues requiring attention. We provide comprehensive water quality testing and treatment solutions for:

  • Hard water causing scale in pipes, water heaters, and fixtures
  • Iron and manganese causing orange or black staining
  • Occasional bacteria requiring UV disinfection or chlorination
  • Turbidity from fine sediment or clay particles
  • Total dissolved solids (TDS) affecting taste
  • Sulfur odors (hydrogen sulfide) from certain formations

We recommend baseline water testing for all new wells and periodic retesting every 2-3 years, or sooner if you notice changes in taste, odor, color, or if anyone in your household has health concerns.

Understanding Lakeside's Geology & Groundwater

Lakeside sits in one of East County's most geologically interesting settings—a broad river valley flanked by granite hills. Understanding this geology is essential for successful well drilling and realistic expectations.

San Diego River Valley Aquifers

The San Diego River has spent millions of years carving through rock and depositing sediments throughout the Lakeside valley. Properties near the river and its historic floodplain benefit from thick deposits of alluvial sand and gravel—excellent aquifer materials that readily transmit groundwater. These valley wells often produce abundant water at moderate depths (200-350 feet) with excellent yields.

The river valley aquifer is partially recharged by river flows during wet winters, though decades of upstream development have reduced natural recharge rates. Still, valley properties generally enjoy more stable water tables than hillside locations.

Hillside & Mountain Geology

Move away from the river valley into areas like Blossom Valley, Moreno, or up toward El Cajon Mountain, and geology transitions to decomposed granite and crystalline bedrock. These formations produce water primarily through fractures rather than continuous porous layers. Well productivity depends on intersecting productive fracture zones, making local drilling experience critically important.

Hillside wells typically require deeper drilling (350-500+ feet) and may show more seasonal variation in water levels compared to valley wells. However, properly drilled hillside wells provide excellent long-term reliability.

Seasonal Water Table Changes

Like most of San Diego County, Lakeside experiences seasonal groundwater fluctuations. Water tables rise during wet winters and decline through dry summers and drought periods. Valley wells near the river typically show less dramatic fluctuation than hillside wells in fractured bedrock.

We design wells with these fluctuations in mind—drilling to depths that provide adequate water even during multi-year droughts, and setting pumps deep enough to accommodate typical seasonal drawdown. This upfront planning prevents problems during inevitable dry cycles.

Common Well Problems in Lakeside

After nearly four decades serving Lakeside properties, we've identified the most frequent well issues East County homeowners encounter:

Sediment in Water

Lakeside's alluvial valley aquifers can introduce fine sand or silt into wells, particularly in areas with unconsolidated sediments. River valley wells may also experience temporary turbidity after heavy rain events that increase groundwater recharge. Proper well construction with correctly sized screens and gravel packing minimizes sediment intrusion. Sand separators and whole-house sediment filters provide additional protection for plumbing and appliances.

Iron Staining and Hard Water

Many Lakeside properties experience moderate to high water hardness, with some also encountering dissolved iron that causes orange staining on fixtures and laundry. While neither poses health risks, both frustrate homeowners and reduce appliance efficiency. Water softeners effectively address hardness, while oxidizing filters or treatment systems remove iron before it causes staining.

Declining Well Yields

Older wells sometimes show gradually decreasing water production over time. This can result from regional water table decline, mineral buildup reducing well screen effectiveness, siltation from poor well construction, or increased pumping by neighboring properties drawing from the same aquifer. Professional evaluation determines whether rehabilitation can restore capacity or if deeper drilling is required.

Pressure Tank Failures

Pressure tanks typically last 7-15 years before the internal bladder fails and the tank becomes "waterlogged." This causes pumps to cycle on and off rapidly, dramatically shortening pump life. If you notice your pump running more frequently or hearing it cycle on and off every few minutes, have your pressure tank inspected immediately to prevent pump damage.
