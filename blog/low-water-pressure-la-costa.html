How Your Pressure System Delivers Water

On a private system, water does not arrive pressurized on its own. A pump moves water into a pressure tank, where a sealed air charge presses on the water so your taps flow steadily. A pressure switch turns the pump on and off, typically holding 40 to 60 PSI. Many La Costa estate properties also run a booster pump or constant-pressure system to serve large homes, multiple bathrooms, and extensive landscape irrigation. Low pressure means one of these links has slipped — and pinpointing which is the heart of good diagnosis.

Common Causes of Low Pressure in La Costa

  • Waterlogged pressure tank. The most frequent culprit. A failed bladder makes pressure surge and sag and the pump short-cycle.
  • Undersized or worn booster pump. Large La Costa homes demand a lot of water at once; a tired or undersized booster cannot keep up when several bathrooms and the irrigation system run together.
  • Irrigation drawing down the system. Extensive landscaping on these hillside lots can pull pressure away from the house when zones run — a scheduling or sizing issue we see often.
  • Pressure switch problems. Corroded or misadjusted switch contacts cap pressure below where it should be.
  • Hard water and scale. Mineral-rich North County groundwater deposits scale that clogs pumps, screens, filters, and fixtures over time.
  • Old or undersized supply lines. On larger parcels, long or corroded runs of pipe from the well to the house can quietly strangle pressure.

Simple Checks You Can Make First

  1. One fixture or the whole house? A single weak faucet usually means a clogged aerator; whole-house loss points upstream to the pump, tank, or main line.
  2. Does irrigation make it worse? If pressure drops when sprinkler zones run, the system may be undersized or the irrigation may need to be isolated or rescheduled.
  3. Read the gauge. Healthy pressure cycles between roughly 40 and 60 PSI. Stuck-low or wildly swinging readings are a strong clue.
  4. Tap the tank. Hollow on top, solid on the bottom is normal; full-sounding all the way up means a dead bladder.
  5. Listen for short-cycling. Rapid pump clicking points to a waterlogged tank or a failing switch.

If the basics look fine but pressure stays weak, a licensed professional should take over.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does my La Costa home lose pressure when the sprinklers run?

Large hillside lots often have extensive irrigation that competes with the house for water. If the well, pump, or supply line cannot serve both at once, pressure drops when zones turn on. We can resize the system, add a dedicated irrigation supply, or reschedule zones to solve it.

How deep are wells in the La Costa area?

It varies with location and elevation across eastern Carlsbad, but many local wells fall in the 150 to 400 foot range. Depth and the mix of sedimentary and granitic ground affect pump sizing, so we review your well log when one is available.

My big house has multiple bathrooms — do I need a booster?

Possibly. Large homes with simultaneous demand often benefit from a constant-pressure or booster system that holds steady pressure no matter how many fixtures run. We will assess your real peak demand and tell you honestly what your home needs.

Is hard water hurting my pressure?

It can. North County groundwater carries minerals that deposit scale inside pumps, filters, and fixtures, slowly choking flow. The right softening or treatment protects your equipment and restores pressure.

Can a low-yield North County well be improved without drilling a new one?

Often, yes.
