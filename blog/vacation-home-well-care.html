Common Vacation Home Well Problems

Wells at seasonal properties face unique challenges that full-time homes don't experience:

Stagnant Water Issues

  • Bacterial growth — Still water is a breeding ground for bacteria
  • Biofilm buildup — Slimy bacterial colonies form inside pipes
  • Sulfur odors — Sulfur-reducing bacteria thrive in stagnant conditions
  • Sediment settling — Particles accumulate at low points
  • Hot water tank issues — Legionella and other bacteria grow in warm, still water

Equipment Problems

  • Pump seal drying — Extended non-use can cause seals to dry and crack
  • Pressure tank issues — Air charge can leak over time
  • Check valve failure — Valves can stick or fail after sitting
  • Electrical issues — Rodents chew wires, insects nest in control boxes
  • Undetected leaks — Small leaks become big problems without anyone noticing

Environmental Threats

  • Freezing — Mountain properties face freeze damage
  • Wildfire — Fire can destroy surface equipment
  • Pest intrusion — Rodents, insects, and snakes find their way in
  • Vandalism — Remote properties are targets for theft
  • Power surges — Lightning and grid issues damage equipment

Before You Leave: Departure Checklist

For Short Absences (1-2 Weeks)

  • ☐ Check pressure tank reading (normal is 40-60 PSI)
  • ☐ Note the water meter reading (if equipped)
  • ☐ Set water heater to "vacation" or lowest setting
  • ☐ Close toilet lids (prevents evaporation and pests)
  • ☐ Check for any existing leaks
  • ☐ Leave pump power ON (minor usage from timers, etc. keeps water fresh)

For Extended Absences (2+ Weeks)

  • ☐ Complete the short-absence checklist above
  • ☐ Turn off water to the house at the main shutoff
  • ☐ Consider turning off power to the well pump
  • ☐ Turn off water heater
  • ☐ Pour RV antifreeze in drain traps (prevents sewer gas and drying)
  • ☐ Set HVAC to prevent freeze (if applicable)
  • ☐ Check filter condition and replace if needed
  • ☐ Take photos of gauge readings for reference

For Winter Shutdown (Mountain Properties)

  • ☐ Complete extended absence checklist
  • ☐ Turn off power to well pump at breaker
  • ☐ Drain the pressure tank
  • ☐ Open lowest faucets to drain house pipes
  • ☐ Blow out remaining water with compressed air (optional but thorough)
  • ☐ Open all faucets slightly to prevent pressure buildup
  • ☐ Drain water heater completely
  • ☐ Add RV antifreeze to all drain traps
  • ☐ Flush toilets, then add antifreeze to bowls and tanks
  • ☐ Insulate any exposed pipes that can't be drained
  • ☐ Turn off main water supply at the wellhead (if possible)

Winterization for Mountain Properties

Properties in Julian, Palomar Mountain, Pine Valley, and other mountain areas need winterization when temperatures drop below 32°F.

What Freezes First

  1. Exposed outdoor faucets — Most vulnerable
  2. Pipes in crawl spaces — Especially on north-facing sides
  3. Pressure tank — If in unheated space
  4. Pipes in exterior walls — Limited insulation
  5. Water heater (if in garage) — Exposed to cold

Winterization Steps

  1. Turn off the well pump at the circuit breaker
  2. Close the main shutoff near the pressure tank
  3. Open the lowest faucet (usually outdoor) to drain pipes by gravity
  4. Open all faucets including showers and tub faucets
  5. Flush all toilets multiple times to empty tanks and bowls
  6. Drain the water heater using the bottom valve
  7. Open the pressure tank drain (bottom petcock or valve)
  8. Use compressed air to blow remaining water from lines (optional)
  9. Add antifreeze to all P-traps (sinks, showers, toilets)
  10. Disconnect outdoor hoses and open hose bibs

Antifreeze Tips

  • Use RV/marine antifreeze (propylene glycol)—NOT automotive antifreeze
  • RV antifreeze is safe, non-toxic, and biodegradable
  • Use 1-2 cups per drain trap
  • Add to toilet bowls and tanks
  • Don't forget the washing machine drain and dishwasher

Spring Startup Procedure

Proper startup after extended non-use is critical for water quality and equipment health.

Visual Inspection First

  • ☐ Check wellhead for damage, pest entry, or vandalism
  • ☐ Inspect pressure tank for rust, leaks, or damage
  • ☐ Look at all visible pipes for cracks or breaks
  • ☐ Check control box for rodent damage or insect nests
  • ☐ Inspect water heater for issues
  • ☐ Look under sinks for leak evidence

Startup Steps

  1. Close all faucets that were left open
  2. Close the pressure tank drain
  3. Check pressure tank air charge (should be 2 PSI below cut-in)
  4. Turn on power to the well pump
  5. Listen for pump operation — It should start and build pressure
  6. Watch the pressure gauge — Should reach cut-off pressure and stop
  7. Open an outdoor faucet and let water run 10-20 minutes
  8. Let water clear — Initial rust or sediment is normal
  9. Flush each faucet for several minutes
  10. Run water heater only after lines are flushed
  11. Check all fixtures for proper operation

Water Quality After Startup

  • Don't drink the water immediately — Flush thoroughly first
  • Run water until clear — Initial discoloration is normal
  • Smell test — Sulfur odor indicates bacteria growth
  • Consider testing — Bacteria test recommended after extended non-use
  • Shock chlorinate if needed — Kills bacteria throughout the system

Remote Monitoring Options

Technology can alert you to problems before they become emergencies.

Water Leak Detectors

  • Moen Flo — Monitors whole-house flow, detects leaks, can auto-shutoff
  • Phyn Plus — Similar flow monitoring with smartphone alerts
  • SimpliSafe Water Sensor — Basic leak detection with alerts
  • Govee Water Detectors — Affordable WiFi-connected sensors

Pressure Monitoring

  • Smart pressure gauges — Alert if pressure drops or spikes
  • Well pump monitors — Track pump cycles and runtime

Temperature Monitoring

  • WiFi temperature sensors — Alert before pipes freeze
  • Smart thermostats — Can alert to HVAC failures

Power Monitoring

  • Smart plugs with monitoring — Track pump power usage
  • Power outage alerts — Many security systems include this

Dealing with Stagnant Water

Signs of Stagnation

  • Sulfur (rotten egg) smell when water first runs
  • Discolored or cloudy water
  • Slick or slimy feel
  • Unusual taste

Flushing Procedure

  1. Start at the faucet closest to the pressure tank
  2. Run cold water for 5-10 minutes at full pressure
  3. Move to the next faucet, working through the house
  4. Run hot water separately (after cold is clear)
  5. Flush toilets multiple times
  6. Run showers and tubs
  7. Run washing machine through an empty cycle
  8. Run dishwasher through an empty cycle

When Flushing Isn't Enough

If odors or discoloration persist after thorough flushing, you may need:

  • Shock chlorination — Kills bacteria throughout the system
  • Hot water heater flush — Sediment and bacteria accumulate in tanks
  • Water testing — To identify specific contamination
  • Professional cleaning — For persistent biofilm problems

Property Manager and Caretaker Tips

If someone checks on your property, here's what they should know:

Monthly Check Tasks

  • Run water for 5-10 minutes at multiple faucets
  • Note pressure gauge reading
  • Check for visible leaks
  • Look for pest intrusion around equipment
  • Verify pump is operating normally (listen for unusual sounds)
  • Check salt level in water softener (if equipped)

What to Report Immediately

  • No water or very low pressure
  • Pump running continuously
  • Visible leaks anywhere
  • Unusual sounds from pump or tank
  • Strong sulfur smell
  • Discolored water that doesn't clear

Emergency Situations

Frozen Pipes

  1. Turn off water at the main shutoff
  2. Turn off power to the well pump
  3. Open faucets to relieve pressure
  4. Do NOT use open flames to thaw pipes
  5. Use hair dryer or heat lamp to thaw slowly
  6. Inspect for cracks after thawing
  7. Call a professional if pipes have burst

Pump Won't Start After Absence

  1. Check circuit breaker (may have tripped)
  2. Check for blown fuse
  3. Look for rodent damage to wiring
  4. Check pressure switch for corrosion
  5. Listen for humming (indicates seized motor)
  6. Call a professional if you can't identify the issue

Contaminated Water

  1. Don't drink the water
  2. Test the water to identify contamination
  3. Shock chlorinate the well and system
  4. Retest after treatment
  5. Investigate source of contamination

Frequently Asked Questions

How long can a well sit unused?

A well can sit unused for months or even years without permanent damage to the well itself, but water quality issues develop quickly. After 2-4 weeks of non-use, bacteria can grow in stagnant water. After extended periods, sediment settles, biofilm develops inside pipes, and the pump may have startup issues from dried seals. Always flush thoroughly before drinking water from an unused well, and consider water testing after absences of a month or more.

Should I turn off my well pump when away?

For short trips (1-2 weeks), leaving the pump on is generally fine—it may even keep water fresher if anything draws water (ice maker, humidifier, timers). For longer absences, turn off power to the pump to prevent damage from undetected leaks. If a pipe breaks with the pump running, it can burn out trying to maintain pressure. Shut off the breaker or disconnect, and consider installing a leak detector with auto-shutoff for peace of mind.

Do I need to winterize my well in California?

It depends on your property's location and elevation. Mountain properties in Julian, Palomar Mountain, Pine Valley, or anywhere above about 3,500 feet can definitely freeze and need winterization. Coastal and valley areas rarely require full winterization but should protect exposed pipes during occasional cold snaps (we do get freezing nights sometimes). When in doubt, insulate exposed components and know how to drain pipes quickly if a freeze warning occurs.

How do I start a well after it's been sitting?

First, visually inspect all components for damage, pest intrusion, or vandalism. Turn on power and listen for normal pump operation. Open an outdoor faucet (to direct initial dirty water outside) and let water run for 10-20 minutes until clear. Check that pressure readings are normal. Flush all fixtures in the house. Don't drink the water until it runs clear, and ideally get a bacteria test if it's been unused for months.

My vacation home water smells like rotten eggs when I return. What should I do?

Sulfur odor after non-use is common—sulfur-reducing bacteria thrive in stagnant water. Run all faucets for 10-20 minutes to flush the system. If the smell persists, flush your water heater (bacteria love warm, still water). If it still smells, shock chlorinate the well and entire plumbing system. This is a good reason to have someone run water monthly at vacation properties, or install a circulation timer that briefly runs water to prevent stagnation.
