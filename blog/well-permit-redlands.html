San Bernardino County Well Permit Requirements

In San Bernardino County, well permits are required for:

  • Drilling a new domestic or agricultural well
  • Deepening an existing well
  • Repairing or reconstructing well casing
  • Decommissioning (destroying) an unused well
  • Converting a well to a different use

The Permit Process

  1. Site Evaluation: We assess your property for optimal well location
  2. Application: Submit permit application with site plans
  3. Review: County reviews for setback requirements and regulations
  4. Approval: Permit issued (typically 2-4 weeks)
  5. Construction: Well work proceeds per permit specifications
  6. Inspection: County may inspect completed work
  7. Completion Report: We file required documentation

Setback Requirements

Wells must be located minimum distances from:

  • Septic tanks: 50-100 feet
  • Leach fields: 100-150 feet
  • Property lines: 5-10 feet
  • Buildings: 5 feet minimum
  • Other wells: varies

Exact requirements depend on San Bernardino County regulations and local conditions.

Well Permits in Redlands: What Property Owners Should Know

Redlands, set among the citrus groves of the San Bernardino Valley, has a long agricultural heritage, and its grove parcels and foothill estates frequently rely on private wells for irrigation and domestic use. If you intend to drill a new well, deepen an existing one, or destroy an abandoned well on your Redlands property, California's Water Well Standards (Department of Water Resources Bulletins 74-81 and 74-90) apply, and the permit is issued locally by San Bernardino County. Southern California Well Service has handled these permits for property owners across the county for more than 30 years, managing the whole process turnkey so you do not have to navigate the county yourself.

Who Issues Well Permits in Redlands?

Because Redlands is in San Bernardino County, well permits are issued by the San Bernardino County Department of Public Health, Division of Environmental Health Services (EHS). The EHS office is located at 385 N. Arrowhead Avenue, San Bernardino, CA 92415 and can be reached at (800) 442-2283. EHS administers the Application for Well Permit under the County's groundwater ordinance and the state Water Well Standards. The city of Redlands does not run its own well program, so the county EHS is the single permitting authority for your project.

Step-by-Step: The San Bernardino County Well Permit Process

Here is how a Redlands well permit typically moves forward:

  1. Site evaluation. We assess your property, locate the best aquifer access, and confirm required setbacks can be met.
  2. Application for Well Permit. EHS requires a completed Application for Well Permit with a site plan showing the proposed well, property lines, septic systems, structures, and potential contamination sources, plus the proposed depth and construction details.
  3. County review. EHS reviews the application against state standards (Bulletins 74-81 and 74-90) and the County Code, confirming the well will be built by a California-licensed driller.
  4. Permit issuance. Once approved, the permit is issued and work can begin.
  5. Construction. A licensed C-57 driller constructs the well to the permitted specifications, including the required annular surface seal.
  6. Sealing, grouting, and inspection. The annular space is sealed with cement or bentonite grout to protect groundwater, and EHS may require a physical inspection of the well's surface features.
  7. Well driller's report. The driller must submit the Water Well Drillers Report to EHS within 30 days of completion, documenting depth, casing, perforations, and yield. We file this for you.

Permit Requirements and Construction Standards

For a Redlands well, San Bernardino County EHS enforces the state minimum standards plus county requirements:

  • Setbacks. Minimum separations from septic tanks (about 50 feet), leach fields and sewage disposal (about 100 feet), property lines, and surface water. Exact distances depend on site conditions.
  • Surface seal. A continuous annular seal, typically at least 20 feet deep, grouted to keep contaminants out of the aquifer per Bulletin 74-81.
  • Licensed driller. Wells must be constructed (or destroyed) by a California State licensed well driller.
  • Destruction standards. Unused wells must be properly destroyed by filling and sealing per Bulletin 74-90.
  • Reporting. The Water Well Drillers Report must reach EHS within 30 days of completion.

Timeline and Costs in Redlands

For a standard Redlands residential well, plan on roughly 2 to 4 weeks for EHS to review and issue the permit once the application is complete. Projects in special groundwater zones, near sensitive features, or needing a variance can take longer.

San Bernardino County administers well permits on a fee schedule set by County Code, and the basic county well permit fee generally falls in the $300 to $1,200 range, with additional charges possible for special review or certification. When you hire us for a turnkey job, the permit is included. Complete turnkey drilling with the permit typically runs $18,000 to $42,000 depending on depth, geology, and casing. A site diagnostic visit is $125, credited toward your project.

When Do You Need a Permit?

In Redlands you need a San Bernardino County EHS permit to:

  • Drill a new well (domestic, agricultural, or commercial).
  • Deepen or modify an existing well.
  • Reconstruct or repair casing in a way that alters the well.
  • Destroy or abandon an unused well.

A straightforward pump replacement generally does not require a well construction permit, but any work that changes the borehole, casing, or seal does. If you are uncertain, call us first.

Common Well Permitting Scenarios in Redlands

How your Redlands permit is handled depends on the project. These are the situations we see most often under San Bernardino County rules:

  • New home on unserved land. Where there is no water connection, a domestic well permit is part of making the Redlands-area parcel buildable. We coordinate it with your other county approvals.
  • Replacing or deepening a tired well. When yield drops, a new or deepened well requires its own permit, reviewed against current state standards and the county groundwater ordinance.
  • Destroying an abandoned well. Unused wells must be sealed per Bulletin 74-90, and the Water Well Drillers Report filed with EHS. This frequently surfaces during a sale, and we handle the destruction permit and paperwork.
  • Agricultural and foothill wells. Grove, ranch, and foothill wells around Redlands may involve larger casing and yield testing, which we document for EHS certification.

Which agency issues well permits for Redlands?

The San Bernardino County Department of Public Health, Division of Environmental Health Services (EHS) at 385 N. Arrowhead Avenue, San Bernardino, reachable at (800) 442-2283. The city does not run its own well program.

How long does a Redlands well permit take?

Standard residential reviews usually take about 2 to 4 weeks once a complete application is submitted. Special zones or variance requests can extend the timeline.

When must the driller's report be filed?

San Bernardino County requires the Water Well Drillers Report to be submitted to EHS within 30 days of well completion. We handle this filing for you.
