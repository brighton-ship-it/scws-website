What is Hydrofracturing?

Hydrofracturing is a well rehabilitation technique that uses high-pressure water to clean and expand existing fractures in the bedrock surrounding your well. This process:

  • Clears mineral deposits and sediment blocking water flow
  • Opens new pathways for groundwater to reach your well
  • Can increase well yield by 2-10 times in many cases
  • Extends the life of your existing well

Unlike oil and gas fracking, well hydrofracturing uses only clean water—no chemicals are involved.

Signs Your San Ysidro Well Needs Hydrofracturing

  • Declining water production over time
  • Well runs dry during peak usage
  • Slow recovery rate after pumping
  • Pump cycling on and off frequently
  • Lower water pressure than previous years

The Hydrofracturing Process

  1. Initial Assessment: We evaluate your well's current yield and condition
  2. Equipment Setup: Specialized high-pressure equipment is positioned at your well
  3. Isolation: Packers seal off sections of the well for targeted treatment
  4. Pressurization: Water is pumped at 500-3,000 PSI to fracture bedrock
  5. Development: Well is cleaned and tested for improved yield

Many San Ysidro wells that appear to be "going dry" can be successfully rehabilitated through hydrofracturing, saving homeowners tens of thousands of dollars.

Hydrofracturing in San Ysidro: An Honest Look

San Ysidro is a community in the southernmost part of the City of San Diego, in San Diego County, pressed against the U.S.–Mexico border and the busy San Ysidro Port of Entry. It is a dense, urban, largely built-out neighborhood sitting near the Tijuana River valley and the coastal lowlands. The honest reality for San Ysidro is this: almost every home and business here is served by the City of San Diego's municipal water system, on treated, imported water delivered through a meter. Where there is no private well, there is nothing for hydrofracturing to enhance — and we would rather say that plainly than pretend otherwise.

Hydrofracturing (also called hydrofracking) is a well-yield enhancement service. It boosts flow on low-yield wells drilled into fractured crystalline bedrock by injecting high-pressure water to clean and open existing fractures. In an urban, coastal community on municipal supply, that simply is not a service most residents will ever need. We have written this page so that, if you do happen to own a rare private well in or near San Ysidro, you can judge for yourself whether the technique applies — and so that no one here is sold a treatment that does not fit their situation.

Why Hydrofracturing Rarely Applies in San Ysidro

Two facts about San Ysidro make hydrofracturing a weak fit. First, the area is on municipal water; there are very few private wells inside the community. Second, the local geology near the Tijuana River valley and the coastal plain is dominated by sedimentary deposits and alluvial fill — sand, silt, and gravel — rather than the fractured granite that hydrofracturing depends on. Even where a well exists, if it draws from loose, granular material there are no rock fractures to open, and high-pressure injection accomplishes little. Hydrofracturing helps only wells finished in fractured crystalline or granitic bedrock, which is found inland and in the foothills, not in the coastal flats of the far south county.

It is worth understanding the setting a little more. San Ysidro sits at the southern end of the coastal lowlands, close to the Tijuana River as it crosses into the United States and runs toward the estuary near Imperial Beach. This is young, soft ground — river and coastal sediments layered over the marine sedimentary formations of the San Diego embayment. Groundwater that does exist here moves through sand and gravel, not through cracks in hard rock. That is the opposite of the conditions hydrofracturing needs, and it is the geological reason the technique belongs inland, in the granite of the foothills and mountains, rather than here on the coastal flats.

How Hydrofracturing Works, for the Rare Bedrock Well

If you do own a genuine hard-rock well — perhaps on higher ground east of the urban core toward the inland hills — here is what the process involves. A bedrock well produces water through a network of thin natural fractures in granite. Over time those fractures clog with sediment and mineral scale, and yield falls. We seal a section of the cased borehole with inflatable packers and inject clean water — no chemicals — at roughly 1,000 to 3,000 PSI, flushing the fractures and opening hairline cracks so the well connects to more water-bearing rock. The job usually takes four to eight hours.

Some homeowners come to us after seeing hydrofracturing advertised and wonder whether it could improve their water at home. In a community like San Ysidro the answer is usually no, and we think it is more useful to explain why than to book a job that won't deliver. Spending three to eight thousand dollars on a fracturing treatment makes sense only when you have a hard-rock well that the technique can actually act on. If you are on city water, that money is far better spent on a plumbing repair, a pressure adjustment, or a treatment system, depending on what is actually bothering you.

Better Options for Most San Ysidro Situations

For the overwhelming majority of San Ysidro addresses, the right answer to a water concern is not hydrofracturing at all. If you are on city water and your pressure or quality is poor, the issue lies in household plumbing, a pressure regulator, or a point-of-use treatment need — not in a well. In the rare case of a private well in coastal sediment, screen rehabilitation, deepening, a new well, or a treatment system will outperform fracturing every time. We will always steer you toward the option that actually fits your water source rather than toward a service that doesn't.

The Process Step by Step

  1. Diagnostic and baseline test: We confirm whether you even have a well, and whether it is in bedrock or sediment, then measure yield. The $125 diagnostic is credited toward any work.
  2. Camera survey: A downhole inspection assesses casing and, in a rock well, identifies fracture zones.
  3. Packer isolation: Inflatable packers seal the treatment interval (bedrock wells only).
  4. High-pressure injection: Clean water flushes and opens fractures over four to eight hours.
  5. Development and re-test: We develop the well, re-measure, and document the results.

Realistic Results and Expectations

On genuine fractured-bedrock wells anywhere in San Diego County, hydrofracturing improves yield in roughly 70 to 80 percent of cases, commonly two to five times the starting flow. But that figure applies to hard-rock wells in the foothills and back country — not to San Ysidro's urban, coastal, municipal-water setting, where the technique rarely has anything to act on. We never promise a specific result before testing, and in San Ysidro our most common honest recommendation is that hydrofracturing is not the answer at all.

What to Check Before You Call

  • Do you have a well? Most San Ysidro properties are on city water — confirm first.
  • Formation: If you do have a well, does the log say granite ("hard rock") or sand and gravel? Only the former is a candidate.
  • Water source: A monthly city water bill means municipal supply, not a well.
  • Pressure vs. yield: Low pressure on city water is usually a plumbing or regulator issue, not a well issue.
  • Quality concerns: Taste, odor, or hardness are treatment questions, not fracturing ones.

When to Call a Professional

If you are among the few in or near San Ysidro who own a private hard-rock well and it is running short, a licensed contractor can evaluate whether hydrofracturing makes sense. If you are on city water, a licensed plumber or water-treatment specialist is the right call instead. Because hydrofracturing uses high pressure inside a casing, it is never a do-it-yourself task — but for most San Ysidro residents the more useful service we provide is an honest answer about whether you need it at all.

Frequently Asked Questions

I live in San Ysidro — can hydrofracturing help my water?

Almost certainly not. San Ysidro is on city water, so most homes have no well to enhance, and the local coastal geology is not the fractured bedrock hydrofracturing requires.

How do I know if I'm on a well or city water?

If you receive a monthly water bill from the City of San Diego, you are on municipal supply, not a well.

Is well hydrofracturing the same as oil-and-gas fracking?

No. We inject only clean water to clean and open existing fractures in a water well — no chemicals and no new deep drilling.

My city water pressure is low — is that a hydrofracturing issue?

No. Low pressure on municipal water is usually a plumbing or pressure-regulator problem, and a plumber is the right professional to call.

What if I really do have a private well?

Then we will run the diagnostic to confirm whether it is in bedrock or sediment and recommend the right fix — which may or may not be hydrofracturing.
