Mountain Wells at Lake Arrowhead

Cedar Glen is a small mountain community in the San Bernardino Mountains of San Bernardino County, perched at roughly 5,300 feet just east of Lake Arrowhead. It is a world apart from the wells we service down in the valleys. Up here the ground is not deep sand and gravel; it is fractured granitic hard rock, and that single fact changes everything about how a Cedar Glen well behaves and how it needs to be inspected. Water does not sit in a broad, forgiving aquifer. It travels through cracks and fractures in the bedrock, so a well only produces as much as the fractures it happens to intersect will feed it.

That is why yields in Cedar Glen are so variable. One property can have a well that supplies a household comfortably, while a neighboring parcel with a borehole just a few hundred feet away struggles to keep up. Add in winter at altitude, where hard freezes threaten any exposed wellhead, pressure tank, or pipe, and you have a setting where a careful, locally-informed inspection is worth far more than a generic checklist. Southern California Well Service is a licensed C-57 well contractor with local experience since 2020 and a 4.9-star rating, and we bring the right equipment up the mountain to inspect these fractured-rock wells the way they actually need to be inspected.

Buyers, Sellers, and Yearly Care

Most Cedar Glen inspections fall into one of two situations. The first is a property sale. Mountain cabins and homes near Lake Arrowhead change hands often, and when a listing depends on a private well, that well deserves as much scrutiny as the roof or the foundation. For a buyer, a pre-purchase inspection answers the question that matters most in fractured-rock country: will this well actually keep up with the way I intend to live here? We measure output and, just as importantly, recovery, so you are not relying on a five-minute faucet test that tells you nothing about sustained demand. For a seller, presenting a recent, documented inspection turns the well from an unknown into a selling point and heads off the eleventh-hour surprises that derail mountain escrows.

The second situation is ongoing care. Full-time residents and weekend cabin owners alike benefit from a periodic inspection, and in Cedar Glen the timing matters. Catching a failing pressure switch, a waterlogged tank, or worn freeze protection before the first hard freeze is far cheaper and far less disruptive than discovering it when the system quits in January. A well that is checked on a schedule is a well that keeps running when the mountain is at its least forgiving.

What Our Cedar Glen Inspection Covers

We inspect the whole system, and on a mountain well we weight our attention toward the things that matter most at altitude. A full inspection includes:

  • Pump performance: We measure flow rate in gallons per minute, check pressure through the pump's on and off cycle, and read motor amperage under load to confirm the pump is healthy and not overworking against a low-yield well.
  • Pressure tank and switch: We check the tank's air charge and bladder condition and verify the pressure switch cut-in and cut-out points. On a fractured-rock well a properly functioning tank is especially important, because it buffers demand between the well's slower inflow cycles.
  • Electrical, control box, and wiring: We open the control box and inspect contactors, capacitors, terminals, and conductors for corrosion, moisture intrusion, and freeze-related damage, and confirm proper grounding.
  • Static and pumping water levels, recovery, and yield: We record the static level, draw the well down, and time recovery. In Cedar Glen's fractured granitic rock this recovery test is the truest measure of what the well can sustain, far more telling than instantaneous flow alone.
  • Wellhead, sanitary seal, and casing: We inspect the wellhead, cap, and sanitary seal, and check the casing for cracks or gaps, paying close attention to freeze exposure and any pathway that could let surface water or contaminants reach the water column.

We also evaluate the wellhead's winter readiness directly, checking insulation, heat tape, and any enclosure, because freeze protection is not an optional extra at this elevation.

Water Testing and Freeze Protection

Water from fractured granitic bedrock in the San Bernardino Mountains is often reasonably clean, but quality varies from well to well, so we recommend testing on every inspection. We screen for bacteria, chiefly total coliform and E. coli, which can enter through a cracked casing or a wellhead that has been compromised by freeze-thaw cycling. We also run a mineral panel to characterize what is actually coming out of the tap. What we are watching for in Cedar Glen is less about agricultural nitrate and more about whether the physical integrity of the well, stressed by cold and altitude, is letting anything unwanted into an otherwise good hard-rock source.

Freeze protection deserves its own emphasis. At roughly 5,300 feet, an exposed pressure tank, switch, or run of pipe can freeze solid and split in a single cold snap, taking the whole water system down. During the inspection we assess whether wellhead components are properly insulated, whether heat tape is present and functioning, and whether enclosures are sound. Pairing a clean water test with verified freeze protection is exactly the combination a Cedar Glen owner wants going into winter.

The Written Report, Timing, and Cost

You receive a written report at the end of every inspection. It records the pump's flow rate and pressure, motor amperage, pressure tank and switch condition, the electrical and control components, static and pumping water levels with the all-important recovery data, wellhead and casing condition, and freeze-protection status. Any water testing results are attached once the laboratory reports back. The report ends with plain-language conclusions: what is sound, what needs attention, and what to monitor, together with estimates for any recommended work. That documentation is what buyers, sellers, and lenders rely on during a Lake Arrowhead area sale.

Timing runs one to three hours on site, longer when a full recovery and yield test is part of the visit, and steep or snowy mountain access can add time. Laboratory water results generally return within a few business days. On cost, a standard inspection runs $150 to $400 depending on scope and access, water testing adds $100 to $300 depending on the panel, and a dedicated flow and yield test can be added when documented gallons-per-minute and recovery figures are needed. If the appointment turns into a diagnostic troubleshooting call, the $125 diagnostic fee is credited toward any repair we carry out.

Call a professional right away if you notice pressure dropping off during normal use, the pump running longer and longer to keep up, air or sputtering at the taps, or any sign of a frozen or leaking component. In a fractured-rock, high-altitude setting, a small warning sign can be the first hint of a well being pushed past its sustainable yield, and it is always worth checking before a mountain property changes hands.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is well yield so variable in Cedar Glen?

Cedar Glen sits in the San Bernardino Mountains near Lake Arrowhead, where wells are drilled into fractured granitic hard rock rather than a deep sandy aquifer. Water moves through cracks and fractures in the bedrock, so a well's output depends heavily on how many water-bearing fractures the borehole happens to intersect. That is why one parcel can have a strong well and the property next door a weak one, and why a recovery test is essential to understanding what a mountain well can really sustain.

Do I need freeze protection for my Cedar Glen wellhead?

Yes. At roughly 5,300 feet of elevation, Cedar Glen sees hard winter freezes, and exposed wellheads, pressure tanks, pipes, and pressure switches can freeze and crack if they are not protected. Part of our inspection is checking insulation, heat tape, and enclosure condition so your system keeps running through winter. Freeze damage is one of the most common and most preventable cold-weather failures we see in the Lake Arrowhead area.

How important is a recovery test on a mountain well?

It is critical in fractured hard-rock country like Cedar Glen. Because these wells rely on slow inflow through rock fractures, we measure the static water level, draw the well down, and time how quickly it recovers. A well that recovers slowly may pump fine for short bursts but run short during peak household demand. The recovery test is the single best measure of a Cedar Glen well's true sustainable yield.

How much does a well inspection cost in Cedar Glen?

A standard well inspection runs $150 to $400 depending on scope and access, which can be tighter on steep mountain lots. Water testing adds $100 to $300 depending on the panel, and a flow and yield test can be added when a buyer or lender needs documented gallons-per-minute and recovery data. If the visit becomes a diagnostic troubleshooting call, the $125 diagnostic fee is credited toward any repair we perform.

Should I test the water on a Cedar Glen well?

We recommend it on every inspection. We test for total coliform and E. coli bacteria, which can enter through a compromised wellhead or casing, and screen for minerals. Fractured granitic groundwater in the San Bernardino Mountains is often reasonably good quality, but conditions vary well to well, and freeze-damaged or aging casings can create pathways for contamination that only a lab test will catch.
