San Diego County's Well Drilling Experts

San Diego County encompasses over 4,500 square miles of diverse terrain—from coastal bluffs to fertile valleys to rugged mountains to desert floor. Southern California Well Service has drilled wells across this entire landscape since 2008, with our main office in Ramona at the heart of the county's rural backcountry.

Whether you're developing an estate in Rancho Santa Fe, building a ranch in Valley Center, homesteading in Julian, or establishing an off-grid retreat in Borrego Springs, we provide complete well drilling services tailored to your location's specific geology and water needs.

San Diego County Regions We Serve

North County Inland

Communities: Ramona, Valley Center, Fallbrook, Bonsall, Escondido, San Marcos, Poway

The inland valleys of North County feature decomposed granite and alluvial soils with generally favorable drilling conditions. This is our home territory—we know every neighborhood's geology.

  • Typical depths: 150-400 feet
  • Good water quality with some hardness
  • Strong agricultural history (avocados, citrus, horses)
  • Mix of established ranches and new development

Mountain Communities

Communities: Julian, Santa Ysabel, Wynola, Pine Valley, Mount Laguna, Palomar Mountain, Warner Springs

San Diego's mountains require specialized drilling through fractured granite bedrock. Water follows cracks and fault lines, making proper well siting critical.

  • Typical depths: 200-600+ feet
  • Excellent water quality when fractures are hit
  • Hard rock drilling expertise required
  • Freeze protection needed at higher elevations

East County

Communities: Alpine, Jamul, Dulzura, Lakeside, El Cajon, Santee

East County ranges from urban-adjacent areas to truly rural backcountry. Drilling conditions vary from easy alluvial to challenging granite.

  • Typical depths: 150-450 feet
  • Variable geology by location
  • Many agricultural and equestrian properties
  • Fire-conscious water system design

North County Coastal

Communities: Rancho Santa Fe, Del Mar, Encinitas, Carlsbad, Oceanside

Coastal areas have unique considerations including potential saltwater intrusion near the ocean. Site-specific evaluation is essential.

  • Typical depths: 100-300 feet
  • Saltwater intrusion risk near coast
  • Estate properties with high water demands
  • Careful site selection required

Desert (Anza-Borrego)

Communities: Borrego Springs, Shelter Valley, Ranchita, Ocotillo Wells

The desert region of San Diego County requires deep drilling and often water treatment. We're experienced in these challenging conditions.

  • Typical depths: 300-700+ feet
  • Water quality varies—treatment often needed
  • Off-grid solar systems common
  • Extreme temperature considerations

San Diego County Well Permits

All new wells require permits through the San Diego County Department of Environmental Health:

Requirements

  • Application with site plan
  • Setback verification:
    • 50 feet from septic tank
    • 100 feet from leach lines
    • Minimum distance from property lines
  • Licensed C-57 contractor
  • Well completion report

Permit Timeline

  • Application: 1-2 days
  • Review & approval: 2-4 weeks
  • Drilling: 1-5 days depending on depth
  • Final inspection: 1-2 weeks

SCWS handles all permitting as part of our turnkey service.

Frequently Asked Questions

How deep are wells in San Diego County?

Depths vary dramatically by region. Coastal areas typically need 100-300 feet. Inland valleys (Ramona, Valley Center) range 150-400 feet. Mountain communities (Julian, Palomar) may require 200-600+ feet through fractured granite. Desert areas (Borrego) can exceed 500 feet. We review area well logs to estimate depth for your property.

How much does a well cost in San Diego County?

Complete systems typically cost $20,000-$90,000+ depending on depth and geology. Softer formations cost $45-75/foot; hard granite runs $80-150/foot. We provide free site evaluations and detailed quotes.

Do I need a permit?

Yes. San Diego County requires permits through the Department of Environmental Health. We handle all permitting as part of our complete service.

What areas do you serve?

All of San Diego County including Ramona, Julian, Valley Center, Fallbrook, Poway, Alpine, Jamul, Lakeside, Escondido, Bonsall, Borrego Springs, Warner Springs, and every rural community. Our office is in Ramona.

How long does it take to drill a well?

Active drilling takes 1-5 days depending on depth and geology. Permit processing adds 2-4 weeks. Complete project timeline from site visit to flowing water is usually 4-8 weeks.

Is San Diego County well water safe?

Generally yes. Most areas produce good quality water suitable for all uses. Some locations have elevated hardness, iron, or other minerals requiring treatment. We test every well and recommend appropriate treatment.
