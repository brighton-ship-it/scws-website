Well Drilling in Mira Mesa

Mira Mesa is one of San Diego's largest suburban communities, served primarily by City of San Diego municipal water. Unlike rural areas where private wells are the only water source, well drilling in Mira Mesa serves specific purposes — commercial operations, large-scale irrigation, and backup water systems.

Southern California Well Service provides well drilling for commercial and irrigation applications throughout Mira Mesa and the greater Miramar/Sorrento Valley area. We help businesses and property owners evaluate whether a private well makes sense for their needs.

Permits & Regulations

Well drilling in Mira Mesa requires permits from the San Diego County Department of Environmental Health. Urban areas have additional considerations:

Permit Requirements

  • Well construction permit — Required before drilling
  • Site plan — Showing well location, setbacks
  • Setback compliance — From septic (if any), buildings, property lines
  • Contamination assessment — May be required near industrial sites
  • Intended use declaration — Irrigation, commercial, potable

City of San Diego Coordination

For properties in the City of San Diego:

  • May need city approval for wells in certain zones
  • Cross-connection prevention required if municipal supply also used
  • Annual backflow testing typically required

Environmental Considerations

Near Miramar industrial areas or former military facilities:

  • Groundwater contamination screening may be required
  • Some areas have restricted groundwater use
  • We advise on site-specific requirements during evaluation

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I drill a well in Mira Mesa?

Well drilling in urban Mira Mesa is possible but limited. Most residential properties use City of San Diego water. However, commercial properties, larger lots, and those seeking irrigation or backup water may qualify for well permits.

How deep are wells in the Mira Mesa area?

Wells in the greater Mira Mesa/Miramar area typically range from 200-400 feet. The mesa formations consist of sedimentary layers with variable water-bearing zones.

What is the cost of a well in Mira Mesa?

Commercial and irrigation wells in the Mira Mesa area typically cost $25,000-$60,000 depending on depth, capacity requirements, and site conditions.

Is the water quality good for irrigation?

Yes, most wells in the area produce water suitable for irrigation. TDS levels that might not meet drinking water preferences are typically fine for landscape use.

Do you help with permits in San Diego?

Yes, we handle the entire permit process including application preparation, coordination with County DEH, and required inspections.
