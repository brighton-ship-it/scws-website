Well Pump Runs But No Water: 9 Causes & Fixes

Updated February 2026 | By Southern California Well Service

Quick Answer: If your pump motor runs but no water comes out, common causes are: well water level dropped, broken/clogged drop pipe, failed check valve, worn impellers, air lock in pump, or leak in the system. First check: is the well dry? Then check for obvious leaks. Most other causes require professional diagnosis.

9 Common Causes

1. Low Water Level in Well

The most common cause, especially during drought or high-usage periods. The pump is below the water level and pulling air instead of water.

  • Signs: Worked fine before, sputtering/air, seasonal pattern
  • Fix: Wait for recovery, reduce usage, or lower pump

2. Broken or Disconnected Drop Pipe

The pipe connecting the pump to the surface can crack, corrode, or disconnect. Water pumps out but never reaches the surface.

  • Signs: Sudden loss of water, pump runs normally
  • Fix: Pull pump and inspect/replace pipe

3. Failed Check Valve

The check valve prevents water from draining back. If stuck closed, water can't flow up. If stuck open, water drains back between cycles.

  • Signs: Pump has to "catch up" every time, no immediate water
  • Fix: Replace check valve

4. Worn Pump Impellers

Impellers spin to move water. Over time they wear, especially in sandy water. The motor runs but can't move water efficiently.

  • Signs: Gradual decline in pressure/flow over months
  • Fix: Replace pump

5. Air Lock

Air trapped in the pump or pipes prevents water flow. More common after the system was opened or well ran dry.

  • Signs: Problem started after repairs or running dry
  • Fix: Prime pump, bleed air from system

6. Clogged Pressure Tank

Sediment or scale can block the tank inlet, preventing water from reaching the house.

  • Signs: Pump builds pressure but water barely trickles
  • Fix: Flush or replace tank

7. Broken Foot Valve (Jet Pumps)

For above-ground jet pumps, the foot valve at the bottom of the suction pipe keeps it primed. If it fails, the pump loses prime.

  • Signs: Pump runs, pressure builds then drops, jet pump
  • Fix: Replace foot valve, re-prime

8. Major Leak in System

A significant leak can prevent pressure from building even though water is being pumped.

  • Signs: Wet areas, running water sounds, pump never stops
  • Fix: Find and repair leak

9. Pump Motor Spinning But Shaft Broken

Rare but possible—the motor runs but internal shaft connecting to impellers has broken.

  • Signs: Motor sounds different, no vibration, sudden failure
  • Fix: Replace pump

How to Diagnose

Step 1: Check Water Level

  1. If you know your well depth and static level, calculate
  2. Drop a weighted string down well to check
  3. Consider recent drought or heavy usage

Step 2: Check for Leaks

  1. Walk the property looking for wet spots
  2. Check all faucets, toilets, irrigation
  3. Listen for running water when nothing is on

Step 3: Check Pressure Readings

  • Pressure builds then drops = leak or check valve
  • Pressure never builds = pump issue or major leak
  • Pressure builds slowly = worn impellers or clog

Step 4: Check Amp Draw

For professionals: comparing actual amp draw to nameplate can indicate pump condition.

  • Low amps = not moving water (dry or broken shaft)
  • High amps = working hard (sand, blockage, worn bearings)
  • Normal amps = problem is downstream of pump

Fixes by Cause

Well Ran Dry

  • Turn off pump immediately (prevents damage)
  • Wait 24-48 hours for recovery
  • Reduce water usage
  • Long-term: lower pump, drill deeper, or hydrofracture

Check Valve Failure

  • Above-ground check valve: $150-$300 to replace
  • Down-hole check valve: $400-$800 (requires pulling pump)

Drop Pipe Break

  • Requires pulling pump
  • Replace damaged sections or entire pipe
  • Cost: $500-$2,000+ depending on depth

Worn Pump

  • Replace entire pump assembly
  • Cost: $1,500-$4,000 installed

Air Lock (Jet Pump)

  1. Turn off power
  2. Remove prime plug
  3. Fill pump casing with water
  4. Replace plug, restore power
  5. May need to repeat several times

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is my well pump running but no water coming out?

Most commonly: well water level dropped, drop pipe broke, check valve failed, or pump impellers are worn. The motor running doesn't mean water is moving.

Can a well pump run dry and still work?

Briefly, but it causes damage. Submersible pumps need water for cooling. Running dry overheats and damages seals. Turn off immediately if well runs dry.

How do I know if my well is dry?

Signs: sputtering/air from faucets, pressure drops quickly, problem worse during peak usage. Measure water level or call a professional to check.

Why would a well suddenly stop producing water?

Sudden stop usually means: pipe break, check valve stuck, or well ran dry. Gradual decline suggests worn pump or slowly dropping water level.

How long should I wait if my well runs dry?

At least 24-48 hours minimum. Some wells recover in hours, others take days or weeks. Monitor recovery and consider long-term solutions if it happens often.
