What to Check First

A short list of safe checks may bring your water back or help us diagnose the issue more quickly. Take them in order, and never open the well cap or handle wet electrical components yourself:

  1. Check the breaker for your well pump. If it has tripped, switch it fully off and then back on a single time.
  2. Read the pressure gauge. Zero pressure points to a pump or supply problem; normal pressure with no flow suggests a different cause.
  3. Tap the pressure tank. A hollow-sounding top and solid bottom is normal; a tank that feels heavy all over is likely waterlogged.
  4. Listen at the pressure switch for a click or hum when water is demanded. Silence can indicate a failed switch.
  5. Verify the grid is on in your area, since an outage or shutoff can stop your pump even if some circuits still work.

What to Do While You Wait

After you call, treat the water in your pressure tank and any stored containers as a reserve. Hold off on laundry, the dishwasher, and repeated flushing. If you suspect a leak, close the supply valve at the tank to protect your remaining water and to keep the wellhead area from flooding. Filling a few jugs for drinking and basic needs is wise while our truck heads your way. On a hot Lake Elsinore afternoon, a small water reserve makes the wait much easier on everyone in the household.

The community's position right against the Cleveland National Forest also means wildfire season brings real considerations for well owners. Public-safety power shutoffs are designed to reduce fire risk, but they take well pumps offline along with everything else. Households that depend on a well are wise to keep a water reserve during fire season and to make sure their system is in good shape before the dry months arrive. We are happy to inspect and advise so you are not caught off guard.
