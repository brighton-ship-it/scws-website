What Makes Well Water Hard?

Hard water contains high concentrations of dissolved calcium and magnesium, picked up as groundwater moves through limestone, chalk, and other mineral-rich rock formations. Wells in areas with these geological features commonly produce hard to very hard water. The hardness level depends on the minerals present in your specific aquifer and how long water remains in contact with rock before reaching your well.

Problems Caused by Hard Water

Scale buildup in pipes restricts water flow and reduces pressure over time. Water heaters work harder and fail sooner when scale coats heating elements. Dishwashers and washing machines experience reduced efficiency and shorter lifespans. Soap doesn't lather well, leaving residue on skin, hair, and laundry. White crusty deposits form on faucets, showerheads, and fixtures.

Testing Water Hardness

A professional water test measures hardness in grains per gallon (gpg) or milligrams per liter (mg/L). Soft water is under 1 gpg, slightly hard is 1-3.5 gpg, moderately hard is 3.5-7 gpg, hard is 7-10.5 gpg, and very hard exceeds 10.5 gpg. Testing also reveals iron content, which affects softener selection and sizing.

Choosing the Right Water Softener

Salt-based ion exchange softeners are most effective for very hard water. Size your softener based on water hardness and daily household usage—undersized units regenerate too frequently and waste salt. Salt-free conditioners don't actually remove hardness but prevent scale from adhering to surfaces. For well water with iron, choose a softener rated for iron removal or install a separate iron filter upstream.

Frequently Asked Questions

How hard is too hard for well water?

Water over 7 grains per gallon (gpg) or 120 mg/L is considered hard and will cause noticeable problems. Very hard water (over 10.5 gpg) accelerates scale buildup significantly and warrants treatment.

Do water softeners remove other contaminants?

Standard water softeners only remove hardness minerals (calcium and magnesium) through ion exchange. They don't remove bacteria, nitrates, iron, or other contaminants. However, some systems combine softening with other treatment methods.

How often should I add salt to my water softener?

Most households need to add salt every 6-8 weeks, but this varies based on water hardness, usage, and softener size. Check your brine tank monthly and keep salt level at least one-quarter full for optimal performance.
