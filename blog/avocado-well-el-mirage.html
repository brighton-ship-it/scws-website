Avocado Water Demands

Avocados are thirsty trees:

  • Per acre: 4-6 acre-feet per year
  • Critical periods: Fruit set and sizing

A reliable well is essential for profitable avocado production in San Bernardino County.

Chloride Sensitivity

Avocados are highly sensitive to chloride in irrigation water. If your El Mirage well has elevated chloride:

  • Blending with lower-chloride water source
  • Leaching irrigation to flush salts
  • Rootstock selection for salt tolerance
  • Regular soil and leaf testing

Desert Well Service for El Mirage

Let us be honest about the setting. El Mirage is a high-desert community in San Bernardino County, spread around the wide, flat El Mirage Dry Lake in the Mojave's Victor Valley, near Adelanto and Phelan. This is a land of scorching summers, hard winter freezes, single-digit annual rainfall and alkaline desert soils. It is wonderful country for off-road recreation, dry-lake land sailing and wide-open living, but it is not avocado country, and no honest contractor would tell you otherwise. Avocados are frost- and salt-sensitive subtropical trees that simply will not survive the El Mirage climate as a crop. What does matter out here is a dependable desert well.

How a Desert Well System Works at El Mirage

A typical El Mirage well uses a submersible pump set deep in the casing, frequently a few hundred feet or more down, because the regional Mojave water table sits well below the surface. Water is lifted to a storage tank, which is especially valuable in the desert where you want a buffer against pump runtime and power interruptions, then a pressure tank or constant-pressure system delivers it to the house, livestock, and any drought-tolerant landscaping. Storage and a robust pump are not luxuries here; they are how you stay ahead of heat and distance from service.

Desert Realities and Water Quality

Two things define groundwater in this part of the high desert. First, depth: the water table is deep and has declined in places under long-term pumping, so wells are often substantial and yield varies widely across the basin. Second, chemistry: desert groundwater commonly carries elevated total dissolved solids and hardness, and sometimes naturally occurring constituents such as fluoride or arsenic. Testing is important for drinking-water safety and for any irrigation, and treatment is frequently the right call. We test and recommend systems sized to the actual result.

Common Local Scenarios and Causes

  • Deep static water levels and the larger pumps and longer drop pipe they require.
  • Declining yield as desert water tables drop, sometimes calling for deepening or hydrofracturing.
  • High TDS and hardness scaling equipment and stressing plants.
  • Heat and dust stressing wellheads, wiring and surface equipment.
  • Pressure tank waterlogging causing short-cycling and premature pump failure.

What to Check Before You Call

Confirm the well breaker has not tripped, since desert power can be inconsistent. Read the pressure gauge for a pinned or jumping needle. Listen for rapid pump cycling that signals a failed tank. Check that storage tanks are filling. Note water color, odor and staining. Above all, do not keep restarting a pump that will not build pressure, because in a deep desert well a dry-running motor can be ruined in minutes and is costly to pull and replace.

When to Call a Professional

Deep desert wells are not a DIY proposition. Anything below the wellhead or in the electrical control box, and any water-treatment decision, belongs to a licensed crew with the rigging to handle hundreds of feet of pipe and pump. If your water has stopped, your pressure has collapsed, your pump runs nonstop, or your yield is fading, call us.

Designing a Desert Water System That Lasts

A smart El Mirage system is built around the realities of heat, depth and distance. We size the submersible pump to the true lift and demand, then route water into ample storage so the household, any livestock and drought-tolerant landscaping ride through pump cycles and power blips without running dry. Seasonal Care for Deep Desert Wells

Desert wells reward vigilance. Before the brutal summer, check the pump's amp draw, the pressure tank's air charge, and storage tank condition, and confirm controls handle the heat. Through summer, watch for any drop in yield that signals a declining water table, and keep an eye on equipment baking in the sun. As temperatures swing into winter, guard wellheads and exposed lines against the hard freezes the high desert delivers. Across all of it, do not ride a struggling pump; a deep desert motor pulled and replaced is among the costliest repairs we do, so catching trouble early is the cheapest insurance a desert owner can buy. We are happy to set up a simple seasonal check so your El Mirage well keeps serving you through every extreme the Mojave throws at it.
