Our Well Inspection Services

Murrieta sits at the heart of the Murrieta-Temecula groundwater basin, one of the largest and most productive basins in the San Diego hydrologic region. Homes on the wine-country fringes toward De Luz, the rural parcels along Murrieta Creek and Warm Springs Creek, and the horse properties out toward the Santa Rosa Plateau frequently depend on a private well rather than a Rancho California Water District or Western Municipal Water District connection. If you own one of these wells, or you are about to buy a home that has one, a professional inspection is the single best way to protect a five- and six-figure investment. Southern California Well Service is a licensed C-57 well-drilling contractor with more than 30 years serving Riverside County, and we perform well inspections across Murrieta every week.

A well is a complex system with parts you cannot see from the surface. The pump may be set several hundred feet down; the casing, the sanitary seal, and the water level are all hidden. Our inspection brings that hidden system into focus with measurements, testing, and a written report you can hand to a lender, a title company, or simply keep for your own records. We provide two main types of inspection in Murrieta: the pre-purchase (real-estate) inspection, ordered during escrow, and the annual maintenance inspection for owners who want to catch small problems before they become emergencies.

How a Murrieta Well Inspection Works

When our technician arrives at a Murrieta property, the inspection follows a consistent sequence so nothing is missed. We begin at the wellhead, then work outward to the pressure system, the electrical controls, and finally the water itself. A typical residential inspection takes one to two hours on site, with laboratory water results following a few days later when a potability test is included.

First we perform a visual survey of the wellhead and the surrounding area. We confirm the well cap is vermin-proof and properly seated, check that the casing extends the required distance above grade, and look for signs that surface water could pond around the head and carry contaminants down the annular space. This sanitary survey matters in Murrieta because many older parcels near the creeks were developed before current setback standards, and irrigation runoff from adjacent vineyards or pastures can compromise a poorly sealed well.

Next we measure the static water level (the resting level with the pump off) and then run the pump to record the pumping water level and the drawdown between the two. We conduct a timed flow test, measuring output in gallons per minute, and we watch how quickly the well recovers after the pump shuts off. These numbers tell us whether the well can keep up with the demands of the household, and whether production has declined since the well was drilled. We simultaneously check system pressure at the tank and the pressure switch cut-in and cut-out points.

What We Check in Murrieta Wells

A thorough Murrieta well inspection documents each of the following components:

  • Pump flow test (GPM): A timed measurement of how many gallons per minute the well delivers under load, compared against household demand.
  • Static and pumping water levels: The resting level and the level while pumping, which reveal drawdown, recovery rate, and long-term aquifer health.
  • System pressure: Verification that the pressure tank and switch maintain a steady, correct range without short cycling.
  • Wellhead, cap, and sanitary seal: Inspection for a secure, sealed cap and a sound seal that keeps surface contaminants out of the borehole.
  • Casing condition: Assessment of the visible casing for corrosion, cracks, or separation, especially important in the granitic and alluvial formations beneath Murrieta.
  • Pressure tank: A check of the tank's air charge and bladder, since a waterlogged tank is one of the most common causes of pump failure.
  • Electrical and controls: Inspection of the pump wiring, breaker, control box, and any pressure or float switches for safe, code-compliant operation.
  • Water quality and bacteria testing: A screen for total coliform and E. coli plus a mineral panel that identifies hardness, iron, manganese, nitrates, and total dissolved solids.

Water quality deserves special attention in Murrieta. Groundwater in the Murrieta-Temecula basin is largely sodium and calcium bicarbonate in character, which typically produces moderately hard water and scale on fixtures and appliances. Nitrate can appear in agricultural areas, and some wells show elevated iron or manganese. Our lab screen turns these unknowns into hard numbers so you can make an informed decision about treatment rather than guessing.

Common Murrieta Well Scenarios

Because Murrieta wells vary widely in depth and age, the problems we uncover vary too. A few scenarios come up again and again.

The pre-closing surprise. A buyer is a week from closing on a rural home near Clinton Keith Road when the listing agent mentions the well "has always been fine." Our inspection finds a static level far lower than expected and a flow rate that barely supports a single bathroom. That single report gives the buyer the leverage to renegotiate or to budget for a pump lowering before they take ownership.

The waterlogged pressure tank. An owner on the east side of Murrieta calls because the pump is running constantly and the water hammers when a faucet opens. During the inspection we find the pressure tank has lost its air charge, causing the pump to short cycle. Left alone, that cycling would burn out the pump motor within months. Replacing the tank is far cheaper than replacing the pump.

The slow decline. A homeowner who has skipped inspections for years notices the water is not what it used to be. A flow test shows production has dropped steadily as the water table fluctuated through recent dry and wet cycles. Catching this early lets us discuss options, from lowering the pump to well rehabilitation, before the household runs dry.

When to Call a Professional

Some situations call for an inspection right away rather than waiting for the annual visit. Call us if you notice any of the following in your Murrieta home:

  • A drop in water pressure or flow, or water that sputters and spits air from the taps.
  • The pump running constantly or cycling on and off every few seconds.
  • Cloudy, sandy, or discolored water, or a sudden change in taste or smell.
  • A well that has never been tested and is about to serve a real-estate transaction.
  • Any positive bacteria result on a home test kit.

Surface tasks such as resetting a breaker or adjusting a pressure switch are safe for a handy owner. Anything that requires pulling the pump or entering the casing should be left to a licensed contractor. The equipment weighs hundreds of pounds, the electrical connections are high-voltage, and a dropped pump can wreck a well beyond economical repair.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a well inspection cost in Murrieta?

A standard well inspection in Murrieta typically runs $150 to $400 depending on the depth of the well and the scope of testing requested. A focused diagnostic visit is $125 and is credited toward any repair we perform. Pre-purchase inspections that include laboratory water quality and bacteria testing fall at the higher end because of lab fees and the detailed written report lenders require.

Do I need a well inspection to buy a home in Murrieta?

While Riverside County does not mandate a well inspection for every sale, most lenders and title companies require a satisfactory well report and a potability (bacteria) test before funding a loan on a property served by a private well. Even for cash purchases, a pre-purchase inspection is the only way to verify flow rate, water level, and equipment condition before you commit.

How often should I have my Murrieta well inspected?

We recommend an annual inspection for wells that supply a household, and a bacteria test at least once a year. Wells near Murrieta Creek or in areas with seasonal water-table swings benefit from twice-yearly water level checks so declining production is caught early.

What is checked during a well inspection?

A full inspection covers the wellhead, cap and sanitary seal, casing, static and pumping water levels, a pump flow test measured in gallons per minute, system pressure, the pressure tank and switch, the electrical controls, and a water quality screen. We deliver a written report with photos and recommendations.

Why is my Murrieta well water hard or high in minerals?

Groundwater in the Murrieta-Temecula basin is largely sodium and calcium bicarbonate in character, which produces moderately hard water and scale. A water quality screen during the inspection identifies hardness, iron, and other minerals so you can decide whether a softener or filtration system makes sense.

Can you inspect a well the same day I call?

For urgent situations such as a well that has stopped producing before a scheduled closing, we offer same-day service across Murrieta and Riverside County. Routine pre-purchase and annual inspections are usually scheduled within a few business days.
