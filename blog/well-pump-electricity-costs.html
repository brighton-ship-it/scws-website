Understanding Pump Power Consumption

Well pump electricity use depends on motor size (horsepower), efficiency rating, and run time. A 1 HP motor draws approximately 1,000 watts while running. However, startup draws 3-5 times more power for a few seconds. Frequent cycling (starting and stopping) significantly increases total consumption compared to longer, steadier run times.

Calculating Your Operating Costs

To estimate monthly costs: multiply your pump's wattage by average daily run hours, then by 30 days, divide by 1,000 for kilowatt-hours, and multiply by your electricity rate. For example: 1,000 watts × 2 hours × 30 days ÷ 1,000 × $0.30 = $18/month. Your actual run time varies with household water use—typically 1-3 hours daily for most families.

Factors That Increase Operating Costs

Short cycling (pump turning on and off frequently) wastes energy due to high startup current. Causes include a waterlogged pressure tank, undersized tank, leaking check valve, or plumbing leaks. A failing pump that can't maintain pressure will also run longer than necessary, increasing both electricity use and wear.

Energy-Efficient Upgrades to Consider

Constant pressure systems use variable speed drives to match pump output to demand, reducing cycling and saving 30-50% on electricity. Larger pressure tanks allow the pump to run longer per cycle but less frequently, improving efficiency. If your pump is over 15 years old, newer motors are significantly more efficient and may pay for themselves in energy savings.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to run a well pump?

A typical 1 HP residential well pump uses about 1-1.5 kWh per hour of operation. At California electricity rates (averaging $0.25-0.35/kWh), running a pump for 2 hours daily costs $15-30 monthly. Actual costs vary based on pump efficiency, well depth, and water usage patterns.

Do well pumps use a lot of electricity?

Well pumps are efficient compared to many alternatives. A properly sized system running only when needed typically accounts for 5-10% of a home's electricity use. However, problems like leaks, failing check valves, or undersized pressure tanks can cause excessive cycling that significantly increases energy costs.

How can I reduce my well pump electricity costs?

Ensure your pressure tank is properly sized and charged, fix any leaks promptly, and install a constant pressure system to reduce motor startup surges. Also check that your pump is properly sized—an oversized pump wastes energy while an undersized one runs too long.
