Why Is My Well Water Sputtering?

Updated February 2026 • By SCWS

Sputtering, spitting, or air-mixed water from your faucets is more than annoying—it indicates a problem with your well system. Air shouldn't be in your water lines. Finding and fixing the source prevents pump damage and restores normal operation.

6 Common Causes of Sputtering Well Water

1. Low Water Level in Well

Most common cause. If the water level in your well has dropped below the pump intake, the pump draws a mix of air and water.

Signs: Sputtering worse during heavy use or drought. May clear up after letting well rest overnight.

Solution: Lower pump deeper in well, or wait for water table to recover.

2. Failing Check Valve

Check valves prevent water from draining back into the well when the pump stops. A failed valve allows water to fall back, letting air into the system.

Signs: Sputtering when pump first starts. Pump runs longer than normal to build pressure.

Solution: Replace check valve ($300-$600 installed).

3. Waterlogged Pressure Tank

When a pressure tank's bladder fails, it loses its air cushion and becomes waterlogged. This causes rapid pump cycling and can introduce air.

Signs: Pump cycles on/off rapidly (every few seconds). Pressure fluctuates.

Solution: Replace pressure tank ($400-$1,200 installed).

4. Leak in Drop Pipe

The drop pipe connects your pump to the surface. A crack or loose fitting above the water line lets air into the system.

Signs: Consistent air in water. May hear gurgling in well.

Solution: Pull pump and inspect/repair drop pipe connections.

5. Pump Set Too Shallow

If your pump was installed near the water surface, normal water level fluctuations can expose the intake to air.

Signs: Sputtering varies with seasons or usage patterns.

Solution: Lower pump to safe depth below water level.

6. Cracked Well Casing

Cracks in the well casing above water level can allow air infiltration, especially if there's a vacuum effect when pumping.

Signs: May also see sediment or surface water contamination.

Solution: Casing liner or repair (can be complex).

Temporary vs. Ongoing Sputtering

Temporary sputtering after the system has been off (pump replacement, power outage) is normal—air is being purged. Run water until clear.

Ongoing sputtering indicates a problem that needs diagnosis and repair.

Why This Matters

Beyond the annoyance, air in your well system can:

  • Damage pump seals and bearings
  • Reduce pump efficiency and lifespan
  • Cause pressure fluctuations throughout house
  • Indicate well yield problems that will worsen
