How New-Well Drilling Works

A turnkey well is an engineered project that moves through a predictable series of stages. Understanding the sequence helps you know what to expect and where your investment goes.

  1. Site assessment and well-log research — We evaluate your parcel, confirm setbacks from septic systems and property lines, and pull California Department of Water Resources completion reports for neighboring wells to estimate depth, yield, and the formations we'll drill through.
  2. Permitting — We prepare and submit the Riverside County well permit and coordinate the required inspections. You never have to navigate the county paperwork yourself.
  3. Drilling the borehole — Our rotary rig drills the hole. On the Santa Ana valley floor we typically use mud rotary through the sands and gravels; where firmer bedrock appears at the valley margins we switch to air rotary or a down-hole hammer.
  4. Casing and sanitary seal — We install casing (usually PVC, with steel surface casing where conditions call for it) and place a grout seal at the top to keep surface water and contaminants out of your aquifer.
  5. Gravel pack and development — We set a graded gravel pack around the screen, then surge and pump the well to clear fines and open up the aquifer so it produces at full capacity.
  6. Pump and pressure system — We size and install the submersible pump, drop pipe, wiring, control box, pressure tank, and switch, then test flow and pressure so the system is complete the day we finish.

Well Drilling in Home Gardens

Home Gardens properties in Riverside County sit on the alluvial deposits of the Santa Ana River basin — layered sands, gravels, and clays washed down from the surrounding mountains over thousands of years. This is fundamentally different, and generally friendlier, drilling ground than the hard granite of the higher Peninsular Ranges to the east. These alluvial sediments store water in their pore spaces, which means productive water-bearing zones are often reached without extreme depths, and drilling progresses relatively quickly. Local geology still shapes everything from drilling depth and cost to water quality and pump selection, so a proper site assessment is always the first step.

Drilling Conditions in Home Gardens

Well drilling in Home Gardens typically encounters the unconsolidated alluvium of the Santa Ana River valley, with sand and gravel aquifers interbedded with clay lenses. Most wells fall in the 150-to-400-foot range, and many can be completed in one to three days under normal conditions. The moderate depths and softer formations tend to keep drilling costs reasonable compared with hard-rock sites, though shifting sand and clay layers require careful casing and screen placement to keep the well clean and productive.

Riverside County requires a well permit from the Department of Environmental Health before drilling can begin. The permit process typically takes two to three weeks. We handle the entire permit process for Home Gardens properties, including the application, inspections, and the state well completion report.

What to Check Before You Drill

  • Setbacks — Riverside County mandates minimum distances from septic tanks, leach fields, and property lines; we verify these during the assessment.
  • Rig access — A drilling rig needs a clear path and a level pad, which matters on the compact lots common in Home Gardens.
  • Neighboring well data — Nearby completion reports are the single best predictor of your likely depth and yield.
  • Electrical service — The pump needs adequate power; older properties may require a panel upgrade.
  • Water quality goals — Alluvial water can carry hardness, iron, or nitrates, so we test and recommend treatment only if your results call for it.

When to Call a Pro

New-well drilling is licensed-contractor work: it involves permits, heavy equipment, sanitary sealing, and electrical connections, and errors are costly to correct. If an existing well is losing pressure, producing sand or cloudy water, spitting air from the faucets, or running dry earlier each summer, don't wait for a total no-water emergency. As a licensed C-57 contractor with same-day emergency response, we can evaluate the problem and tell you honestly whether a repair or a new well makes more sense.

Well Drilling Costs

A complete turnkey well in Home Gardens — drilling, casing, development, submersible pump, pressure tank, and switch — typically totals $18,000 to $42,000. Because Home Gardens sits on relatively shallow alluvial aquifers, many projects fall toward the lower and middle of that range. Total depth is the biggest single cost driver, since both footage drilled and casing scale with it.

Individual components can also be serviced on their own. For reference, a pressure switch runs $150-$350, a pressure tank $600-$1,500, a submersible pump $2,500-$5,500, and a control box or capacitor $400-$900. If your water needs conditioning, sediment filtration is $300-$900, a water softener $1,500-$3,500, UV disinfection $800-$1,800, and a reverse-osmosis drinking system $300-$1,200. For strong, steady irrigation pressure across a hobby farm, a constant-pressure booster runs $2,000-$4,500. A standalone well inspection is $150-$400, and our $125 diagnostic fee is credited toward any repair we perform.

Frequently Asked Questions

How deep do wells need to be drilled in Home Gardens?

Well depth in Home Gardens typically ranges from 150 to 400 feet depending on local geology and groundwater levels. Because Home Gardens sits on the alluvial floor of the Santa Ana River valley, many wells reach productive sand-and-gravel aquifers on the shallower end. Our team conducts a site assessment to determine the optimal depth before drilling begins.

How long does it take to drill a new well?

Most residential wells take 1-3 days to drill, depending on depth and rock conditions. The complete process including pump installation and plumbing typically takes 3-5 business days, with Riverside County permitting adding a couple of weeks on the front end.

How much does well drilling cost in Home Gardens?

A complete turnkey well typically totals $18,000-$42,000 depending on depth, casing requirements, and equipment needed, with many Home Gardens wells landing toward the lower end thanks to shallower alluvial aquifers. We provide free estimates before any work begins.

Do I need a permit to drill a well in Home Gardens?

Yes. Riverside County requires a permit from the Department of Environmental Health for every new well. We handle the entire application, inspections, and the state well completion report on your behalf.

Will my Home Gardens well water need treatment?

Possibly. Alluvial groundwater in the Santa Ana valley can carry hardness, iron, or nitrates. We test the water after completion and can size sediment filtration, a softener, UV disinfection, or reverse osmosis only if your specific results call for it.

Can you drill an irrigation well for my hobby farm?

Absolutely. Many Home Gardens parcels run gardens, citrus, and small livestock. We design the well, pump, storage, and any booster system around your real irrigation demand so household use is never starved for pressure.
