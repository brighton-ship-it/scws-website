Groundwater and Geology Around Yermo

Yermo sits in the High Desert of San Bernardino County, just northeast of Barstow and adjacent to Daggett, at roughly 1,900 feet of elevation. It lies in the Lower Mojave River Valley Groundwater Basin, where the Mojave River flows underground through desert alluvium most of the year. Recharge here is limited and episodic, coming mainly from intermittent winter streamflow in the Mojave River and, since 2003, from managed imports of California State Water Project water delivered to recharge basins near Daggett and Yermo.

The subsurface is dominated by unconsolidated sand, gravel, and clay of the river valley, underlain by older alluvium and, at depth, consolidated rock. Studies near the Marine Corps Logistics Base Yermo Annex have mapped two main aquifer systems and several faults that segment the basin and locally control how water moves and where it collects. Because of that faulting and the long, evaporation-driven residence time of water in this lower basin, both water levels and water chemistry vary from parcel to parcel. We review the local well record for your site before committing to a depth.

The Turnkey New-Well Process

Every new well we drill in Yermo follows the same disciplined sequence:

  1. Site evaluation. We assess access, review nearby DWR well logs, and choose a location that meets county setbacks from septic systems, structures, and property lines.
  2. Permitting. We file the San Bernardino County well-construction permit and coordinate with the Mojave Water Agency watermaster on basin production.
  3. Drilling. Our rotary rig advances through the valley alluvium; near Yermo the upper section is typically sand, gravel, and clay grading into firmer older deposits. Most residential wells reach depth in one to three days.
  4. Casing and gravel pack. We install steel or PVC casing with screen across the producing zone and surround it with a graded gravel pack to hold back fines and improve yield.
  5. Development. We surge and pump the well to draw out fine sand and drilling mud so the water clears and the well performs.
  6. Pump and startup. We size and set a submersible pump, connect drop pipe and wire, install the pressure tank, switch, and wellhead, then verify flow and pressure.

Depth and Cost Expectations in Yermo

Yermo wells typically finish between roughly 150 and 400 feet, with a local average close to 245 feet; outlying parcels toward the basin margins can go considerably deeper. A complete turnkey well generally costs $18,000 to $42,000, driven by final depth, casing diameter and material, formation hardness, and the pump and pressure equipment selected. A standalone well inspection is $150-$400, and our $125 diagnostic fee is credited toward any work we do. Because the deeper basin here can mean higher pumping lift, we size pumps carefully so you are not overspending on energy for the life of the well.

One thing that sets Yermo apart from wetter parts of California is how much the timing of recharge matters. In many years the Mojave River delivers almost no natural inflow, and the basin leans on imported State Water Project deliveries to hold water levels steady. For a well owner, that reinforces two practical points: drill deep enough to stay below the water table through dry cycles, and size the pump for the possibility of increased lift over the life of the well. We factor both into the design so your Yermo well keeps producing even in the leaner years.

Common Well Scenarios in Yermo

  • New wells for rural homesteads and small operations on parcels off Yermo Road and the Interstate 15 corridor that have never had a water supply.
  • Deepening or replacing aging wells that have lost production as regional water levels declined between recharge events.
  • Pump and control failures where a submersible pump, capacitor, or pressure tank replacement restores service without redrilling.
  • Water treatment for high TDS and hardness that are common in this evaporation-influenced lower basin, addressed with softening, filtration, UV, or reverse osmosis.

What to Check Before Drilling in Yermo

  • Basin water rights: confirm your Mojave Basin Area production allocation before sizing the well for irrigation or livestock.
  • Setbacks and easements: verify distances from septic, utility easements, and railroad or highway right-of-way common in the Yermo corridor.
  • Rig access: ensure a firm path in, since soft blow-sand can bog down heavy equipment.
  • Water test plan: budget for a post-completion test and likely treatment given local mineralization.
  • Power supply: confirm adequate electrical service for the pump, or plan for a solar or generator arrangement on remote parcels.

Frequently Asked Questions

How deep do wells go in Yermo?

Wells around Yermo commonly finish in the 150 to 400 foot range, with a local average near 245 feet, though recorded depths in the surrounding Lower Mojave River Valley run from very shallow floodplain wells to 800 feet on the outer basin margins. Depth to water depends heavily on how close you are to the Mojave River channel and to the Waterman and Calico fault zones that segment this part of the basin. We check nearby well-completion records for your exact location before quoting a depth.

Is Yermo in San Bernardino County, and do I need a permit?

Yes. Yermo is an unincorporated High Desert community in San Bernardino County, just northeast of Barstow near Daggett. A well construction permit is required through San Bernardino County Environmental Health, and the finished well must be reported to the California Department of Water Resources. Yermo also lies within the adjudicated Mojave Basin Area, so groundwater production is administered by the Mojave Water Agency watermaster. We handle the full permit and reporting process for you.

What does a new well cost in Yermo?

A complete turnkey well in Yermo, drilling through pump startup, typically costs between $18,000 and $42,000. At Yermo's usual depths the drilling portion often lands in the low-to-mid teens of thousands, and the balance is casing, gravel pack, development, and the pump and pressure system. Total cost hinges on depth, casing size and material, formation hardness, and equipment. We provide a written estimate up front, and the $125 diagnostic fee is credited toward the job.

Why is water quality a concern near Yermo?

The Lower Mojave River Valley near Yermo and Daggett has long groundwater residence times and high desert evaporation, which tends to concentrate dissolved minerals. Many wells show elevated total dissolved solids and hardness, and some carry iron, manganese, or nitrate. After we complete a well we recommend a water test. Typical treatment costs are $300-$900 for sediment filtration, $1,500-$3,500 for a softener, $800-$1,800 for UV disinfection, and $300-$1,200 for a reverse-osmosis drinking-water unit.

How long does drilling take in Yermo?

Drilling a typical Yermo residential well takes one to three days once the permit is approved. Setting casing, placing the gravel pack, developing the well, and installing the pump and pressure equipment usually adds another two to three days, so most projects deliver running water within about three to five working days of the rig arriving on site.

Can you fix low water pressure without drilling a new well?

Often, yes. Low pressure, sputtering faucets, or a pump that runs constantly usually point to the pump or pressure controls rather than the well itself. A submersible pump replacement runs $2,500-$5,500, a control box or capacitor is $400-$900, a pressure switch is $150-$350, and a pressure tank is $600-$1,500. If your home needs steadier pressure across many fixtures or long irrigation runs, a constant-pressure or booster system runs $2,000-$4,500. We always diagnose before recommending a new well.
