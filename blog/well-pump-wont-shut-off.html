Why Your Well Pump Won't Shut Off

A well pump that runs forever—never reaching cutoff pressure—is working overtime and wearing out fast. Your electric bill climbs while the pump heads toward failure. The good news: the causes are identifiable, and most are fixable without replacing the pump.

Common Causes

  • Waterlogged Pressure Tank: Without air cushion, the pump can never build the pressure needed to trigger shutoff.
  • Water Leak: Even a small leak constantly drains pressure, preventing the system from reaching cutoff.
  • Failed Pressure Switch: A switch with welded or stuck contacts won't disengage when pressure rises.
  • Running Fixture: A stuck toilet fill valve, open hose bib, or running irrigation keeps pressure from building.
  • Pressure Set Too High: If the cutoff pressure exceeds pump capacity, it will run forever trying to reach an impossible target.
  • Pump Capacity Decline: A worn pump may no longer produce enough pressure to reach the cutoff setting.

Warning Signs to Watch For

  • Pump runs continuously without cycling off
  • Pressure gauge shows lower than cutoff pressure
  • Electric meter spinning faster than normal
  • Pump or motor feels hot
  • Can hear pump running at all hours

How to Fix This Problem

1. Turn Off the Pump

Continuous running damages the pump. Turn off the breaker until you can diagnose the problem.

2. Check for Running Water

Walk through the property checking every fixture. Listen at toilets, check outdoor faucets, verify irrigation is off.

3. Test Pressure Tank

Press the air valve—water spraying out means the tank needs replacement.

4. Inspect Pressure Switch

With power off, examine the switch contacts. Burned or pitted contacts may be stuck closed.

5. Check for Leaks

Walk the water line path looking for wet spots. Check crawl spaces and basements for dripping pipes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will a well pump burn out if it runs constantly?

Yes, eventually. Continuous running causes overheating and accelerates wear. Address the problem immediately to prevent pump failure.

How do I stop my well pump from running constantly?

Find and fix the cause—usually a waterlogged tank, leak, or pressure switch problem. Don't just let it run hoping it will fix itself.

Can a stuck pressure switch cause pump to run continuously?

Absolutely. If the switch contacts weld together or stick closed, they'll keep sending power regardless of pressure.

How much electricity does a constantly running well pump use?

Most well pumps use 1-5 kW per hour. Running 24/7 adds $100-300+ monthly to your electric bill.
