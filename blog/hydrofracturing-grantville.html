What is Hydrofracturing?

Hydrofracturing is a well rehabilitation technique that uses high-pressure water to clean and expand existing fractures in the bedrock surrounding your well. This process:

  • Clears mineral deposits and sediment blocking water flow
  • Opens new pathways for groundwater to reach your well
  • Can increase well yield by 2-10 times in many cases
  • Extends the life of your existing well

Unlike oil and gas fracking, well hydrofracturing uses only clean water—no chemicals are involved.

Signs Your Grantville Well Needs Hydrofracturing

  • Declining water production over time
  • Well runs dry during peak usage
  • Slow recovery rate after pumping
  • Pump cycling on and off frequently
  • Lower water pressure than previous years

The Hydrofracturing Process

  1. Initial Assessment: We evaluate your well's current yield and condition
  2. Equipment Setup: Specialized high-pressure equipment is positioned at your well
  3. Isolation: Packers seal off sections of the well for targeted treatment
  4. Pressurization: Water is pumped at 500-3,000 PSI to fracture bedrock
  5. Development: Well is cleaned and tested for improved yield

Hydrofracturing for Low-Yield Wells in Grantville

That fractured volcanic rock is prime territory for hydrofracturing.

When a Grantville-area well underperforms, a new well is the costly last resort. How Hydrofracturing Works

Hydrofracturing uses only clean water at high pressure — no chemicals. We isolate a section of the open bedrock borehole with an inflatable packer and inject water at roughly 1,000 to 3,000 PSI. The pressure flushes out the silt, mineral scale, and cuttings clogging the rock's natural fractures and opens and connects those cracks to a wider water-bearing zone, increasing flow into the well.

The Santiago Peak Volcanics that underlie the eastern Grantville and Mission Gorge area are dense, hard rock where water moves almost entirely through fractures. A well that intersected a thin fracture set can sit near a far more productive one and never reach it. Fracturing bridges that gap. The treatment usually takes four to eight hours on site, with the well back to normal service within a day or two.

Why Grantville-Area Wells Lose Yield

  • Fracture plugging: Mineral precipitate and sediment seal the cracks feeding a bedrock well over the years.
  • Hard-rock variability: Yields in the Santiago Peak Volcanics swing widely with fracture density.
  • River-valley sediment: Wells near the San Diego River and Alvarado Canyon can take on fine sand and silt that reduce inflow.
  • Dry-year drawdown: When the regional table falls, marginal wells run short during peak summer demand.

Signs Your Grantville Well Needs Hydrofracturing

  • The well no longer keeps up with demand the way it used to.
  • The pump short-cycles or runs for very long stretches.
  • Air, sputtering, or sediment appears at the taps under heavy use.
  • Recovery after pumping has slowed dramatically.
  • You've gotten a new-well estimate and want a cheaper option first.

Fracturing is for wells finished in hard, fractured rock like the volcanics. A shallow well in the river-valley alluvium usually needs redevelopment or pump service instead. Our diagnostic determines which applies before any pressure equipment is used.

What to Check Before You Call

  1. Find the well log — depth, casing, static water level. San Diego County and the state DWR keep copies.
  2. Rule out cheap failures — a pressure switch ($150–$350) or a waterlogged tank ($600–$1,500) can mimic a failing well.
  3. Watch the pump — rapid cycling means the well is drawn down faster than it recovers.
  4. Note the timeline — gradual decline points to the formation; a sudden loss to equipment.

When to Call a Professional

Hydrofracturing requires pressure equipment, packers, and experienced judgment — not a DIY project. Call when yield is clearly down, when a new well has been recommended, or when you want a straight answer. The $125 diagnostic is credited toward any work we do.

Reading the Layers Beneath Grantville

What makes the Grantville and Mission Gorge area interesting for well work is how quickly the geology changes. Down along the San Diego River and Alvarado Canyon you have young, soft alluvium; up on the mesas you hit the Stadium Conglomerate and Lindavista Formation; and toward the eastern canyon walls you reach the hard, ancient Santiago Peak Volcanics. A well's behavior depends almost entirely on which of these it draws from. The volcanic-rock wells are the classic hydrofracturing candidates, because that dense rock carries water only through fractures — and fractures can be cleaned and connected.

When we assess a low-yield well in this area, the well log and a downhole camera tell us where the borehole bottoms out and where the productive zones are. If the well reaches fractured volcanic or crystalline rock and has simply lost output to plugging, the odds of a strong recovery are good. If it's a shallow alluvial well in the river valley, we steer you toward redevelopment or pump service instead — fracturing wouldn't be the right tool, and we won't sell it where it doesn't fit.

With a turnkey new well running $18,000 to $42,000 in this part of San Diego County, the case for trying a $3,000-$8,000 rehabilitation first is straightforward, especially for the older wells common on the larger Navajo-community parcels.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the Santiago Peak Volcanic rock respond well to fracturing?

Yes — dense, hard volcanic rock carries water through fractures, which is exactly what fracturing cleans and connects. We verify the well is finished in suitable rock with a camera survey first.

Will the process affect the nearby San Diego River?

No. Treatment is confined to your borehole and the rock immediately around it. It uses only clean water and does not impact surface waterways.

Are chemicals involved?

None — only clean water under pressure. We disinfect the well afterward.

How soon will I know if it worked?

We test flow before treatment and again within 24–48 hours, giving you a documented before-and-after.

How long do the results last?

Often many years, depending on the rock and pumping demand.

What if my well isn't suited to fracturing?

We'll tell you upfront. Wells in valley alluvium respond better to redevelopment or pump work, which we also provide.
