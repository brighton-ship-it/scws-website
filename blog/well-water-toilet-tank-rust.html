Quick Answer: Rust = iron from well water. Dissolved iron oxidizes and deposits in tank. Clean with rust remover or vinegar. To prevent: install iron filter or use water softener (for low iron). Treating the source is the only permanent fix.

Why This Happens

Iron in Well Water

  • Iron is naturally present in groundwater
  • May be dissolved (clear water iron)
  • Oxidizes when exposed to air
  • Deposits on surfaces as rust

Why Toilet Tanks Show It First

  • Water sits for extended periods
  • Air contact accelerates oxidation
  • White porcelain shows staining
  • Tank is easy to look into

Iron Bacteria

  • Sometimes rust is from iron bacteria
  • Slimy, rusty deposits
  • May have swampy odor
  • Harder to treat than mineral iron

Iron Levels

  • 0.3 ppm: Staining begins
  • 1+ ppm: Noticeable deposits
  • 3+ ppm: Significant buildup

Cleaning the Tank

What You'll Need

  • Rust remover (Iron Out, CLR, Bar Keepers Friend)
  • Or white vinegar
  • Scrub brush
  • Rubber gloves

Steps

  1. Turn off water supply to toilet
  2. Flush to drain tank
  3. Apply rust remover to stained areas
  4. Let sit 10-15 minutes
  5. Scrub with brush
  6. Wipe down components
  7. Turn water back on
  8. Flush several times to rinse

For Heavy Buildup

  • May need multiple treatments
  • Stronger commercial rust removers
  • Replace severely stained components

Cleaning Products

Product Strength Notes
White vinegar Mild Natural, safe
Lemon juice Mild Natural acid
Bar Keepers Friend Moderate Contains oxalic acid
Iron Out Strong Designed for rust
CLR Strong Calcium, lime, rust

Don't Use Bleach

  • Bleach can set iron stains
  • Makes rust harder to remove
  • Use rust-specific products

Prevention

Treat the Water Source

Cleaning is temporary—iron keeps coming. The only permanent solution is treating your water.

Treatment Options

Water Softener

  • Removes low iron (under 2-3 ppm)
  • Also removes hardness
  • May need iron-removing salt

Iron Filter

  • Designed for higher iron
  • Air injection or media-based
  • Handles 5-15+ ppm

For Iron Bacteria

  • Shock chlorinate well
  • May need ongoing chlorination
  • Harder to control than mineral iron

Tank Tablets

  • Drop-in products reduce buildup
  • Not a solution—just slows it
  • Still need water treatment

Related Problems

Toilet Bowl Staining

  • Same iron, same cause
  • Use bowl cleaner for iron
  • Don't use chlorine bleach

Orange/Brown Ring

  • At water line in bowl
  • Iron deposits
  • Pumice stone can remove

Black Slime

  • May be manganese or iron bacteria
  • More concerning than orange rust
  • May need well disinfection

Components Sticking

  • Iron buildup on flapper, fill valve
  • Causes running toilet
  • Clean or replace components

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is there rust in my toilet tank?

Iron in your well water oxidizes and deposits as rust. Common with well water containing 0.3+ ppm iron.

How do I clean rust from my toilet tank?

Empty tank, apply rust remover (Iron Out, CLR, vinegar), scrub, rinse. Don't use bleach—it sets iron stains.

Will the rust keep coming back?

Yes, until you treat the water source. Cleaning is temporary. Install iron filter or softener for permanent solution.

Is toilet tank rust harmful?

Iron isn't harmful to health. It's a nuisance issue—stains fixtures, laundry, and tastes metallic.

What's the slimy orange stuff?

Likely iron bacteria. Different from mineral iron—needs well disinfection to address. May have swampy odor too.
