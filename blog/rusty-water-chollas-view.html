What Causes Rusty Well Water?

  • Dissolved Iron: Naturally occurring iron in San Diego County groundwater
  • Iron Bacteria: Bacteria that feed on iron, creating rusty slime
  • Corroding Casing: Old steel well casing breaking down
  • Corroding Pipes: Galvanized or iron plumbing in your home
  • Failing Water Heater: Rusting anode rod or tank

Is Rusty Water Safe to Drink?

Iron itself isn't a health hazard at typical concentrations. However, rusty water:

  • Tastes metallic and unpleasant
  • Stains sinks, toilets, and laundry
  • Can clog pipes and fixtures over time
  • May indicate other water quality issues

We recommend water testing to identify the specific cause and concentration of iron in your Chollas View water.

Rusty Water in Chollas View: Causes and Solutions for an Older San Diego Neighborhood

Chollas View is a historic, mostly residential community in southeastern San Diego, part of the broader Mid-City and Southeastern San Diego area within San Diego County. The neighborhood is built up and connected to the City of San Diego's municipal water system, so genuine private wells are rare within its boundaries. What is not rare here is rusty, orange, or brown water, and in a neighborhood with so many homes dating to the mid-twentieth century, the usual culprit is corroding galvanized-steel plumbing rather than well water. For the small number of properties on the eastern and rural fringes of the region that do draw from private wells, dissolved iron in groundwater is the more likely cause. This guide explains both, so you can tell which one is affecting your home.

Galvanized Plumbing: The Most Common Cause in Chollas View

Many Chollas View homes were built in the 1940s, 1950s, and 1960s, an era when galvanized steel was the standard for interior water lines. Galvanized pipe is steel coated in zinc to slow corrosion, but that coating wears away over decades. Once it does, the bare steel underneath begins to rust from the inside out. The rust flakes loose and travels to your faucets, which is why an older San Diego home on perfectly clean city water can still pour out a glass of orange water. The classic signature is rust that is worst first thing in the morning, after water has sat motionless in the pipes all night, and clears after a minute of running. Over time, the same corrosion narrows the pipe interior, dropping your water pressure as well.

How Iron Enters Well Water on Rural Parcels

If your property is one of the rural parcels in San Diego County's East County that still uses a private well, the rust story is different. Groundwater moving through soil and fractured rock dissolves naturally occurring iron, which stays invisible in its dissolved form underground. When that water meets oxygen at your tap, the iron oxidizes into the rust-colored particles you see. Well water can also harbor iron bacteria, harmless-to-health organisms that feed on iron and leave a slimy reddish residue and a musty smell. Treatment for well-sourced iron involves oxidizing the iron and filtering it out, and treatment for iron bacteria adds shock chlorination of the well.

How the Fixes Differ

Because the two causes are so different, so are the solutions. For galvanized-plumbing corrosion in a Chollas View home, the lasting fix is repiping the affected lines, or the whole house, with modern PEX or copper. A whole-house sediment filter can reduce the visible particles in the meantime, but it treats the symptom rather than the cause. For well-sourced iron, the answer is a treatment system sized to your water test, ranging from a softener for light iron to a dedicated oxidizing filter for heavier loads, plus well disinfection if bacteria are present. Diagnosing correctly first is what prevents wasted money on the wrong equipment.

What to Check Before Calling

  • Run the cold water at several faucets. If rust appears at every tap, the source is upstream of the house; if only at some, it is the pipes serving those fixtures.
  • Compare hot and cold. Rust only on the hot side usually means the water heater, often a corroded anode rod or rusting tank.
  • Note the timing. Morning-only rust that clears after running is the hallmark of corroding galvanized pipe.
  • Check the home's age and plumbing. Pre-1970 San Diego homes are prime candidates for original galvanized lines.
  • If you have a private well, look in the toilet tank for orange slime and note any musty odor, both signs of iron bacteria.

When to Bring in a Professional

It is worth a professional call when the discoloration is constant, when it stains laundry and fixtures, when pressure has been dropping alongside the rust, or when you are unsure whether your plumbing is galvanized. For the rare Chollas View-area property on a private well, any persistent rust or odor warrants a water test, especially if the well has never been evaluated. How Rusty Water Affects Your Home Over Time

Beyond the unpleasant look and taste, rusty water takes a quiet toll on a Chollas View household. Iron particles stain porcelain sinks, tubs, and toilet bowls with stubborn orange marks, and they discolor white laundry, sometimes permanently. Inside the plumbing, accumulating rust narrows pipe diameters and roughens their interior, which steadily reduces water pressure and forces fixtures and appliances to work harder. Water heaters fill with sediment and lose efficiency, and the heating elements or burners wear faster. Dishwashers and washing machines develop clogged inlet screens. Catching and correcting the source of rust early is far cheaper than replacing stained fixtures and failing appliances down the road, which is why a prompt diagnosis pays for itself.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Chollas View have private wells?

Very few. The neighborhood is on City of San Diego municipal water, so rusty water here is most often caused by corroding galvanized plumbing inside older homes. Private wells are mainly found on rural parcels in the wider East County.

Why is my water rusty only in the morning?

Morning-only rust that clears after running is the classic sign of galvanized pipe corrosion. Water sitting still overnight picks up rust from the pipe walls, then flushes out once you run the tap.

Is the rusty water safe to use?

Iron is not a recognized health hazard at typical levels, but it stains and tastes metallic. If you are on a private well and unsure of the source, have the water tested before using it for drinking.

Can a filter fix galvanized pipe corrosion?

A sediment filter reduces visible particles but does not stop pipes from rusting. The permanent fix for corroded galvanized plumbing is repiping with PEX or copper.

How do I know if my pipes are galvanized?

Galvanized pipe is dull gray steel that a magnet will stick to. A plumber can confirm it quickly, and the home's age, pre-1970, is a strong clue in Chollas View.
