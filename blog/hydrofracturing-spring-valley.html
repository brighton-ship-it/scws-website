What is Hydrofracturing?

Hydrofracturing is a well rehabilitation technique that uses high-pressure water to clean and expand existing fractures in the bedrock surrounding your well. This process:

  • Clears mineral deposits and sediment blocking water flow
  • Opens new pathways for groundwater to reach your well
  • Can increase well yield by 2-10 times in many cases
  • Extends the life of your existing well

Unlike oil and gas fracking, well hydrofracturing uses only clean water—no chemicals are involved.

Signs Your Spring Valley Well Needs Hydrofracturing

  • Declining water production over time
  • Well runs dry during peak usage
  • Slow recovery rate after pumping
  • Pump cycling on and off frequently
  • Lower water pressure than previous years

The Hydrofracturing Process

  1. Initial Assessment: We evaluate your well's current yield and condition
  2. Equipment Setup: Specialized high-pressure equipment is positioned at your well
  3. Isolation: Packers seal off sections of the well for targeted treatment
  4. Pressurization: Water is pumped at 500-3,000 PSI to fracture bedrock
  5. Development: Well is cleaned and tested for improved yield

Hydrofracturing Wells in Spring Valley, California

While much of central Spring Valley is connected to Helix Water District and Otay Water District mains, the higher-elevation parcels along Dictionary Hill, the Sweetwater corridor, and the unincorporated pockets bordering Jamul and Rancho San Diego still depend on private wells drilled into hard crystalline bedrock. If your Spring Valley well has been giving you less water than it did a few years ago, hydrofracturing (also spelled hydrofracking) is one of the most cost-effective ways to restore and even increase its yield without the expense of drilling a brand-new well.

Southern California Well Service has worked since 2020 working the granite country of San Diego County, and Spring Valley's geology is exactly the kind of fractured-rock environment where well-yield enhancement through hydrofracturing tends to perform well. Below we explain how the process works, when it is the right call for a Spring Valley property, and when a different repair or a new well makes more sense.

How Hydrofracturing Works

A water well in fractured bedrock does not draw from an open underground lake. Instead, it collects water from a network of thin cracks, joints, and seams in the rock. Over years of pumping, those fractures can clog with fine sediment, mineral scale, iron and manganese deposits, and silt, slowly choking off the flow. Hydrofracturing reverses that process. Our crew lowers an inflatable device called a packer into the borehole below the casing, seals off a target section of the open hole, and then injects clean water at high pressure, typically between 1,000 and 3,000 PSI. That pressure scours out the clogged fractures, widens the existing seams, and can connect your borehole to nearby water-bearing fractures it was never linked to before.

Unlike oil-and-gas fracking, residential water-well hydrofracturing uses nothing but clean water. No chemicals, no sand proppants, and no permanent alteration of the surrounding aquifer. It is a recognized, decades-old well-rehabilitation technique used throughout the granitic regions of the western United States.

Why Spring Valley's Geology Matters

Hydrofracturing only helps wells that draw from fractured crystalline or granitic bedrock, and that is precisely what underlies the hills of eastern Spring Valley. The Peninsular Ranges batholith, the same mass of Cretaceous-age granite that forms the spine of San Diego County's backcountry, surfaces here as the decomposed granite and tonalite you see in road cuts around Dictionary Hill and the slopes leading toward Jamul. Wells in this rock typically run anywhere from 200 to 600 feet deep and produce their water from discrete fracture zones rather than from a continuous sand-and-gravel layer. When those fractures get throttled by sediment or scale, hydrofracturing is one of the few interventions that can genuinely reopen them.

When Hydrofracturing Helps and When It Does Not

We believe in giving Spring Valley homeowners honest guidance, so here is the straight version. Hydrofracturing is a strong candidate when:

  • Your well is drilled into hard granite or fractured rock and its yield has declined over the years.
  • A newly drilled well came in weak and the driller hit good rock but disappointing flow.
  • Your well runs low only during the dry summer and fall months, suggesting marginal fracture connectivity rather than a failed pump.
  • You want to exhaust the lower-cost options before committing to a new borehole.

It is usually the wrong tool when the problem is not the rock at all. If your home is on Helix or Otay municipal water, hydrofracturing is irrelevant. If your well is actually suffering from a failing pump, a cracked drop pipe, a bad pressure switch, or a waterlogged pressure tank, no amount of fracturing will help, and a straightforward pump or component repair is the real fix. And in the rare case where a Spring Valley well bottoms out in loose sand or weathered, crumbling material rather than competent rock, fracturing has little to grab onto and we will tell you so up front.

The Hydrofracturing Process, Step by Step

  1. Diagnostic visit and baseline flow test. We measure your current yield in gallons per minute, check static and pumping water levels, and review your well log if you have one. Our diagnostic fee is $125 and is credited toward the work if you proceed.
  2. Downhole camera inspection. A video survey shows us casing condition, the depth of the open borehole, and where the productive fracture zones are located.
  3. Packer placement. We set an inflatable packer to isolate the target zone so all the pressure is directed exactly where it will do the most good.
  4. High-pressure injection. Clean water is pumped at 1,000 to 3,000 PSI to flush and widen the fractures. We may treat several zones in a single visit.
  5. Development and post-test. We surge and clear the well, then run a fresh flow test, usually within 24 to 48 hours, and document the before-and-after numbers for you.

Most Spring Valley jobs are completed in a single day, typically four to eight hours on site.

Realistic Results and Expectations

Across the granitic terrain of San Diego County, hydrofracturing improves yield on roughly 70 to 80 percent of the wells we treat, with typical gains in the range of two to five times the pre-treatment flow rate. Some wells jump dramatically; others see a modest but meaningful bump that gets a household comfortably through the summer. We cannot promise a specific number before we see your well, and any contractor who guarantees an exact gallon figure sight unseen is overselling. What we can promise is an honest baseline test, a documented result, and a clear recommendation if hydrofracturing turns out not to be the answer for your property.

What to Check Before You Call

  • Is your water source actually a private well, or are you on Helix or Otay municipal water?
  • Has the yield genuinely declined, or is the symptom intermittent (which can point to a pump or pressure-tank issue instead)?
  • Do you know the well's depth and whether it was drilled into rock? An old well log is gold here.
  • When was the pump last serviced? Ruling out a pump fault first can save you money.
