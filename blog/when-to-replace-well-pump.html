When to Replace Your Well Pump

Warning Signs, Lifespan & What to Expect

SC

By SCWS Team

Published February 17, 2026 · 10 min read

Your well pump works hard—cycling on and off thousands of times each year, often 100+ feet underground where you can't see or hear it. Knowing when it's time for replacement can save you from the inconvenience of complete failure and potential damage to other components.

Well Pump Lifespans

Pump TypeAverage LifespanIdeal Conditions
Submersible (residential)8-15 years15-25 years
Submersible (commercial grade)15-20 years20-30 years
Jet pump (shallow well)10-15 years15-20 years
Jet pump (deep well)8-12 years12-18 years

Factors That Shorten Pump Life

  • Sandy or silty water: Abrasive particles destroy impellers and wear rings
  • Frequent cycling: Starting and stopping wears motor; sized pressure tank prevents this
  • Electrical issues: Voltage fluctuations, lightning strikes, poor grounding
  • Running dry: Loss of prime or low water level destroys seals and bearings
  • Corrosive water: Low pH attacks metal components
  • Oversized pump: Short cycling damages motor

Warning Signs Your Pump Is Failing

🚨 Signs Requiring Immediate Attention

  • No water at all: Complete pump failure (could also be electrical issue)
  • Pump runs but no water: Seized impeller or lost prime
  • Burning smell: Motor overheating—turn off immediately
  • Visible sparks or smoke: Electrical emergency—shut off breaker

⚠️ Warning Signs—Schedule Evaluation

  • Declining pressure over months: Worn impeller or motor losing power
  • Pump runs constantly: Can't build pressure; various possible causes
  • Short cycling: Rapid on/off (often pressure tank, but can be pump)
  • Grinding or screaming sounds: Bearing failure; replacement needed soon
  • Increased electricity usage: Struggling motor draws more amps
  • Sandy or dirty water: Screen failure or aquifer changes
  • Air sputtering: Drop pipe leak or falling water level

Repair vs. Replace Decision

When to Repair

  • Pump is less than 7-8 years old
  • Problem is minor (capacitor, pressure switch, etc.)
  • Repair cost is less than 40-50% of replacement
  • No history of recurring problems

When to Replace

  • Pump is over 10-12 years old
  • Multiple repairs in recent years
  • Repair cost exceeds 50% of replacement
  • Chronic performance issues
  • Pump is undersized for current needs
  • Motor windings have failed
  • Severe bearing or impeller damage

Consider this: For submersible pumps, the labor to pull the pump for inspection often costs $500-1,000+. If the pump is older and the diagnosis reveals significant wear, you've already paid most of the labor for replacement. Installing a new pump at that point adds only material cost.

The Replacement Process

  1. Diagnosis: Confirm the pump is the problem (not electrical, pressure tank, etc.)
  2. Turn off power: Breaker to pump circuit
  3. Remove wellhead fittings: Disconnect pitless adapter or other connections
  4. Pull the pump: Using rig or winch, bring up pipe, wire, and pump
  5. Inspect components: Check pipe, wire, torque arrestor, check valve
  6. Install new pump: Connect wire, install check valve, lower into well
  7. Connect at surface: Pitless adapter, electrical connections
  8. Test system: Verify pressure, flow rate, amp draw

Most residential submersible pump replacements take 3-6 hours for an experienced crew.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long do well pumps last?

Submersible well pumps typically last 8-15 years, with quality pumps reaching 15-25 years under ideal conditions. Jet pumps last 10-15 years on average. Actual lifespan depends on water quality, cycling frequency, and electrical quality.

What are the signs my well pump is failing?

Key warning signs include declining water pressure over time, pump running constantly without shutting off, rapid cycling, unusual sounds (grinding, clicking, humming), dirty or sandy water, air sputtering, and higher electricity bills.

How much does it cost to replace a well pump?

Submersible pump replacement typically costs $1,500-4,000+ including pump, labor, and materials. Budget $2,000-3,000 for a typical residential submersible pump replacement. Jet pump replacement is usually $800-1,800.
