What is Hydrofracturing?

Hydrofracturing is a well rehabilitation technique that uses high-pressure water to clean and expand existing fractures in the bedrock surrounding your well. This process:

  • Clears mineral deposits and sediment blocking water flow
  • Opens new pathways for groundwater to reach your well
  • Can increase well yield by 2-10 times in many cases
  • Extends the life of your existing well

Unlike oil and gas fracking, well hydrofracturing uses only clean water—no chemicals are involved.

Signs Your Sunset Cliffs Well Needs Hydrofracturing

  • Declining water production over time
  • Well runs dry during peak usage
  • Slow recovery rate after pumping
  • Pump cycling on and off frequently
  • Lower water pressure than previous years

The Hydrofracturing Process

  1. Initial Assessment: We evaluate your well's current yield and condition
  2. Equipment Setup: Specialized high-pressure equipment is positioned at your well
  3. Isolation: Packers seal off sections of the well for targeted treatment
  4. Pressurization: Water is pumped at 500-3,000 PSI to fracture bedrock
  5. Development: Well is cleaned and tested for improved yield

Many Sunset Cliffs wells that appear to be "going dry" can be successfully rehabilitated through hydrofracturing, saving homeowners tens of thousands of dollars.

Hydrofracturing and Well Water in Sunset Cliffs, San Diego

Sunset Cliffs is one of San Diego's most scenic coastal neighborhoods, perched on the western edge of the Point Loma peninsula where the bluffs of Sunset Cliffs Natural Park drop straight into the Pacific. Bordered by Ocean Beach to the north and the Point Loma communities to the east, it is a dense, fully urbanized residential area of San Diego County. We want to be straight with you from the start: hydrofracturing is rarely the right service for a property in Sunset Cliffs, and the reasons come down to geology and infrastructure. This page explains what hydrofracturing actually does, why it is usually a weak fit on the Point Loma coast, and what the better options are if you are dealing with a water concern here.

What Hydrofracturing Is

Hydrofracturing, also called hydrofracking, is a well-yield enhancement technique. It applies only to water wells that have been drilled into hard, fractured crystalline or granitic bedrock. In that setting a well draws its water from a network of thin cracks in the rock. Over time those cracks clog with sediment and mineral scale, and the well's flow drops. Hydrofracturing uses an inflatable packer to isolate a section of the borehole, then injects clean water at 1,000 to 3,000 PSI to flush and widen those fractures, reconnecting the well to water-bearing seams and restoring flow. It is an effective tool, but only where there is fractured bedrock and a private well drawing from it.

Why It Usually Does Not Apply in Sunset Cliffs

Two things make Sunset Cliffs a poor match for hydrofracturing. First, nearly every property here is served by the City of San Diego's municipal water system. When your water arrives through a meter from the city main, there is no private well to enhance, and hydrofracturing is simply not relevant. Second, the geology of the Point Loma peninsula is not the fractured granite that fracturing depends on. The Point Loma bluffs are built from the Point Loma Formation, a sequence of marine sedimentary rock, sandstones and siltstones, capped by terrace deposits. This is layered sedimentary and coastal material, not the crystalline batholith rock found in San Diego's eastern backcountry. Even in the rare case where a coastal property had some kind of legacy well, the formation would offer little of the rock-fracture network that hydrofracturing is designed to reopen. On top of that, any shallow groundwater this close to the ocean is prone to saltwater intrusion, making it a poor drinking-water source regardless of flow.

When Hydrofracturing Does Make Sense, and When It Does Not

To be useful, here is the honest framework. Hydrofracturing is a strong candidate when all of these are true:

  • You have a private water well, not a municipal connection.
  • That well is drilled into fractured granite or crystalline bedrock.
  • The well's yield has declined or came in low to begin with.

In Sunset Cliffs, those conditions almost never line up. That is not a knock on the technique; it is just the wrong neighborhood for it. The communities where hydrofracturing shines are the granite foothills and mountains of East County and the Riverside County backcountry, places like Ramona, Julian, Alpine, Anza, and the rural parcels around Jamul, where private rock wells are the norm.

Better Options for Sunset Cliffs Water Concerns

If you are reading this because you have a water problem in Sunset Cliffs, the right solution is almost certainly something other than hydrofracturing:

  • Low water pressure on city water. This is usually a plumbing or pressure-regulator issue inside your home, not a well problem. A plumber or your water provider is the right call.
  • Water quality and taste concerns. A whole-house or point-of-use water treatment and filtration system addresses hardness, taste, and contaminants. This is a common request even on municipal supply.
  • A legacy or abandoned well on the property. Older San Diego parcels sometimes have a long-disused well. These typically need proper assessment and, in many cases, destruction or sealing to county standards rather than rehabilitation.
  • You are planning a property elsewhere with a real well. If you own land in the backcountry where a private rock well exists, that is where our well services, including hydrofracturing, genuinely apply.

If You Actually Have a Rock Well: The Process

For the small number of readers who do own a genuine fractured-rock well somewhere in our service area, here is what a hydrofracturing visit looks like:

  1. Diagnostic and baseline flow test to measure current yield. The $125 diagnostic fee is credited toward any work performed.
  2. Downhole camera inspection to confirm the well is in rock and to locate fracture zones.
  3. Packer isolation of the target zone.
  4. High-pressure injection of clean water at 1,000 to 3,000 PSI.
  5. Development and a post-treatment flow test to document the improvement.

Where the geology is right, this work improves yield in roughly 70 to 80 percent of wells, with gains of two to five times the starting flow. None of that, however, changes the fact that a municipally served coastal lot in Sunset Cliffs is not the place for it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I hydrofracture a well in Sunset Cliffs to get free water?

Almost certainly not. Sunset Cliffs is served by city water, and the Point Loma coastal geology is marine sedimentary rock, not the fractured granite that hydrofracturing requires. There is generally no suitable private well to enhance.

I have low water pressure. Is that a well problem?

On municipal water it is not. It is usually a pressure regulator or in-home plumbing issue. Your water provider or a plumber can resolve it.

There is an old well on my property. What should I do?

Have it assessed. Many legacy coastal wells are best properly sealed or destroyed to county standards rather than rehabilitated, especially given saltwater intrusion risk near the coast.

Where does hydrofracturing actually work?

In the granite foothills and mountains of San Diego and Riverside Counties, where private rock wells are common, such as Ramona, Julian, Alpine, Anza, and the rural parcels around Jamul and Descanso.

Do you offer water treatment for city water?

Yes. Whole-house and point-of-use treatment can improve hardness, taste, and quality even on municipal supply.

Will you give me an honest answer if hydrofracturing is not right for me?

Always. We would rather tell you the truth than sell you a service that the geology and your water source say will not help.
