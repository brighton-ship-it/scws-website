Understanding PSPS Events

PSPS (Public Safety Power Shutoff) is SDG&E's program to de-energize power lines during dangerous fire weather—typically when:

  • Santa Ana winds exceed 25-40 mph (gusts higher)
  • Humidity drops below 20%
  • Fire Weather Watches or Red Flag Warnings are issued

For inland and backcountry San Diego—Ramona, Julian, Valley Center, Fallbrook, and east county—PSPS events are increasingly common.

Typical PSPS Duration

  • Short events: 12-24 hours
  • Typical events: 24-48 hours
  • Extended events: 48-72+ hours

Power isn't restored immediately when winds die down—SDG&E must inspect lines before re-energizing, which adds hours or even days.

The Problem: No Power = No Water

Unlike city water (which has its own backup systems), your well pump runs on electricity. During a PSPS:

  • No water for drinking, cooking, or sanitation
  • No water for firefighting if fire approaches
  • No water for livestock or pets
  • Pressure tank provides only 10-50 gallons before emptying

Without preparation, a 48-hour outage becomes a serious emergency.

Option 1: Portable Generator

The most common solution for occasional outages.

Generator Sizing

Pump Size Running Watts Starting Watts Minimum Generator
1/2 HP 1,000W 2,000-3,000W 3,000W
3/4 HP 1,500W 3,000-4,000W 4,000W
1 HP 2,000W 4,000-5,000W 5,000W
1.5 HP 2,500W 5,000-6,000W 6,500W
2 HP 3,000W 6,000-7,500W 7,500W

Key point: Size for starting watts, not running watts. Motors draw 2-3x normal current when starting.

Connection Options

Transfer switch (recommended):

  • Installed by electrician ($300-$800)
  • Safely isolates generator from utility power
  • Prevents backfeed that endangers utility workers
  • Required by code for permanent installations

Generator inlet box:

  • Dedicated outlet for generator connection
  • Connected through interlock or transfer switch
  • Quick, safe connection during outages

Fuel Planning

For a 48-hour outage running pump intermittently:

  • 5,000W generator uses ~0.5-1 gallon/hour under load
  • Running pump 2-3 hours/day = 3-6 gallons/day
  • Keep 10-15 gallons on hand minimum
  • Store fuel safely, away from structures

Cost

  • Quality portable generator: $800-$2,000
  • Transfer switch installation: $300-$800
  • Total: $1,100-$2,800

Option 2: Whole-House Standby Generator

Automatic backup for frequent outages or critical needs.

Features

  • Starts automatically when power fails
  • Runs on natural gas or propane (no refueling)
  • Powers entire home, not just well
  • Can run indefinitely with fuel supply

Sizing for Well + Home

  • 12-16 kW: Well pump + essential circuits
  • 20-24 kW: Well pump + most home loads
  • 30+ kW: Whole home including AC

Cost

  • Generator unit: $4,000-$15,000
  • Installation: $3,000-$8,000
  • Propane tank (if needed): $1,500-$3,000
  • Total: $8,500-$26,000

Option 3: Battery Backup Systems

Increasingly popular for solar homes and clean power.

How It Works

  • Battery bank stores power when grid is up
  • Inverter converts DC to AC during outage
  • Can be paired with solar for extended runtime

Considerations for Well Pumps

Well pumps have high starting loads that challenge battery systems:

  • Need inverter rated for motor starting surge
  • Tesla Powerwall can handle some well pumps (check specs)
  • Larger systems (Enphase, Generac PWRcell) better for bigger pumps
  • May need soft-start kit to reduce inrush current

Runtime

A typical 13.5 kWh battery (like Tesla Powerwall) might provide:

  • 6-8 pump cycles before depletion (without solar recharge)
  • With solar: potentially indefinite runtime during daylight

Cost

  • Battery system: $10,000-$20,000 installed
  • May qualify for federal tax credits (30% as of 2026)

Option 4: Gravity-Fed Storage Tank

Simple, reliable, works without any power.

How It Works

  • Large tank (500-2,500+ gallons) installed on hill above house
  • Well pump fills tank (when power is on)
  • Gravity provides pressure to house (~0.43 PSI per foot elevation)
  • During outage, water flows by gravity until tank empties

Pressure Considerations

  • 50 feet elevation = ~20 PSI (enough for basic use)
  • 100 feet elevation = ~43 PSI (good pressure)
  • May need booster pump for normal operations (powered by generator during outage)

Cost

  • 2,500-gallon poly tank: $1,500-$2,500
  • Installation with plumbing: $3,000-$8,000
  • Total: $4,500-$10,500

Option 5: Pre-Outage Preparation

The simplest approach for occasional PSPS events.

Before PSPS Is Announced

  • Fill bathtubs (50+ gallons each) for flushing toilets
  • Fill large containers for drinking water (1 gallon/person/day minimum)
  • Run pressure tank through a cycle to ensure it's full
  • Fill stock tanks for animals
  • Run dishwasher and laundry before shutoff

Water Storage Guidelines

  • Drinking/cooking: 1 gallon per person per day
  • Sanitation: 2-3 gallons per person per day
  • Pets: 1 gallon per large dog per day
  • Horses: 10-15 gallons per horse per day

For a family of 4 for 3 days: minimum 12 gallons drinking + 24-36 gallons sanitation = ~50 gallons

PSPS Checklist

When PSPS Is Announced

  1. Confirm your address is in the shutoff zone (SDG&E website/app)
  2. Fill all water storage containers
  3. Fill bathtubs for toilet flushing
  4. Test generator (if you have one)
  5. Get fuel for generator
  6. Charge all devices and battery backups
  7. Fill vehicle gas tanks (stations may lose power too)
  8. Water livestock extra before shutoff

During PSPS

  • Conserve stored water—short showers, minimal flushing
  • Run generator only when needed to refill pressure tank
  • Keep refrigerator/freezer closed to preserve food
  • Monitor SDG&E for restoration updates

After Power Returns

  • Run water for a few minutes before drinking (flush stagnant water)
  • Check pressure tank is cycling normally
  • Inspect well area for any damage from winds
  • Refill storage containers for next event

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a PSPS and how does it affect my well?

PSPS (Public Safety Power Shutoff) is when SDG&E intentionally turns off power during high fire-risk conditions—typically Santa Ana wind events. If you have a well, no power means no water. PSPS events can last 24-72+ hours, making backup power or water storage essential.

What size generator do I need for my well pump?

Most residential well pumps need a generator with 3,000-5,000 running watts. A 1 HP pump requires about 2,000 running watts (but 4,000+ starting watts). A 2 HP pump needs about 3,000 running watts. Always size for starting watts, which are 2-3x running watts.

How can I get water from my well without electricity?

Options include: portable generator, whole-house generator, battery backup system, gravity-fed storage tank (if elevated), or manual hand pump (shallow wells only). Pre-filling storage containers before a scheduled PSPS is the simplest solution.

How much water should I store for a PSPS event?

Plan for 3-5 gallons per person per day (drinking + sanitation). For a family of 4 during a 3-day outage, store at least 40-60 gallons. Add more for pets, livestock, and gardens.
