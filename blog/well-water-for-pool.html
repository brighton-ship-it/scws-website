Using Well Water for Swimming Pools: What You Need to Know

Updated February 2026 | By Southern California Well Service

Quick Answer: You can use well water for pools, but test for metals first. Iron turns green/brown when chlorinated. Add metal sequestering agent before chlorinating. Don't run well continuously—let it recover. A 15,000-gallon pool takes 25-50 hours to fill depending on well flow rate.

Key Considerations

Can Your Well Handle It?

  • Pool filling requires thousands of gallons
  • Average pool: 15,000-20,000 gallons
  • Low-yield wells may struggle
  • Running dry damages pump

What's in Your Water?

  • Iron → staining when chlorinated
  • Copper → blue-green stains
  • Manganese → brown/black staining
  • High calcium → scaling
  • Test before filling

Pros of Well Water for Pools

  • Free (no water bill)
  • No chloramines (like city water)
  • Always available

Cons

  • Metal staining risk
  • Takes time
  • Can stress well
  • May need treatment

Don't Run Continuously

  • Fill for 4-6 hours, then rest 2-4 hours
  • Allows well to recover
  • Prevents pumping air (damaging)
  • Monitor water level in well if possible

Signs of Low Water

  • Sputtering from hose
  • Air in lines
  • Pump cycling frequently
  • Reduced flow
  • Stop immediately if these occur

Metal Issues

Iron (Most Common Problem)

  • Clear water turns green/brown when chlorinated
  • Stains pool surfaces
  • Even 0.3 ppm causes problems
  • Must treat before adding chlorine

Copper

  • Blue-green staining
  • Can stain hair blonde/green
  • Common in areas with acidic water

Manganese

  • Brown or black staining
  • Often present with iron
  • Treat same as iron

Solution: Metal Sequestrant

  • Add to pool BEFORE chlorine
  • Binds metals so they don't stain
  • Brands: Metal Free, Metal Magic, etc.
  • Follow product directions for dosage
  • May need to filter after treatment

Water Chemistry

Well Water vs City Water

Factor Well Water City Water
Metals Often present Usually treated
pH Varies (may be low or high) Usually 7.0-7.5
Hardness Often high Varies
Chlorine None Present

Adjustments Needed

  • pH: Adjust to 7.2-7.6
  • Alkalinity: 80-120 ppm
  • Calcium hardness: 200-400 ppm
  • Then chlorine: 1-3 ppm

High Calcium (Hard Water)

  • Can cause scaling
  • May need to blend with softened water
  • Or use scale inhibitor

Step-by-Step Guide

Before Filling

  1. Test well water for metals, pH, hardness
  2. Know your well's flow rate
  3. Have metal sequestrant ready
  4. Calculate fill time and plan breaks

During Filling

  1. Start hose—don't leave unattended
  2. Fill for 4-6 hours, rest 2-4 hours
  3. Watch for signs of well struggling
  4. Add sequestrant as water enters (per product directions)

After Filling

  1. Test water chemistry
  2. Ensure metals are sequestered
  3. Balance pH and alkalinity first
  4. Run filter for 24 hours
  5. Then add chlorine shock
  6. If water colors, you may need more sequestrant

If Staining Occurs

  • Stop adding chlorine
  • Add more sequestrant
  • Run filter continuously
  • May need vitamin C treatment for stains
  • Consult pool professional if severe

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use well water to fill my pool?

Yes, but test for metals first and use sequestering agent. Don't run well continuously—allow recovery time.

How long does it take to fill a pool with well water?

25-50+ hours depending on well flow rate and pool size. Plan for breaks to let well recover.

Why did my pool turn green after filling with well water?

Iron in the water oxidized when chlorinated. Add metal sequestrant before chlorinating next time.

Will filling my pool damage my well?

Can if you run it dry. Take breaks during filling. Monitor for air or sputtering.

Should I hire water delivery instead?

Consider it if: very low-yield well, very high iron, or you need pool ready quickly. Costs $500-$1,500+.
