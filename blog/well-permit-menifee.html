Riverside County Well Permit Requirements

In Riverside County, well permits are required for:

  • Drilling a new domestic or agricultural well
  • Deepening an existing well
  • Repairing or reconstructing well casing
  • Decommissioning (destroying) an unused well
  • Converting a well to a different use

The Permit Process

  1. Site Evaluation: We assess your property for optimal well location
  2. Application: Submit permit application with site plans
  3. Review: County reviews for setback requirements and regulations
  4. Approval: Permit issued (typically 2-4 weeks)
  5. Construction: Well work proceeds per permit specifications
  6. Inspection: County may inspect completed work
  7. Completion Report: We file required documentation

Setback Requirements

Wells must be located minimum distances from:

  • Septic tanks: 50-100 feet
  • Leach fields: 100-150 feet
  • Property lines: 5-10 feet
  • Buildings: 5 feet minimum
  • Other wells: varies

Exact requirements depend on Riverside County regulations and local conditions.

Well Permits in Menifee: A Local Guide

Menifee, in central Riverside County, has grown quickly from rural ranch land, and many established parcels around the city's edges still depend on private wells for household and agricultural water. If you are planning to drill a new well, deepen an existing one, or destroy an old well on your Menifee property, California's Water Well Standards (Department of Water Resources Bulletins 74-81 and 74-90) apply, and the permit is issued and enforced locally by Riverside County. Southern California Well Service has been handling these permits for property owners across the county for more than 30 years, managing the entire process on a turnkey basis so you do not have to deal with county paperwork yourself.

Who Issues Well Permits in Menifee?

Because Menifee is in Riverside County, well permits are issued by the Riverside County Department of Environmental Health (DEH). The DEH well program operates out of 4080 Lemon Street, Riverside, CA 92501 and can be reached at (951) 955-8980. Riverside County administers water well, monitoring well, cathodic protection well, and geothermal heat exchange well permits under County Ordinance No. 682 and the County Code's water well chapter. The city of Menifee does not run its own well program, so the county is the single permitting authority whether your parcel is inside city limits or in an unincorporated area nearby.

Step-by-Step: The Riverside County Well Permit Process

Here is how a Menifee well permit typically proceeds:

  1. Site evaluation. We assess your property, locate the best position for the well, and confirm all required setbacks can be met.
  2. Application and plot plan. Riverside County DEH requires a permit application with a plot plan showing the proposed well location within a 500-foot radius, a vicinity map with legal description, and the proposed well depth including casing size and perforation zones.
  3. County review. DEH reviews the application against state standards and County Ordinance 682. Notably, Riverside County must approve or deny a complete and accurate application within six working days, or it is deemed approved.
  4. Permit issuance. Once approved, the permit is issued and construction can begin.
  5. Construction. A licensed C-57 driller constructs the well to the permitted specifications, including the required annular surface seal.
  6. Sealing, grouting, and inspection. The annular space is sealed with cement or bentonite grout to protect groundwater, and the county may inspect the work.
  7. Well completion report. The driller files the Well Completion Report (Driller's Report) with the county and the Department of Water Resources documenting depth, casing, and yield. We handle this filing.

Permit Requirements and Construction Standards

For a Menifee well, Riverside County applies state minimum standards plus its own ordinance requirements:

  • Setbacks. Minimum separations from septic tanks (about 50 feet), leach fields and sewage disposal (about 100 feet), property lines, and surface water and drainage courses. Exact distances depend on site conditions.
  • Surface seal. A continuous annular seal, typically at least 20 feet deep, grouted to keep contaminants out of the aquifer.
  • Casing and construction. Approved casing, proper diameter, and a sanitary, vermin-proof surface completion.
  • Destruction standards. Unused wells must be properly destroyed by filling and sealing per Bulletin 74-90.
  • Completion reporting. The Well Completion Report must be filed after the work is finished.

Timeline and Costs in Menifee

Riverside County is notably fast on paper: a complete, accurate application must be approved or denied within six working days. In practice, gathering the plot plan, legal description, and any required clearances is what drives the real-world timeline, so plan for a couple of weeks from start to permit in hand on a standard Menifee residential well. Sensitive sites or variance requests take longer.

The county well permit fee in Riverside County generally falls in the $300 to $1,200 range for the basic permit, with additional charges possible for special reviews. When you hire us for a turnkey job, the permit is included. Complete turnkey drilling with the permit typically runs $18,000 to $42,000 depending on depth, geology, and casing. A site diagnostic visit is $125, credited toward your project.

When Do You Need a Permit?

In Menifee you need a Riverside County DEH permit to:

  • Drill a new well (domestic, agricultural, or commercial).
  • Deepen or modify an existing well.
  • Reconstruct or repair casing in a way that alters the well.
  • Destroy or abandon an unused well.

A simple pump swap generally does not require a well construction permit, but any change to the borehole, casing, or seal does. If you are not sure, call us before work begins.

Common Well Permitting Scenarios in Menifee

The right permitting path for your Menifee project depends on the work involved. These are the scenarios we handle most often under Riverside County rules:

  • New construction on rural land. Many Menifee-area parcels are sold without water service, so a domestic well permit is part of making the lot buildable. We line up the well permit with your other approvals.
  • Reviving a low-producing or dry well. Deepening or replacing a well needs a fresh permit; Riverside County reviews the new construction against current standards and your 500-foot plot plan.
  • Sealing an abandoned well. Old, unused wells must be destroyed and sealed per Bulletin 74-90. This often comes up during a property sale, and we pull the destruction permit and file the report.
  • Agricultural and estate wells. Vineyard, ranch, and large-lot wells common around Menifee may involve larger casing and yield testing, which we document for the county.

Documents and Information You Will Need

To keep your Menifee permit moving without delays, it helps to have a few things ready. Riverside County DEH will want the assessor parcel number and legal description for the property, a clear plot plan showing the proposed well within a 500-foot radius along with septic systems, structures, and any other wells, and the proposed well specifications including target depth, casing size, and perforation zones. If your parcel sits near a watercourse, in a flood zone, or within a special groundwater management area, expect the county to ask for additional detail. When you work with us, we gather and prepare all of this for you, so the only thing you typically need to provide is access to the property and basic ownership information.
