How an Agricultural Well System Works in Menifee

An irrigation or livestock well is a different animal from a residential well. Because Menifee's agricultural wells average around 439 feet deep and can reach far deeper through the mixed alluvium and crystalline basement rock of the Peninsular Ranges, the pump has to overcome significant total dynamic head while still delivering the high, sustained flow that crops and stock need. A typical system pairs a large-diameter submersible pump, sized to the well's tested yield, with a control package that may include a variable frequency drive to match output to demand, a storage tank to buffer peak draw, and a booster or pressurized manifold feeding drip, micro-spray, or flood irrigation lines. The goal is steady delivery without overpumping: drawing the water level down faster than the aquifer recharges damages both the well and the pump.

Common Agricultural Well Scenarios We See in Menifee

  • Declining yield in late summer: Heavy irrigation demand combined with a dropping inland water table can starve an undersized pump. We test yield and resize or stage pumping to match.
  • Pump short-cycling or burnout: Common when a pump is oversized for the well or when a pressure or VFD setting drifts. We diagnose the control package and right-size the system.
  • Mineral scaling and reduced flow: Menifee's hard, mineral-rich groundwater builds scale on screens, drop pipe, and impellers, choking flow over time. Well rehabilitation restores capacity.
  • Off-grid or remote pasture wells: For livestock wells far from power, solar-driven pump systems keep stock tanks full without a utility connection.
  • Power fluctuations: Rural feeders see voltage swings that damage motors and controls. Surge protection and correctly sized wiring protect the investment.

What to Check Before You Call

A few observations help us scope an agricultural well call quickly. Note whether flow has dropped gradually over a season or suddenly, and whether it worsens during peak midday irrigation. Listen to the pump: rapid on-off cycling or grinding points to control or mechanical trouble. Check the pressure gauge at the tank or manifold against its normal range, and watch for air spitting from irrigation heads, which can signal the well drawing down to the pump intake. Look for wet spots along buried mainlines that may indicate a leak bleeding off pressure. If you keep well-completion or pump-installation records, have the depth, horsepower, and tested gallons-per-minute handy. None of this requires special tools, and it lets our technicians arrive prepared.

When to Call a Professional

Agricultural pumps run on higher voltage and heavier equipment than residential systems, and pulling 400-plus feet of drop pipe and a large submersible is not a do-it-yourself job. Call us when yield falls off and will not recover, when the pump short-cycles or trips its breaker, when you lose flow entirely during the irrigation season, when water turns sandy or cloudy, or when you are planning a new planting and need a well sized for the added demand. Well work in Riverside County also requires permits and must follow California Department of Water Resources standards; we handle permitting and ensure the job is done to code, protecting both your operation and your water right.

How much water does an agricultural well in Menifee produce?

How deep are agricultural wells in Menifee?

Menifee's average well depth is around 439 feet, with a recorded range from roughly 20 to 1,800 feet. Shallower wells tap alluvial aquifers near drainages, while deeper wells reach the crystalline basement rock for more reliable supply.

What size pump does my irrigation well need?

Pump sizing depends on depth, total dynamic head, and the flow your crops or livestock require, often 1 to 5 horsepower for typical Menifee operations and larger for high-demand acreage. Can I run an agricultural well on solar power?

Yes. For remote pasture and livestock wells away from utility power, solar-driven submersible pump systems are a reliable, low-operating-cost option that keeps stock tanks and small irrigation zones supplied.

Do I need a permit for an agricultural well in Menifee?

Yes. Riverside County requires permits for new wells and major well work, and the work must meet California Department of Water Resources standards. We handle permitting and compliance as part of the job.
