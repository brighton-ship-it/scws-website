Well Check Valve: What It Does, Signs of Failure & Replacement

Updated February 2026 | By Southern California Well Service

Quick Answer: A check valve is a one-way valve that prevents water from draining back down the well when the pump stops. Signs it's failing: water hammer, pump cycling, loss of pressure, sputtering faucets. Replacement costs $150-$400 for the valve itself, plus labor if it's down-hole (which requires pulling the pump).

What Is a Check Valve?

Purpose

A check valve is a one-way valve that allows water to flow in only one direction—up from the well. When the pump runs, water pushes through. When the pump stops, the valve closes to prevent water from flowing back down.

Why It's Critical

  • Maintains prime: Keeps water in the drop pipe
  • Holds pressure: Prevents system from losing pressure
  • Protects pump: Reduces stress on pump motor
  • Prevents water hammer: Cushions pressure spikes

Where It's Located

  • Foot valve: At bottom of pump (most common)
  • In-line check: Above pump in drop pipe
  • At wellhead: Where pipe exits well
  • Multiple locations: Deep wells often have several

Types of Check Valves

Foot Valve

  • Located at bottom of pump intake
  • Has built-in screen to block debris
  • Most common in submersible pump systems
  • Part of the pump assembly

Swing Check Valve

  • Hinged disc swings open with flow
  • Gravity or spring closes it
  • Common in surface installations
  • Simple, reliable design

Spring Check Valve

  • Spring-loaded disc or ball
  • Closes faster than swing type
  • Better for preventing water hammer
  • Can be installed any orientation

Double Check Assembly

  • Two check valves in series
  • Required for backflow prevention in some areas
  • Testable with ports

Signs of Check Valve Failure

Water Hammer

Loud banging or knocking when pump starts or stops. Water rushing back hits a partially closed valve.

Pump Short Cycling

Pump turns on and off rapidly (every few seconds). Water drains back, pressure drops, pump kicks on again.

Slow Pressure Buildup

Pump runs longer to build pressure. Some water leaks back past failed valve while pump runs.

Sputtering Water

Air and sputtering when first turning on faucet. System lost prime overnight.

Loss of Pressure Overnight

Full pressure at night, low or none in morning. Water drains back while system sits idle.

Pressure Gauge Drops While Pump Off

Watch gauge after pump stops. Should hold steady. Steady drop indicates check valve leak.

Problems Caused by Failed Check Valve

Pump Damage

  • Excessive cycling wears motor
  • Hydraulic shock stresses components
  • Shortened pump life

Higher Energy Bills

  • Pump runs more often
  • Repeatedly lifting same water
  • Can add 20-50% to pumping cost

Pressure Tank Issues

  • Constant cycling stresses tank bladder
  • May cause premature tank failure

Pipe Damage

  • Water hammer stresses fittings
  • Can cause leaks over time

Replacement

When to Replace

  • Any of the failure signs above
  • Age 10-15 years (preventive)
  • When pulling pump for other service

Valve Costs

Installation Costs

  • At wellhead (accessible): $100-$200 labor
  • Down-hole (requires pulling pump): $500-$1,500 labor
  • Down-hole replacement usually done with pump service

DIY or Professional?

  • Accessible check valve at wellhead: DIY possible
  • Down-hole check valve: Professional required (need pump pulling equipment)
  • When in doubt, call a pro

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a check valve do in a well system?

Prevents water from flowing back down the well when the pump stops. Keeps the system primed and maintains pressure.

How do I know if my well check valve is bad?

Signs include water hammer, pump cycling rapidly, loss of pressure overnight, sputtering faucets, and pressure gauge dropping while pump is off.

How long do well check valves last?

Typically 10-15 years, depending on water quality and usage. Hard water and sediment shorten life.

Can I replace a well check valve myself?

If it's at the wellhead (accessible), yes with basic plumbing skills. If it's down-hole, you need to pull the pump—call a professional.

How many check valves does a well system need?

Typically 1-3. Most have at least one at or near the pump. Deep wells may have additional check valves every 200 feet to reduce water column weight.
