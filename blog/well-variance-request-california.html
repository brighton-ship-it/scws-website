Well Variance Requests in California: When & How to Apply

What happens when your property can't meet standard well setback requirements? Rather than giving up on well water, you may be able to request a variance—an official exception that allows well construction with alternative protective measures.

Understanding Well Setback Requirements

California law requires minimum distances between wells and potential contamination sources. These setbacks protect groundwater quality by ensuring contaminated surface water can't easily reach your drinking water supply.

Standard California Setbacks

Feature Minimum Setback
Septic tank 50 feet
Septic leach field 100 feet
Sewer line 50 feet
Property line 5 feet (varies by county)
Domestic animal enclosure 100 feet
Cesspool 150 feet
Landfill/dump 1,000 feet
Chemical storage 50-100 feet (varies)

These requirements come from California Water Well Standards (Bulletin 74-90) and may be modified by local health departments.

When You Might Need a Variance

Common Scenarios

  • Small lots: Properties under 1-2 acres often can't fit a well, septic system, and house with all required setbacks
  • Existing development: Adding a well to a property with an existing septic system in a location that limits options
  • Unusual property shape: Narrow lots or lots with easements that restrict buildable area
  • Neighboring septic systems: Your neighbor's septic may be closer than allowed, even if yours is far away
  • Geological constraints: Only viable drilling location conflicts with setback requirements
  • Replacing existing wells: Old well locations may not meet current standards

What Can Be Varied

Not all setbacks are treated equally:

  • Easier to vary: Property line setbacks, distance to structures
  • Moderate difficulty: Sewer line setbacks, animal enclosure distances
  • Harder to vary: Septic tank setbacks
  • Very difficult: Septic leach field setbacks (highest contamination risk)

San Diego County Variance Process

Step 1: Pre-Application Consultation

Before formally applying, contact the San Diego County Department of Environmental Health (DEH) Well Permitting Program:

  • Explain your situation
  • Ask whether a variance is possible
  • Get guidance on required documentation
  • Understand any conditions that might be required

This consultation can save time and money if the variance is unlikely to be approved.

Step 2: Prepare Documentation

A complete variance request typically includes:

Site Plan

  • Drawn to scale (1" = 20' or similar)
  • Property boundaries with dimensions
  • All existing structures
  • Septic system components (tank, leach field)
  • Proposed well location
  • All setback distances clearly marked
  • North arrow and scale

Narrative Explanation

  • Why standard setbacks cannot be met
  • What alternatives were considered
  • Proposed protective measures
  • Why the proposed location is the best option

Supporting Information

  • Parcel map/assessor's map
  • Existing septic system permit (if available)
  • Soil conditions if known
  • Depth to groundwater if known
  • Neighbor notification if required

Step 3: Submit Application

Submit to DEH with:

  • Completed well permit application
  • Variance request letter
  • All supporting documentation
  • Required fees

Step 4: Review Process

DEH reviews the request, which may include:

  • Site visit by inspector
  • Technical review of soil/groundwater conditions
  • Consultation with other departments if needed
  • Request for additional information

Step 5: Decision

DEH issues a decision:

  • Approved: With specific conditions
  • Approved with modifications: Different location or requirements than requested
  • Denied: With explanation

If denied, you may be able to appeal or revise and resubmit.

Conditions Often Required

Variances typically come with extra requirements to compensate for reduced setbacks:

Enhanced Well Construction

  • Deeper surface seal (grouting to greater depth)
  • Larger casing diameter
  • Additional casing depth
  • Pressure grouting instead of gravity grouting

Well Location Requirements

  • Uphill/upgradient from contamination source
  • Maximum feasible distance achieved
  • Specific positioning relative to structures

Testing Requirements

  • Water quality testing before use
  • Annual testing for bacteria
  • Monitoring well installation in some cases

Other Measures

  • Septic system upgrade or relocation
  • Treatment system installation
  • Drainage improvements
  • Recording of deed restrictions

Tips for Variance Approval

Do Your Homework

  • Understand exactly what setbacks apply to your situation
  • Know the distances you're dealing with
  • Research similar variance approvals in your area

Show Good Faith

  • Demonstrate you've explored alternatives
  • Don't ask for more variance than necessary
  • Be willing to accept reasonable conditions
  • Propose protective measures proactively

Work with Professionals

  • Licensed well driller familiar with variance process
  • Surveyor for accurate site plan
  • Septic designer if relocating/upgrading septic
  • Consider hydrogeologist for complex situations

Be Patient

  • Variance review takes longer than standard permits
  • Expect back-and-forth with the department
  • Budget for additional time and costs

Costs and Timeline

Additional Costs

  • Variance review fee: Varies by county; typically $200-500 in San Diego County
  • Professional site plan: $200-500 if surveyor required
  • Enhanced construction: $500-2,000+ depending on requirements
  • Additional testing: $100-500 for water quality tests

Timeline

  • Standard permit: 2-4 weeks
  • With variance request: 4-12 weeks
  • Complex variances: May take several months

Alternatives to Variances

Before requesting a variance, consider whether you can meet requirements through other means:

Relocate the Septic System

Sometimes moving or replacing the septic system is more practical than getting a well variance. A new septic permit may be easier to obtain than a variance.

Shared Well Agreement

If a neighbor has a compliant well, you might arrange a shared well with legal agreement. This avoids permitting issues entirely.

Connect to Water District

If municipal water is available (even at significant cost), it may be simpler than the variance process.

Purchase Additional Land

In some cases, buying a small adjoining parcel provides enough room for compliant well placement.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a well variance?
A well variance is an official exception to standard well construction requirements—usually setback distances from septic systems, property lines, or other contamination sources. If your property can't meet minimum requirements, you can request a variance that allows construction with alternative protective measures.
How do I get a well variance in San Diego County?
Submit a variance request to the San Diego County Department of Environmental Health (DEH) Well Permitting Program. Include a site plan showing the proposed location, existing septic/contamination sources, property boundaries, and an explanation of why standard setbacks cannot be met. DEH reviews each request individually.
What are the setback requirements for wells in California?
Standard California setbacks include: 100 feet from septic leach fields, 50 feet from septic tanks, 50 feet from sewer lines, 5 feet from property lines, and various distances from other contamination sources. Local jurisdictions may have stricter requirements. San Diego County follows these standards with some local modifications.
Will my variance request be approved?
Approval depends on: the specific setback being reduced, whether alternative protective measures are proposed, site conditions (soil type, depth to groundwater), and the health department's assessment of contamination risk. Requests for reduced septic setbacks are harder to get approved than property line setbacks.
How long does a variance review take?
A standard well permit takes 2-4 weeks. With a variance request, expect 4-12 weeks depending on complexity. Complex variances involving multiple setbacks or requiring additional technical review may take several months.
What happens if my variance is denied?
If denied, you can: request reconsideration with additional information, modify your proposal to address concerns, consider alternatives like relocating the septic system, or appeal the decision through the county's administrative process. Many denials result from incomplete applications that can be corrected.
