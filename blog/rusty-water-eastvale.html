What Causes Rusty Water?

  • Dissolved Iron: Naturally occurring iron in Riverside County groundwater
  • Iron Bacteria: Bacteria that feed on iron, creating rusty slime
  • Corroding Casing: Old steel well casing breaking down
  • Corroding Pipes: Galvanized or iron plumbing in your home
  • Failing Water Heater: Rusting anode rod or tank

Is Rusty Water Safe to Drink?

Iron itself isn't a health hazard at typical concentrations. However, rusty water:

  • Tastes metallic and unpleasant
  • Stains sinks, toilets, and laundry
  • Can clog pipes and fixtures over time
  • May indicate other water quality issues

We recommend water testing to identify the specific cause and concentration of iron in your Eastvale water.

Solutions for Rusty Water

Iron Removal Systems

Water treatment systems designed specifically for iron removal, including oxidizing filters, water softeners, and chemical injection systems.

Well Rehabilitation

Casing Replacement

Severely corroded steel casing may need to be lined or replaced to stop rust contamination.

Plumbing Updates

Replacing old galvanized pipes with PEX or copper eliminates household sources of rust.

Rusty Water in Eastvale: What's Really Going On

Eastvale is one of the newest cities in Riverside County, a master-planned community on the western edge of the Inland Empire where former dairyland has been built out into neighborhoods of relatively new tract and semi-custom homes. Unlike the rural backcountry, Eastvale is not well country: homes here are served by the Jurupa Community Services District, which delivers treated municipal water through a distribution system rather than private wells. So when an Eastvale homeowner turns on a tap and gets orange, brown, or yellow water, the cause is almost never an aquifer problem. It is almost always corrosion inside the home or a temporary disturbance in the public mains.

That distinction changes everything about the fix. In a private-well home, rusty water points to the well, the casing, or the aquifer. In an Eastvale home on JCSD water, the same symptom points to your water heater, your household plumbing, or work happening on the street. The good news is that these are usually straightforward to diagnose and repair, and figuring out which one you have takes only a few minutes of simple testing before any money is spent.

The Common Causes in a Municipal-Water City

  • Water heater corrosion. This is the single most common cause of rusty water in newer suburban homes. A water heater's sacrificial anode rod is designed to corrode so the tank does not, and once it is used up the steel tank begins to rust, pushing discolored hot water out of every faucet.
  • Galvanized or aging interior plumbing. Even relatively newer homes can have galvanized fittings, and any home that has had partial repairs or older sections can shed rust from the inside of the pipe walls, especially after the water has sat overnight.
  • Municipal main flushing or repairs. When JCSD flushes hydrants, repairs a main, or a water-main break disturbs the system, settled iron and mineral sediment in the pipes gets stirred up and can briefly reach your taps. This kind of rust usually clears within a few hours.
  • A neighborhood pressure event. A sudden surge or shutoff and restart can dislodge sediment in the service line and show up as short-lived discoloration.

The quickest diagnostic: if only the hot water is rusty, the water heater is the prime suspect. If both hot and cold are rusty but clear after running for a few minutes, suspect main flushing or sediment in your service line. If cold water stays rusty all the time, suspect your interior plumbing.

Is It Safe to Use?

Iron and the rust it forms are aesthetic, secondary contaminants, so the discoloration itself is generally not an acute health hazard for healthy adults. It is still worth addressing: rusty water tastes metallic, stains laundry and fixtures, and signals that a component, usually your water heater, is deteriorating and may be heading toward a leak or failure. If the discoloration is widespread and persistent across the neighborhood, it is worth contacting JCSD as well, since that points to a system-side issue rather than something in your home. When in doubt, a simple water test removes the guesswork.

How We Fix Rusty Water in Eastvale Homes

Water Heater Service or Replacement

If the rust is on the hot side, the fix is usually the water heater. Replacing a worn anode rod can extend the life of a tank that is caught early, while a tank that has already begun to rust through needs replacement, generally $600 to $1,500 depending on size and type. We can advise whether a flush and anode swap will buy you years or whether the tank is past saving.

Plumbing Repair and Repipe

Where interior galvanized or corroded piping is the source, replacing the affected lines with PEX or copper eliminates the rust at its origin. We can target a problem section or, for a home with widespread old pipe, scope a full repipe.

Whole-House and Point-of-Use Filtration

For homeowners who want extra protection against sediment from the municipal system, a sediment or whole-house filtration setup ($300 to $900 for sediment-focused systems) captures rust particles before they reach fixtures and appliances. If your water is also hard, which is common in the Inland Empire, a softener or combination unit ($1,500 to $3,500) can be added.

Pressure and Booster Solutions

While most Eastvale homes have adequate municipal pressure, properties at the edges of a zone or with their own irrigation demands sometimes benefit from a booster or constant-pressure system ($2,000 to $4,500).

Why a Newer City Still Gets Rusty Water

Homeowners are often surprised to see rusty water in a community as new as Eastvale, but age is relative when it comes to water heaters and fittings. A standard tank water heater's anode rod can be exhausted in as little as five to ten years depending on water hardness, and Inland Empire water is on the harder side, which speeds anode wear. Add in the normal sediment that settles in any municipal distribution system and the occasional main repair, and even a fifteen-year-old subdivision can produce the same orange water you would expect from a much older home. The reassuring part is that the fixes are well understood and rarely involve anything as dramatic as a new well.

What to Check Before You Call

  1. Run both hot and cold taps and note which is discolored.
  2. Let the cold water run two to three minutes to see if it clears.
  3. Check whether neighbors have the same issue, which points to the mains.
  4. Look at the age of your water heater and any rust around its base.
  5. Fill a clear glass and let it sit to see if particles settle.
  6. Note whether the problem is worst first thing in the morning.

When to Call a Professional

Reach out if the rust persists after flushing, if it is isolated to your hot water, if you see moisture or corrosion around the water heater, or if discoloration keeps returning. A rusting water heater can progress to a sudden tank failure and water damage, so catching it early is worth it. Frequently Asked Questions

Why is only my hot water rusty in Eastvale?

That is the classic sign of a water heater problem. The anode rod has worn out and the steel tank is corroding. A flush and anode replacement may help if caught early; otherwise the tank needs replacing.

My whole street has brown water. What happened?

That usually means the Jurupa Community Services District flushed a hydrant, repaired a main, or had a main break that stirred up sediment. It typically clears within a few hours, and you can report persistent issues to JCSD.

Do I have a well? Why would a well company help me?

Most Eastvale homes are on municipal water, not wells. We are a full-water-systems contractor, so we diagnose and fix water heaters, plumbing, filtration, and pressure issues, not just wells.

Is the rusty water going to make us sick?

Iron is an aesthetic contaminant and generally not an acute health hazard at these levels, but it tastes and looks bad and signals corrosion that should be addressed. A water test can confirm what is present.

How much does it cost to fix?

A water heater replacement runs about $600 to $1,500, sediment filtration $300 to $900, and a softener or combination system $1,500 to $3,500. A $125 diagnostic pinpoints the cause and is credited toward the repair.

Can you add a filter to stop sediment from the city water?

Yes. A sediment or whole-house filtration system captures rust and grit before it reaches your fixtures and appliances, and we can pair it with a softener if your water is also hard.
