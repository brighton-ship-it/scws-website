Video Camera Inspection Pricing

Basic camera inspection for wells under 200 feet runs $200-$350. Standard inspection for wells 200-400 feet costs $350-$500. Deep well inspection over 400 feet ranges from $500-$800. Prices include video recording for your records. Some contractors charge per-foot rates of $1-$2 per foot.

What Camera Inspections Reveal

Downhole cameras identify casing cracks, holes, or corrosion damage. They show screen condition and sediment buildup, collapsed or shifted casing sections, and debris, pump parts, or dropped equipment. Cameras also reveal water entry points and aquifer zones.

When You Need a Camera Inspection

Schedule a camera inspection for sudden drops in water production, sand or sediment appearing in water, before purchasing property with an older well, when planning rehabilitation or deepening work, or after earthquake activity in your area.

What to Expect During the Inspection

Pump removal may be required, adding $200-$400. Inspection takes 1-3 hours depending on depth. You can view real-time video with the technician. A digital recording is provided on USB or via email, along with a written report identifying issues and recommendations.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to remove my pump for a camera inspection?

Usually yes. Most residential wells require pump removal to get the camera past. Some contractors have slim cameras that fit alongside the pump, but image quality may be limited. Pump removal adds $200-$400 to the total cost.

How often should I get a camera inspection?

Most wells don't need routine camera inspections. Schedule one if you notice decreased flow, sediment in water, or are planning major work. Pre-purchase inspections for property buyers are highly recommended.

Can a camera inspection tell me how much water my well produces?

No, camera inspections show physical condition only. You need a separate flow test to measure water production. However, cameras can identify problems like collapsed screens that reduce flow.
