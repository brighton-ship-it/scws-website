What is Hydrofracturing?

Hydrofracturing is a well rehabilitation technique that uses high-pressure water to clean and expand existing fractures in the bedrock surrounding your well. This process:

  • Clears mineral deposits and sediment blocking water flow
  • Opens new pathways for groundwater to reach your well
  • Can increase well yield by 2-10 times in many cases
  • Extends the life of your existing well

Unlike oil and gas fracking, well hydrofracturing uses only clean water—no chemicals are involved.

Signs Your Thermal Well Needs Hydrofracturing

  • Declining water production over time
  • Well runs dry during peak usage
  • Slow recovery rate after pumping
  • Pump cycling on and off frequently
  • Lower water pressure than previous years

The Hydrofracturing Process

  1. Initial Assessment: We evaluate your well's current yield and condition
  2. Equipment Setup: Specialized high-pressure equipment is positioned at your well
  3. Isolation: Packers seal off sections of the well for targeted treatment
  4. Pressurization: Water is pumped at 500-3,000 PSI to fracture bedrock
  5. Development: Well is cleaned and tested for improved yield

Cost vs. New Well Drilling in Thermal

In Thermal, drilling a new well can cost $15,000-$50,000+ depending on depth. Hydrofracturing typically costs a fraction of that—often $3,000-$8,000—making it an excellent first option before considering a new well.

Many Thermal wells that appear to be "going dry" can be successfully rehabilitated through hydrofracturing, saving homeowners tens of thousands of dollars.

Hydrofracturing and Wells in Thermal, California

Thermal is an unincorporated community on the floor of the eastern Coachella Valley in Riverside County, southeast of Indio and Coachella and north of the Salton Sea, surrounded by the date groves and agricultural fields that define the valley's farm belt. It is hot, arid desert country, and the way water works here is fundamentally different from the granite foothills where we do most of our hydrofracturing. We will be straightforward: hydrofracturing is usually a weak fit for wells in Thermal, because the geology beneath the valley floor is deep sand-and-gravel basin fill rather than fractured rock. This page explains why, and what the better options are for Thermal property owners.

What Hydrofracturing Does, and What It Needs

Hydrofracturing, also called hydrofracking, is a well-yield enhancement technique designed for wells drilled into hard, fractured crystalline or granitic bedrock. In that kind of well, water enters through a network of narrow cracks in the rock, and over time those cracks clog with sediment and scale, cutting the flow. Hydrofracturing isolates a section of the borehole with an inflatable packer and injects clean water at 1,000 to 3,000 PSI to scour and widen the fractures, restoring flow. The entire premise depends on there being a rock-fracture network to reopen. Where the aquifer is loose sand and gravel instead of fractured rock, there is nothing for the high pressure to open up, and the technique has little to offer.

Why Thermal's Geology Makes Hydrofracturing a Poor Fit

Thermal sits in the Indio subbasin of the Coachella Valley groundwater basin, one of the largest and deepest alluvial aquifers in Southern California. The water-bearing material here is unconsolidated to partly consolidated alluvial and lacustrine deposits, gravel, sand, silt, and clay washed down from the surrounding mountains and laid down over the valley floor. The U.S. Geological Survey estimates the basin holds tens of millions of acre-feet of water down to about 1,000 feet. This is a sand-and-gravel basin, not a fractured-bedrock system, and that distinction is everything for hydrofracturing. A well that draws from this kind of porous, granular aquifer gains nothing from high-pressure fracturing because there are no rock seams to open; the water already moves through the pore spaces between the grains.

It is also worth noting that the eastern and southern Coachella Valley aquifer, including the area around Thermal near the Salton Sea, can carry naturally elevated arsenic and higher dissolved-solids levels from the surrounding sediments and upwelling thermal waters, which makes water treatment, not fracturing, the relevant concern for many wells here.

Farm Country and Deep Basin Wells

Thermal's agricultural heritage shapes its wells. The community has long supported date palms, citrus, table grapes, and row crops, and farm and domestic wells here are typically drilled and screened to draw from the thick alluvial fill that the basin provides in abundance. These are productive wells when they are healthy, precisely because the sand-and-gravel aquifer is so large and water moves freely through it. The flip side is that when one of these wells declines, the cause is almost never a clogged rock fracture, because there is no rock fracture in the first place. It is far more likely to be a fouled or encrusted screen, a falling regional water table, or a pump that has worn out after years of duty in gritty desert water. Treating those causes correctly is what restores a Thermal well, and it is why we lead with a diagnosis rather than a one-size-fits-all treatment.

Many Thermal-area parcels are also served by the Coachella Valley Water District for domestic supply, so if your home is on a district connection rather than a private well, well services of any kind, fracturing included, simply will not apply, and a plumbing or treatment solution is the right direction.

When Hydrofracturing Is and Is Not the Answer

The honest test is simple. Hydrofracturing helps only when a well is:

  • A private well drilled into fractured granite or crystalline bedrock, and
  • Experiencing declining or disappointing yield from clogged rock fractures.

A typical Thermal well, completed in the deep alluvial basin, does not meet the first condition, so fracturing is not the right tool. The better paths for a sand-and-gravel well that has lost capacity are different:

  • Well screen and gravel-pack rehabilitation to clear sediment, scale, and biofouling from the screen that lets water into the casing.
  • Well deepening or a new well if water levels have dropped below the screen or the well has aged out.
  • Water treatment to address arsenic, hardness, or total dissolved solids, which are common quality issues in this part of the valley.
  • Pump system repair or replacement when the real problem is mechanical rather than the aquifer.

What Actually Helps a Thermal Well

Most water complaints we see on the valley floor come down to one of a few things: a worn or undersized pump, a screen clogged with fine sediment, a dropping water table that has left the pump intake too high, or water-quality problems that need filtration. Each of those has a real fix, and none of them is hydrofracturing. Our role in Thermal is to diagnose which of these you are actually facing and recommend the solution that fits, rather than apply a hard-rock technique to a soft-rock basin.

If your Thermal well has lost pressure, your pump is short-cycling, or you are getting sand and air at the tap, reach out for same-day emergency service. Desert wells in deep alluvium have their own failure patterns, and an accurate diagnosis is what keeps you from paying for the wrong fix, including a fracturing treatment that the geology says will not help.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can hydrofracturing increase my Thermal well's yield?

Usually not. Thermal wells are completed in the deep sand-and-gravel alluvium of the Coachella Valley basin, where there are no rock fractures to open. Fracturing is a hard-rock technique and is a weak fit here.

My well's flow has dropped. What should I do instead?

The likely fixes are well screen rehabilitation, deepening, a pump repair, or treatment, depending on the cause. A diagnostic visit identifies which one you need.

Why does my water taste off or test high for arsenic?

Parts of the eastern Coachella Valley aquifer carry naturally elevated arsenic and dissolved solids. A water-treatment system is the appropriate solution, not well fracturing.

Could deepening my well help?

Often yes, if water levels have fallen below your screen or pump. Costs vary with depth and conditions, and we can advise after inspecting the well.

Do you still serve Thermal even if I do not need hydrofracturing?

Absolutely. We handle pump work, new wells, deepening, and diagnostics throughout the Coachella Valley.

Will you tell me honestly if fracturing will not help?

Yes. We would rather give you the straight answer and recommend the option that actually fits a sand-and-gravel basin well.
