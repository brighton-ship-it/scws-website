Well Installation Cost Guide: San Diego County 2026

Published February 2, 2026 | Cost Guide | 17 min read

How much does it cost to install a well? If you're considering a private water well for your San Diego County property, you need accurate cost information to budget properly. Well installation isn't cheap—but it can provide decades of water independence and potentially save you money over municipal water rates.

This guide breaks down well installation costs for San Diego County in 2026, including drilling, equipment, permits, and hidden expenses that can catch homeowners off guard. We've been drilling wells in this region for over 30 years, so these estimates reflect real-world project costs—not national averages that don't apply to Southern California's unique geology.

Quick Cost Summary

Complete residential well installation in San Diego County:

$15,000 – $50,000+

Actual cost depends primarily on depth required. Average depth in San Diego County: 300-500 feet.

Well Installation Cost Breakdown

Understanding where your money goes helps you evaluate quotes and make informed decisions. Here's a detailed breakdown of well installation costs:

Component Cost Range Notes
Drilling $35–$75/foot Main variable cost; geology matters
Casing (steel/PVC) $15–$30/foot Often included in drilling price
Grout sealing $1,000–$2,500 Required by code; protects aquifer
Submersible pump $800–$2,500 Depends on HP and depth
Drop pipe & wire $500–$2,000 Based on depth
Pressure tank $300–$1,200 20-80 gallon typical
Control box & switch $200–$500 Electrical controls
Well cap & seal $100–$300 Sanitary protection
Permits $300–$1,500 County requirements
Water testing $100–$500 Basic to comprehensive
Electrical hookup $500–$2,000 If not included; distance matters

Drilling Cost Per Foot: San Diego County Rates

Drilling is the largest single cost in well installation, and rates vary based on geology:

Lower Cost Areas

$35-$50/foot

  • • Softer alluvial soils
  • • Valley locations
  • • Areas with shallow water tables
  • • Good access for drilling equipment

Examples: Parts of Valley Center, Ramona valleys, some Escondido areas

Higher Cost Areas

$55-$75+/foot

  • • Hard granite rock
  • • Mountain terrain
  • • Deep water tables
  • • Difficult access requiring smaller rigs

Examples: Julian, Cuyamaca, parts of Alpine, mountain communities

Hidden Costs to Budget For

Beyond the basic installation, these additional costs can affect your total investment:

⚠️ Potential Additional Costs

  • Water treatment system: $2,000-$8,000+ if your water has iron, hardness, bacteria, or other issues requiring treatment
  • Storage tank: $1,500-$5,000 if you need additional water storage for low-yield wells
  • Access road improvements: $500-$3,000 if heavy drilling equipment can't reach your site
  • Well that doesn't produce: Rare, but you may need to drill deeper or drill a second hole
  • Trenching to house: $500-$2,000+ if not included, depends on distance
  • Generator hookup: $500-$1,500 for power outage protection

Permits and Regulations in San Diego County

San Diego County requires permits for all new well installations. Here's what you need to know:

San Diego County Well Permit Requirements

  • Permit cost: $300-$1,500 depending on well type and location
  • Processing time: 2-6 weeks typically
  • Setback requirements: Minimum distances from septic systems, property lines, etc.
  • Water testing: Required for domestic wells
  • Well log filing: Mandatory documentation of drilling results
  • Inspections: County inspector must approve installation

Your contractor should handle the permit process. See our San Diego well permit guide for details.

Is a Well Worth the Cost?

A private well is a significant investment. Here's how to evaluate whether it makes financial sense for your situation:

Comparing to Municipal Water Costs

San Diego County has some of the highest water rates in California. Here's a rough comparison:

Municipal Water Costs

  • Average: $150-$300/month for homes with irrigation
  • Agricultural: Often $500-$2,000+/month
  • Rates increase 5-8% annually
  • Subject to drought restrictions

20-year cost: $36,000-$72,000+

Well Water Costs

  • Installation: $15,000-$50,000
  • Electricity: $30-$80/month
  • Maintenance: $200-$500/year average
  • Pump replacement: ~$3,000 every 10-15 years

20-year cost: $25,000-$65,000

Beyond the Numbers

Financial calculations aside, many San Diego County homeowners value wells for:

  • Independence: No water utility bills or rate increases
  • No restrictions: Use water as needed during droughts
  • Reliability: Water supply you control
  • Property value: Wells can increase rural property values
  • Agricultural use: Essential for livestock and irrigation
  • Taste: Many people prefer well water to treated municipal water

Financing Your Well Installation

Few homeowners pay cash for well installation. Here are common financing options:

Home Equity Loan/HELOC

Most popular option. Lower interest rates (often 7-9%), tax-deductible interest, and longer repayment terms.

Personal Loan

Faster approval than HELOCs. Rates of 8-15% typical. Good for smaller wells or when home equity isn't available.

USDA Rural Development

Low-interest loans for qualifying rural properties. Check eligibility by address at rd.usda.gov.

Contractor Financing

Some well contractors partner with lenders. Convenient but compare rates carefully.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to install a well in San Diego County?

Well installation in San Diego County typically costs between $15,000 and $50,000 or more. This includes drilling, casing, pump, pressure tank, electrical connections, and permits. The primary cost driver is well depth—local wells often range from 200 to 600+ feet.

What factors affect well installation cost?

The main factors are: depth required (deeper = more expensive), geology (hard rock costs more), site access (difficult terrain adds cost), pump system size, permit requirements, and water treatment needs based on quality.

How much does well drilling cost per foot in San Diego?

Well drilling in San Diego County typically costs $35-$75 per foot, averaging $45-$55/foot. Harder rock formations like granite cost more than softer soils. A 400-foot well would have drilling costs of roughly $14,000-$30,000 before equipment.

What's included in well installation cost?

Complete installation typically includes: drilling, steel/PVC casing, grout sealing, well cap, submersible pump, drop pipe, pressure tank, pressure switch, electrical wiring, permit fees, and inspection. Water testing may or may not be included.

Do I need a permit to install a well in San Diego County?

Yes, San Diego County requires permits for all new wells. Costs range from $300-$1,500. Your contractor should handle permits and inspections. Drilling without a permit is illegal and can result in fines.

How deep do wells need to be in San Diego County?

Depth varies dramatically by location. Coastal/valley areas may find water at 100-200 feet, while mountain areas often need 400-600+ feet. Some areas require wells over 800 feet deep. Your contractor should research local well logs before estimating.

Is installing a well worth the cost?

A well can be worthwhile for many properties. Benefits include no monthly water bills, water independence, no drought restrictions, and potential property value increase. A well typically pays for itself in 8-15 years compared to municipal water costs.

Can I finance well installation?

Yes, options include: home equity loans/HELOCs (most popular), personal loans, USDA Rural Development loans for qualifying areas, and contractor financing programs. HELOCs typically offer the best rates.
