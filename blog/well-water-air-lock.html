Well Pump Air Lock: What It Is & How to Fix It

Updated February 2026 | By Southern California Well Service

Quick Answer: Air lock = air trapped in pump/pipes, blocking water flow. Fix: open highest faucet to bleed air, run until steady flow. Jet pumps need priming. Submersibles rarely get air locked. If it keeps happening, find the air source.

What Is Air Lock

The Problem

  • Air gets trapped in the pump or pipes
  • Air bubble blocks water flow
  • Pump runs but little/no water comes out
  • May hear pump running without delivering water

Symptoms

  • No water or very little water
  • Sputtering faucets
  • Pump runs but pressure doesn't build
  • Air bursts from faucets

Types of Pumps Affected

  • Jet pumps: Most susceptible
  • Centrifugal pumps: Can air lock
  • Submersible pumps: Rarely air lock (always submerged)

Causes of Air Lock

1. Lost Prime (Jet Pumps)

  • Suction line leak allows air in
  • Low water level exposes suction
  • Bad foot valve lets water drain back

2. After System Work

  • Pipes drained for repairs
  • New pump installation
  • Filter cartridge change

3. Power Outage

  • System drains partially
  • Air enters when water falls back
  • Common with bad check valves

4. Low Water Level

  • Well draws down, pump sucks air
  • Especially during heavy use
  • Drought conditions

5. Leak in Suction Line

  • Even tiny leak can introduce air
  • Connection not tight
  • Cracked fitting

How to Fix It

For Most Systems: Bleed the Air

  1. Turn off pump if running
  2. Open highest faucet in house (usually bathroom sink or shower)
  3. Turn pump back on
  4. Let water run—will sputter at first
  5. Continue until flow is steady (may take minutes)
  6. Close that faucet, open next highest
  7. Repeat until all faucets have steady flow

For Jet Pumps: Priming

Jet pumps need water in the impeller housing to create suction.

  1. Turn off power
  2. Remove priming plug on pump housing
  3. Pour water into pump until full
  4. Replace plug
  5. Turn on power
  6. Watch for water flow
  7. May need to repeat if not catching

Stubborn Air Lock

  • Open drain at pressure tank to release air there
  • Disconnect and refill pipes manually
  • Check for air entry point (leak)

Submersible Pumps

  • Rarely air lock since pump is underwater
  • Air in lines = something else wrong
  • Check for low water level
  • Check for drop pipe leak

Prevention

Check Valves

  • Install foot valve on jet pump suction
  • Check valve prevents backflow
  • Replace if leaking

Fix Leaks

  • Any leak in suction line = air entry
  • Check all fittings
  • Use thread sealant

Monitor Water Level

  • Don't let well run dry
  • Consider low-water cutoff
  • Reduce usage during drought

After Power Outages

  • Be ready to bleed air
  • Check pressure before heavy use

After Service

  • Always bleed air after filter changes
  • Prime jet pumps after any work
  • Run each faucet to clear

Frequently Asked Questions

What is air lock in a well pump?

Air trapped in pump or pipes that blocks water flow. Pump runs but delivers little or no water.

How do I get air out of my well system?

Open highest faucet and let water run until steady. Air will be pushed out. Work down through other faucets.

Why does my well pump keep losing prime?

Usually a leak in the suction line or bad foot valve. Air enters, prime is lost. Find and fix the leak.

Can submersible pumps get air locked?

Rarely—they're always underwater. If you're getting air from a submersible, check water level or look for pipe leaks.

How long does it take to clear air lock?

Usually a few minutes of running water. Severe cases may take longer. If it won't clear, there's an ongoing air source.
