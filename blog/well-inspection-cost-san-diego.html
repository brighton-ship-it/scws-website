What's Included in a San Diego Well Inspection?

A thorough well inspection in San Diego evaluates your entire water system. Here's what qualified inspectors check:

Basic Inspection ($350-$500)

  • Wellhead inspection: Cap seal, casing condition, surface drainage
  • Flow rate test: Gallons per minute measurement (crucial for sizing)
  • Pressure system check: Tank condition, pressure switch operation
  • Pump performance: Amp draw, cycling behavior, runtime
  • Visual equipment assessment: Piping, electrical, general condition
  • Written summary: Basic findings and recommendations

Standard Real Estate Inspection ($400-$600)

Everything in basic, plus:

  • Water quality testing: Bacteria (coliform/E.coli), nitrates, pH
  • Recovery rate test: How quickly the well recharges after pumping
  • Static water level: Measurement of water depth in well
  • Detailed written report: Suitable for real estate transactions
  • Cost estimates: For any recommended repairs

Comprehensive Inspection ($500-$800)

Everything above, plus:

  • Video camera inspection: Visual inspection of casing and screen
  • Extended water panel: Minerals, hardness, TDS, heavy metals
  • Well depth verification: Actual measurement vs. records
  • Historical comparison: Review of well logs and past testing
  • Long-term recommendations: Maintenance schedule and projections

🎯 Which Inspection Do You Need?

Buying a home: Standard real estate inspection minimum; comprehensive if well is over 20 years old or property has been vacant.

Selling a home: Standard inspection shows buyers the well is in good condition and can expedite closing.

Routine maintenance: Basic inspection every 3-5 years; water testing annually.

Problem diagnosis: Comprehensive inspection with video to identify hidden issues.

Factors That Affect Inspection Cost in San Diego

Several factors specific to San Diego County can influence your inspection cost:

1. Well Depth

San Diego wells are often deeper than national averages—300 to 600 feet is common in areas like Ramona, Julian, and Valley Center. Deeper wells require more time for testing and may require specialized equipment for accurate measurements.

2. Well Age and Documentation

Older wells without records require more detective work. If you don't have a well log, the inspector may need to measure depth and research permit records, adding time and cost.

3. Location and Access

Properties in remote areas like Boulevard, Jacumba, or the backcountry near Anza-Borrego may have travel charges. Difficult access to the wellhead can also add time.

4. Water Testing Requirements

Basic bacteria testing is included in standard inspections, but San Diego's geology may warrant additional tests:

  • Basic panel (bacteria, nitrates): Included or +$50-$100
  • Comprehensive panel: +$150-$300
  • Heavy metals (arsenic, uranium): +$75-$150
  • Volatile organic compounds: +$100-$200
  • Full potability panel: +$200-$400

5. Video Camera Inspection

Downhole video inspection lets you see the actual condition of your well casing, screen, and even the pump. This is especially valuable for:

  • Wells over 20 years old
  • Unexplained drops in water production
  • Sand or sediment in water
  • Property purchases with older wells

Video inspection adds $200-$400 to your total but can reveal problems invisible to surface inspection.

When Do You Need a Well Inspection in San Diego?

Buying a Home

This is the most critical time for an inspection. San Diego home prices are high, and discovering well problems after purchase can cost $5,000-$50,000+ to fix. A $400-$600 inspection is cheap insurance that:

  • Reveals the well's true condition and production capacity
  • Identifies potential repair needs you can negotiate
  • Confirms water quality meets drinking water standards
  • Provides documentation for your records and lender

Selling a Home

Proactive sellers get inspections to:

  • Address issues before listing
  • Provide documentation that builds buyer confidence
  • Prevent last-minute surprises that derail closings
  • Justify asking price for well-maintained systems

Routine Maintenance

Even if nothing seems wrong, periodic inspections catch problems early:

  • Water quality testing: Annually
  • Equipment inspection: Every 3-5 years
  • Comprehensive evaluation: Every 10 years or when problems arise

Warning Signs

Schedule an inspection immediately if you notice:

  • Reduced water pressure or flow
  • Sputtering faucets or air in lines
  • Changes in water taste, color, or odor
  • Pump running constantly or cycling frequently
  • Unexplained increase in electric bills
  • Sediment or sand in water

San Diego County's Unique Well Challenges

San Diego's geology and climate create specific conditions inspectors evaluate:

Deep Wells in Fractured Rock

Much of San Diego County sits on decomposed granite. Wells here tap water from fractures in rock rather than underground aquifers. This means:

  • Well production can vary significantly with rainfall
  • Recovery rates may be slower than in sandy soils
  • Water quality can change with drought conditions

Hard Water Issues

San Diego well water is typically very hard (150-400+ ppm). Inspectors check for:

  • Scale buildup in pressure tanks and pipes
  • Water heater and appliance impacts
  • Treatment system needs and recommendations

Drought and Water Table Changes

California's drought cycles affect well performance. A good inspection includes:

  • Static water level measurement
  • Comparison to historical records (if available)
  • Assessment of well depth relative to water level
  • Recommendations if water table has dropped

How to Prepare for Your Well Inspection

Help your inspection go smoothly and get the most value:

  1. Locate your wellhead: Know where it is and ensure access is clear
  2. Gather documentation: Well logs, past inspections, repair records
  3. Note any concerns: Water quality changes, pressure issues, unusual sounds
  4. Ensure power is on: Pump needs electricity to test
  5. Plan for water use: Inspector will run water; normal use is fine during testing
  6. Allow 2-3 hours: For comprehensive inspection; less for basic

Choosing a Well Inspector in San Diego

Not all home inspectors are qualified to evaluate wells. Look for:

  • C-57 Well Drilling License: California's specific license for well work
  • Local experience: Knowledge of San Diego geology and common issues
  • Proper equipment: Flow meters, pressure gauges, water level indicators
  • Lab relationships: For certified water testing
  • Detailed reporting: Written report with photos and data
  • Insurance and bonding: Protects you if something goes wrong

⚠️ Red Flags to Avoid

  • • Inspectors who don't carry proper licensing
  • • "Drive-by" inspections without testing
  • • No written report or documentation
  • • Unusually low prices (may cut corners)
  • • Pressure to immediately drill new well or make repairs

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a well inspection cost in San Diego?

A well inspection in San Diego costs $350-$800 depending on the type. Basic inspections run $350-$500, while comprehensive inspections with water testing and video camera inspection cost $500-$800. Real estate transaction inspections typically fall in the $400-$600 range.

What does a San Diego well inspection include?

A standard San Diego well inspection includes visual assessment of wellhead and equipment, flow rate testing, pressure system evaluation, basic water quality screening (bacteria and nitrates), pump performance check, and a written report. Comprehensive inspections add video camera inspection and extended water quality panels.

Do I need a well inspection when buying a home in San Diego?

Yes, absolutely. San Diego County has unique challenges including deep wells (often 300-600 feet), hard water, and varying water tables. A pre-purchase inspection reveals condition, capacity, and water quality issues that could cost thousands to fix after closing.

How long does a well inspection take in San Diego?

A standard well inspection takes 1-2 hours on-site. Water quality test results typically take 3-5 business days from the lab. Comprehensive inspections with video camera work may take 2-3 hours. We provide preliminary findings same-day with a full written report within a week.

Are well inspections required in San Diego County?

Well inspections aren't legally required for home sales in San Diego County, but they're strongly recommended and often requested by buyers or lenders. Some areas may have local requirements, and FHA/VA loans typically require well water testing.
