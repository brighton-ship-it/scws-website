Immediate Actions After Well Flooding

Do NOT use the well water for any purpose—drinking, cooking, bathing, or even flushing toilets (unless necessary). Turn off the well pump at the breaker to prevent circulating contaminated water through your plumbing. If floodwater is still present, stay away from the wellhead—electrical hazards and contaminated water create serious risks.

Health Risks From Flooded Wells

Floodwater contamination introduces bacteria (E. coli, Salmonella, Cholera), viruses (Hepatitis A, Norovirus), parasites (Giardia, Cryptosporidium), and chemical contaminants (pesticides, fuel, sewage). Symptoms may not appear for days or weeks. Children, elderly, and immunocompromised individuals face highest risk. This is why professional testing is non-negotiable.

Professional Restoration Process

A qualified well technician will: inspect the wellhead and seal for damage, assess electrical components for water damage, pump the well to remove contaminated water (multiple well volumes), perform shock chlorination to kill pathogens, flush the system, and collect samples for laboratory testing. Only after test results confirm safety should you resume using the water.

When to Call for Professional Help

Call a well professional as soon as floodwaters recede enough for safe access. Don't attempt shock chlorination yourself without proper knowledge—incorrect procedures can damage your well or fail to eliminate contamination. If your well casing was submerged, structural damage may require more extensive repairs or even well replacement.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is my well water safe to drink after flooding?

No. Never drink well water after flooding until it's been tested and cleared. Floodwater carries sewage, agricultural runoff, chemicals, and pathogens. Even if water looks clear, dangerous bacteria like E. coli can be present. Get a certified water test before using.

How long after flooding before I can use my well?

Plan for 2-4 weeks minimum. The well needs inspection, possible shock chlorination, pumping to clear contaminated water, and laboratory testing (results take 3-7 days). Don't rush this—waterborne illness can be severe or fatal.

Does homeowner's insurance cover flooded well restoration?

Standard homeowner's policies typically exclude flood damage. If you have flood insurance through FEMA's National Flood Insurance Program, well contamination and restoration may be partially covered. Document everything with photos and keep all receipts.
