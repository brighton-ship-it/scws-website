Choosing the Right Well Installation Contractor

Your contractor choice significantly impacts both installation quality and long-term well performance. Here's how to choose wisely:

Essential Contractor Qualifications

✓ Must-Have Contractor Credentials

✓

California C-57 Well Drilling License

Required by law for well drilling. Verify at CSLB.ca.gov. Do not hire unlicensed drillers—you'll have permit problems and no recourse if issues arise.

✓

Insurance Coverage

General liability ($1M+) and workers compensation. Request certificates of insurance. Drilling involves heavy equipment and significant risk.

✓

Local San Diego County Experience

Area geology, aquifer knowledge, and permit familiarity matter. Ask how many wells they've drilled in your specific area.

✓

Modern Equipment

Updated drilling rigs and pump installation equipment. Old equipment increases risks and project timelines.

✓

Verifiable References

Recent project references in your area. Contact at least 2-3 past customers before hiring.

Red Flags to Avoid

🚩 Warning Signs of Problem Contractors

  • • No valid C-57 license or license in someone else's name
  • • Requests large upfront deposits (50%+) before work begins
  • • Won't provide written estimates or contracts
  • • Can't provide recent local references
  • • Quotes seem dramatically lower than competitors (too good to be true usually is)
  • • Rushes you to sign without time for questions or comparison
  • • Vague about permit requirements or suggests skipping permits

Questions to Ask Contractors

  1. How many wells have you drilled in San Diego County in the past year? Local experience matters for geology and permits.
  2. What's your estimated drilling depth based on area well data? They should have researched local well logs.
  3. What's included in your quoted price? Ensure it covers complete installation, not just drilling.
  4. Do you handle all permit applications? Most reputable contractors manage the entire permit process.
  5. What pump brands do you install and why? Quality pumps from known manufacturers (Grundfos, Franklin, Goulds) last longer.
  6. What warranties do you provide? Look for workmanship warranty plus manufacturer equipment warranties.
  7. What happens if you don't hit water or yield is insufficient? Understand the risk-sharing arrangement upfront.
  8. How do you handle rock formations or difficult drilling? San Diego has diverse geology; experience with hard rock matters.

Well Installation Regulations in San Diego County

California has strict well construction standards to protect groundwater and public health. Understanding key regulations prevents permit problems:

Key Regulatory Requirements

  • Minimum setbacks: 50 feet from septic tanks, 100 feet from leach fields, 100 feet from animal enclosures
  • Casing requirements: Steel or PVC casing to specified depth based on local geology
  • Sanitary seal: Grout seal around casing to prevent surface water intrusion
  • Well cap: Secure, vermin-proof cap with screened vent
  • Depth requirements: Varies by location; typically 100+ feet in San Diego County
  • Completion report: Filed within 30 days of completion with Department of Water Resources
  • Water quality testing: Bacteria testing required before well can be used for drinking water

Permit Violations Are Serious

Unpermitted wells create major problems. The county can require you to abandon (destroy) an unpermitted well at your expense, fine you, and prevent you from selling your property until the violation is resolved. Always obtain proper permits—they protect your investment and ensure legal water rights.

After Installation: Well Startup & Maintenance

Your well installation includes system startup, but long-term performance requires proper care:

First 30 Days After Installation

  • Monitor water quality: Slight discoloration or sediment is normal initially as well settles
  • Watch for leaks: Check all connections and fittings
  • Note pressure readings: Familiarize yourself with normal operating pressure (typically 40-60 psi)
  • Test water again: Retest bacteria after 2-4 weeks to ensure initial test wasn't contaminated
  • Document everything: Keep all permits, completion reports, and contractor documentation

Long-Term Maintenance Schedule

Frequency Maintenance Task
Monthly Visual inspection of wellhead area, check pressure gauge reading
Annually Professional well inspection, test water quality, check pressure tank
Every 3 years Comprehensive water quality panel including minerals and contaminants
Every 10-15 years Expect pump replacement, consider pressure tank replacement

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does water well installation cost in San Diego?

Complete water well installation in San Diego County typically costs $18,000-$50,000+. This includes drilling ($25-$60/foot), pump installation ($2,000-$5,000), pressure system ($800-$2,000), electrical ($1,500-$3,000), and permits ($1,000-$3,000). Well depth is the primary cost driver, with most San Diego County wells ranging 250-600 feet deep.

How long does water well installation take?

Well installation typically takes 3-10 days for the physical work. However, the complete process including permits, site evaluation, and testing can take 4-12 weeks total. Permit approval (2-8 weeks) is usually the longest part of the timeline. Once drilling begins, most residential wells are complete within one week.

Do I need a permit for water well installation in San Diego?

Yes, all new wells in California require permits from San Diego County Environmental Health. You'll need a Well Construction Permit before drilling and must submit completion reports afterward. Your licensed C-57 well drilling contractor typically handles permit applications. Permit costs range $1,000-$3,000 depending on well type and location.

What's included in professional well installation?

Complete well installation includes: site evaluation, permit applications, well drilling to productive aquifer, casing installation, grouting and sealing, pump selection and installation, pressure tank and control system, electrical hookup, water testing, and system startup. Reputable contractors provide turnkey service from permits through final inspection.

How do I choose a well installation contractor?

Choose a licensed California C-57 (Well Drilling) contractor with San Diego County experience. Verify their license is active, check references from recent projects, ensure they carry proper insurance, and get detailed written estimates. Experienced local contractors understand area geology, aquifers, and permitting requirements.
