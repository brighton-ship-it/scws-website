Orange County Well Statistics 2026: 40+ Facts & Data

Updated February 2026 by Southern California Well Service

Orange County, California represents a unique model of groundwater management. Unlike neighboring counties with extensive private wells, Orange County's groundwater is primarily managed by the Orange County Water District (OCWD), which operates one of the world's most advanced groundwater replenishment systems.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many private wells are in Orange County?

Orange County has approximately 1,850 registered wells, but only about 420 are active domestic private wells. Unlike rural counties, most Orange County water supply comes from managed municipal systems. The Orange County Water District provides groundwater to 19 cities and agencies, serving 2.5 million people, with 77% of supply coming from groundwater.

Can I drill a private well in Orange County?

New domestic well permits in Orange County are very limited due to the managed groundwater basin system. The Orange County Water District controls basin pumping rights and charges $459/AF for groundwater extraction. Most residents receive water through municipal systems rather than private wells. Contact OCWD before pursuing a private well permit.

What is the Groundwater Replenishment System (GWRS)?

The GWRS is the world's largest indirect potable reuse facility, treating 130 million gallons per day of recycled water. It injects approximately 100,000 acre-feet annually into the groundwater basin, supplying water for 850,000 people. The $1.1 billion system uses microfiltration, reverse osmosis, and UV treatment.

How does Orange County prevent seawater intrusion?

OCWD operates seawater barriers that inject purified recycled water and imported water into the aquifer to create a hydraulic barrier against seawater intrusion. This program began in 1965 and has effectively protected the basin. The GWRS provides a significant portion of barrier injection water.

What is the Orange County groundwater quality?

Orange County groundwater quality is excellent, with average TDS of 350 ppm and all regulated contaminants below maximum contaminant levels. OCWD conducts monthly water quality monitoring. The GWRS achieves 95% contaminant removal efficiency, producing water that exceeds all state and federal drinking water standards.

How much does groundwater cost in Orange County?

The OCWD basin pumping charge is $459 per acre-foot (2024). This fee funds basin replenishment, water quality protection, and infrastructure maintenance. The 19 member agencies pump approximately 325,000 acre-feet annually. Anaheim is the largest pumper at 45,000 AF/year.
