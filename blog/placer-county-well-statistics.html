Placer County Well Overview

Placer County spans from the Sacramento Valley floor through the Sierra Nevada foothills to Lake Tahoe. This diverse geography creates varied groundwater conditions, from alluvial valley aquifers near Lincoln to fractured granite in the mountains.

Key Placer County Well Facts

  • 68,000+ residents rely on private wells for drinking water
  • 18% of Placer County population depends on domestic wells
  • 1,502 square miles of county area
  • 15.2% increase in well permits during 2025 vs. 2024 (highest growth rate in region)
  • Rapid population growth in Lincoln and surrounding areas driving demand
  • Average well age: 26 years
  • 20% of domestic wells are over 40 years old
  • Western vs. Eastern: Very different geology and aquifer characteristics

Lincoln/Sheridan Area

  • Total wells: 4,650
  • Primary aquifer: Valley alluvium, some fractured rock
  • Average depth: 245 feet
  • Typical yield: 10-35 GPM
  • Water quality: Generally good; some hardness, occasional nitrates
  • Rapid development: Many new permits for rural residential parcels

Granite Bay

  • Total wells: 2,980
  • Primary aquifer: Fractured granite/gabbro
  • Average depth: 310 feet
  • Typical yield: 5-20 GPM
  • Water quality: Good; low TDS, some iron
  • Notes: Large-lot residential community heavily dependent on wells

Foresthill

  • Total wells: 1,720
  • Primary aquifer: Fractured granite
  • Average depth: 425 feet
  • Typical yield: 2-12 GPM
  • Water quality: Excellent; very low TDS
  • Challenges: Hard rock drilling, deeper wells required

Permit Requirements

  • Domestic well permit fee: $1,420 (2026)
  • Processing time: 10-14 business days
  • Setback from septic: 100 feet minimum
  • Water quality test: Required within 30 days
  • Well completion report: Filed with DWR within 60 days

Frequently Asked Questions

How many private wells are in Placer County?

Placer County has approximately 28,400 registered private water wells as of 2026, with about 22,500 active domestic wells serving residential properties.

What is the average well depth in Placer County?

The average residential well depth is 345 feet, varying from 195 feet in the Tahoe Basin to 485 feet in Alta/Dutch Flat areas.

How much does it cost to drill a well in Placer County?

Average complete well system cost is $48,500 in 2026, ranging from $28,000 in favorable areas to over $105,000 for deep wells in hard rock.

What is the groundwater trend in Placer County?

Groundwater levels remain relatively stable in the foothills, with slight declines of 3-6 feet in western valley areas due to development.

Do I need a permit to drill a well in Placer County?

Yes, a well construction permit from Placer County Environmental Health is required. The fee is approximately $1,420 for domestic wells (2026).
