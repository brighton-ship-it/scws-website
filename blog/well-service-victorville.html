Understanding Victorville's Groundwater

Successful well ownership in the High Desert requires understanding the unique hydrogeology that determines where water can be found and how to access it reliably.

The Mojave River Basin

Victorville sits within the Mojave River groundwater basin, one of the High Desert's most important aquifer systems. The Mojave River—though usually dry on the surface—flows underground through alluvial sediments, providing the primary water source for wells near the river corridor. Properties closer to the historic river channel typically enjoy better well production and shallower drilling depths.

Groundwater Challenges in the High Desert

The High Desert faces ongoing groundwater challenges that affect well owners. Decades of pumping have lowered water tables in some areas, meaning wells drilled years ago may no longer perform adequately. The Mojave Water Agency manages the basin through adjudication, but individual well owners must still cope with changing conditions.

Common Well Problems in the High Desert

The extreme conditions of the High Desert create unique challenges for well systems. Understanding these common issues helps you recognize problems early.

Declining Well Production

The most common complaint from Victorville well owners is gradually decreasing water output. This can result from lowering water tables requiring a deeper pump setting, worn pumps losing efficiency, or mineral buildup restricting flow. A proper diagnosis determines the most cost-effective solution—sometimes a simple adjustment, other times a more significant intervention.

Sand and Sediment in Water

High Desert aquifers consist largely of alluvial sand and gravel. When wells aren't properly constructed or water tables drop, pumps can draw sand that damages equipment and makes water unusable. We can install sand separators, adjust pump depth, or repair deteriorated well screens to eliminate sediment problems.

Electrical Failures

Rural High Desert properties often experience voltage fluctuations that stress pump motors and control systems. Additionally, extreme heat accelerates degradation of electrical components. Pump control boxes, capacitors, and wiring may fail more frequently than in milder climates. Quality surge protection and properly sized wiring help prevent these issues.

Hard Water and Mineral Deposits

High Desert groundwater is typically very hard, with high calcium and magnesium content that causes scale buildup in pipes, water heaters, and fixtures. While not a health concern, untreated hard water reduces appliance efficiency and leaves deposits throughout your plumbing system. Water softeners are essential for most Victorville well owners.

Frequently Asked Questions

How deep are wells in Victorville, CA?

Wells in Victorville typically range from 200 to 600 feet deep. Properties closer to the Mojave River often find water at shallower depths (200-350 feet), while those in outlying areas or higher elevations may require 400-600 feet or more. The High Desert's groundwater levels have declined in some areas, making proper depth planning essential.

How much does well pump repair cost in Victorville?

Well pump repair in Victorville typically costs between $500 and $2,500 depending on the problem. Complete pump replacement, including a new submersible pump and labor, ranges from $2,500 to $5,000 for most residential wells. Deeper wells or larger pumps cost more.

Why is my Victorville well producing sand?

Sand in your well water usually indicates a deteriorating well screen, pump set too deep, or declining water table causing the pump to draw from sandy zones. This is common in the High Desert's alluvial aquifers. We can diagnose the cause and recommend repairs or modifications to eliminate the problem.
