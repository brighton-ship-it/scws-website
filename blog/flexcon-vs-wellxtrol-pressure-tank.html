Understanding Pressure Tanks

Before comparing brands, let's ensure you understand what a pressure tank does and why it matters:

How Bladder Pressure Tanks Work

Modern pressure tanks use a bladder system:

  • Bladder: A flexible membrane that separates water from air
  • Air charge: Compressed air behind the bladder (pre-charged at factory)
  • Water side: Fills with water from your pump
  • Function: As water enters, it compresses the air; when you open a tap, the compressed air pushes water out

Why Tank Quality Matters

A quality pressure tank provides:

  • Consistent pressure: Even flow from every fixture
  • Pump protection: Prevents short-cycling that kills pumps
  • Energy savings: Fewer pump starts means lower electricity bills
  • Water on demand: Immediate flow without waiting for pump to start

A failing tank can cause your pump to cycle every few seconds—dramatically shortening its lifespan and wasting energy.

The Two Major Brands

Well-X-Trol by Amtrol

Amtrol has been making expansion tanks since 1946 and introduced the Well-X-Trol line specifically for well systems. Key characteristics:

  • Heritage: The original premium bladder tank
  • Construction: Heavy-gauge steel shell with thick butyl bladder
  • Manufacturing: Made in USA (Rhode Island)
  • Reputation: The "name brand" many professionals specify
  • Innovation: Introduced many features now industry standard

Flexcon Industries

Flexcon has been manufacturing pressure tanks with 50+ years combined experience and has grown to be a major competitor. Key characteristics:

  • Value focus: Comparable quality at competitive prices
  • Construction: Heavy-gauge steel with quality butyl bladder
  • Manufacturing: Made in USA (Massachusetts)
  • Market position: Strong challenger that's earned professional respect
  • Product range: Full line of residential and commercial tanks

Construction & Build Quality

The Steel Shell

Both brands use heavy-gauge steel for their tank shells:

  • Well-X-Trol: Uses their proprietary deep-drawn steel forming process, resulting in seamless construction with no welds in the tank body
  • Flexcon: Uses roll-formed and welded construction, but with quality welds and thorough testing

In practice, both construction methods produce reliable tanks. The Well-X-Trol seamless design is theoretically superior, but we rarely see shell failures in either brand before the bladder fails.

The Bladder

The bladder is typically what fails first in any pressure tank. Here's how they compare:

🔍 Bladder Comparison

Well-X-Trol: Uses their "Controlled Action" bladder design with a thicker, more durable butyl rubber membrane. The bladder is specifically designed to flex in a controlled pattern to reduce stress points.

Flexcon: Uses a quality butyl rubber bladder with good flexibility and durability. Slightly thinner material than Well-X-Trol but performs well in normal conditions.

In our experience, Well-X-Trol bladders do last slightly longer on average—perhaps 2-3 years more. Whether that justifies the price premium depends on your situation.

Coatings and Corrosion Protection

Both brands apply protective coatings:

  • Exterior: Both use baked-on enamel or epoxy coating
  • Interior: The bladder isolates water from the steel shell, preventing interior corrosion
  • Connection fittings: Both use stainless steel or brass fittings

Sizing Guide for Both Brands

Proper sizing is more important than brand choice. An undersized tank will cause short-cycling regardless of quality.

Understanding Drawdown

"Drawdown" is the usable water capacity—the amount of water you get between pump cycles:

  • A "86 gallon" tank doesn't hold 86 gallons of usable water
  • Typical drawdown is about 25-30% of total tank volume
  • An 86-gallon tank provides approximately 25-28 gallons of drawdown

The One-Minute Rule

A good rule of thumb: your tank should provide enough drawdown for at least one minute of pump run time. This means:

  • 10 GPM pump × 1 minute = 10 gallons minimum drawdown
  • Most experts recommend 2 minutes for pump longevity
  • Larger tanks = fewer pump cycles = longer pump life

What Causes Pressure Tanks to Fail?

Common Failure Modes

Understanding failure helps you maintain your tank:

  • Bladder rupture: Most common failure; bladder develops hole or tear
  • Waterlogging: Tank fills with water, loses air charge, causes rapid cycling
  • Air leak: Valve core or bladder seal leaks, losing pressure
  • Corrosion: External rust (cosmetic) or internal if bladder fails

Factors That Shorten Tank Life

  • Chlorine/chloramine: Accelerates rubber breakdown
  • High minerals: Sediment can stress bladder
  • Short-cycling: Undersized tank causes excessive flexing
  • Wrong air charge: Improper pre-charge damages bladder
  • Temperature extremes: Tanks in unheated spaces may age faster

⚠️ Signs Your Tank Is Failing

  • • Pump cycles rapidly (every few seconds)
  • • Tank feels heavy (waterlogged) when empty
  • • Air spurting from faucets
  • • Pressure fluctuates wildly
  • • Visible rust or damage on shell

Frequently Asked Questions

Which is better, Flexcon or Well-X-Trol pressure tank?

Both are quality brands with slight differences. Well-X-Trol (by Amtrol) has a longer track record and slightly more robust bladder design. Flexcon offers excellent value with comparable performance. For most residential wells, either brand will perform well for 10-15 years. We slightly prefer Flexcon for value and Well-X-Trol for maximum longevity.

How long do Flexcon and Well-X-Trol tanks last?

Both brands typically last 10-15 years with proper installation and normal water quality. The bladder usually fails before the tank itself. Factors affecting lifespan include water quality (chlorine, minerals), proper air charge maintenance, and whether the tank is sized correctly to avoid short-cycling.

What size pressure tank do I need for my well?

Tank size depends on pump flow rate and desired cycle time. General rule: tank should provide at least 1-2 minutes of run time per cycle. For a 10 GPM pump at 40/60 pressure, an 86-gallon tank provides about 25 gallons of drawdown. Most residential wells use 44-86 gallon tanks; larger homes or multiple fixtures may need 119+ gallon tanks.

Are pressure tank bladders replaceable?

Yes, both Flexcon and Well-X-Trol offer replacement bladders for some models. However, replacement is often impractical—labor cost approaches that of new tank installation, and other tank components may be aged. Most technicians recommend full replacement rather than bladder-only replacement for tanks over 8-10 years old.

What's the proper air charge for my pressure tank?

Air charge should be set to 2 PSI below your cut-in (pump start) pressure. For a 40/60 system (pump starts at 40, stops at 60), set air charge to 38 PSI. Check air pressure with the tank empty (water drained). Both Flexcon and Well-X-Trol come pre-charged to approximately 38 PSI from the factory.

The Bottom Line

Both Flexcon and Well-X-Trol make excellent pressure tanks that will serve you well. The differences are relatively minor:

  • Well-X-Trol offers slightly better construction and potentially longer lifespan, justifying a modest premium for those who value longevity.
  • Flexcon offers comparable performance at a better price point, making it an excellent value for budget-conscious homeowners.

More important than brand choice is proper sizing and installation. An undersized cheap tank will cause problems regardless of brand, while a properly sized tank of either brand will provide years of reliable service.
