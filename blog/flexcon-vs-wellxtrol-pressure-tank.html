Flexcon vs Well-X-Trol Pressure Tank

When it's time to replace your well pressure tank, Flexcon (by Flexcon Industries) and Well-X-Trol (by Amtrol) are two of the most trusted names in the industry. Both offer quality bladder-style tanks, but there are differences worth understanding. As Southern California well contractors for 30+ years, we've installed thousands of both brands. Here's our honest comparison.

Quick Answer: Which Is Better?

Both are excellent quality tanks. Well-X-Trol is more common and slightly less expensive. Flexcon has a reputation for slightly longer lifespan in our experience. For most homeowners, either brand will serve you well for 7-12 years. We install whichever is available at the best price when you need service.

Detailed Comparison

Build Quality

Both brands: Heavy-gauge steel construction, high-quality butyl rubber bladders, durable epoxy coatings, NSF-certified for potable water.

Flexcon: Slightly heavier tank wall thickness in comparable models. Some contractors report marginally better corrosion resistance over time.

Well-X-Trol: Excellent quality with massive market presence. Their tanks consistently perform well in all climates.

Bladder Durability

Both use butyl rubber bladders, which are the industry standard for durability and resistance to chlorine and other water treatment chemicals.

Anecdotal observation from our field experience: Flexcon bladders seem to last slightly longer on average (1-2 years more), but this could be due to sample size and specific water conditions. Both regularly exceed their 5-year warranty.

Size Options

Both offer: Complete range from 2-gallon to 120-gallon tanks for residential use, plus larger commercial sizes.

Most common residential sizes:

  • 20-gallon: Small homes, low GPM wells
  • 40-gallon: Standard for most homes
  • 60-80 gallon: Large homes, high water demand, or lower-yield wells

Price

Well-X-Trol typically costs 10-15% less than comparable Flexcon tanks. This isn't due to quality differences but market positioning and economies of scale (Amtrol is the larger company).

Installed pricing examples (San Diego area, 2026):

  • 40-gallon Flexcon: $900-$1,100 installed
  • 40-gallon Well-X-Trol: $800-$1,000 installed
  • 60-gallon Flexcon: $1,100-$1,400 installed
  • 60-gallon Well-X-Trol: $1,000-$1,300 installed

Availability

Well-X-Trol has better distribution and availability. Most supply houses stock them. If you need same-day replacement, Well-X-Trol is more likely to be in stock.

Flexcon may require ordering, especially for less common sizes. Lead time can be 1-3 days.

Warranty

Both offer 5-year warranties, which is standard for quality pressure tanks. Both manufacturers honor warranties without excessive hassle in our experience.

What About Other Brands?

While Flexcon and Well-X-Trol dominate the quality market, other options exist:

Goulds (by Xylem)

Another excellent USA-made brand, comparable to Well-X-Trol in quality and price. Less common in Southern California but equally good.

Budget/Generic Brands

Our recommendation: Avoid no-name pressure tanks.

Budget tanks from unknown manufacturers often fail within 3-5 years. The $100-$150 you save initially will cost you more in repeat replacement labor. Stick with Flexcon, Well-X-Trol, Goulds, or other established brands.

Which Should You Choose?

Choose Flexcon if:

  • You want the absolute longest lifespan (marginal difference)
  • Price difference is negligible in your situation
  • Your contractor has it in stock and recommends it

Choose Well-X-Trol if:

  • You want to save 10-15% on cost
  • You need same-day replacement (better availability)
  • Your contractor stocks and prefers it

Honest Contractor Opinion

After installing thousands of both brands over 30+ years, here's the truth: The difference between Flexcon and Well-X-Trol is minimal. Both will give you 7-12+ years of reliable service if properly sized and maintained.

What matters MORE than brand:

  • Correct sizing for your well and household needs
  • Proper installation with correct pre-charge pressure
  • Water quality – aggressive water shortens any tank's life
  • Pump cycling frequency – excessive cycles wear out bladders faster

Sizing: More Important Than Brand

An incorrectly sized tank, regardless of brand, causes problems:

Too Small:

  • Pump cycles too frequently
  • Shortened pump and bladder lifespan
  • Pressure fluctuations

Too Large:

  • Water sits stagnant longer (water quality concerns)
  • Unnecessary cost
  • Takes up more space

Proper Sizing Guidelines:

Tank size depends on pump GPM and pressure switch settings. Quick reference:

  • 7-10 GPM pump: 20-30 gallon tank
  • 10-15 GPM pump: 40-60 gallon tank
  • 15-20 GPM pump: 60-80 gallon tank

Installation Matters

Even the best tank will fail prematurely if installed incorrectly:

  • Pre-charge pressure must be correct: Typically 2 PSI below cut-in pressure
  • Tank must be properly supported: Secured to prevent movement
  • Connections must be sealed: No leaks at tank inlet
  • Orientation matters: Vertical tanks should be vertical, horizontal tanks horizontal
