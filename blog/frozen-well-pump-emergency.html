Immediate Response to Frozen Well System

If your well stops flowing during freezing weather: check the weather (if above freezing soon, minor freezes may self-resolve). Open faucets slightly to allow flow when thawing occurs. Turn off the pump at the breaker to prevent damage from running against a blockage. Identify the frozen section by touch (frozen pipes are ice-cold) and begin safe thawing.

Safe Thawing Procedures

Work from the faucet back toward the frozen section so melted water can escape. For accessible pipes, wrap with towels soaked in hot water and refresh frequently. Use a hair dryer on the low or medium setting, moving constantly to avoid concentrated heat. Heat tape can be wrapped and left in place. Never use propane torches, kerosene heaters, or other open flames near pipes or structures.

Inspecting for Freeze Damage

Once water flows, inspect carefully: look for spurting water at fittings, wet spots on walls or floors, puddles near pressure tank, and hissing sounds indicating pinhole leaks. Check the pressure tank for cracks or deformation. Inspect the wellhead seal and any exposed piping. Even small cracks will worsen with next freeze cycle.

Preventing Future Freeze Damage

Install heat tape on vulnerable pipes and keep it plugged in during freeze warnings. Insulate exposed pipes with foam insulation and weatherproof wrap. Ensure pump houses and pressure tank areas are heated or well-insulated. In extreme cold, let a small stream of water run overnight (moving water resists freezing). Install a low-temp alarm in pump houses to alert you before freezing occurs.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which parts of my well system can freeze?

Vulnerable components include: exposed pipes above ground, the pressure tank if in unheated space, piping in uninsulated crawl spaces, and even the wellhead if above the frost line. The submersible pump itself rarely freezes (it's below frost depth), but the water in pipes leading from it can.

How do I thaw frozen well pipes safely?

Never use open flame—this causes fires and can crack pipes. Safe thawing methods: wrap pipes with hot towels and replace repeatedly, use a hair dryer or heat gun on low, apply electrical heat tape, or use portable space heaters in enclosed areas. Thaw from faucet toward frozen section to allow water escape.

Can frozen pipes burst and damage my well system?

Yes. Water expands when freezing and can crack pipes, fittings, and even pressure tanks. Burst pipes often aren't visible until thawing, when water starts flooding. After any freeze event, inspect the entire system for leaks once water flows again.
