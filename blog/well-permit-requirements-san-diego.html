Well Permit Requirements in San Diego County

Updated February 2026 | By Southern California Well Service

Quick Answer: All water wells in San Diego County require permits from the County Department of Environmental Health. Permit fees run $800-$1,500+. Processing takes 2-4 weeks. Licensed C-57 well drillers typically handle permit applications as part of their service. Work without permits can result in fines and complications when selling property.

Types of Well Permits

New Well Permit

  • Required for drilling any new water well
  • Includes site evaluation and approval
  • Most comprehensive permit type

Well Modification Permit

  • Deepening an existing well
  • Changing casing
  • Rehabilitating a well

Well Repair Permit

  • Major repairs (not routine maintenance)
  • Pump replacement usually doesn't require permit
  • Check with DEH if unsure

Well Destruction/Abandonment Permit

  • Required to properly abandon an unused well
  • Prevents contamination pathway
  • Required before selling property with abandoned well

Permit Requirements

Who Can Apply

  • Property owner
  • Licensed C-57 well drilling contractor (on owner's behalf)

Required Information

  • Property address and APN (Assessor's Parcel Number)
  • Site plan showing proposed well location
  • Distance to septic systems, property lines, buildings
  • Proposed well depth and construction
  • Licensed contractor information

Who Does the Work

All well drilling and major well work must be performed by a California-licensed C-57 Well Drilling Contractor.

Permit Costs

Permit Type Approximate Fee
New well permit $800-$1,500

Note: Fees change periodically. Check with DEH for current fees.

Additional Costs May Apply

  • Environmental review (some areas)
  • Special district fees
  • Groundwater sustainability fees (some basins)

Application Process

Step 1: Site Evaluation

  • Determine setback requirements
  • Identify any special restrictions
  • Your driller usually handles this

Step 2: Submit Application

  • Complete application form
  • Include site plan with measurements
  • Pay permit fee

Step 3: County Review

  • DEH reviews application (2-4 weeks typical)
  • May request additional information
  • Coordinates with other agencies if needed

Step 4: Permit Issued

  • Permit valid for specified period (typically 1 year)
  • Work must begin within timeframe

Step 5: Inspections

  • Pre-drilling inspection (sometimes)
  • Final inspection after completion
  • Water quality testing may be required

Step 6: Well Completion Report

  • Driller files completion report with state
  • Documents depth, construction, yield
  • Becomes permanent record

Setback Requirements

Minimum Distances from Well

Feature Minimum Distance
Septic tank 50 feet
Septic leach field 100 feet
Sewer line 50 feet
Property line 10-20 feet (varies)
Animal enclosures 50-100 feet

Note: These are general guidelines. Actual requirements may vary by location.

Special Considerations

  • Some areas have groundwater management plans
  • Certain zones may restrict new wells
  • Environmental sensitive areas have additional requirements

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a permit to drill a well in San Diego County?

Yes. All water wells require permits from County DEH. This includes new wells, modifications, and abandonment.

How long does it take to get a well permit?

Typically 2-4 weeks for standard permits. Complex projects or special circumstances may take longer.

Do I need a permit to replace my well pump?

Generally no. Routine maintenance and pump replacement typically don't require permits. Major modifications do.

What happens if I drill without a permit?

You may face fines, be required to destroy the well, and have problems selling your property. Unpermitted wells create liability issues.

Can I drill a well anywhere on my property?

Location is limited by setback requirements from septic systems, property lines, and other features. Some areas have additional restrictions.

Who handles the permit—me or the driller?

Most drilling contractors handle permit applications as part of their service. They have experience navigating the process efficiently.
