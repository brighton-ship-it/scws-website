Avocado Water Demands

Avocados are thirsty trees:

  • Mature tree: 40-70 gallons per day in summer
  • Per acre: 4-6 acre-feet per year
  • Critical periods: Fruit set and sizing

A reliable well is essential for profitable avocado production in San Diego County.

Chloride Sensitivity

Avocados are highly sensitive to chloride in irrigation water. If your South Park well has elevated chloride:

  • Blending with lower-chloride water source
  • Leaching irrigation to flush salts
  • Rootstock selection for salt tolerance
  • Regular soil and leaf testing

How a Private Well System Actually Works

A modern residential or small-agricultural well in San Diego County has five main parts working together. Understanding them makes troubleshooting far less mysterious.

  • The well and casing: a drilled borehole, usually 6 to 8 inches in diameter, lined with steel or PVC casing. In the granite and fractured bedrock common across inland San Diego County, wells frequently run 200 to 600 feet deep to reach a dependable water-bearing fracture.
  • The submersible pump: set down in the water column, it pushes water up to the surface. Most residential pumps draw 1/2 to 5 horsepower depending on depth and demand.
  • The pressure tank: stores water under pressure so the pump isn't forced to start every time you open a tap. A healthy tank cycles the pump less and makes it last longer.
  • The pressure switch and controls: the inexpensive but critical component that tells the pump when to turn on and off, typically between 40 and 60 psi.
  • Treatment and storage (when needed): sediment filters, softeners, iron or manganese removal, and storage tanks that buffer peak irrigation demand.

When any one of these fails, the symptom often shows up somewhere else in the system, which is why a methodical diagnosis beats guesswork.

Common Well Scenarios and What Causes Them

Across the inland San Diego County properties we serve, a handful of issues account for the majority of service calls.

Sudden Loss of Water

No water at any fixture usually points to a tripped breaker, a failed pressure switch, a burned-out pump, or a control box problem. Less often it is a dropped water table after a dry stretch. Because the same symptom has several causes, this is the classic case where a $125 diagnostic visit saves you from replacing the wrong part.

Low or Fluctuating Pressure

If pressure sags when two fixtures run, or the pump rapidly clicks on and off, the most common culprit is a waterlogged pressure tank that has lost its air charge. Other causes include a clogged sediment screen, a failing pump, or a leak in the line between the well and the house.

Gritty, Cloudy, or Smelly Water

Sediment in the water can mean a worn pump pulling sand, or a screen problem down the well. A rotten-egg odor usually signals hydrogen sulfide or sulfur bacteria, while reddish-brown staining points to iron and manganese. None of these are unusual in our region's mineral-rich groundwater, and all are treatable.

Short Pump Life

Pumps that fail every few years are often a symptom of rapid cycling, oversized or undersized equipment, mineral scaling, or electrical issues from rural power fluctuations. Fixing the underlying cause is cheaper than replacing pumps on repeat.

What You Can Check Before You Call

A few safe checks help you describe the problem accurately and sometimes solve it outright.

  1. Check the breaker. Find the well pump breaker in your panel and reset it once. If it trips again immediately, stop and call a professional; that is an electrical fault, not a nuisance trip.
  2. Read the pressure gauge. Note the psi at the pressure tank. A gauge stuck at zero or pinned high tells a technician a lot before they arrive.
  3. Tap the pressure tank. Knock on the tank from top to bottom. A healthy tank sounds hollow up top and solid at the bottom. If it sounds full of water all the way up, it is likely waterlogged.
  4. Listen at the wellhead. A faint hum may mean the pump is running but not delivering; silence with the breaker on may mean a failed pump or control.
  5. Note the timing. Did the problem start after a storm, a power outage, or gradually over weeks? That history narrows the diagnosis.

Avoid pulling wiring, opening the control box, or trying to lift a submersible pump yourself. Those tasks involve high voltage and heavy equipment best left to crews with the right tools.

No. South Park is a dense urban neighborhood near Balboa Park, not farmland. Commercial avocado groves in San Diego County are concentrated inland and north, around areas like Valley Center, Fallbrook, and Pauma Valley. We serve those growers from our Ramona office.

Is my South Park home on a private well?

Almost certainly not. South Park is served by City of San Diego municipal water. Private wells in the county are typically on larger inland and rural parcels. We are happy to confirm your situation if you are unsure.

How deep are wells in inland San Diego County?

It varies widely with the fractured granite geology, but many inland wells fall in the 200 to 600 foot range. Depth depends on where a productive water-bearing fracture is found, which is why local drilling experience matters.
