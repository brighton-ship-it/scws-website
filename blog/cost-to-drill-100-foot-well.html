Cost to Drill a 100-Foot Well in Southern California

If you're considering drilling a well on your property in Southern California, understanding the costs involved is essential for proper planning and budgeting. A 100-foot well represents one of the shallower drilling options and is often suitable for properties where groundwater tables are relatively close to the surface. In this comprehensive guide, we'll break down everything you need to know about the cost to drill a 100-foot well, including pricing factors, what's included in professional drilling services, and how to ensure you get the best value for your investment.

Average Cost to Drill a 100-Foot Well

The cost to drill a 100-foot well in Southern California typically ranges from $3,500 to $7,500, with most homeowners paying around $5,000 to $6,000 for a complete installation. This price generally includes the drilling itself, basic casing, and a well cap. However, several factors can push this cost higher or lower depending on your specific situation and location.

It's important to understand that the per-foot drilling cost in Southern California generally ranges from $35 to $75 per foot, depending on the drilling method required and soil conditions. For a 100-foot well, this base drilling cost would be between $3,500 and $7,500 before any additional components or services are added.

Factors That Affect 100-Foot Well Drilling Costs

Soil and Rock Conditions

The geological makeup of your property is perhaps the most significant factor affecting drilling costs. Southern California features diverse terrain, from sandy coastal soils to hard granite in mountainous regions. Drilling through soft soil or sand is considerably less expensive than boring through solid rock. If your property sits on rocky terrain, expect to pay a premium for the specialized equipment and additional time required to penetrate harder materials.

Drilling Method

Several drilling methods exist, each with different cost implications. Rotary drilling is common for deeper wells and harder soils, while cable tool drilling may be used in certain conditions. Air rotary drilling is often employed in rocky areas. Your well drilling contractor will assess your property and recommend the most appropriate and cost-effective method for your specific conditions.

Location and Accessibility

The accessibility of your drilling site plays a role in overall costs. If the drilling rig can easily access the location, costs stay lower. However, if your property has limited access, steep terrain, or requires the drilling team to navigate around obstacles, additional labor and equipment costs may apply. Remote locations may also incur travel fees.

Permits and Regulations

Southern California has specific regulations governing well drilling, and permit costs vary by county. In San Diego County, Riverside County, and other areas we serve, permits typically range from $200 to $500. Your well drilling contractor should handle the permitting process, but these costs are usually passed on to the homeowner.

Well Casing Requirements

The type and diameter of well casing affects your total cost. Standard residential wells typically use 4-inch or 6-inch PVC or steel casing. Larger diameter casings cost more but may be necessary depending on your water needs and pump requirements. The casing protects your well from contamination and collapse, making it a critical component of any well system.

What's Included in Professional 100-Foot Well Drilling

When you hire a reputable well drilling company like Southern California Well Service, your quote should include several essential components and services:

Site Assessment and Consultation

Before drilling begins, a professional contractor will evaluate your property to determine the best drilling location. This assessment considers factors like existing utilities, septic systems, property boundaries, and geological conditions. This preliminary work helps ensure the drilling process goes smoothly and the well is positioned optimally.

Drilling and Boring

The actual drilling process involves bringing specialized equipment to your property and boring down to the required depth. For a 100-foot well, this typically takes one to two days depending on soil conditions. The drilling team monitors progress continuously to ensure proper technique and safety.

Well Casing Installation

Once the borehole is complete, the well casing is installed to prevent collapse and protect against surface contamination. The casing extends from the surface down into the water-bearing zone, creating a stable, sanitary pathway for water extraction.

Well Screen

At the bottom of the well, a screen allows water to enter while keeping sand and sediment out. Proper screen selection is crucial for well performance and longevity. The screen type and slot size are chosen based on the aquifer material at your specific location.

Grout Sealing

The space between the casing and the borehole wall is filled with grout to prevent surface water from contaminating the groundwater. This sealing process is required by California regulations and protects both your well and the broader aquifer.

Well Cap and Basic Completion

A sanitary well cap is installed at the top of the casing to prevent contamination from insects, animals, and debris. This cap also provides access for pump installation and future maintenance.

Additional Costs to Consider

While the drilling itself is the major expense, several additional costs may apply to complete your water system:

Well Pump Installation

A 100-foot well typically requires a submersible pump, which adds $1,000 to $2,500 to your total project cost, including the pump, wiring, and installation labor. The pump size depends on your water needs and the well's flow rate.

Pressure Tank and Controls

Your well system needs a pressure tank and control switches to maintain consistent water pressure throughout your home. This equipment typically costs $300 to $800 installed.

Water Testing

After drilling, water quality testing is essential to ensure your new well produces safe, potable water. Basic testing costs $100 to $300, while comprehensive panels testing for a wider range of contaminants may cost more.

Electrical Work

Connecting your well pump to your electrical system may require an electrician, especially if a new circuit or subpanel is needed. This can add $200 to $500 to your project cost.

Water Treatment

Depending on water quality test results, you may need treatment equipment such as a water softener, iron filter, or UV sterilization system. These systems range from a few hundred to several thousand dollars.

Is a 100-Foot Well Deep Enough?

Whether a 100-foot well is sufficient depends entirely on local groundwater conditions. In some areas of Southern California, excellent water-bearing zones exist at relatively shallow depths, making a 100-foot well perfectly adequate. However, other areas may require deeper drilling to reach reliable water sources.

A professional well driller familiar with your area can provide guidance based on nearby well logs, geological surveys, and local knowledge. At Southern California Well Service, we always discuss depth expectations with our clients before drilling begins, and we provide transparent pricing for any additional depth that may be needed.

Why Choose Professional Well Drilling

While it may be tempting to cut costs by attempting DIY well drilling or hiring an unlicensed contractor, professional well drilling offers significant advantages. Licensed contractors understand California's well construction standards, carry proper insurance, and have the experience to handle unexpected challenges. A properly drilled and constructed well can last 30 to 50 years with proper maintenance, making professional installation a worthwhile investment.
