Hot Water Smells Like Rotten Eggs? Here's Why (And How to Fix It)

When only your hot water smells but cold water is fine, the problem isn't your well — it's your water heater. Here's what's causing it and how to fix it.

🔍 Quick Diagnosis

Hot water smells, cold water is fine? → Water heater problem (this article)

Both hot AND cold water smell? → Well water sulfur problem

Only certain faucets smell? → Drain bacteria (not water heater)

Why Does Hot Water Smell Like Rotten Eggs?

That rotten egg smell is hydrogen sulfide (H₂S) gas. When it's only in your hot water, the source is almost always your water heater — not your well.

Here's what's happening inside your water heater:

The Chemistry

  1. 1. Your water has some naturally-occurring sulfate (common in well water)
  2. 2. Sulfur-reducing bacteria (SRB) live in your water heater's warm, low-oxygen environment
  3. 3. The bacteria feed on sulfates and produce hydrogen sulfide as a byproduct
  4. 4. The magnesium anode rod in your water heater makes this worse by releasing electrons that the bacteria love

This is why cold water is fine — the bacteria thrive in the warm water heater tank, not in your well or cold water pipes.

The 3 Main Causes (And How to Fix Each)

Cause #1: Magnesium Anode Rod Reaction (Most Common)

Your water heater has an anode rod to prevent tank corrosion. Most come with magnesium anode rods, which work great for preventing rust — but they can feed sulfur bacteria.

✅ The Fix

Replace the magnesium anode with:

  • • Aluminum/zinc anode ($30-50, DIY)
  • • Powered anode ($100-200, best option)

📋 Details

A powered anode uses electricity instead of sacrificial metal, eliminating the bacteria's food source while still protecting the tank.

Cause #2: Bacteria Colony in the Tank

Sulfur-reducing bacteria have colonized your water heater. This often happens when:

  • Water heater is set below 120°F
  • Hot water sits unused for extended periods (vacation)
  • Water heater is old and has sediment buildup

✅ The Fix

  1. 1. Turn off water heater
  2. 2. Drain the tank completely
  3. 3. Add 1-2 gallons of household bleach
  4. 4. Fill and let sit 2-4 hours
  5. 5. Drain and flush thoroughly
  6. 6. Refill and restore to normal

⚠️ Important

This kills existing bacteria but they may return if you don't also address the anode rod issue.

Cause #3: Water Heater Temperature Too Low

If your water heater is set below 120°F, bacteria can thrive. The "danger zone" for bacteria growth is 40-140°F.

✅ The Fix

Increase water heater temperature to at least 120°F (many recommend 130-140°F to kill bacteria).

Temporary bacteria kill: Set to 140°F+ for 8 hours, then return to normal.

⚠️ Scald Warning

Water at 140°F can cause burns in seconds. If you raise the temperature, be careful at faucets and consider anti-scald devices. If you have young children, keep it at 120°F and use other solutions.

Step-by-Step: How to Fix Smelly Hot Water

  1. Confirm the diagnosis: Run cold water for 2 minutes — does it smell? Run hot water — does it smell? If only hot water smells, it's the water heater.
  2. Check your water heater temperature: Should be at least 120°F. If lower, increase it.
  3. Flush and disinfect the water heater: Drain, add bleach, let sit, flush. This kills existing bacteria.
  4. Replace the anode rod: Switch from magnesium to aluminum/zinc or a powered anode. This prevents the problem from returning.
  5. Consider a water treatment system: If your well water has high sulfate levels, an oxidizing filter or chlorine injection system can help.

When to Call a Professional

You can often fix smelly hot water yourself, but call a professional if:

  • The smell persists after trying the fixes above
  • Both hot AND cold water smell (well water issue)
  • Your water heater is old or in poor condition
  • You're not comfortable working on your water heater
  • You want to install a water treatment system

Is the Smell Dangerous?

At the concentrations typically found in household water, hydrogen sulfide is more unpleasant than dangerous. However:

  • Corrosion: H₂S can corrode pipes, fittings, and fixtures over time
  • Staining: Can tarnish silverware and stain laundry
  • Taste: Makes water taste bad, even after cooking
  • High concentrations: Very high levels (rare) can cause health issues

While not typically a health emergency, it's worth fixing for comfort and to prevent long-term damage.

Prevention Tips

  • Keep water heater at 120°F or higher
  • Use an aluminum/zinc or powered anode rod
  • Flush your water heater annually
  • Don't let hot water sit unused for long periods
  • Address well water sulfate issues with treatment if needed
