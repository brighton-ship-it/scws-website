What is Hydrofracturing?

Hydrofracturing is a well rehabilitation technique that uses high-pressure water to clean and expand existing fractures in the bedrock surrounding your well. This process:

  • Clears mineral deposits and sediment blocking water flow
  • Opens new pathways for groundwater to reach your well
  • Can increase well yield by 2-10 times in many cases
  • Extends the life of your existing well

Unlike oil and gas fracking, well hydrofracturing uses only clean water—no chemicals are involved.

  • Well runs dry during peak usage

The Hydrofracturing Process

  1. Initial Assessment: We evaluate your well's current yield and condition
  2. Equipment Setup: Specialized high-pressure equipment is positioned at your well
  3. Isolation: Packers seal off sections of the well for targeted treatment
  4. Pressurization: Water is pumped at 500-3,000 PSI to fracture bedrock
  5. Development: Well is cleaned and tested for improved yield

What Hydrofracturing Does

Hydrofracturing injects clean water under high pressure into the bedrock surrounding your well bore. In the fractured granite and consolidated sedimentary rock common across inland San Diego County, the pressure widens and reconnects the natural cracks that carry groundwater, flushes out the silt and mineral scale plugging them, and opens new pathways into the well. The usual result is a measurable jump in both the well's flow rate and how quickly it recovers after pumping.

This is not the oil-and-gas "fracking" you read about in the news. Water-well hydrofracturing uses clean water only, with no chemicals and no proppants, and it has been performed safely on residential, commercial, and agricultural wells for decades. In San Diego County's hard rock, where a well's output depends on its connection to the fracture network, it is one of the most reliable tools for boosting a tired well.

For a commercial property that depends on its own well for landscape irrigation or process water, an unexpected drop in output can disrupt daily operations. Reviving the existing well in a single day, rather than enduring the weeks-long timeline of permitting and drilling a replacement, is often the practical difference that keeps a site running.

How the Process Works

We begin by pulling the pump and running a flow test to measure your well's true starting yield in gallons per minute. An inflatable packer, often paired with a second to bracket a target zone, is lowered into the borehole to seal off the section we intend to treat. Clean water is then injected through that isolated interval at pressures typically between 1,000 and 3,000 PSI, opening the existing fracture network feeding the well. We frequently treat multiple zones in sequence, then redevelop and re-test the well to confirm the improvement. Most jobs are finished in a single day, generally four to eight hours on site.

Why Wells Lose Yield in Inland San Diego County

Low production around the Kearny Mesa area and the inland communities beyond it usually traces to one or more of these causes:

  • Tightened or healed fractures. Wells in decomposed granite rely entirely on cracks for flow, and over the years sediment and fines pack those cracks until the well chokes down.
  • Mineral scale. Hard local groundwater leaves calcium and iron deposits that constrict the openings feeding the well.
  • Drought-driven water-table decline. Southern California's recurring dry years lower groundwater levels, sometimes leaving the most productive fracture zone partly above the water line.
  • A well that was marginal from the start. Some wells simply missed the best fracture set when drilled and never produced as well as the owner hoped.

Hydrofracturing addresses each of these except a fully dewatered aquifer, and it is especially effective on the fracture-dependent wells common in this region.

Signs Your Well May Need Hydrofracturing

  • The well runs low or dry during peak demand or irrigation.
  • Recovery is slow, taking hours to refill after heavy use.
  • The pump short-cycles, switching on and off every few minutes.
  • A recently drilled well came in below the yield you expected.

What to Check Before You Call

A little observation up front helps us pinpoint the problem and can save you a service call if the cause is simpler than yield:

  1. Rule out the equipment. A waterlogged pressure tank, a failing pressure switch, or a worn pump can all imitate low yield. A pressure switch is a $150-$350 part and a tank typically runs $600-$1,500, both far cheaper than well work.
  2. Note the timing. Trouble only under heavy simultaneous demand points toward yield; an all-day pressure drop more often points toward equipment.
  3. Locate your well log. The total depth, static water level, and the rock the driller logged tell us whether your well is the fractured-rock type that responds best to hydrofracturing. Many wells in inland San Diego County run from roughly 200 to 600 feet deep.
  4. Watch the water. Sand, cloudiness, or air spitting at the faucets are all useful clues to mention when you call.

When to Call a Professional

Is hydrofracturing safe for a well near Kearny Mesa?

Yes. It uses clean water only, no chemicals, and has been performed on residential, commercial, and agricultural wells for decades. It does not contaminate the aquifer and, done correctly, does not damage a sound casing.

How much can hydrofracturing improve my yield?

Most wells that respond see a two to five times increase in flow rate, depending on the rock formation, the well's depth, and what is limiting flow.

Does it work on every well?

No. It works best in the fractured rock common across inland San Diego County. Wells drawing from loose sand or gravel are poor candidates, and we will tell you up front if yours is one.
