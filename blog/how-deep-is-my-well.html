Why Well Depth Matters

Knowing your well depth is essential for:

  • Pump replacement — Correct pump sizing
  • Water level monitoring — Track changes over time
  • Property records — Accurate documentation
  • Treatment planning — Water quality varies by depth

How to Find Your Well Depth

1. Check Your Well Records

When your well was drilled, a well completion report was filed with the county. This includes total depth, casing depth, and water-bearing zones. Contact your county environmental health department.

2. Check Property Records

The well report may have been provided when you purchased your home. Check your closing documents or home inspection report.

3. Measure It Yourself

If you can access your well (and the pump is out), use a weighted measuring tape (well sounder) to measure from ground level to bottom.

4. Estimate from Pump Depth

If you know when the pump was last pulled, the installer may have recorded the pump setting depth. The well is typically deeper than the pump setting.

5. Professional Measurement

We can measure your well depth during a service call or inspection. We use calibrated equipment for accurate results.

What About Water Level?

Total depth is not the same as water level. Your well may be 400 feet deep, but the water level (static water level) might be at 150 feet. The difference matters for pump sizing and well capacity.
