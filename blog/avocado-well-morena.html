Avocado Water Demands

Avocados are thirsty trees:

  • Mature tree: 40-70 gallons per day in summer
  • Per acre: 4-6 acre-feet per year
  • Critical periods: Fruit set and sizing

A reliable well is essential for profitable avocado production in San Diego County.

Well Systems for Avocado Groves

  • High-capacity agricultural wells
  • Storage tanks for peak demand periods
  • Drip irrigation systems for efficiency
  • Micro-sprinklers for young trees
  • Pressure regulation for uniform coverage

Chloride Sensitivity

Avocados are highly sensitive to chloride in irrigation water. If your Morena well has elevated chloride:

  • Blending with lower-chloride water source
  • Leaching irrigation to flush salts
  • Rootstock selection for salt tolerance
  • Regular soil and leaf testing

We test well water for avocado-critical parameters.

How a Pressure and Irrigation System Works

On a hillside property, steady pressure usually depends on a booster pump and a pressure tank working together: the booster lifts pressure to overcome elevation, the tank stores water and smooths out demand, and a pressure switch — or a variable-frequency drive on a constant-pressure system — keeps everything even as faucets and irrigation zones open and close. If the property has a private well, a submersible pump down in the casing feeds that system, and sediment or mineral filtration conditions the water.

The failure signs are familiar. Pressure that fades on the upper floors or far zones points to an undersized or failing booster. Pressure that surges and drops with rapid cycling means a waterlogged tank or bad switch. Gritty water suggests a sediment or well-screen issue. A licensed technician can identify the cause in one visit.

San Diego County Geology Around Morena

Morena sits on uplifted marine terrace deposits cut by the canyons that drain toward Mission Bay. As you move inland and into the surrounding unincorporated county, the ground transitions to the decomposed granite and fractured crystalline rock that characterizes much of San Diego County, where well yields vary sharply from parcel to parcel. That variability is exactly why a contractor who knows local ground will outperform a generic out-of-area outfit.

What to Check Before You Call

  • Confirm any well or booster breaker has not tripped
  • Note pressure-gauge readings during the problem
  • Determine whether the issue is the whole house or just irrigation
  • Listen for a pump constantly running or rapidly clicking
  • Look for leaks at the tank, switch, or relief valve

When to Call a Licensed Professional

Work on a well casing, submersible pump, booster wiring, or water treatment should always go to a licensed C-57 contractor. These systems combine high-voltage electricity with pressurized water, and errors are expensive and unsafe. If you have lost water, your pump or booster is short-cycling, or testing reveals a water-quality problem, call us the same day.

Managing Pressure and Efficiency on Morena's Slopes

Because Morena is built across hillsides and canyon rims, pressure management is the recurring theme. Homes high on a slope, or with irrigation zones that climb away from the meter, frequently see pressure fade in the afternoon when demand is highest. A constant-pressure or booster system levels that out, delivering steady flow to every fixture and zone regardless of elevation — and it does so more efficiently than an oversized pump that short-cycles.

Efficiency is the other half of the equation on these terraced, canyon-view lots. Overhead spray on sloped ground runs off before it soaks in, wasting water and money through San Diego's long dry season. Converting to drip and micro-spray, zoning by sun exposure, and adding pressure regulation reduce waste and ease the load on your pump. We are glad to evaluate a hillside property and recommend only the changes that will genuinely help.
