Best Well Pump for Deep Wells (300+ Feet): Complete Buyer's Guide

SC

By SCWS Team

February 9, 2026 · 14 min read

When your well reaches 300, 400, or even 500+ feet deep, choosing the right pump becomes critical. The wrong pump won't just underperform—it may fail completely, leaving you without water and facing a costly extraction and replacement. This comprehensive guide covers everything you need to know about selecting the best pump for your deep well in Southern California.

Deep wells are increasingly common throughout San Diego, Riverside, and the surrounding counties. The region's complex geology and variable groundwater levels often require drilling to 300-600 feet or more to find reliable water. At these depths, pump selection isn't just about brand preference—it's about matching the right equipment to the specific demands of your well.

Why Deep Wells Require Special Pumps

The physics of pumping water from extreme depths creates unique challenges that standard pumps can't handle. Understanding these challenges helps explain why certain pump specifications matter so much.

The Challenge of Lift

Every foot of depth adds to the "total dynamic head"—the total pressure the pump must overcome. At 300+ feet, this includes:

  • Static head: The vertical distance from water level to surface (your primary challenge)
  • Friction losses: Resistance from pipe walls, fittings, and bends
  • Pressure requirements: The PSI needed at your pressure tank (typically 40-60 PSI)
  • Drawdown: How much the water level drops when pumping

A pump rated for 400 feet might only deliver 10 GPM at the surface if friction and pressure requirements aren't factored in. This is why professional sizing is essential for deep wells.

Why Submersibles Are the Only Option

Jet pumps—both shallow and deep well varieties—can't reliably lift water from more than 100-150 feet. For wells over 300 feet, submersible pumps are the only practical choice. Here's why they excel:

  • Push vs. pull: Submersibles push water up from the bottom, which is far more efficient than surface pumps trying to create suction
  • Cooled by water: The motor is submerged and cooled by surrounding water, preventing overheating
  • Higher pressures: Multi-stage impellers can generate the pressure needed to overcome extreme depths
  • Quiet operation: All mechanical components are underground, eliminating surface noise
  • Protected from elements: No freeze protection needed; pump stays at stable underground temperatures

Key Specifications for Deep Well Pumps

When shopping for a deep well pump, these specifications matter most. Don't just look at the brand—evaluate these performance metrics.

Horsepower (HP) Requirements

HP determines how much work the pump can do. For deep wells, you'll generally need more horsepower than shallower installations:

Typical HP Requirements by Depth

Well Depth Typical HP Flow Rate (GPM)
300-400 ft 1 - 1.5 HP 8-15 GPM
400-500 ft 1.5 - 2 HP 8-12 GPM
500-600 ft 2 - 3 HP 7-10 GPM
600+ ft 3 - 5 HP 5-8 GPM

*These are general guidelines. Actual requirements depend on your well's specific conditions and household demand.

Number of Impeller Stages

Deep well pumps use multiple impellers stacked in series, with each stage adding pressure. More stages = higher lift capability:

  • 10-15 stages: Suitable for 200-350 foot wells
  • 15-22 stages: Designed for 350-500 foot wells
  • 22-30+ stages: Required for 500+ foot wells

The impeller material also matters. Stainless steel impellers resist corrosion and wear far better than plastic or composite options—essential for the 15-20 year lifespan you want from a quality deep well pump.

Motor Specifications

The motor is where many cheap pumps fail. Key motor features to look for:

  • Franklin Electric motors: Industry standard for reliability and parts availability
  • Stainless steel shell: Resists corrosion in aggressive water conditions
  • Hermetically sealed: Prevents water intrusion into motor windings
  • Built-in protection: Thermal overload protection prevents burnout
  • 230V rating: Higher voltage is more efficient for deep wells than 115V

💡 Pro Tip

For wells over 400 feet, always specify a Franklin Electric motor—even if you prefer a different pump brand. Many manufacturers offer Franklin motor options because of their proven reliability in extreme applications.

Best Deep Well Pump Brands Compared

Not all pump brands are created equal. Based on our experience installing and servicing pumps throughout Southern California, here's our professional assessment of the top brands for deep well applications.

Grundfos

Best for: Premium installations where efficiency and longevity matter most

  • Danish engineering with exceptional build quality
  • Industry-leading energy efficiency (lower electricity costs)
  • All stainless steel construction standard
  • Excellent warranty support (2-5 years depending on model)
  • Higher upfront cost, but often lowest total cost of ownership
  • Popular models: SQ, SQE (constant pressure), SP series

Franklin Electric

Best for: Reliable performance with excellent parts availability

  • American-made motors that are industry standard
  • Most widely serviced brand—parts available everywhere
  • SubDrive (VFD) systems for constant pressure applications
  • Excellent balance of quality and value
  • Strong track record in Southern California's varied water conditions
  • Popular models: FPS, Little Giant submersibles

Goulds (Xylem)

Best for: Heavy-duty commercial and high-demand residential applications

  • Built for demanding conditions and high flow rates
  • Excellent for wells with sand or sediment issues
  • Strong commercial reputation carries over to residential
  • Good warranty support through distribution network
  • Popular models: GS, HS series

Myers

Best for: Budget-conscious installations in good water conditions

  • Lower cost than premium brands
  • Adequate performance for less demanding applications
  • Often uses Franklin Electric motors
  • May not last as long in aggressive water conditions

⚠️ Brands to Avoid for Deep Wells

Avoid generic or unknown brands, especially for wells over 300 feet. The cost of pulling a failed pump—$1,500-$3,000 in labor alone—makes cheap pumps extremely expensive when they fail prematurely. Big box store brands may seem attractive but often lack the build quality needed for deep well applications.

Sizing Your Deep Well Pump Correctly

Proper sizing is critical. An undersized pump can't meet your needs; an oversized pump short-cycles, wearing out faster and wasting electricity.

Calculate Your Water Demand

Start by estimating your peak daily water use:

  • Per person: 50-75 gallons per day
  • Irrigation: Varies widely; measure your system's flow rate
  • Livestock: 5-20 gallons per animal per day
  • Peak demand: Morning and evening spikes when showers, laundry, and dishwashers run simultaneously

Match Pump Output to Well Capacity

Your well's yield (GPM capacity) may limit pump selection:

  • Never exceed well yield: A pump pulling more water than the well produces will run dry and burn out
  • Consider drawdown: How far the water level drops during pumping affects true pumping depth
  • Recovery rate: A slow-recovery well may need a smaller pump with storage tank

Professional Sizing Matters

For deep wells, professional pump sizing using your well's actual data (depth, static level, yield, drawdown) is essential. We use manufacturer sizing software to calculate exact specifications. This ensures your pump performs optimally for years to come.

Installation Considerations for Deep Wells

Installing a pump in a 300+ foot well is not a DIY project. Here's what proper deep well pump installation involves.

Specialized Equipment Required

  • Well rig or crane: Hundreds of feet of pipe and pump weigh too much for manual installation
  • Pipe handling equipment: Drop pipe sections must be carefully threaded and supported
  • Torque arrestor: Prevents pump from spinning and damaging the well casing
  • Safety cable: Stainless steel cable provides backup if pipe connections fail

Wire and Pipe Specifications

At extreme depths, wire gauge and pipe diameter become critical:

  • Wire sizing: Voltage drop over long distances requires heavier gauge wire (typically 10 AWG or larger)
  • Splices: All connections must use proper submersible splice kits rated for underground use
  • Pipe diameter: Usually 1¼" or larger to minimize friction losses
  • Pipe material: Schedule 80 PVC or poly pipe rated for the pressure

Extending Your Deep Well Pump's Life

Given the significant investment and effort required to install a deep well pump, maximizing its lifespan is essential.

Protect Against Common Killers

  • Sand and sediment: Install a sand separator if your well produces sediment. Abrasives destroy impellers quickly.
  • Dry running: A low-water cutoff switch prevents the pump from running when water levels drop too low
  • Power surges: Lightning arrestors and surge protection save motors from electrical damage
  • Short cycling: Proper pressure tank sizing prevents the pump from cycling too frequently

Regular Maintenance

  • Monitor performance: Note any changes in pressure, flow, or pump cycling patterns
  • Check amp draw: Increasing amperage indicates developing problems
  • Water quality testing: Aggressive water chemistry can accelerate wear
  • Professional inspection: Every 3-5 years, have a professional evaluate the system

Frequently Asked Questions

What type of pump is best for a 300+ foot deep well?

Submersible pumps are the only practical option for wells over 300 feet deep. They're designed to push water up from great depths and can handle 400-800+ feet with proper sizing. Look for 4-inch diameter stainless steel construction, 1-3 HP motors, and quality brands like Grundfos, Franklin Electric, or Goulds.

How much does a deep well pump cost installed?

For wells 300-500 feet deep, expect to pay $3,500-$7,000 for a quality submersible pump fully installed in Southern California. This includes the pump, motor, drop pipe, wiring, and labor. Wells over 500 feet may cost $6,000-$10,000+ due to additional materials and specialized equipment needed.

How long do deep well pumps last?

Quality deep well submersible pumps typically last 12-20 years with proper installation and water conditions. Franklin Electric and Grundfos pumps often exceed 15 years. Factors affecting lifespan include water quality (sand and sediment cause wear), proper sizing, and installation quality. Budget pumps may fail in 5-8 years.

What HP pump do I need for a 400 foot well?

For a 400-foot well, most residential applications need a 1.5 to 2 HP submersible pump. The exact HP depends on your required flow rate (GPM), the well's static water level, and household demand. A 2 HP pump can typically deliver 10-15 GPM from 400 feet. A well professional should calculate your specific requirements.

Are expensive well pumps worth it?

Yes, for deep wells especially. Premium pumps from Grundfos, Franklin Electric, or Goulds cost 30-50% more but last significantly longer, use less electricity, and have better warranties. The cost difference of $500-$1,500 is recovered through longer lifespan and lower operating costs. Pulling a failed pump from a 400-foot well costs $1,500-$3,000 in labor alone.

Making Your Decision

Choosing a pump for a deep well is a significant decision that affects your water supply for the next 15-20 years. Here's our bottom-line advice:

  • Don't skimp on quality: The labor to install or replace a deep well pump costs more than the difference between budget and premium pumps
  • Get professional sizing: Your well's specific conditions determine the right pump—there's no one-size-fits-all answer
  • Consider energy efficiency: A more efficient pump can save hundreds of dollars per year in electricity
  • Plan for the future: If you might expand water use (irrigation, pool, ADU), size accordingly now
