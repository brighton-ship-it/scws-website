Avocado Water Demands

Avocados are thirsty trees:

  • Mature tree: 40-70 gallons per day in summer
  • Per acre: 4-6 acre-feet per year
  • Critical periods: Fruit set and sizing

A reliable well is essential for profitable avocado production in San Bernardino County.

Well Systems for Avocado Groves

Chloride Sensitivity

Avocados are highly sensitive to chloride in irrigation water. If your Montclair well has elevated chloride:

  • Blending with lower-chloride water source
  • Leaching irrigation to flush salts
  • Rootstock selection for salt tolerance
  • Regular soil and leaf testing

The Water Issues Montclair-Area Owners Actually Face

This part of San Bernardino County sits on the broad alluvial fans that spill out of the San Gabriel Mountains — deep deposits of sand, gravel, and cobble that form the Chino and Cucamonga groundwater basins. That is why the region historically had productive agricultural wells before suburbanization, and why some larger properties on the northern and rural fringes still rely on or could benefit from private wells. The water here also tends to be hard, with mineral content that scales fixtures and stresses appliances.

For most Montclair homes on city water, the common complaints are hard-water scaling, taste and odor issues, and pressure inconsistencies. For larger landscape and rural properties nearby, irrigation wells and booster systems are the realistic conversation.

How a Well and Pressure System Works

On a private well in the foothills near Montclair, a submersible pump lifts groundwater from the casing to a pressure tank that stores water and holds steady pressure under the control of a pressure switch. Treatment equipment — commonly a softener or iron filter in this hard-water region — conditions the water before it reaches the house or irrigation. The whole system is designed to deliver consistent, clean water on demand.

When it fails, the symptoms guide the diagnosis. Surging pressure and rapid pump cycling usually mean a waterlogged tank or a faulty switch. No water can mean a tripped breaker or a failed pump. Cloudy or gritty water suggests the well is over-pumping or the screen is failing. A licensed technician can isolate the issue quickly.

San Bernardino County Groundwater Beneath Montclair

Montclair overlies the deep alluvial aquifers of the Chino Basin, recharged by runoff from the San Gabriels and managed as one of Southern California's major groundwater basins. Wells in these fans can be deep and productive, but the basin is actively managed and permitted, and water hardness is a defining trait. Understanding the basin and county rules is essential to doing this work correctly — another reason to use a contractor who knows San Bernardino County ground rather than an out-of-area crew.

What to Check Before You Call

  • Confirm the pump or well breaker has not tripped
  • Note the pressure-gauge reading during the problem
  • Listen for the pump constantly running or rapidly clicking
  • Determine whether the issue is whole-house or irrigation-only
  • Look for leaks at the tank, switch, or relief valve

When to Call a Licensed Professional

Work on the well casing, the submersible pump, pressure-switch wiring, or water treatment belongs with a licensed C-57 contractor. These systems mix high-voltage power and pressurized water, and mistakes are costly and dangerous. If you have lost water, your pump is short-cycling, or a water test shows a problem, call us the same day.

Hard Water, Heat, and Seasonal Maintenance in Montclair

Two realities shape water service in the Montclair area: hard, mineral-rich groundwater and hot inland summers. The hardness scales water heaters, clogs fixtures, and shortens appliance life, which is why a properly sized softener is one of the most common upgrades we install here. On the heat side, summer demand strains pumps and pressure systems on larger landscapes and rural parcels, so a late-spring inspection of the pump, tank, switch, and filtration pays for itself by heading off a mid-summer breakdown.

For owners with private wells on the foothill fringe, we also recommend periodic water testing. Groundwater chemistry in the Chino Basin can shift over time, and catching a rise in iron, manganese, or hardness early lets you adjust treatment before it stains laundry or damages equipment. As always, we give a straight assessment — if your water is fine, we will tell you so rather than sell you a system you do not need.
