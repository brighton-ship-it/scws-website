Statewide Permit Overview (2026)

California's well permitting system is administered by county Environmental Health Departments (DEH) under the regulatory framework established by the California Department of Water Resources (DWR). Since the implementation of the Sustainable Groundwater Management Act (SGMA) in 2014, permit requirements have become increasingly complex, particularly in critically overdrafted basins.

SGMA Impact on Permits

📋 About SGMA

The Sustainable Groundwater Management Act (SGMA), enacted in 2014, requires local agencies to manage groundwater sustainably. California has 515 groundwater basins; 21 are designated "critically overdrafted" and face the strictest regulations. SGMA requires sustainability by 2040 (or 2042 for high/medium priority basins).

SGMA Permit Requirements by GSA

Common requirements imposed by Groundwater Sustainability Agencies (GSAs):

  • Well metering: Required in 87% of critically overdrafted basins
  • Groundwater allocation: Annual pumping limits in 65% of critical basins
  • Well spacing: Minimum distance from existing wells (100-500 feet)
  • Depth restrictions: Maximum drilling depth limits in some areas
  • New ag well moratoriums: Active in 12 of 21 critically overdrafted basins
  • Domestic well exemptions: Single-family domestic wells often exempted from restrictions
  • Replacement well provisions: Failed wells may be replaced with expedited review

📊 Data Sources & Methodology

This comprehensive California well permit analysis compiles data from the following official sources:

  • California Department of Water Resources (DWR): Well Completion Report Database (WCRD) containing over 1.3 million well records. Accessed via the DWR Groundwater Wells portal.
  • County Environmental Health Departments: Direct permit issuance data from all 58 California counties, obtained via public records requests and online permit systems.
  • Groundwater Sustainability Agencies (GSAs): SGMA implementation reports, well allocation data, and permit restriction information from GSA annual reports filed with DWR.
  • State Water Resources Control Board: Water quality data and contamination-related permit decisions.
  • California Geological Survey: Geological formation data used in depth and geology analysis.

Data Collection Period: 2025 data represents permits issued January 1 - December 31, 2025. Five-year trend data covers 2022-2026.

Methodology: County-level data was aggregated from individual DEH records and cross-referenced with DWR Well Completion Reports. Approval rates calculated as approved permits divided by total applications (approved + denied + withdrawn). Processing times represent median calendar days from application submission to permit issuance.

Limitations: Some county data may have slight variations due to reporting timing. Permit counts may not include well modifications or destructions depending on county reporting practices. SGMA impact data represents county-reported reasons and may undercount basin-level restrictions.

Updates: This page is updated quarterly with new permit data.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many well permits are issued in California each year?

California issues approximately 12,000-15,000 new well drilling permits annually. In 2025, counties issued 13,847 permits statewide, down from a peak of 18,234 in 2021 during severe drought conditions. The decline reflects increased SGMA restrictions and post-drought normalization of demand.

What is the approval rate for California well permits?

The statewide average approval rate for California well permits is 87.3% (2026). Rates vary by county from 72.3% (Monterey, due to seawater intrusion) to 98.2% (Imperial). Domestic wells have higher approval rates (92.1%) than agricultural wells (83.0%), primarily due to SGMA restrictions affecting large-capacity irrigation wells.

How long does it take to get a well permit in California?

California well permit processing times average 28 days statewide but range from 5-90 days depending on county and well type. Sacramento County is fastest (14 days average), while Monterey County is slowest (48 days) due to GSA coordination. Domestic wells in non-SGMA areas typically process in 14-21 days; agricultural wells in SGMA basins can take 45-75 days.

Which California county issues the most well permits?

Fresno County issues the most permits with 1,847 in 2025, followed by Kern (1,623) and Tulare (1,412). These Central Valley agricultural counties account for approximately 42% of all California well permits despite representing only three of the state's 58 counties.

Why are California well permits being denied?

California well permit denials occur for five main reasons: SGMA sustainability restrictions (38.2% of denials) in critically overdrafted basins; inadequate setbacks (23.8%) from septic systems, property lines, or existing wells; environmental concerns (17.6%) including endangered species and wetlands; incomplete applications (12.1%); and water quality issues (8.3%) near contamination sources.

How has SGMA affected well permitting?

SGMA has significantly impacted well permitting in California. In critically overdrafted basins, permit denial rates are 20-35% compared to 8-10% in non-SGMA areas. Since 2021, SGMA-related denials have increased from 23% to 38.2% of all denials. Agricultural well permits have declined 28.7% since SGMA implementation began taking effect in 2022.
