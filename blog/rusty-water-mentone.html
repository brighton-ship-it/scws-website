Seeing rusty, orange, or red-tinted water from your Mentone well? This common issue is usually caused by iron in your groundwater or corroding well components. The good news: it's fixable.

What Causes Rusty Well Water?

  • Dissolved Iron: Naturally occurring iron in San Bernardino County groundwater
  • Iron Bacteria: Bacteria that feed on iron, creating rusty slime
  • Corroding Casing: Old steel well casing breaking down
  • Corroding Pipes: Galvanized or iron plumbing in your home
  • Failing Water Heater: Rusting anode rod or tank

Is Rusty Water Safe to Drink?

Iron itself isn't a health hazard at typical well water concentrations. However, rusty water:

  • Tastes metallic and unpleasant
  • Stains sinks, toilets, and laundry
  • Can clog pipes and fixtures over time
  • May indicate other water quality issues

We recommend water testing to identify the specific cause and concentration of iron in your Mentone well.

Rusty Well Water in Mentone: A Wash-Country Problem With a Clear Fix

Mentone occupies a distinctive slice of San Bernardino County, a semi-rural foothill community just east of Redlands where the Santa Ana River and the Mill Creek wash spill out of the San Bernardino Mountains near the Crafton Hills. Many Mentone properties sit on private wells drawing from a blend of alluvial wash sediments and the granitic foothill rock beneath them. That setting is beautiful and well-watered by mountain runoff, but it also explains why so many Mentone homeowners eventually open a faucet to find rusty, orange, or sediment-laden water. If that describes your tap, you are not alone, and the problem is almost always solvable once you know what is driving it.

The wash sediments under Mentone carry fine silt and iron-bearing minerals down from the mountains, while the underlying granitic rock contributes naturally dissolved iron and manganese to the groundwater. Add in the aging steel well casings common on older Mentone parcels, and you have the three ingredients that most often turn well water rusty: sediment, dissolved minerals, and corroding metal.

The Common Sources of Rust in Mentone Wells

Rusty water is a symptom, not a diagnosis. In Mentone we see five recurring causes, and identifying yours is the key to choosing a fix that actually works.

Sediment From the Wash

Because so many Mentone wells tap alluvial wash material, fine sand and silt are a frequent companion to rust. Sediment can carry iron particles, cloud the water, and abrade pump components. Gritty, cloudy water that settles into a layer at the bottom of a glass is the telltale sign.

Dissolved Iron and Manganese

The granitic rock under the foothills releases iron and manganese into groundwater. Iron stains reddish-brown; manganese leaves darker black or brown marks. Both are invisible underground and only reveal themselves as rust once the water meets air at your fixtures.

Corroding Steel Casing

A great many Mentone wells were cased in steel decades ago. Constant contact with mineral-rich wash water eventually corrodes that steel, and the casing begins shedding rust directly into your water. When previously clear water turns rusty seemingly overnight, a failing casing is high on our list.

Iron Bacteria

Iron bacteria feed on the iron in your well and leave a slimy, rust-colored residue in tanks and fixtures, sometimes accompanied by a musty smell. They are harmless to health but a nuisance that clogs screens and recurs if not treated thoroughly.

Household Plumbing and Water Heater

Not every rust problem starts in the ground. Older Mentone homes plumbed with galvanized steel corrode internally over time, and a water heater with a depleted anode rod or rusting tank will discolor your hot water specifically. These household sources are easy to confirm with a few simple checks.

Well-Source Rust Versus Household Rust: How to Tell

The single most useful thing you can do before calling anyone is to figure out whether the rust is coming from your well or from inside your house. Try these checks:

  1. Test hot versus cold. If only the hot water is rusty, look at your water heater first. If both are affected, the source is your well or the main line.
  2. Check the nearest outdoor spigot. Water drawn before it enters the house plumbing tells you what the well is actually producing. Rust there means a well-side problem.
  3. Let a glass settle. Sediment that sinks to the bottom points to wash sand and silt; water that starts clear and turns orange points to dissolved iron.
  4. Inspect a toilet tank. Rusty slime on the tank walls is a strong indicator of iron bacteria.
  5. Note whether it is one fixture or all. A single rusty faucet usually means a corroded section of galvanized pipe, while whole-house rust implicates the well.

These observations point you in the right direction, but a laboratory water test is what removes the guesswork. It measures exact iron and manganese levels, screens for bacteria, and reveals the pH and hardness of your Mentone water so treatment can be sized correctly.

Is Rusty Water in Mentone Safe?

Iron at the levels found in most Mentone wells is considered an aesthetic issue rather than a health danger, and it generally will not make a healthy person ill. The real costs are practical: a metallic taste, stained laundry and fixtures, clogged aerators, and shortened appliance life. Sediment compounds the problem by wearing on pumps and valves. And because rust can sometimes accompany other water-quality concerns, a test is the responsible way to confirm exactly what is in your water before you decide how to handle it.

How We Solve Rusty Water for Mentone Homes

Every effective solution starts with knowing the cause. Here is how we treat each scenario and what it typically costs.

Sediment and Iron Filtration

For Mentone's common combination of wash sediment and iron, a sediment pre-filter paired with an oxidizing iron filter is often the ideal setup. The pre-filter captures sand and silt while the iron filter, frequently a greensand or catalytic media, oxidizes and traps dissolved iron. Iron and sediment filtration systems generally run $300 to $900.

Water Softeners

Where iron is modest and hardness is high, a softener removes both, protecting your plumbing and water heater. Softeners typically cost $1,500 to $3,500 installed.

Chlorine Injection

For heavy iron or persistent iron bacteria, a chlorine injection system dosing ahead of a contact tank and filter delivers reliable, continuous oxidation and disinfection.

Shock Chlorination and Well Cleaning

An active iron bacteria colony calls for shock chlorination to disinfect the well and plumbing, usually combined with ongoing treatment so the bacteria do not return.

Casing Lining or Replacement

If a corroded steel casing is the rust source, we can line or replace it to stop the contamination at its origin. For a Mentone well failing in multiple ways, a full new turnkey well runs $18,000 to $42,000, but that is reserved for wells genuinely beyond repair.

Repiping and Water Heater Service

When the rust originates in old galvanized pipe, repiping in PEX or copper eliminates it for good. If the water heater is at fault, replacing the anode rod or unit clears the hot-water discoloration. We also service the supporting equipment that keeps your system healthy: pressure switches ($150 to $350), pressure tanks ($600 to $1,500), submersible pumps ($2,500 to $5,500), and booster or constant-pressure systems ($2,000 to $4,500).

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does my Mentone well water have grit and sediment along with the rust?

Many Mentone wells draw from alluvial wash material, so fine sand and silt come up with the water and often carry iron particles. A sediment pre-filter ahead of an iron filter usually handles both the grit and the rust together.

My neighbor's well is fine but mine is rusty. How is that possible?

Well depth, casing age, and the exact mix of wash sediment and granitic rock can vary from one Mentone parcel to the next. Your neighbor's well may tap a cleaner layer or have newer casing, so two properties close together can produce very different water.

Is the iron in my Mentone water bad for my health?

At the concentrations typical of local wells, iron is an aesthetic nuisance rather than a health hazard and generally will not make a healthy person sick. It does stain and taste metallic, and it can hide other concerns, which is why we recommend a test before treating.

Can I fix rusty water myself in Mentone?

You can run the diagnostic checks and replace a water-heater anode yourself. Treating well-source iron, sediment, or bacteria, or addressing a corroding casing, calls for professional equipment and a proper diagnosis so you do not spend money treating the wrong cause.

What is shock chlorination and will it permanently fix iron bacteria?

Shock chlorination floods the well and plumbing with a strong chlorine solution to kill the iron bacteria colony. It clears the immediate problem, but bacteria can return, so we usually pair it with ongoing treatment to keep your Mentone well clear long term.

How quickly can you respond if my Mentone water suddenly turns rusty?

We offer same-day emergency service for our service area. A sudden change in water color can signal a corroding casing or a failing component, so it is worth getting it diagnosed promptly before the problem worsens.
