Tecate's Groundwater Geology

Tecate lies within the Peninsular Ranges geological province, characterized by granitic and metamorphic bedrock covered by varying depths of decomposed granite. Groundwater is stored primarily in fracture systems within this bedrock.

Fractured Bedrock Aquifers

Unlike valley areas with sandy aquifers, Tecate's water flows through fractures in crystalline rock. Finding productive wells means intersecting these fracture systems—a task requiring geological knowledge and proper drilling techniques to identify water-bearing zones.

Valley vs. Hillside

Properties in the Tecate valley floor and along drainage corridors may find water at somewhat shallower depths (200-350 feet) due to accumulated alluvium and enhanced recharge. Hillside and ridge properties typically require deeper drilling (350-500+ feet).

Drilling Conditions in Tecate

Well drilling in Tecate typically encounters the Peninsular Ranges batholith, primarily granitic and metamorphic rock. Most wells can be completed in 1-3 days under normal conditions. The relatively moderate depths keep drilling costs reasonable, though rocky formations can slow progress.

San Diego County requires a well permit from the Department of Environmental Health (DEH) before drilling can begin. The permit process typically takes 2-4 weeks and costs $800-$1,500 depending on the parcel. We handle the entire permit process for Tecate properties.
