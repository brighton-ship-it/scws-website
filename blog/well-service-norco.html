Well Services for Norco Horse Properties

Norco properties have higher water demands than typical residential homes. Our services are tailored to meet those needs:

High-Volume Well Drilling

Standard residential wells might produce 3-5 GPM, but horse properties often need 10-20+ GPM to keep up with livestock, irrigation, and household use. Our well drilling services are designed to maximize production from Norco's favorable aquifer conditions.

The good news: Norco sits above the Santa Ana River alluvial aquifer, one of the best groundwater sources in Riverside County. Wells here are typically shallower than mountain areas, which helps control drilling costs while providing excellent flow rates.

Livestock-Ready Pump Systems

Horse troughs need consistent water pressure. Automatic waterers require reliable supply. Our pump systems are sized for agricultural demands:

  • High-capacity submersible pumps (10-25 GPM)
  • Larger pressure tanks for peak demand
  • VFD systems for efficient, constant pressure
  • Backup power connections for emergencies
  • Multiple zone capability for barn, house, and arena

Water Quality for Livestock

Horses are sensitive to water quality—high mineral content or contamination can affect their health. Our water testing checks for equine-safe parameters and recommends treatment if needed.

Norco's Favorable Groundwater Conditions

Santa Ana River Aquifer

Norco benefits from its location along the Santa Ana River, which has deposited thick layers of permeable sand and gravel over thousands of years. These alluvial deposits create excellent aquifer conditions with high flow rates and relatively shallow water tables.

Typical Norco Well Depths by Area

  • Near Santa Ana River/Riverdale: 80-150 feet (excellent production)
  • Central Norco/6th Street area: 100-200 feet
  • Hidden Valley/Hillside properties: 150-300 feet
  • Near Corona border: 120-220 feet
  • Crestview/Sierra area: 150-250 feet

Why Norco Wells Are Shallower

Unlike mountain foothill areas that drill through granite, Norco wells penetrate loose alluvial materials. This means faster drilling, easier pump installation, and typically better flow rates. Many Norco properties can achieve 15-25 GPM—plenty for a large horse operation.

Norco Horse Property Tip

Planning water for horses? Calculate 10-12 gallons per horse per day for drinking, plus water for cleaning stalls, washing horses, and dust control in arenas. A property with 4 horses needs a well that can reliably produce at least 8-10 GPM to meet peak demands.

Common Well Needs for Norco Properties

Arena & Pasture Irrigation

Keeping arena footing watered and pastures green requires significant water volume. Many Norco properties install dedicated irrigation wells separate from their domestic supply to ensure both systems perform optimally.

Automatic Waterer Systems

Modern horse facilities use automatic waterers that need consistent pressure. If your waterers are running dry or slow to fill, your pump or pressure tank may need attention.

Well Upgrades for Growing Operations

Started with two horses but now have six? Your original well system may not have been sized for increased demand. We can evaluate your current system and recommend upgrades.

Frequently Asked Questions

How deep are wells in Norco, CA?

Wells in Norco typically range from 100 to 250 feet deep—much shallower than mountain areas. The Santa Ana River alluvial deposits provide excellent aquifer conditions with good flow rates.

How much water do I need for horses?

Horses need 5-10 gallons per day for drinking, plus water for cleaning and cooling. A well for a horse property should produce at least 5-10 GPM; larger operations need more.

How much does it cost to drill a well in Norco?

Well drilling in Norco typically costs $15,000-$28,000 for residential use. Horse properties needing high-volume wells with larger pumps may cost more.

Can my well handle both household and irrigation?

Many Norco wells can support both uses with proper pump sizing and storage. Large properties often benefit from separate irrigation wells.
