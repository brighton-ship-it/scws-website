Riverside County Well Permit Overview

The Transportation and Land Management Agency (TLMA) Building & Safety department handles well permits in Riverside County. All new wells, deepenings, and destructions require permits. There are different requirements for domestic vs. agricultural wells, and online applications are available through the Riverside County permit portal for convenience.

Required Documents and Fees

You'll need a well permit application with the property owner's signature, a plot plan showing the well location relative to structures and septic systems, a copy of the deed or assessor's parcel information. Permit fees range from $400-$700 based on well type, with additional fees for plan check and inspections in some areas.

Setbacks and Location Requirements

Riverside County requires a minimum of 50 feet from septic tanks and 100 feet from disposal fields. Wells must be 50 feet from sewer lines and property boundaries, and 100 feet from any source of contamination. Wells in desert areas may have additional groundwater restrictions, and the Coachella Valley Water District has specific requirements in its jurisdiction.

Inspection and Completion Process

Call for inspection before drilling begins. Your well driller must be C-57 licensed in California. The well completion report must be filed with DWR and the county within 60 days. Water quality testing is required for potable use, and the sanitary seal inspection ensures proper wellhead protection.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a permit to deepen an existing well in Riverside County?

Yes, deepening or modifying an existing well requires a permit from Riverside County TLMA. You'll need to submit a new application showing the proposed modifications and ensure the well still meets all setback requirements. The permit fee is typically the same as a new well permit.

What special requirements exist for wells in the Coachella Valley?

Wells in the Coachella Valley must comply with both county requirements and Coachella Valley Water District (CVWD) regulations. CVWD requires additional permits for new wells, charges replenishment fees, and may restrict pumping in certain areas. Contact CVWD at (760) 398-2651 before applying.

How do I apply for a well permit in Riverside County online?

Visit the Riverside County TLMA online permit portal at rfrweb.rivcoca.org. Create an account, select 'Well Permit' as your application type, upload required documents, and pay fees online. Processing typically takes 2-3 weeks for standard applications.
