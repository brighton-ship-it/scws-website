Irrigation Well Drilling: Complete Guide for Southern California Farms, Orchards & Vineyards

Published July 11, 2025 | 18 min read

For Southern California's agricultural producers—from Fallbrook's legendary avocado groves to Temecula's award-winning vineyards—reliable irrigation water is the lifeblood of their operations. With municipal water rates climbing past $1,200 per acre-foot and drought restrictions becoming routine, irrigation well drilling has become the smart investment for farms and orchards throughout San Diego and Riverside counties.

Key Takeaway: A properly designed irrigation well typically costs $45,000-$120,000 but can save agricultural operations $40,000-$100,000+ annually in water costs. Most farm irrigation wells pay for themselves within 2-5 years while providing drought-proof water independence.

What Is an Irrigation Well?

An irrigation well is a high-capacity water well specifically designed and engineered to supply the large volumes of water needed for agricultural irrigation. Unlike residential wells that might produce 5-15 gallons per minute (GPM), agricultural irrigation wells are built to deliver 50-500+ GPM continuously throughout the growing season.

These wells feature larger casing diameters (8-16 inches vs. 6 inches for residential), more powerful pump systems (25-200+ HP motors), and are engineered for sustained daily operation rather than intermittent household use.

Why Southern California Farms Need Irrigation Wells

  • Water cost savings: Well water costs $150-300/acre-foot vs. $800-1,500 for municipal water
  • Drought protection: No mandatory cutbacks during water emergencies
  • Schedule independence: Irrigate on your timeline, not rationing schedules
  • Crop quality: Consistent water availability produces better yields
  • Property value: A producing irrigation well adds significant value to agricultural property

Irrigation Well Flow Rates by Crop Type

Properly sizing your irrigation well requires understanding your specific crop's water demands. Undersizing leads to stressed crops; oversizing wastes money on unnecessary capacity.

Avocado Groves

San Diego County produces more avocados than any other county in the nation. These valuable trees are also among the thirstiest:

  • Annual water need: 40-60 inches (3-4 acre-feet per acre)
  • Peak summer demand: 1.5-2 inches per week
  • Required well capacity: 20-30 GPM per acre
  • Irrigation method: Micro-sprinklers or drip systems
  • Critical periods: Fruit set (spring) and sizing (summer)

Example: A 15-acre Fallbrook avocado grove needs approximately 45-60 acre-feet of water annually. During peak July-August demand, this requires roughly 150-200 GPM capacity if pumping 12-16 hours daily. We typically recommend a 175-200 GPM well for this operation to provide adequate margin.

Citrus Orchards (Oranges, Lemons, Grapefruit)

  • Annual water need: 36-48 inches (3-4 acre-feet per acre)
  • Peak summer demand: 1-1.5 inches per week
  • Required well capacity: 15-25 GPM per acre
  • Irrigation method: Drip, micro-sprinklers, or flood
  • Advantage: Moderate drought tolerance allows some scheduling flexibility

Vineyards

Temecula Valley's wine industry benefits from grapevines' relatively efficient water use:

  • Annual water need: 12-24 inches (1-2 acre-feet per acre)
  • Peak demand: 0.5-1 inch per week
  • Required well capacity: 8-15 GPM per acre
  • Irrigation method: Drip irrigation (highly efficient)
  • Note: Deficit irrigation often practiced to enhance grape quality

Vineyard Advantage: A 40-acre Temecula vineyard using drip irrigation may only require a well producing 100-125 GPM—about half what an equivalent avocado operation would need. This often allows for smaller, more economical well systems.

Row Crops and Vegetables

  • Annual water need: 18-36 inches depending on crop
  • Required well capacity: 15-25 GPM per acre for overhead irrigation
  • Irrigation methods: Drip, furrow, or overhead sprinklers
  • Consideration: May not pump year-round (seasonal operations)

High-Capacity Pump Systems for Irrigation Wells

The pump system represents 30-40% of your total irrigation well investment. Choosing the right pump determines your operating costs and system reliability for decades.

Submersible Turbine Pumps

The workhorse of irrigation wells, submersible turbines offer the best combination of reliability and efficiency for most agricultural applications:

  • Capacity range: 50-500+ GPM
  • Motor sizes: 25-150 HP typical for agriculture
  • Best for: Wells 200-600 feet deep
  • Efficiency: 70-85% at design point
  • Lifespan: 12-20 years with proper maintenance
  • Cost: $15,000-$45,000 installed

Line Shaft Turbine Pumps

For very high capacity or shallow wells, line shaft turbines provide:

  • Capacity range: 100-1,500+ GPM
  • Motor location: Surface-mounted (easier service access)
  • Best for: Wells under 400 feet, very high flow rates
  • Advantage: Motor accessible without pulling entire pump
  • Cost: $25,000-$75,000 installed

Variable Frequency Drives (VFDs): Essential for Modern Irrigation

We strongly recommend VFDs for all agricultural irrigation wells. The benefits far outweigh the additional cost:

  • Energy savings: 20-50% reduction in electricity costs
  • Soft start: Eliminates high-amperage startup surges that stress equipment
  • Pressure control: Maintains consistent irrigation pressure regardless of demand
  • Extended pump life: Reduced mechanical stress adds years to pump lifespan
  • Flow matching: Automatically adjusts output to actual irrigation needs
  • Cost: $4,000-$15,000 depending on HP
  • ROI: Typically pays for itself in 2-4 years through energy savings

Irrigation Well Permit Requirements in California

Permitting for agricultural irrigation wells has become more complex in recent years due to groundwater sustainability regulations. Here's what you need to know:

County Well Permits

All irrigation wells require permits from your county's environmental health or water resources department:

San Diego County

  • • Department of Environmental Health
  • • Permit fee: $500-$1,200
  • • Processing time: 2-6 weeks
  • • Requirements: Site plan, setback verification, well design specs

Riverside County

  • • Department of Environmental Health
  • • Permit fee: $400-$1,000
  • • Processing time: 2-4 weeks
  • • May require additional GSA approval in managed basins

SGMA Compliance (Sustainable Groundwater Management Act)

California's SGMA affects irrigation wells in designated groundwater basins. Key considerations:

  • Metering requirements: Many basins now require production meters on ag wells
  • Annual reporting: Water use reporting may be mandatory
  • Extraction fees: Some managed basins charge per acre-foot pumped
  • Allocation limits: Certain basins cap water extraction per acre
  • New well restrictions: Overdrafted basins may limit new permits

We help navigate SGMA requirements as part of our irrigation well planning process. Read more about San Diego County well permits.

Environmental Review (CEQA)

Most agricultural irrigation wells qualify for categorical exemptions from CEQA review. However, enhanced review may be required for:

  • Wells in sensitive environmental areas
  • Very high capacity wells (500+ GPM)
  • Projects in critically overdrafted basins
  • Wells near protected waterways or wetlands

The Irrigation Well Drilling Process

Understanding the process helps you plan your project timeline and know what to expect:

Phase 1: Site Assessment & Planning (2-4 weeks)

  • Geological survey and analysis of existing well logs in your area
  • Water requirement calculation based on your crop and acreage
  • Site selection considering access, setbacks, and aquifer location
  • Pump system engineering and electrical planning
  • Detailed project proposal and cost estimate

Phase 2: Permitting (2-8 weeks)

  • County well permit application
  • SGMA/GSA compliance verification (if applicable)
  • Electrical permit for pump installation
  • Environmental review exemption or study (if required)

Phase 3: Drilling (1-3 weeks)

  • Equipment mobilization to site
  • Drilling to target depth (typically 50-100 feet per day)
  • Casing and well screen installation
  • Gravel pack placement around screen
  • Annular seal (grouting) for well protection
  • Well development (surging and cleaning)
  • Yield testing to confirm production capacity

Phase 4: Pump Installation (1-2 weeks)

  • Pump and motor installation
  • Electrical connections and panel installation
  • VFD programming and optimization
  • Control system setup and testing
  • Performance verification under load

Phase 5: Completion

  • Water quality testing (if desired)
  • Well completion report filed with county
  • Connection to your irrigation system
  • Operator training on system controls
  • Warranty documentation

Total timeline: 8-16 weeks from initial consultation to operational irrigation well.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does irrigation well drilling cost in Southern California?

Irrigation well drilling in Southern California typically costs $45,000 to $120,000+, depending on depth, diameter, and required flow rate. A small farm irrigation well (50-100 GPM) costs around $45,000-$65,000, while high-capacity orchard wells producing 150-300 GPM typically range from $75,000-$120,000 including pump systems.

How deep are irrigation wells in San Diego and Riverside counties?

Irrigation wells in San Diego and Riverside counties typically range from 400 to 800 feet deep, though some areas may require depths of 1,000+ feet. Coastal and valley areas often have shallower aquifers (300-500 feet), while foothill and mountain areas commonly need wells 500-800+ feet deep.

What GPM do I need for an irrigation well?

The required GPM (gallons per minute) depends on your acreage and crop type. General guidelines: avocados need 20-30 GPM per acre, citrus requires 15-25 GPM per acre, vineyards with drip irrigation need 8-15 GPM per acre, and row crops typically require 15-25 GPM per acre. A 15-acre avocado grove would need approximately 150-200 GPM capacity.

Do I need a permit for an irrigation well in California?

Yes, all irrigation wells in California require permits from the county environmental health or water authority. San Diego County permits cost $500-$1,200, and Riverside County permits range from $400-$1,000. Additional requirements may include SGMA compliance in managed groundwater basins, water use reporting, and in some cases environmental review (CEQA).

How long does it take to drill an irrigation well?

The complete irrigation well drilling process takes 8-16 weeks from initial consultation to operational well. This includes 2-4 weeks for site assessment and planning, 2-8 weeks for permitting, 1-3 weeks for drilling and development, and 1-2 weeks for pump installation and testing. Actual drilling typically progresses at 50-100 feet per day.

What type of pump is best for irrigation wells?

Submersible turbine pumps are most common for irrigation wells under 500 feet deep, offering 50-500+ GPM capacity with 25-100+ HP motors. Line shaft turbine pumps are preferred for very high capacity needs (100-1,000+ GPM) or shallower wells. Adding a Variable Frequency Drive (VFD) can reduce energy costs by 20-50% and extend pump life.
