Escondido is one of San Diego County's largest cities, spanning from developed urban areas to extensive rural backcountry. While the City of Escondido provides municipal water to urban areas, much of Greater Escondido—especially the surrounding unincorporated areas, rural estates, and agricultural properties—relies on private wells. Southern California Well Service (SCWS) brings specialized rural drilling expertise to Escondido and its outlying areas, helping property owners develop reliable water supplies for their land.

Escondido's Groundwater

Escondido's varied terrain creates diverse groundwater conditions across the area.

Valley Areas

Properties in Escondido's valley areas often have good aquifer access. Alluvial deposits can support productive wells for both domestic and agricultural use.

Hillside Properties

The hills surrounding Escondido feature decomposed granite and fractured rock. These areas produce reliable wells but may require deeper drilling and specialized techniques.

Variable Conditions

Conditions vary significantly across Greater Escondido. Site evaluation is essential for accurate expectations about depth and production.

Location Typical Depth Production Notes

Well Rehabilitation

Existing wells may benefit from rehabilitation:

  • Hydrofracturing: Opening new water-bearing fractures
  • Well Cleaning: Removing mineral deposits
  • Pump Upgrades: Modern, efficient equipment
  • Deepening: Accessing additional water

Rural Property Considerations

Water Cost Savings

For properties with high irrigation demand, wells offer significant savings. Agricultural well water costs $0.25-0.50 per thousand gallons versus $4-6+ for city water.

Fire Safety

Escondido's fire history makes water storage important. We can integrate large tanks with well systems for firefighting reserves.

Hybrid Systems

Many properties use city water for household needs while using well water for irrigation—the best of both worlds for reliability and cost savings.

FAQs

Should I get a well if I have city water?

Depends on your needs. For large irrigation demands (groves, pastures, big landscapes), wells often make economic sense. Many properties use wells for irrigation while keeping city water for household use.

Is rural Escondido good for wells?

Generally yes, though conditions vary. Valley areas often have good potential; hillside properties may need deeper drilling. Free site evaluations help set accurate expectations.

What about fire protection?

Important for rural Escondido. We can design systems with dedicated fire storage—beneficial for both safety and insurance rates.

Can you handle agricultural irrigation?

Absolutely. We have extensive experience with grove and agricultural systems—proper capacity, filtration for drip emitters, and integration with existing irrigation.
