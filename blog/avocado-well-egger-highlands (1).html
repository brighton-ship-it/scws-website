Avocado Water Demands

Avocados are thirsty trees:

  • Mature tree: 40-70 gallons per day in summer
  • Per acre: 4-6 acre-feet per year
  • Critical periods: Fruit set and sizing

A reliable well is essential for profitable avocado production in San Diego County.

Well Systems for Avocado Groves

  • High-capacity agricultural wells
  • Storage tanks for peak demand periods
  • Drip irrigation systems for efficiency
  • Micro-sprinklers for young trees
  • Pressure regulation for uniform coverage

Chloride Sensitivity

Avocados are highly sensitive to chloride in irrigation water. If your Egger Highlands well has elevated chloride:

  • Blending with lower-chloride water source
  • Leaching irrigation to flush salts
  • Rootstock selection for salt tolerance
  • Regular soil and leaf testing

We test well water for avocado-critical parameters.

Well Service Context for Egger Highlands

We will give it to you straight. Egger Highlands is a settled residential neighborhood in the South Bay of San Diego County, tucked just north of Imperial Beach and west of the Otay River valley near the coast. It is served by municipal water. There are no commercial avocado groves here, and essentially no working private wells under these blocks. Rather than dress that up, this page explains the real water picture for Egger Highlands and shows where private well expertise actually matters for people connected to the neighborhood.

Southern California Well Service is a licensed C-57 water well contractor with more than 30 years of experience across the county. Most of our work is inland and rural, but many of our clients live in coastal communities like Egger Highlands while owning land where wells are very much in play.

How Water Works in Egger Highlands

Homes here connect to a municipal distribution system, not to the ground. There is no pump to maintain, no pressure tank in the garage, no water testing to schedule. Part of the reason the South Bay coast developed on municipal water is geology and chemistry: shallow coastal groundwater in this zone tends to be brackish and vulnerable to seawater intrusion, which makes it poorly suited to private wells or grove irrigation. The usable agricultural groundwater is inland, away from the coast.

The Regional Context: Avocados Are an Inland Story

Even though Egger Highlands itself is not grove country, it sits within a county famous for avocados. The difference is distance from the coast. Head inland and uphill, toward the El Cajon valley, Jamul, Valley Center, Ramona and Fallbrook, and you reach the well-fed grove country that defines San Diego County agriculture. The contrast is the useful lesson: the same county that pipes treated water to your South Bay home depends on private wells to keep its inland orchards productive.

When an Egger Highlands Owner Needs a Well Contractor

  • Buying rural land: An offer on backcountry acreage should hinge on a sound, tested well.
  • Inherited or investment property: Aging wells on family or rental land need real assessment.
  • Development: Abandoned wells must be properly destroyed and documented, often as a permit condition.
  • Grove or hobby-farm dreams: If you plan to plant avocados on inland land, the water system is the first thing to get right.

What to Check on an Out-of-Area Property

Before we visit a well-served parcel you own elsewhere, gather the well completion report if it exists, the pump's age and type, the well depth, any recent water test, and whether there is a pressure tank or storage. Note whether plants look stressed, whether water runs clear, and whether power to the pump is steady. Those details let us tell you quickly whether you are facing a small service call or a larger investment.

When to Call a Professional

For anything involving a well, pump, pressure system or well destruction, use a licensed C-57 contractor. The work is regulated, the rigging specialized, and proper documentation protects you. For your Egger Highlands residence, water issues belong to your utility, not a well company.

Due Diligence on an Inland Well

When we assess a well for a South Bay client, we work a consistent path. We review the completion report and permit history, inspect the wellhead, casing and sanitary seal, and evaluate the pump, motor, wiring and pressure or storage equipment. We measure real performance, drawdown and recovery rather than trusting an old number, and we sample water for the parameters that matter on that specific property, which inland can include bacteria, nitrate, arsenic, chloride and TDS. Then we give you a plain summary: what works, what does not, what repairs will cost, and whether the supply will support your plans, whether that is a household, a hobby grove or a small farm.

Well Destruction and Coastal Lots

Even coastal and South Bay parcels occasionally turn up an old, forgotten well, sometimes uncovered during a remodel or a lot split. When that happens, the well usually needs proper destruction to County standards, both for safety and to satisfy permitting, because an improperly abandoned well is a contamination pathway and a liability. We handle destruction correctly, securing the permit, sealing the borehole to specification, and filing the documentation that proves it was done right, so a surprise well does not stall your project.
