Cannabis Water Requirements by Cultivation Type

Outdoor cannabis has the highest water demands—mature plants in California's summer heat can transpire 6-15 gallons daily during flowering. A 1-acre outdoor canopy (roughly 2,000-4,000 plants) needs 15,000-40,000 gallons daily at peak. Indoor cultivation is more efficient: 1-3 gallons per plant daily in controlled environments. Greenhouse operations fall between, with light-dep facilities typically needing 50-75% of outdoor requirements. Calculate your licensed canopy size to determine minimum well capacity—outdoor grows generally need 20-40 GPM per acre.

Well Registration and Water Rights

California cannabis regulations require documented water rights. New wells must be registered with the Department of Water Resources before drilling. Existing wells should have well completion reports on file. Your cannabis license application must include water source documentation. In groundwater basins governed by SGMA, you may need approval from the local groundwater sustainability agency. Some basins restrict new agricultural wells, potentially limiting cannabis cultivation locations. Consult with both cannabis licensing authorities and water agencies before selecting a cultivation site.

Water Quality Considerations

Cannabis is more sensitive to water quality than many crops. Test well water for: pH (ideal 6.0-7.0), EC/TDS (under 500 ppm preferred), hardness (calcium/magnesium ratios), sodium and chloride (can cause toxicity), iron and manganese (can stain flowers), and bacterial contamination (especially for foliar application). Many wells require treatment: pH adjustment, reverse osmosis for high TDS, iron removal, or UV sterilization. Indoor and greenhouse operations often use RO-filtered well water with added nutrients for precise control.

Compliance and Monitoring Requirements

Licensed cannabis cultivators face water reporting requirements. Track and report water use monthly or annually to your licensing authority. Install a water meter on your well for accurate measurement. Document your irrigation practices as part of cultivation plans. The State Water Board may conduct inspections verifying water source compliance. Cannabis in water-stressed areas faces additional scrutiny—some counties limit licenses based on water availability. Maintaining detailed water records protects your license and demonstrates responsible cultivation practices.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much water does cannabis need per plant?

Mature outdoor cannabis plants use 6-10 gallons daily during peak flowering, with some large plants exceeding 15 gallons. Indoor cultivation is more controlled at 1-3 gallons per plant daily. A 10,000 square foot canopy (typical small commercial license) might need 1,000-2,000 gallons daily. Light deprivation greenhouses fall between outdoor and indoor requirements.

Do I need a water permit for cannabis cultivation?

Yes. California cannabis licenses require documented legal water sources. Well water requires a registered well and proof of groundwater rights. The State Water Resources Control Board oversees cannabis water use, and you must demonstrate your water source won't impact fish, wildlife, or neighboring water users. Some counties require additional water availability studies.

What water quality do cannabis plants need?

Cannabis is sensitive to water quality, particularly pH (optimal 6.0-7.0), chlorine (should be minimal), and sodium levels. EC (electrical conductivity) should stay below 1.5 mS/cm for most strains. Well water often needs pH adjustment and filtration. Hard water (high calcium/magnesium) can cause nutrient lockout. Test your well water thoroughly before cultivation and monitor throughout the grow cycle.
