Avocado Water Demands

Avocados are thirsty trees:

  • Mature tree: 40-70 gallons per day in summer
  • Per acre: 4-6 acre-feet per year
  • Critical periods: Fruit set and sizing

A reliable well is essential for profitable avocado production in San Diego County.

Chloride Sensitivity

Avocados are highly sensitive to chloride in irrigation water. If your Teralta West well has elevated chloride:

  • Blending with lower-chloride water source
  • Leaching irrigation to flush salts
  • Rootstock selection for salt tolerance
  • Regular soil and leaf testing

How a Private Well System Functions

A well is a chain of parts, and a fault in one place usually surfaces as a symptom somewhere else. Knowing the chain helps everyone.

  • Casing and borehole: the lined drilled shaft. Inland county wells in granite bedrock commonly reach 200 to 600 feet.
  • Submersible pump: lifts water to the surface, sized to depth and household demand.
  • Pressure tank: holds pressurized water so the pump is not constantly starting.
  • Pressure switch: the small, inexpensive control that turns the pump on and off.
  • Treatment and storage: filters, softeners, and tanks added when water quality or peak demand calls for them.

The Problems We See Most

No Water

A total loss of water usually means a tripped breaker, a failed pressure switch, a burned-out pump, or a control fault. Because the same symptom has several causes, a structured diagnostic visit prevents wasted spending.

Low or Surging Pressure

When pressure drops as fixtures run, or the pump cycles rapidly, the usual cause is a pressure tank that has lost its air charge. Clogged filters and developing leaks can mimic the symptom.

Hard Water, Iron, and Odor

Mineral-rich groundwater scales pipes and leaves rusty stains, while a sulfur smell indicates hydrogen sulfide. All are common in the region and all are treatable once tested.

Repeated Pump Failures

Pumps that die every few years usually point to rapid cycling, incorrect sizing, scaling, or power fluctuations on rural lines. Treating the cause is cheaper than replacing pumps repeatedly.

Checks You Can Make Safely

  1. Reset the breaker once. If it trips again immediately, that is an electrical fault for a professional.
  2. Read the pressure gauge. Note the psi at the tank for the technician.
  3. Tap the tank. A waterlogged pressure tank feels heavy all the way up.
  4. Look for leaks. Damp soil along the line can flag a hidden leak.
  5. Note recent changes. A storm, an outage, or a slow decline each suggests different causes.

Anything involving the pump itself or well wiring should be handled by a licensed crew with the proper equipment.

Maintenance That Prevents Emergencies

Routine attention heads off most failures. We suggest an annual check of pressure-tank charge and pump cycling, a water test every year or two for bacteria, nitrates, iron, and hardness, and scheduled sediment-filter changes. A well older than fifteen or twenty years deserves a proactive inspection so the pump can be replaced on your schedule instead of mid-failure. Owners with irrigation benefit from a spring walk-through of their drip lines to clear emitters before summer demand peaks.

Smart Irrigation for Urban Gardens

Many Teralta West residents grow remarkable amounts of food and ornamentals on small city lots, and water efficiency makes a real difference here. Even on municipal water, a well-designed drip or micro-sprinkler system delivers moisture straight to the roots and cuts waste, while a smart controller adjusts watering to the weather instead of a fixed timer. For homeowners interested in capturing winter rain, a compact cistern with a small pump can store runoff for the dry season. We build these systems with the same pumps, tanks, and controls used on wells, so the design is well within our wheelhouse, and a thoughtful layout pays for itself in lower bills and healthier plants.

What Makes a Local Contractor Different

Water-well work is a specialized trade that sits outside what a general plumber or handyman typically handles. Pulling a submersible pump from several hundred feet, diagnosing a control box, or drilling a new borehole calls for a properly licensed C-57 contractor with the right rigs and safety practices. An out-of-area company may not understand how San Diego County's decomposed-granite geology, variable water tables, and rural electrical service shape a well's behavior. We have spent decades working specifically in this region, so when we arrive we already know the conditions a well in this county is likely to face. Planning Ahead for Family Land

A number of Teralta West residents are connected to family-owned rural parcels in the county's backcountry, and questions about an aging well often come up when a property changes hands or a relative can no longer manage it. Thinking ahead pays off. Knowing a well's depth, age, and recent service history, keeping a basic maintenance schedule, and testing the water periodically all protect the value of the land and the people who depend on it. If a well needs to be retired, proper decommissioning prevents contamination and keeps the parcel in compliance. We are glad to walk families through these decisions without pressure, laying out the options and the realistic costs so everyone can plan with clear information.

Why does inland well water leave rust stains?

That is iron, sometimes with manganese, dissolved in the groundwater. It is usually cosmetic and is readily corrected with an iron-removal or filtration system.

How deep are wells in inland San Diego County?

Many fall in the 200 to 600 foot range, depending on where a productive water-bearing fracture is found in the fractured granite.
