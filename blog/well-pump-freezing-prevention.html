Understanding Freeze Risk in SoCal Mountains

Southern California's mountain communities—Big Bear, Lake Arrowhead, Idyllwild, Wrightwood, and others—regularly experience freezing temperatures from November through March. While locals know to expect winter weather, many well systems were installed without adequate freeze protection, or insulation has deteriorated. Vacation cabins are especially vulnerable when unoccupied. A single hard freeze can crack a pressure tank, burst pipes, or damage your pressure switch and gauge—repairs costing hundreds or thousands of dollars.

Vulnerable Components

The pressure tank is the biggest concern—water inside expands when frozen, cracking the steel shell or destroying the internal bladder. Exposed piping between the well head and house is vulnerable, especially at fittings, elbows, and valves where water pools. The pressure switch and gauge have small ports that freeze easily. The wellhead seal where the pipe exits the ground can allow freezing air into the well casing. Pump houses or enclosures are only as protective as their insulation—an uninsulated enclosure offers little protection.

Winterization Methods

Insulate all exposed pipes with foam pipe insulation, paying extra attention to fittings. Wrap self-regulating heat tape around pipes before insulating for active freeze protection. Install an insulated pressure tank cover or jacket. Seal any gaps in pump house enclosures with spray foam or weatherstripping. Add a thermostatically-controlled heat lamp or ceramic heater to pump enclosures—set to turn on at 35°F. Ensure the well head seal is intact. For vacation properties, consider a freeze alarm that texts you when temperatures drop.

Emergency and Seasonal Procedures

Before winter, inspect all insulation and heat tape, test heaters, and verify your freeze alarm if you have one. During freezes, let a faucet drip slightly—moving water resists freezing. If losing power during freezing weather, drain the pressure tank and exposed pipes by opening the lowest faucet and the tank drain valve. For unoccupied properties, full winterization involves draining the system completely and possibly adding RV antifreeze to fixture traps. Post-freeze, inspect everything carefully before restoring pressure.

Frequently Asked Questions

At what temperature will my well equipment freeze?

Any time temperatures drop below 32°F for extended periods, exposed well equipment is at risk. Pressure tanks, exposed piping, pressure switches, and gauge ports are vulnerable. The pump itself, being underground below the frost line, won't freeze—but everything above ground will. Most damage occurs during overnight freezes when temperatures stay below freezing for 4+ hours.

Can I use heat tape on well pump pipes?

Yes, heat tape (also called heat cable) is an effective freeze protection method. Self-regulating heat tape adjusts output based on temperature and is safer than constant-wattage types. Wrap it along pipes, paying attention to elbows and fittings where freezing often starts. Cover with pipe insulation. Ensure proper electrical connection—heat tape must be plugged in to work. Check annually before winter.

What should I do if my well system freezes?

Turn off the pump immediately to prevent motor burnout from running dry. Don't try to force frozen valves. Apply gentle heat using a hair dryer, heat lamp, or space heater—never open flame. Start at faucet side and work toward the well so melting water can escape. Check for cracks or splits once thawed—frozen pipes often burst. Call for service if you can't locate the freeze or suspect damage.
