What Usually Fails on a Bermuda Dunes Well

Most no-water emergencies we run in Bermuda Dunes trace back to one of a handful of failures. Knowing which one you are facing helps you understand the fix and the cost:

  • Submersible pump burnout — The single most common true emergency. Desert wells run long cycles in summer, and heat plus sediment plus age eventually cooks the motor. Replacement runs $2,500–$5,500 depending on depth and horsepower.
  • Failed pressure switch — A cheap part that stops the pump from turning on or off correctly. Replacement is typically $150–$350 and is often a same-hour fix.
  • Waterlogged pressure tank — When the tank loses its air charge the pump short-cycles, rattling on and off every few seconds. Left alone it destroys the pump motor. A new tank runs $600–$1,500.
  • Control box or capacitor failure — Common on three-wire pumps after a monsoon-season power surge. Repair is usually $400–$900.
  • Electrical faults — Tripped breakers, burned wiring, or a fried control box, frequently after summer lightning storms roll across the valley.
  • Stuck check valve or waterlogged lines — Causes loss of prime and no water at the tap even when the pump runs.

What to Check Before You Call

A few safe checks can sometimes save you a service call, or at least tell us what we are walking into:

  1. Look at the breaker. Find the well pump breaker in your panel. If it has tripped, reset it once. If it trips again immediately or you smell anything burning, leave it off and call us.
  2. Read the pressure gauge. Zero pressure points toward the pump or a lost prime; wildly swinging pressure points toward the tank or switch.
  3. Listen at the pressure tank. Rapid clicking or the pump kicking on and off every few seconds is classic waterlogged-tank behavior — shut the pump off to protect the motor.
  4. Confirm no valve was closed. Make sure no one shut a main supply valve during landscaping or pool work.

Anything beyond a single breaker reset — especially pulling the pump — needs a licensed contractor and specialized equipment. Deep Coachella Valley wells are not a DIY project.

When to Call a Pro Immediately

Call us right away if you have no water at all, if the breaker trips repeatedly, if you smell burning near the pressure tank or control box, or if your water suddenly turns cloudy or sandy.
