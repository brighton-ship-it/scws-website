Fall Well Inspection Checklist

Use this checklist to assess your well system before winter:

Wellhead Inspection

  • ☐ Well cap secure and properly sealed
  • ☐ Concrete pad intact (no cracks allowing surface water in)
  • ☐ Ground slopes AWAY from wellhead
  • ☐ No debris, leaves, or mulch piled near well
  • ☐ Vent screen clean and intact
  • ☐ Well casing visible at least 12" above ground

Pump and Pressure System

  • ☐ Pressure gauge reads within normal range (40-60 PSI typical)
  • ☐ Pressure tank doesn't feel "waterlogged" (heavy all over)
  • ☐ No unusual pump sounds (grinding, clicking, humming)
  • ☐ Pump cycles normally (not short-cycling)
  • ☐ Electrical connections tight and corrosion-free
  • ☐ Pressure switch contacts not burned or pitted

Water Treatment Equipment

  • ☐ Sediment filter changed (or due for change)
  • ☐ Water softener salt level adequate
  • ☐ UV lamp still functioning (check indicator)
  • ☐ All treatment system timers correct

Assessing Summer Wear

Summer's heavy irrigation and household use takes a toll on well systems. Look for these signs of wear:

Performance Indicators

  • Longer run times: Pump running more to maintain pressure = reduced efficiency
  • New sounds: Grinding, clicking, or humming = bearing wear
  • Higher electric bills: Pump working harder = mechanical wear
  • Pressure fluctuations: System not maintaining consistent pressure
  • Sand or sediment: Increased particles = screen or casing issue

Document for Comparison

Record these readings for comparison with spring:

  • Static water level (depth to water when pump is off)
  • Pump running pressure
  • Flow rate (time to fill 5-gallon bucket)
  • Amp draw on pump motor

Preparing for Wet Weather

San Diego's rainy season (November-March) brings flood risks and contamination concerns.

Wellhead Protection

  • Seal inspection: Check that well cap gasket is intact
  • Drainage: Ensure surface water flows away from well
  • Flood risk: If well is in a low area, consider regrading or protective barriers
  • Clear debris: Remove leaves, mulch, and anything that holds moisture near well

After Heavy Rains

  • Check wellhead for any signs of flooding
  • Watch for changes in water quality (cloudiness, odor, color)
  • If flooding occurred, test for bacteria before drinking

Freeze Protection for Mountain Properties

San Diego County mountain communities (Julian, Palomar, Laguna Mountains, Pine Valley) regularly experience freezing temperatures.

Areas at Risk

  • Elevations above 3,000 feet
  • Shaded north-facing slopes
  • Properties in cold air drainages (valleys)

Protection Checklist

  • ☐ Insulate exposed supply pipes
  • ☐ Insulate pressure tank (if not in heated space)
  • ☐ Verify heat tape is working (if installed)
  • ☐ Check pump house/enclosure for gaps
  • ☐ Drain irrigation lines that won't be used
  • ☐ Know where your main shutoff is (in case of burst pipe)

Insulation Materials

  • Foam pipe insulation (minimum R-4 for exposed pipes)
  • Heat tape for especially vulnerable areas
  • Insulated covers for pressure tanks
  • Straw bales around above-ground components (temporary)

Fall Water Quality Testing

Fall is an ideal time for annual water testing:

Why Test in Fall?

  • Catches contamination from summer's heavy use
  • Baseline before potential flooding/contamination from winter rains
  • Time to address issues before winter (when service is harder)

Recommended Tests

  • Bacteria (coliform/E. coli): Essential, especially if any flooding occurred
  • Nitrates: Important in agricultural areas
  • pH and hardness: Affects treatment system performance
  • Iron/manganese: If you have staining issues

Treatment System Maintenance

  • Replace sediment filters
  • Check/refill water softener salt
  • Replace UV lamp if due (typically annual)
  • Clean brine tank if buildup present
  • Verify filter bypass valves work properly

Frequently Asked Questions

Why inspect my well in fall?

Fall inspection catches wear from heavy summer use before winter. It's also the ideal time to prepare for flooding, ensure proper drainage, and address any issues before winter when service access is more difficult.

Should I worry about freezing in San Diego County?

Yes, if you're in the mountains. Julian, Palomar, Pine Valley, and Laguna Mountains regularly experience freezing temperatures. Insulate exposed pipes, pressure tanks, and any above-ground plumbing in these areas.

How do I prepare my wellhead for rainy season?

Ensure the well cap is sealed tight, the concrete pad has no cracks, and ground slopes away from the wellhead. Clear debris and leaves. Verify the vent screen is clean. Consider a protective berm if in a flood-prone area.

How often should I test my well water?

Annual testing is recommended for most wells, with fall being an ideal time. Test more frequently (2-4 times/year) if you've had past contamination issues, are near agriculture, or have a shallow well.

What's the most important fall maintenance task?

Wellhead protection from flooding. Surface water contamination is the most common cause of well water problems. Ensuring your well cap is sealed and drainage is proper prevents most issues.

Should I have a professional inspect my well in fall?

If your well is over 15 years old, you've noticed performance changes, or you're in a flood-prone area, professional inspection is worthwhile. A technician can check components you can't easily access and identify developing problems.
