Common Well Emergencies We Handle

Complete Loss of Water

The most urgent call we receive: nothing comes out when you turn on the tap. This could indicate pump failure, electrical issues, or a depleted well. Our emergency technicians systematically diagnose the problem and get your water flowing as quickly as possible.

Well Pump Failure

When your submersible or jet pump fails, you're without water until it's replaced. We carry common pump models on our service trucks for emergency replacement. For deeper wells or specialty pumps, we can often complete the job within 24 hours.

Electrical Problems

Tripped breakers, burned control boxes, and failed capacitors are common causes of sudden water loss. These above-ground repairs can often be completed in under an hour with parts from our fully-stocked trucks.

Pressure Tank Issues

A ruptured bladder or waterlogged tank causes the pump to short-cycle, potentially burning out the motor. We carry replacement tanks and can swap them quickly to restore proper pressure.

Contamination Events

If your water suddenly changes color, smells strange, or tastes different, there may be contamination. We respond to assess the situation and recommend appropriate treatment or emergency measures.

Before You Call: Quick Troubleshooting

Before calling for emergency well service, quickly check these items—they might save you a service call:

  1. Check the breaker: Look at your electrical panel for a tripped breaker labeled "well" or "pump." Reset it once. If it trips again, call us.
  2. Check the pressure switch: Located near your pressure tank, it should click when you toggle it. No click = possible electrical issue.
  3. Check for visible leaks: A major leak in your plumbing could drain the tank faster than the pump can fill it.
  4. Did you recently lose power? Some systems need manual reset after power outages.

If these quick checks don't reveal the problem, or you're not comfortable inspecting equipment, call us immediately.
