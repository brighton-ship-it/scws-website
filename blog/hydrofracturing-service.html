Hydrofracturing Services to Increase Well Yield

When your well's production has declined or never delivered adequate water, hydrofracturing offers a proven method to increase yield without drilling a new well. Also called hydrofracking, this rehabilitation technique uses high-pressure water to open existing fractures and create new pathways for groundwater to flow into your well. Southern California Well Service provides professional hydrofracturing services throughout San Diego County, helping well owners maximize their water production.

Hydrofracturing isn't the right solution for every underperforming well, but when conditions are favorable, it can dramatically increase production—often doubling or tripling yield at a fraction of the cost of drilling a new well.

How Hydrofracturing Works

Most San Diego County wells draw water from fractured bedrock aquifers. Groundwater moves through cracks, fissures, and fractures in the rock rather than through pore spaces like in sand or gravel aquifers. The amount of water a well produces depends heavily on how many productive fractures intersect the well bore and how open those fractures are.

Hydrofracturing increases production by:

  • Opening Existing Fractures: High-pressure water forces open fractures that may have become restricted by mineral deposits or compression.
  • Extending Fractures: The pressure can extend existing fractures farther into the formation, connecting to additional water sources.
  • Creating New Fractures: Under sufficient pressure, new fractures form perpendicular to existing ones, expanding the well's "catchment area."
  • Cleaning Fractures: The high-pressure flush removes fine particles and mineral deposits that may be restricting flow.

The Hydrofracturing Process

Professional hydrofracturing requires specialized equipment and expertise. Here's how we approach the procedure:

Pre-Treatment Assessment

Before hydrofracturing, we evaluate whether your well is a good candidate. We review well construction records, measure current production, and may perform video inspection to assess well condition. This evaluation prevents wasting money on wells unlikely to respond to treatment.

Equipment Setup

We position our hydrofracturing rig at your wellhead. The system includes high-pressure pumps capable of generating 2,000+ PSI, a water supply (either your well water or tanker-supplied), and specialized packers that isolate sections of the well bore.

Packer Placement

Inflatable packers are lowered into the well and positioned to isolate specific zones. By sealing above and below a target zone, we concentrate all pressure on that section of rock rather than dissipating pressure up the well bore.

Pressure Application

Water is pumped into the isolated zone at gradually increasing pressure. We monitor pressure and flow rate continuously, watching for the characteristic "breakdown" that indicates fractures are opening. Each zone may receive multiple pressure cycles.

Zone-by-Zone Treatment

We typically treat the well in multiple zones, repositioning the packer to systematically work through water-bearing sections. This ensures the entire productive interval benefits from treatment.

Post-Treatment Flush

After fracturing, we flush the well to remove loosened material and displaced sediment. This cleaning step ensures the newly opened pathways remain clear.

Recovery Period

Wells often continue improving for days or weeks after hydrofracturing as fractures stabilize and water finds new pathways to the bore. We recommend waiting at least 24-48 hours before retesting production.

When Does Hydrofracturing Work Best?

Hydrofracturing is most effective in certain geological and well conditions:

  • Fractured Bedrock: Wells in crystalline rock (granite, metamorphic formations) are ideal candidates.
  • Declining Production: Wells that produced well initially but have declined over time often respond excellently.
  • Mineral-Restricted Fractures: When calcium or iron deposits have narrowed fracture openings.
  • Low-Producing New Wells: Some wells underperform from the start due to limited fracture development.
  • Good Well Construction: Structurally sound casing is required to contain high pressures.

When Hydrofracturing May Not Help

We're honest about limitations. Hydrofracturing isn't a miracle solution, and certain conditions make success unlikely:

  • Depleted Aquifer: If regional water levels have dropped significantly, opening fractures won't help if there's no water to flow through them.
  • Sand/Gravel Aquifers: Wells producing from unconsolidated formations don't benefit from fracturing.
  • Damaged Casing: Wells with compromised casing can't contain the pressures required.
  • Very Deep Wells: Extremely deep wells may require pressures beyond practical equipment capabilities.
  • Previous Treatment: Wells that have already been hydrofractured may have limited additional potential.

Success Rates and Expectations

In suitable geological conditions, hydrofracturing improves well production in approximately 70-80% of cases. Improvements range from modest increases (25-50%) to dramatic transformations (300% or more). Average improvements of 50-100% are common.

However, we cannot guarantee specific results before treatment. Geological conditions vary, and fracture networks respond unpredictably. We provide realistic expectations based on well evaluation and regional experience.

Hydrofracturing vs. Other Options

When your well underperforms, several options exist. Here's how hydrofracturing compares:

vs. Drilling Deeper

Deepening a well can access additional fractures but is more expensive than hydrofracturing and may not guarantee improvement. Hydrofracturing is often the logical first step before committing to deepening.

vs. Drilling a New Well

New wells cost $15,000-$40,000+ with no guarantee of better production. Hydrofracturing costs a fraction of that amount, making it a sensible option to try first.

vs. Well Cleaning

Chemical or mechanical well cleaning can remove deposits from screens and casing but doesn't open bedrock fractures. For wells in fractured rock, hydrofracturing addresses the root cause more effectively.

vs. Storage Systems

Storage tanks can buffer low-producing wells during peak demand but don't increase actual production. If your well can't keep up even with storage, hydrofracturing may be needed.

Safety and Environmental Considerations

Well hydrofracturing uses only water—no chemicals, proppants, or additives are involved. This distinguishes it completely from oil/gas fracking, which uses chemical-laden fluids and proppants to hold fractures open. Well hydrofracturing is a clean, environmentally responsible procedure.

The pressures used, while high, are applied locally within isolated zones. Surface disruption is minimal, and the procedure poses no risk to neighboring wells when performed by experienced professionals.

Cost of Hydrofracturing

Hydrofracturing costs vary based on well depth, number of zones treated, and site accessibility. Most residential well treatments fall in the range of $3,000-$8,000—significantly less than drilling alternatives.

We provide detailed estimates after well evaluation, so you know exactly what to expect before committing. Our quotes include all equipment, labor, and post-treatment flushing.

After Treatment Care

Following hydrofracturing:

  • Allow Recovery Time: Production often continues improving for days or weeks.
  • Monitor Production: Track water production to document improvement.
  • Watch for Sediment: Some temporary sediment is normal as the well clears.
  • Test Water Quality: We recommend water testing after treatment as a baseline.
  • Protect Your Investment: Regular maintenance helps sustain improvements.
