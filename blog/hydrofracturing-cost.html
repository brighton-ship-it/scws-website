Hydrofracturing Cost: What Does Hydrofracking a Well Cost?

Is your well producing less water than you need? Hydrofracturing (also called hydrofracking) can dramatically increase well yield without drilling deeper or drilling a new well. In San Diego County, hydrofracturing typically costs $3,000 to $8,000—often providing a significant return on investment through improved water production.

💵 Hydrofracturing Cost Summary

  • • Typical residential well: $3,000 - $6,000
  • • Deep or complex well: $5,000 - $8,000
  • • Multiple zone treatment: $6,000 - $10,000
  • • Assessment/evaluation: $300 - $500
  • • Pump removal/reinstall: Usually included

What is Hydrofracturing?

Hydrofracturing is a water well rehabilitation technique that uses high-pressure water to open existing fractures and create new pathways in rock formations. This increases the connection between your well and water-bearing zones in the surrounding rock.

⚠️ Not the Same as Oil/Gas Fracking

Well hydrofracturing is completely different from the controversial fracking used in oil and gas extraction. Water well hydrofracturing:

  • • Uses only clean water—no chemicals
  • • Operates at much lower pressures
  • • Targets shallow water zones (not deep oil/gas)
  • • Has been safely used for decades
  • • Is environmentally safe and regulated

How Hydrofracturing Works

Understanding the process helps you know what to expect:

Step 1: Assessment

Before recommending hydrofracturing, we evaluate your well to determine if it's a good candidate. This includes:

  • Current flow rate measurement
  • Well depth and casing condition review
  • Geological assessment
  • Video inspection (if needed)

Step 2: Pump Removal

Your submersible pump and drop pipe must be removed to access the well. This equipment is carefully pulled and set aside for reinstallation.

Step 3: Packer Installation

A specialized rubber packer (inflatable seal) is lowered to the target depth—usually just above water-bearing fractures identified during assessment. The packer isolates the treatment zone.

Step 4: High-Pressure Injection

Clean water is pumped into the isolated zone at high pressure (typically 1,500-3,000 PSI). This pressure:

  • Opens existing fractures in the rock
  • May create new micro-fractures
  • Clears sediment and mineral deposits
  • Extends the effective radius of your well

Step 5: Development

After treatment, the well is "developed" by surging and pumping to remove loosened material and maximize water flow through the opened fractures.

Step 6: Completion

Your pump is reinstalled and the system tested. We measure the new flow rate and compare to pre-treatment levels.

⏱️ Typical Timeline

Most hydrofracturing jobs take 1-2 days to complete. You'll be without water during the work, so plan accordingly. We can often provide a temporary water connection if needed.

When Does Hydrofracturing Work Best?

Hydrofracturing is most effective in certain conditions:

Ideal Candidates

  • Fractured rock formations: Granite, gneiss, and other crystalline rock (common in San Diego)
  • Wells that once produced more: Suggests existing fractures have become restricted
  • Low-yield new wells: May not have fully connected to water-bearing fractures
  • Wells with known fracture zones: Video inspection shows promising targets

Poor Candidates

  • Sandy/alluvial formations: No fractures to open
  • Damaged casing: Can't maintain pressure
  • No groundwater present: Can't create water that isn't there
  • Wells near contamination: Could open pathways to polluted water

🎯 San Diego Success Rate

In San Diego County's fractured granite geology, hydrofracturing has approximately a 70-80% success rate when wells are properly evaluated beforehand. Success means a meaningful increase in water production—typically 50% or more improvement.

Factors That Affect Hydrofracturing Cost

1. Well Depth

Deeper wells require more time to pull and reinstall the pump, and may need treatment at multiple depths. A 500-foot well costs more than a 200-foot well.

2. Number of Zones

Some wells benefit from treating multiple fracture zones at different depths. Each zone adds time and cost, but may significantly improve results.

3. Access and Location

The hydrofrac unit is truck-mounted and needs reasonable access to your wellhead. Difficult access may require additional equipment or setup time.

4. Well Condition

If your well needs additional work (casing repairs, debris removal) before hydrofracturing can be performed, costs increase.

5. Water Requirements

Hydrofracturing uses 2,000-5,000+ gallons of clean water. If a water source isn't available nearby, water may need to be trucked in.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does hydrofracturing a well cost?

Hydrofracturing costs $3,000-$8,000 for most residential wells in San Diego County. The price depends on well depth, number of fracture zones treated, and equipment required. Most jobs fall in the $4,000-$6,000 range.

What is hydrofracturing and how does it work?

Hydrofracturing uses high-pressure water (1,500-3,000 PSI) injected into the well to open existing fractures and create new pathways in the rock. This allows more water to flow into the well from the surrounding formation. It's different from oil/gas fracking—no chemicals are used, just clean water.

Does hydrofracturing always work?

Hydrofracturing has about a 70-80% success rate in suitable geology. Success depends on rock type, existing fractures, and available groundwater. Wells in fractured granite (common in San Diego) respond well. It may not help if there's simply no water in the formation.

How much more water will I get after hydrofracturing?

Typical improvements range from 50% to 300% increase in well yield. A well producing 2 GPM might increase to 4-8 GPM after successful hydrofracturing. Results vary significantly based on geology and existing conditions.

Is hydrofracturing safe for my well?

Yes, hydrofracturing is safe when performed by experienced professionals. Only clean water is used—no chemicals. The process is carefully controlled to avoid damaging the well casing. It's been used successfully for decades on residential wells.

How long do hydrofracturing results last?

Results are typically permanent. The opened fractures remain open, providing improved water flow indefinitely. In some cases, mineral deposits may gradually reduce flow over many years, but the well can be re-treated if needed.
