Replace Well Casing Cost

Discovered your well casing is damaged? This is a serious issue that can lead to contamination, pump damage, and well failure. Well casing replacement or repair costs range from $2,500 for a liner to $20,000+ for full replacement. This guide explains your options, costs, and when each solution makes sense.

💲 Quick Cost Summary

  • • Spot repair/patch: $1,500 - $4,000
  • • Casing liner (partial): $2,500 - $5,000
  • • Full casing liner: $4,000 - $8,000
  • • Complete casing replacement: $5,000 - $20,000+
  • • New well (when better option): $15,000 - $50,000

Understanding Well Casing

Well casing is the structural pipe that lines your well bore, serving critical functions:

  • Structural support: Keeps the well bore from collapsing
  • Contamination barrier: Prevents surface water and pollutants from entering
  • Aquifer separation: Isolates different underground water zones
  • Pump housing: Provides space for submersible pump installation

When casing fails, these protections are compromised—potentially contaminating your water and damaging your pump.

Signs of Casing Damage

🏜️ Sand or Sediment in Water

Material entering through casing cracks or holes

🦠 Sudden Bacteria Problems

Surface contamination entering through casing breach

🔽 Declining Water Production

Debris blocking water entry or pump intake

💔 Repeated Pump Failures

Sediment damaging pump components

🔴 Rust at Wellhead

Visible corrosion on exposed casing section

🧪 Water Quality Changes

New tastes, odors, or appearance after rainfall

📹 Video Inspection Is Essential

Before any casing repair decision, a video inspection ($300-$800) is critical. It shows exactly where damage is located, how extensive it is, and what repair options are viable. Never proceed with casing work without seeing the problem first.

Casing Repair Options and Costs

Option 1: Spot Repair / Patch

For localized damage—a small hole, crack, or single failure point—a patch repair may be sufficient.

Cost: $1,500 - $4,000

Best For:

  • Single isolated defect
  • Casing otherwise in good condition
  • Accessible depth (upper portion of well)

Process:

  • Install casing sleeve or patch over damaged area
  • Seal with cement grout or approved sealant
  • Verify repair with video inspection

Limitations:

  • Won't address widespread corrosion
  • May be temporary if casing is generally deteriorating

Option 2: Casing Liner (Most Common Solution)

A liner is a smaller-diameter pipe inserted inside the existing casing, then sealed with cement grout. This "pipe within a pipe" approach is often the best balance of cost and effectiveness.

Liner Type Cost Range Best For
Partial liner (upper section) $2,500 - $5,000 Damage limited to upper 100-200 feet
PVC liner (full length) $4,000 - $6,000 Moderate depths, corrosion throughout
Steel liner (full length) $5,000 - $8,000 Deep wells, structural integrity needed
Stainless steel liner $6,000 - $10,000 Aggressive water, long-term durability

✅ Liner Advantages

Cost Effective

50-70% less than full replacement

Faster Installation

Usually completed in 1-2 days

Preserves Well

No need to drill new well

Long-Lasting

PVC liners can last 50+ years

⚠️ Liner Considerations

Installing a liner reduces the well's internal diameter. A 6" casing with a 4" liner leaves a 4" opening—still adequate for most submersible pumps, but large-capacity pumps may not fit. This is another reason video inspection and professional assessment are important before proceeding.

Option 3: Complete Casing Replacement

In rare cases, the casing must be completely replaced—typically requiring partial or complete redrilling of the well.

Cost: $5,000 - $20,000+

When Necessary:

  • Casing has collapsed or severely deformed
  • Original casing diameter too small for liner
  • Structural integrity completely compromised
  • Well being deepened anyway

Process:

  • May involve pulling old casing (if possible)
  • Often requires redrilling the well bore
  • Installing new casing and screen
  • Grouting and well development

At these cost levels, a new well often makes more sense. See our well drilling cost guide for comparison.

What Causes Casing Failure?

Corrosion (Most Common)

  • Low pH water: Acidic water attacks steel casing
  • High TDS: Dissolved solids accelerate corrosion
  • Oxygen exposure: Water level fluctuations expose casing to air
  • Age: Steel casings typically last 25-50 years

Physical Damage

  • Ground settling: Shifting soil stresses casing joints
  • Earthquake damage: Seismic activity can crack or shear casing
  • Impact: Construction or vehicles near wellhead
  • Dropped objects: Tools or debris damaging casing walls

Installation Issues

  • Poor joint connections: Inadequate threading or welding
  • Insufficient grouting: Gaps in cement seal allow corrosion
  • Wrong material: Steel used where PVC would be better
  • Undersized casing: Walls too thin for conditions

External Factors

  • Stray electrical current: Electrolysis from nearby utilities
  • Road salt: Chlorides accelerate steel corrosion
  • Agricultural chemicals: Fertilizers and pesticides
  • Soil conditions: Some soils are highly corrosive

Casing Liner vs. New Well: Making the Decision

When casing damage is severe, you face a choice between liner installation and drilling a new well:

Factor Favors Liner Favors New Well
Cost Liner under 40% of new well cost Liner exceeds 60% of new well cost
Well condition Only casing is damaged Multiple problems (low yield, etc.)
Well age Relatively young, good construction Very old, unknown construction
Water quality Good water quality otherwise Other quality issues present
Location Well location is ideal Better location available
Future needs Current capacity is adequate Need more water or deeper well

💡 Rule of Thumb

If liner cost is under 40% of what a new well would cost, and the well is otherwise functional, the liner usually makes sense. If liner cost approaches 60%+ of new well cost, strongly consider drilling new—you'll get a completely fresh well with full lifespan ahead.

The Casing Repair Process

Step 1: Inspection and Assessment

  • Video camera inspection to document damage
  • Measure casing diameter and well depth
  • Assess water quality and production
  • Determine appropriate repair method

Step 2: Permitting

  • Submit well rehabilitation permit application
  • Wait for county approval (typically 1-2 weeks)

Step 3: Pump Removal and Cleaning

  • Pull pump and drop pipe
  • Clean debris from well
  • Confirm measurements and conditions

Step 4: Liner Installation

  • Lower liner sections into well
  • Join sections (threaded or welded)
  • Position to cover all damaged areas

Step 5: Grouting

  • Pump cement grout between liner and old casing
  • Allow curing time (typically 24-48 hours)
  • Seal annular space completely

Step 6: Completion

  • Reinstall pump at appropriate depth
  • Test water quality and production
  • File completion report with county

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to replace well casing?

Full casing replacement costs $5,000-$20,000+. A casing liner is more affordable at $2,500-$8,000. Spot repairs for localized damage may cost $1,500-$4,000.

What causes well casing to fail?

Common causes include corrosion from aggressive water, age (steel lasts 25-50 years), physical damage from settling or seismic activity, stray electrical currents, and poor original installation.

What are signs of damaged well casing?

Signs include sand in water, bacteria contamination, decreased production, pump damage, visible rust at wellhead, and water quality changes after rain. Video inspection confirms damage.

What is a well casing liner?

A liner is a smaller pipe inserted inside damaged casing and sealed with grout—a "pipe within a pipe" solution. It restores integrity without removing original casing. Common materials are PVC and stainless steel.

Can well casing be repaired instead of replaced?

Sometimes. Localized damage may be repairable with patches. Widespread corrosion or multiple failures usually requires a liner or new well.

When is a new well better than casing repair?

Consider new well when repair cost exceeds 60-70% of new well cost, well has other issues (low yield, poor quality), original construction was substandard, or aquifer conditions have changed.

Does casing replacement require a permit?

Yes, most California jurisdictions require permits for well rehabilitation including casing repair. The contractor handles permits and completion reports.

How long does casing repair take?

Liner installation takes 1-2 days. Full replacement takes 3-5 days. Permit processing adds 1-2 weeks. You'll be without water during work.
