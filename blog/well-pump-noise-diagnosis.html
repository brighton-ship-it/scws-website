Well Pump Making Noise? Diagnose by Sound

Updated February 2026 | By Southern California Well Service

Quick Answer: Clicking = pressure switch/tank issue. Humming = capacitor or seized motor. Grinding = worn bearings. Screaming/squealing = dry pump or bad bearings. Rapid cycling = waterlogged tank. Each sound points to specific problems—use this guide to narrow it down.

Clicking Sound

Where It Comes From

Usually the pressure switch contacts opening and closing.

Normal vs Problem

  • Normal: One click when pump starts, one when it stops
  • Problem: Rapid clicking every few seconds

Rapid Clicking Causes

Cause Fix
Low tank air pressure Add air to tank
Failed tank bladder Replace tank
Leak in system Find and repair leak
Faulty pressure switch Replace switch ($25-$75)
Check valve failing Replace check valve

Action

Frequent cycling damages pump motor. Address promptly.

Humming Sound

Humming but Running = Normal

Electric motors hum. If pump is working, normal electrical sound.

Humming but NOT Running = Problem

Motor trying to start but can't.

Causes

  • Bad start capacitor: Most common, easy fix
  • Seized motor: Bearings failed, motor locked
  • Low voltage: Not enough power to start
  • Stuck impeller: Debris jamming pump

What to Do

  1. Turn off power immediately (motor overheating)
  2. For jet pump: check/replace capacitor
  3. For submersible: may need professional diagnosis
  4. If capacitor OK, likely motor or impeller issue

Capacitor Replacement

  • Cost: $20-$50 for part
  • Professional service: $100-$200
  • Match µF and voltage ratings exactly

Grinding Sound

What It Indicates

  • Worn bearings
  • Sand or debris in pump
  • Impeller damage
  • Motor shaft problems

Jet Pump Grinding

  • May be repairable
  • Check for debris in impeller
  • Bearing replacement possible on some models

Submersible Pump Grinding

  • Often means pump is failing
  • Sand damage common in sandy aquifers
  • Usually requires pump replacement

Action

Grinding typically worsens. Plan for replacement or rebuild before complete failure.

Screaming/Squealing

High-Pitched Screaming

  • Pump running dry: No water reaching pump—emergency!
  • Cavitation: Pump pulling air, not just water
  • Bad bearings: Metal on metal

Running Dry = Emergency

  • Pump cools itself with water
  • Dry running = rapid overheating
  • Can destroy pump in minutes
  • Turn off immediately

Causes of Dry Running

  • Well ran out of water
  • Pump set too shallow
  • Water level dropped below pump
  • Clog preventing water entry

Action

Stop pump. Wait for well to recover. May need pump lowered or well assessment.

Rapid Cycling

What It Is

Pump turns on and off every few seconds or minutes (not continuous running).

Common Causes

Cause Diagnosis Fix
Waterlogged tank Tank feels heavy, no air cushion Recharge air or replace tank
Leak Look for wet spots, running water Repair leak
Check valve failing Water drains back into well Replace check valve
Pressure switch issue Erratic operation Adjust or replace

Why It Matters

  • Each start is hardest on motor
  • Frequent cycling = rapid wear
  • Can cut pump life by years

Water Hammer

Sound

Loud banging or thudding when pump starts or stops, or when valves close.

Causes

  • Sudden valve closure
  • No check valve (or failed)
  • High water pressure
  • Air in pipes

Fixes

  • Install or replace check valve
  • Add water hammer arrestor
  • Reduce pressure if too high
  • Secure loose pipes

Damage Risk

Repeated water hammer can damage pipes, fittings, and equipment. Address it.

Gurgling/Air Sounds

Air in Lines

  • Sputtering at faucets
  • Gurgling sounds
  • Inconsistent flow

Causes

  • Low water level in well
  • Leak allowing air in
  • Failing check valve
  • Crack in drop pipe

If Persistent

  • May indicate well running low
  • Need pump lowered
  • Or water supply issue

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is my well pump clicking on and off?

Usually low tank air pressure, failed bladder, or leak. Rapid clicking causes excessive pump wear—address promptly.

What does it mean when my pump hums but won't start?

Likely a bad start capacitor (easy fix) or seized motor (more serious). Turn off power to prevent overheating.

Is some pump noise normal?

Yes—electric motors hum, pumps make working sounds. Concern is new noises, louder noises, or grinding/screaming.

Should I keep running my pump if it's making noise?

Depends on noise. Screaming = stop immediately. Grinding = schedule service soon. Clicking = diagnose and fix.

How much does it cost to fix pump noise issues?

Varies widely: capacitor $50-$200, pressure switch $50-$150, tank replacement $500-$1,500, pump replacement $1,500-$4,000.
