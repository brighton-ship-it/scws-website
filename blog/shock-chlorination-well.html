Quick Answer: Shock chlorination kills bacteria in your well using household bleach. Use 3 pints per 100 gallons of water in the well. Pour in bleach, recirculate with hose, run through all faucets until you smell chlorine, wait 12-24 hours, then flush until chlorine is gone. Retest water after 1-2 weeks.

When to Shock Chlorinate

Shock Chlorination Is Needed When:

  • Bacteria test comes back positive (coliform present)
  • After well repair, pump replacement, or any work on the well
  • After flooding or surface contamination event
  • When a new well is drilled
  • If water has odor or slime (iron bacteria)
  • As annual preventive maintenance (optional)

Shock Chlorination Does NOT:

  • Fix the source of contamination (you must address that separately)
  • Provide ongoing disinfection (one-time treatment)
  • Remove nitrates, arsenic, or chemical contaminants

Supplies Needed

Required

  • Unscented household bleach (8.25% sodium hypochlorite)
  • 5-gallon bucket
  • Garden hose that reaches into well
  • Rubber gloves and safety glasses

Helpful

  • Funnel for pouring into well
  • Long-handled brush for scrubbing casing
  • Chlorine test strips

About Chlorine Products

  • Best: Unscented regular bleach (Clorox, etc.)
  • OK: Calcium hypochlorite granules (pool shock)
  • Avoid: Scented bleach, splashless bleach, color-safe bleach

Calculate Chlorine Amount

Formula

Use 3 pints of household bleach per 100 gallons of water in the well.

Estimate Water in Well

Water volume = (Depth of water) × (gallons per foot based on casing diameter)

Casing Diameter Gallons per Foot
4 inch 0.65 gallons
5 inch 1.0 gallons
6 inch 1.5 gallons
8 inch 2.6 gallons

Example Calculation

Well: 6-inch casing, 200 ft deep, static water level at 100 ft

Water depth: 200 - 100 = 100 feet of water

Water volume: 100 ft × 1.5 gal/ft = 150 gallons

Bleach needed: (150 ÷ 100) × 3 pints = 4.5 pints (about 2.25 quarts)

When in Doubt

Use more rather than less. Extra chlorine won't hurt—it just takes longer to flush out.

Step-by-Step Process

Step 1: Prepare

  1. Turn off power to pump
  2. Remove well cap
  3. Bypass any water treatment equipment (softener, filter)
  4. Calculate chlorine amount needed

Step 2: Add Chlorine to Well

  1. Mix calculated bleach with 3-5 gallons of water in bucket
  2. Pour solution directly into well casing
  3. If possible, scrub inside of casing with long-handled brush

Step 3: Recirculate

  1. Turn pump power back on
  2. Attach garden hose to outdoor spigot
  3. Place hose end into well opening
  4. Run water back into well for 15-30 minutes
  5. This circulates chlorine throughout well and mixes it

Step 4: Run Through Plumbing

  1. Remove hose from well
  2. Open each cold water faucet until you smell chlorine
  3. Open each hot water faucet until you smell chlorine
  4. Flush toilets
  5. Run washing machine through rinse cycle
  6. Close all faucets

Step 5: Wait

  1. Replace well cap
  2. Let stand 12-24 hours (overnight is ideal)
  3. Don't use water during this time

Step 6: Flush System

  1. Connect hose to outdoor spigot
  2. Run water away from landscaping and septic
  3. Continue until chlorine smell is gone (may take hours)
  4. Then run indoor faucets until no chlorine smell
  5. Re-install water treatment equipment

Safety Precautions

Personal Safety

  • Wear rubber gloves and safety glasses
  • Work in ventilated area
  • Never mix chlorine with other chemicals
  • Keep children and pets away

Environmental Safety

  • Don't discharge chlorinated water into streams or ponds
  • Avoid discharging near plants (chlorine can damage them)
  • Don't discharge large volumes to septic system at once

System Safety

  • Bypass water softeners and carbon filters (chlorine damages them)
  • Don't use water for drinking until flushed

After Chlorination

Retest Water

  • Wait 1-2 weeks after flushing
  • Test for coliform bacteria again
  • If still positive, may need repeat treatment or address contamination source

If Bacteria Returns

  • Look for contamination source (damaged cap, casing breach, etc.)
  • May need well inspection
  • Consider continuous disinfection (UV or chlorination system)

Maintenance

If bacteria was present, consider annual shock chlorination as preventive measure.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much bleach do I need to shock my well?

Use 3 pints per 100 gallons of water in the well. Calculate based on casing diameter and depth of water.

How long does shock chlorination take?

Allow 2-4 hours for the process, plus 12-24 hours contact time, plus 1-4 hours flushing. Plan for the well to be out of service for about 24-48 hours total.

Can I shock chlorinate my well myself?

Yes, it's a DIY-able project for most homeowners. However, if you're not comfortable or if bacteria keeps returning, call a professional.

How often should I shock chlorinate?

Only when needed (positive bacteria test, after work on well, etc.). Some do it annually as preventive maintenance, but it's not required if tests are clear.

Will chlorine damage my well or pump?

No—chlorine at these levels won't damage the well, casing, or pump. However, bypass water treatment equipment like softeners and carbon filters.
