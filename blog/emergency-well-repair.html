What Constitutes a Well Emergency?

A well emergency is any situation where you've completely lost water or are experiencing a dangerous condition with your well system. Common emergencies we respond to include:

  • Complete Loss of Water: You turn on the tap and nothing comes out—the most urgent emergency.
  • Well Pump Failure: The pump motor has burned out or stopped working entirely.
  • Electrical Problems: Breakers tripping repeatedly, burning smells, or visible electrical damage.
  • Pressure Tank Rupture: A failed bladder or cracked tank causing water hammer or no pressure.
  • Contamination Events: Sudden changes in water color, smell, or taste indicating possible contamination.
  • Visible Damage: Storm damage, vehicle strikes, or other physical damage to wellhead components.

Common Emergency Repairs We Perform

Over decades of emergency service throughout San Diego County, we've seen every type of well failure. Here are the most common emergency repairs we perform:

Well Pump Replacement: When a submersible pump fails, the entire pump assembly must be pulled from the well and replaced. Our crews are experienced with wells of all depths and can perform emergency pump replacements efficiently.

Pressure Switch and Control Box Repairs: Electrical components above ground often fail before the pump itself. We carry common pressure switches, capacitors, and control boxes for same-visit repairs.

Pressure Tank Replacement: A waterlogged or ruptured pressure tank causes rapid pump cycling and loss of pressure. We stock common tank sizes for immediate replacement.

Electrical System Repairs: Lightning strikes, power surges, and rodent damage can disable well electrical systems. We diagnose and repair wiring, breakers, and connections.

Why Well Emergencies Happen

Understanding why wells fail can help you prevent future emergencies. The most common causes of sudden well failure include:

  • Pump Wear and Age: Submersible pumps typically last 10-15 years. As they age, sudden failure becomes more likely.
  • Power Surges: San Diego's summer thunderstorms can send damaging surges through electrical systems.
  • Low Water Levels: During droughts, pumps can run dry and burn out.
  • Sediment Damage: Excess sand or sediment wears pump components and clogs systems.
  • Lack of Maintenance: Wells that aren't regularly serviced are more prone to sudden failure.

Preventing Future Emergencies

The best emergency is one that never happens. Regular well maintenance dramatically reduces the likelihood of sudden failures. We recommend annual well inspections to catch developing problems before they become emergencies. During these inspections, we check pump performance, electrical systems, pressure tank condition, and water quality.

If your pump is approaching the 10-year mark, consider proactive replacement during a scheduled service call rather than waiting for emergency failure. A planned pump replacement is less expensive and far less stressful than an emergency at 2 AM.
