What is Hydrofracturing?

Hydrofracturing is a well rehabilitation technique that uses high-pressure water to clean and expand existing fractures in the bedrock surrounding your well. This process:

  • Clears mineral deposits and sediment blocking water flow
  • Opens new pathways for groundwater to reach your well
  • Can increase well yield by 2-10 times in many cases
  • Extends the life of your existing well

Unlike oil and gas fracking, well hydrofracturing uses only clean water—no chemicals are involved.

Signs Your Kensington Well Needs Hydrofracturing

  • Declining water production over time
  • Well runs dry during peak usage
  • Slow recovery rate after pumping
  • Pump cycling on and off frequently
  • Lower water pressure than previous years

The Hydrofracturing Process

  1. Initial Assessment: We evaluate your well's current yield and condition
  2. Equipment Setup: Specialized high-pressure equipment is positioned at your well
  3. Isolation: Packers seal off sections of the well for targeted treatment
  4. Pressurization: Water is pumped at 500-3,000 PSI to fracture bedrock
  5. Development: Well is cleaned and tested for improved yield

Hydrofracturing for Low-Yield Wells in Kensington

When a private well in Kensington starts producing less water than it once did, the instinct is often to assume the well is dying and a costly new one is the only answer. In most cases, that is not true. Kensington sits on the inland mesas of central San Diego County, near Normal Heights and Talmadge, where the underlying geology trends toward decomposed granite and fractured crystalline bedrock, and bedrock wells in this kind of formation are precisely the wells that respond best to hydrofracturing. By reopening and connecting the natural fractures that feed your well, we can frequently restore strong production for a fraction of what a new well would cost.

Hydrofracturing, sometimes written hydrofracking, is a well rehabilitation technique, not a drilling method. It uses nothing but clean water under high pressure to clear and widen the existing cracks in the rock around your borehole. For homeowners on the historic neighborhoods and canyon-edge parcels around Kensington and nearby Talmadge, it has rescued many wells that appeared to be failing.

How the Process Works in Hard Bedrock

Your well does not draw water from a single underground pool. It draws from a network of fractures running through the surrounding rock. Over the years, those fractures fill with mineral scale and fine sediment, and the well slowly loses its connection to the groundwater moving through the formation. Hydrofracturing reverses that decline.

We lower an inflatable packer into the well to isolate a target section below the casing, then inject water at pressures that build into the thousands of PSI. That pressure flushes the clogged fractures clean and, just as importantly, extends them outward to intersect additional water-bearing cracks the well could not previously reach. The result is a well that is hydraulically reconnected to far more of the aquifer. The injection phase usually takes four to eight hours at the wellhead.

Signs Your Kensington Well May Benefit

  • Steadily declining output over several years with no equipment fault to explain it
  • Running dry during peak demand such as summer irrigation or back-to-back showers
  • Slow recovery where the well takes a long time to refill after heavy use
  • Frequent pump cycling as the pump struggles against a starved well
  • A new well that disappointed, drilled into rock but never reaching the expected yield

If one or more of these describes your situation and your well is set in fractured bedrock, hydrofracturing deserves a serious look before you spend on a replacement.

Cost Compared to Drilling a New Well

The financial case is straightforward. Hydrofracturing an existing Kensington well typically costs $3,000 to $8,000. Drilling a replacement well runs $15,000 to $50,000 or more depending on depth, and a complete turnkey new well, including pump and equipment, generally falls between $18,000 and $42,000. Because hydrofracturing costs a fraction of redrilling and can be done in a single day on site, it is the logical first step. If it does not deliver enough improvement, you have lost relatively little and can move to deepening or a new well with better information about your formation.

Is my Kensington well a good candidate for hydrofracturing?

Hydrofracturing works best on wells drilled into hard, fractured bedrock, which describes much of the terrain in and around Kensington and the inland mesas of central San Diego County. If your well draws from decomposed granite or fractured crystalline rock and its yield has declined, it is usually a strong candidate. Wells set in loose sand or gravel are not good candidates, so we always confirm your geology before recommending the treatment.

How much can hydrofracturing improve my well's yield?

In San Diego County's fractured bedrock, we typically see a 2-to-5-times improvement in flow rate, and occasionally more, with success in roughly 70-80% of wells we treat. Are any chemicals used in the process?

No. Well hydrofracturing uses only clean water under pressure. It is completely different from oil-and-gas fracking. Nothing is added to your groundwater, which is why it is a safe rehabilitation method for a residential drinking-water well.
