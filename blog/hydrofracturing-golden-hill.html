What is Hydrofracturing?

Hydrofracturing is a well rehabilitation technique that uses high-pressure water to clean and expand existing fractures in the bedrock surrounding your well. This process:

  • Clears mineral deposits and sediment blocking water flow
  • Opens new pathways for groundwater to reach your well
  • Can increase well yield by 2-10 times in many cases
  • Extends the life of your existing well

Unlike oil and gas fracking, well hydrofracturing uses only clean water—no chemicals are involved.

Signs Your Golden Hill Well Needs Hydrofracturing

  • Declining water production over time
  • Well runs dry during peak usage
  • Slow recovery rate after pumping
  • Pump cycling on and off frequently
  • Lower water pressure than previous years

The Hydrofracturing Process

  1. Initial Assessment: We evaluate your well's current yield and condition
  2. Equipment Setup: Specialized high-pressure equipment is positioned at your well
  3. Isolation: Packers seal off sections of the well for targeted treatment
  4. Pressurization: Water is pumped at 500-3,000 PSI to fracture bedrock
  5. Development: Well is cleaned and tested for improved yield

The Geology That Governs Local Wells

San Diego County's well country lies within the Peninsular Ranges, built of decomposed granite and fractured crystalline rock. Wells here do not tap a thick, uniform aquifer; they pull water from the maze of fractures threading the bedrock. That single fact drives almost everything about how these wells perform. A borehole that crosses a major fracture flows well; one that misses it struggles, even a few yards away. Over time, even good wells decline as sediment, scale, and iron fouling clog the fractures and drought lowers the water table. The water remains in the rock, but the well loses its connection to it.

Because the yield of a fractured-rock well depends so heavily on chance fracture intersections, the difference hydrofracturing makes can be dramatic. We have seen wells limping along at a gallon or two per minute, barely enough for a household, jump to comfortable, dependable output after treatment opened and connected nearby fractures the original bore had skirted. The groundwater was always there; the well simply lacked the plumbing to reach it.

How Hydrofracturing Reopens the Path

Hydrofracturing rebuilds that connection. A technician isolates a section of the borehole with an inflatable packer, then injects clean water, never any chemicals, at pressures of 1,500 to over 3,000 PSI. The water blasts sediment and scale out of the fractures and forces existing cracks wider, creating new routes for groundwater to flow into the well. A typical job runs four to eight hours. Where a deeper well has several producing intervals, we can treat each one separately to maximize the overall gain.

Why a Well's Output Drops

  • Clogged fractures. Fine sediment and mineral scale gradually seal the cracks that feed the well.
  • Iron and bacterial fouling. Biofilm and iron deposits coat the fracture faces and the screen.
  • Drought. San Diego County's dry cycles lower static levels, pushing marginal wells below the line.
  • Age and under-development. Many wells were never fully developed at completion and have lost capacity since.

San Diego County's well owners also contend with hard, mineral-rich groundwater. That hardness is more than a nuisance for appliances; it actively accelerates the scaling that narrows fractures and perforations, shortening the time between a well's prime and its decline. Part of what hydrofracturing does is strip away that accumulated scale, and we are happy to walk you through water-treatment measures that slow its return so your restored yield lasts longer.

Is Your Well a Candidate?

The hard, fractured rock of San Diego County is well suited to hydrofracturing, and success rates in this geology are strong. It makes sense when your well once met demand and no longer does, when a new well came in low, when production recovers slowly after pumping, or when you want a lower-cost step before drilling new. We are equally clear about its limits: wells in loose sand will not hold an open fracture, and a damaged casing must be repaired first. Our assessment tells you honestly whether treatment is likely to help.

For owners weighing their options, it helps to think of hydrofracturing as the logical first move rather than a last resort. Even in the minority of cases where a treated well ultimately needs replacing, the modest cost of trying rehabilitation first is usually recovered many times over by the wells that respond, and the assessment that comes with it tells you a great deal about your well's condition either way.

Practical Checks to Run First

  • Time the recovery. Slow pressure rebuild after heavy use points to formation yield, not equipment.
  • Examine the pressure tank and switch. A waterlogged tank or worn switch ($150 to $350) can mimic low yield cheaply.
  • Listen for cycling. A pump cycling every minute is often outrunning the well.
  • Note sediment or air in the water, which guides our diagnosis.

Depth varies considerably across the county's well country. Some foothill wells find adequate fractures within a couple hundred feet, while backcountry wells near Julian and the higher elevations may run six hundred feet or deeper to reach reliable producing zones. Deeper wells frequently develop more than one water-bearing interval, and our ability to isolate and treat those intervals one at a time is a real advantage over simply drilling deeper and hoping for the best.

When You Need a Professional

Hydrofracturing uses pressures that can harm people and wells in inexperienced hands, so it is professional-only work. A licensed C-57 contractor performs a pre-treatment flow test, runs a downhole camera inspection to confirm casing integrity, and verifies the well can safely accept treatment before any injection. If your well has dropped sharply or quit, call without delay; we provide same-day emergency service across San Diego County.

What Hydrofracturing Costs

Expect hydrofracturing to run $3,000 to $8,000, varying with depth, the number of zones, and site access. A new turnkey well costs $18,000 to $42,000, so treating your existing well first is almost always the economical choice. Our diagnostic visit is $125, credited toward approved work. If the cause is mechanical, plan on $2,500 to $5,500 for a pump, $600 to $1,500 for a pressure tank, and $150 to $350 for a pressure switch.

There is also a long-term ownership benefit. A well that has been professionally assessed, camera-inspected, and hydrofractured comes with documentation of its condition and a measured before-and-after flow record. That record is valuable if you ever sell the property, reassuring to a lender or buyer, and useful to you for planning future maintenance. Good well stewardship pays dividends well beyond the immediate jump in water.

Golden Hill and San Diego County Hydrofracturing FAQ

Are there wells in Golden Hill itself?

Golden Hill is on municipal water, but it anchors a county where rural and backcountry properties rely heavily on private wells. We serve those well owners throughout San Diego County, using Golden Hill as our central reference point.

Does hydrofracturing use chemicals?

No. Only clean water under pressure. After treatment we develop and flush the well and run a flow test so the water is clear before we leave.

How much can yield improve?

In the county's fractured granite, two to five times the pre-treatment flow is common, and some wells do better. We measure before and after so the result is documented.

What if it is really my pump?

That happens. A waterlogged tank, a failing pressure switch, or a worn pump can mimic low yield. Our diagnostic visit identifies the true cause first.

How long until the well is usable again?

Treatment takes four to eight hours, and after a 24-to-48-hour development and settling period we verify the new flow rate and return the well to service.

Are you licensed for this work?

Yes. We are a licensed C-57 California well contractor with local experience since 2020, fully insured, and we handle all required notifications.
