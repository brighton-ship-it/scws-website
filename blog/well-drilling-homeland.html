Well Drilling in Homeland

Homeland is a growing rural community in southwestern Riverside County, located between Hemet and Perris along Highway 74. With its mix of rural residences, horse properties, and small farms, many Homeland properties rely on private wells for water.

Southern California Well Service has drilled wells throughout Homeland and the surrounding San Jacinto Valley since 2008. We understand the local aquifer conditions, agricultural water needs, and the requirements of rural property owners in this area.

Homeland's Geology & Aquifer

San Jacinto Valley Groundwater Basin

Homeland sits within the San Jacinto groundwater basin, one of Riverside County's important aquifer systems:

  • Alluvial deposits: Sand, gravel, and clay from ancient river channels
  • Multiple aquifer layers: Stacked water-bearing zones at different depths
  • Generally favorable conditions: Good water production in most areas
  • Variable water quality: Some areas have higher TDS or hardness

Recharge Sources

The aquifer is recharged by:

  • San Jacinto River flows
  • Mountain runoff from the San Jacinto Mountains
  • Direct rainfall infiltration

Water Levels

Groundwater levels in the Homeland area have historically been stable, though they vary with drought cycles. Deeper wells provide more reliable long-term production.

Water Quality in Homeland

Homeland well water quality varies by location:

  • TDS: Typically 400-900 ppm (moderate)
  • Hardness: Moderate to hard (10-25 gpg common)
  • pH: Usually neutral to slightly alkaline
  • Common minerals: Calcium, magnesium, sometimes iron

Recommended Treatment

  • Water softener: Most properties need softening
  • Sediment filter: If sand or particles present
  • RO system: For drinking water if TDS is elevated
  • Iron filter: If iron staining occurs

We test every well and provide specific treatment recommendations based on your water chemistry.

Frequently Asked Questions

How deep are wells in Homeland?

Most residential wells range 150-400 feet depending on location. The San Jacinto Valley aquifer provides good water at moderate depths. We review neighbor well logs to estimate depth before drilling.

How much does a well cost in Homeland?

Complete systems typically cost $20,000-$50,000 depending on depth. The alluvial geology is relatively easy to drill, keeping costs moderate compared to rocky mountain areas.

Is Homeland well water safe to drink?

Generally yes with appropriate treatment. Most wells need a water softener due to hardness. Some areas have elevated TDS requiring additional treatment. We test every well and provide recommendations.

Do I need a permit?

Yes. Riverside County requires permits through Environmental Health. We handle all permitting as part of our complete service.

How long does it take to drill a well?

Active drilling takes 1-3 days depending on depth. Permit processing adds 2-4 weeks. Complete project timeline from site visit to flowing water is usually 4-6 weeks.
