What is Hydrofracturing?

Hydrofracturing is a well rehabilitation technique that uses high-pressure water to clean and expand existing fractures in the bedrock surrounding your well. This process:

  • Clears mineral deposits and sediment blocking water flow
  • Opens new pathways for groundwater to reach your well
  • Can increase well yield by 2-10 times in many cases
  • Extends the life of your existing well

Unlike oil and gas fracking, well hydrofracturing uses only clean water—no chemicals are involved.

Signs Your Del Mar Well Needs Hydrofracturing

  • Declining water production over time
  • Well runs dry during peak usage
  • Slow recovery rate after pumping
  • Pump cycling on and off frequently
  • Lower water pressure than previous years

The Hydrofracturing Process

  1. Initial Assessment: We evaluate your well's current yield and condition
  2. Equipment Setup: Specialized high-pressure equipment is positioned at your well
  3. Isolation: Packers seal off sections of the well for targeted treatment
  4. Pressurization: Water is pumped at 500-3,000 PSI to fracture bedrock
  5. Development: Well is cleaned and tested for improved yield

Hydrofracturing for Low-Yield Wells in Del Mar

Del Mar sits on the San Diego County coast between the Los Penasquitos Lagoon and the San Dieguito River, where a thin blanket of marine terrace and alluvial sediment overlies the granitic and metamorphic rock of the Peninsular Ranges batholith. Wells in the flats near the lagoons and the San Dieguito floodplain often draw from shallow alluvium, while properties up in the canyons and on the bluffs of Crest Canyon, the Beach Colony, and Olde Del Mar typically reach into fractured crystalline bedrock at depth. When one of those bedrock wells slows to a trickle, drilling a brand-new well is rarely the first thing you should pay for. Hydrofracturing — high-pressure water injection that reopens and connects the natural fractures feeding your well — is usually the smarter, far less expensive first move.

Southern California Well Service has spent more than 30 years rehabilitating wells across San Diego County, and we bring the same C-57 licensed crew and equipment to Del Mar that we use on the harder granite wells inland. If your water has been dropping off, this guide explains why, what hydrofracturing does, and how to tell whether your well is a good candidate.

How Hydrofracturing Actually Works

Hydrofracturing (sometimes called hydrofracking, though it has nothing to do with the oil-and-gas process) uses nothing but clean water under pressure. We lower an inflatable packer into your well casing and seal off a target zone in the bedrock. Water is then injected at roughly 1,000 to 3,000 PSI. That pressure does two things: it flushes the silt, mineral scale, and drill cuttings that have plugged the existing fractures over the years, and it widens and extends those hairline fractures so they connect to a larger volume of water-bearing rock.

In Del Mar's coastal granite and metamorphic rock, water moves almost entirely through fractures — the rock itself is nearly watertight. A well that was drilled into a poorly connected fracture network can sit right next to a productive one and never know it. Hydrofracturing bridges that gap. The whole treatment usually takes four to eight hours on site, and most homeowners can use their water normally within a day or two once the well settles and clears.

Why Del Mar Wells Lose Yield

A few patterns come up repeatedly in this part of the coast:

  • Fracture plugging: Fine sediment and mineral precipitate slowly seal the cracks that feed a bedrock well, especially in wells 15+ years old.
  • Marginal original yield: Some Del Mar bluff and canyon wells were modest producers from day one because the borehole simply caught a thin fracture set.
  • Seasonal drawdown: Long dry stretches lower the regional water table, and a marginal well is the first to feel it during summer irrigation demand.
  • Saltwater proximity: Near the lagoons and the coastline, over-pumping a low-yield well can pull in poorer-quality water; restoring proper yield reduces the temptation to over-draw.

Signs Your Del Mar Well Is a Candidate

  • The well used to keep up with the house and yard and now runs short during peak use.
  • The pump short-cycles — clicking on and off — because the well can't keep the pressure tank full.
  • Recovery is slow: after heavy use the well takes hours, not minutes, to refill.
  • You're getting air spurts or sediment at the faucets as the water level drops to the pump.
  • You've been quoted a new well and want a cheaper option to try first.

Hydrofracturing works best on wells drilled into hard, fractured bedrock. If your Del Mar well is a shallow alluvial well in loose sand and gravel near the San Dieguito floodplain, fracturing is usually not the answer — those wells respond better to redevelopment, screen cleaning, or a pump adjustment. Our diagnostic visit tells us which situation you're in before any pressure equipment comes out.

What to Check Before You Call

A little homeowner detective work helps us help you faster:

  1. Find your well log. The original driller's report lists total depth, casing depth, and the static water level — gold for diagnosis. San Diego County and the state DWR keep copies if you don't.
  2. Note the symptoms. When did yield drop — gradually over years, or suddenly? Sudden loss often points to a pump or pressure problem, not the formation.
  3. Check the easy stuff. A failed pressure switch ($150–$350) or a waterlogged pressure tank ($600–$1,500) can mimic a "dry well." Rule those out before assuming the well itself is the problem.
  4. Listen to the pump. Frequent rapid cycling usually means the well is being drawn down faster than it recovers — a classic low-yield signature.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will hydrofracturing damage my well casing?

No. We isolate the open-hole bedrock section below the casing with packers, so the high pressure is applied to the rock formation, not your steel or PVC casing. We also camera-inspect the casing first to confirm it's sound.

How long do the results last in a coastal well?

Most successful treatments hold for many years. Results depend on the rock and how the well is used; some Del Mar wells stay improved for a decade or more, while heavily pumped wells may eventually benefit from a second treatment.

Is hydrofracturing safe for my drinking water?

Yes. The process uses only clean water under pressure — no chemicals, no additives. We disinfect the well afterward, and you can have the water tested once it clears.

How soon will I know if it worked?

We run a pre-treatment flow test for a baseline and a post-treatment test, usually within 24–48 hours, so you get a documented before-and-after comparison rather than a guess.

What if my Del Mar well doesn't respond?

A minority of wells — usually those in loose sediment rather than hard rock — don't gain much. We'll tell you upfront if your well is a poor candidate, and if a treated well underperforms we'll walk you through the next options, including a new well.

Do you handle the permits?

We coordinate any required San Diego County notifications and follow state well standards. As a licensed C-57 contractor, keeping your project compliant is part of the job.
