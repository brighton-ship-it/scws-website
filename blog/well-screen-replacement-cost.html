Well Screen Service Pricing

Screen cleaning and rehabilitation costs $800-$2,000. Chemical treatment for scale and deposits runs $1,000-$3,000. Screen replacement for shallow wells costs $2,000-$5,000. Deep well screen replacement ranges from $5,000-$10,000 or more. Pump removal adds $200-$500 to any service.

Signs of Screen Problems

Watch for sand or grit in your water, gradual decline in water pressure over months or years, pump cycling more frequently than normal, sediment clogging fixtures and appliances, and camera inspection showing encrustation or collapse.

Screen Cleaning vs. Replacement

Chemical cleaning works well for mineral buildup. Mechanical brushing removes soft deposits. Collapsed or corroded screens require replacement. Cleaning buys time but isn't a permanent solution. Severely degraded screens often indicate an aging well that may need more comprehensive rehabilitation.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if my screen is clogged vs. damaged?

A video camera inspection is the only way to know for sure. Clogged screens show buildup that can potentially be cleaned. Damaged screens show holes, corrosion, or collapse requiring replacement. The inspection cost ($200-$500) is worth it before committing to expensive repairs.

What causes well screens to fail?

Common causes include mineral encrustation (iron, manganese, calcium), bacterial buildup (iron bacteria especially), corrosion from aggressive water chemistry, and physical damage from pump installation. Screen lifespan varies from 15-40+ years depending on conditions.

Can I prevent screen problems?

Regular chlorination helps control bacteria buildup. Water softeners or treatment systems reduce mineral scaling inside the well. Periodic flow testing catches declining production early. Some wells benefit from annual chemical treatments to maintain screen openings.
