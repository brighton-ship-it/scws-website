What is Hydrofracturing?

Hydrofracturing is a well rehabilitation technique that uses high-pressure water to clean and expand existing fractures in the bedrock surrounding your well. This process:

  • Clears mineral deposits and sediment blocking water flow
  • Opens new pathways for groundwater to reach your well
  • Can increase well yield by 2-10 times in many cases
  • Extends the life of your existing well

Unlike oil and gas fracking, well hydrofracturing uses only clean water—no chemicals are involved.

Signs Your Hinkley Well Needs Hydrofracturing

  • Declining water production over time
  • Well runs dry during peak usage
  • Slow recovery rate after pumping
  • Pump cycling on and off frequently
  • Lower water pressure than previous years

The Hydrofracturing Process

Hydrofracturing injects clean water at high pressure into your existing well to clean and open the natural fractures and water-bearing zones in the surrounding ground. A technician isolates a section of the borehole with an inflatable packer and pumps water against it, commonly between 1,500 and 3,000 PSI. The pressure scours silt and mineral scale from the existing pathways and connects the well to zones that were previously too constricted to deliver water. Wells that draw from consolidated, fractured material respond best; wells set entirely in loose desert alluvium are weaker candidates, which is why a careful on-site assessment comes first.

The process uses clean water exclusively — no chemicals, nothing added to the aquifer.

Why Hinkley Wells Lose Yield

Out on the Hinkley parcels, declining production usually comes down to:

  • Sediment infiltration. Fine desert sand and silt work into the screen and surrounding material, gradually restricting flow.
  • A falling water table. With only a few inches of rain a year, the Mojave's aquifers draw down through dry cycles, and a marginal well begins running short.
  • An underperforming new well. Not every borehole hits a productive zone in this terrain; hydrofracturing such a well is a standard fix.
  • Mineral scaling. Desert groundwater can be hard, leaving deposits that narrow the flow paths over the years.

Domestic wells around Hinkley commonly run 250 to 600 feet deep, varying with the property's position in the valley.

What to Check Before You Call

  • Track when shortages happen. Running short only under heavy simultaneous use points to a yield-and-recovery problem — what hydrofracturing addresses.
  • Watch the pump cycle. Rapid on-off cycling usually means a waterlogged tank or a failing pressure switch, both cheap to fix.
  • Watch for continuous pump running. A pump that runs without building pressure may be drawing the well down faster than it recovers.
  • Listen for air at the taps. Sputtering faucets suggest the water level is reaching the pump intake during use.
  • Find your well log. The original depth, static level, and tested yield give you a baseline against current performance.

When to Call a Professional

If recovery has slowed, the well runs dry under normal demand, or production trails the original log significantly, get a professional flow test. Hydrofracturing relies on specialized high-pressure equipment and an experienced operator reading the borehole, so it is not a do-it-yourself task. A licensed contractor baselines your yield, inspects the casing, and confirms whether your Hinkley well is a candidate before any pressure is applied.

Cost vs. Drilling a New Well in Hinkley

A hydrofracturing treatment typically costs $3,000 to $8,000, depending on depth and zones, with the $125 diagnostic credited toward the job if you proceed. A new turnkey well in this part of San Bernardino County generally runs $18,000 to $42,000 once drilling, casing, pump, and surface equipment are included.

If the trouble turns out to be equipment, the repairs are far cheaper: $2,500 to $5,500 for a pump, $600 to $1,500 for a tank, and $150 to $350 for a pressure switch. Because hydrofracturing costs a fraction of a new well and can multiply output on a suitable well, it is the logical first step.
