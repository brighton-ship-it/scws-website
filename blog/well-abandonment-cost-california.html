Well Abandonment Cost in California

Have an old, unused well on your property? In California, you're legally required to properly abandon it. Well abandonment—also called well destruction—costs $1,500-$10,000 depending on depth and conditions. This guide explains the legal requirements, the process, and what to expect for costs.

Why Proper Well Abandonment Matters

An improperly abandoned well isn't just an eyesore—it's a potential environmental hazard and legal liability:

Environmental Risks

  • Groundwater contamination: Open wells allow surface pollutants to enter aquifers
  • Aquifer cross-contamination: Wells can connect previously separated water zones, spreading contamination
  • Chemical/pesticide infiltration: Agricultural and residential runoff can enter groundwater
  • Bacterial introduction: Surface bacteria can contaminate clean underground water supplies

Legal Liability

  • California Water Code violations: Fines for improper abandonment
  • Contamination liability: Property owners responsible for cleanup costs
  • Real estate transaction issues: Can delay or prevent property sales
  • Safety hazards: Liability for injuries from uncovered wells

⚠️ California Law Requires Proper Abandonment

California Water Code Section 13750-13755 requires that all unused wells be properly destroyed by a licensed C-57 contractor. This is not optional—failure to comply can result in fines and liability for any resulting contamination.

The Well Abandonment Process

Proper well destruction follows a specific process required by California regulations:

Step 1: Permit Application

  • Submit well destruction permit application to county Environmental Health
  • Provide well location, depth, casing details (if known)
  • Pay permit fees
  • Wait for approval (typically 1-2 weeks)

Step 2: Site Preparation

  • Clear access to well location
  • Remove any well house or surface structures
  • Expose well casing if buried

Step 3: Equipment Removal

  • Pull submersible pump and piping
  • Remove any drop pipe, wire, or other equipment
  • Inspect for obstructions that need removal

Step 4: Well Sealing

This is the critical step—filling the well with approved sealing material:

Sealing Requirements:

  • Material: Neat cement grout (Portland cement + water) or concrete
  • Method: Tremie pipe or positive displacement from bottom up
  • Coverage: Entire well depth, filling all voids
  • Multiple aquifers: Cement plugs at each aquifer boundary
  • Quality: No dry cement, no dump-and-hope methods

⚠️ DIY Abandonment Is Illegal

Simply dumping dirt, rocks, or even bags of cement into a well does NOT constitute legal abandonment. The sealing must be done by a licensed C-57 contractor using approved methods that ensure complete sealing of all aquifer zones.

Step 5: Casing Cut-Off

  • Casing cut off at least 2 feet below ground surface
  • Remaining space filled with soil
  • Ground surface restored to grade

Step 6: Documentation

  • Contractor files Well Completion Report with county
  • Copy submitted to California Department of Water Resources
  • Property owner receives copy for records

What If I Want to Keep the Well?

Not every unused well needs to be abandoned. You may want to keep it for:

  • Irrigation: Watering landscaping, gardens, or agricultural use
  • Backup water supply: Emergency use if municipal supply fails
  • Future use: Plans to use the well later

Requirements for Keeping an Inactive Well

To Legally Maintain an Inactive Well:

  • Secure well cap: Watertight, vermin-proof cap in place
  • No cross-connections: Well system isolated from municipal supply
  • Annual inspection: Check for damage, contamination potential
  • Permit compliance: Some areas require permits for inactive wells
  • Proper labeling: Clearly mark as non-potable if not safe to drink

If connecting to city water while keeping your well for irrigation, contact your water district. Many have specific requirements for backflow prevention and cross-connection control.

Real Estate and Well Abandonment

If you're buying or selling property with a well, here's what to know:

Selling Property

  • Disclosure required: California law requires disclosing all known wells on property
  • Buyer expectations: Buyers often require abandonment of unused wells
  • Escrow holds: Funds may be held until abandonment is complete
  • Documentation: Keep well destruction report for closing

Buying Property

  • Title search: Check for recorded wells on property
  • Physical inspection: Look for signs of old wells (depressions, pipes, pump houses)
  • Seller disclosure: Ask specifically about any wells
  • Negotiate: Request abandonment be completed before close

💡 Finding Unknown Wells

Old properties may have undocumented wells. Look for: circular depressions in ground, old pipes or pump platforms, records in county well logs, conversations with previous owners or neighbors, or mentions in old property records.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to abandon a well in California?

Well abandonment typically costs $1,500-$10,000 depending on depth. Shallow wells cost $1,500-$3,000, standard wells $3,000-$6,000, and deep wells $5,000-$10,000+. Add $200-$600 for permit fees.

Is it legal to abandon a well myself?

No—California law requires well destruction by a licensed C-57 contractor. DIY abandonment is illegal and can result in fines and liability for any groundwater contamination that results.

What is the proper abandonment process?

The process includes: obtaining a permit, removing equipment, filling with cement grout from bottom to top, cutting casing below grade, and filing completion reports with county and state.

What materials are used to seal abandoned wells?

Neat cement grout (Portland cement and water) or concrete is required. The material must be placed from the bottom up using a tremie pipe to prevent voids and properly seal all aquifer zones.

How long does well abandonment take?

Physical work takes 1-2 days. Including permits and documentation, the entire process typically takes 2-4 weeks. Deep wells or those with complications may take longer.

Do I have to abandon my well when connecting to city water?

Only if you're permanently abandoning the well. You can keep it for irrigation or backup if properly maintained and not cross-connected to municipal supply. Check with your water district for specific requirements.

Who can legally abandon a well in California?

Only licensed C-57 well drilling contractors. They must obtain permits, perform work according to state standards, and file required completion reports with county and state agencies.

What if I'm selling property with an abandoned well?

Disclose all known wells. If improperly abandoned, you may need to complete proper abandonment before sale. Buyers often require proper destruction of any unused wells as a condition of purchase.
