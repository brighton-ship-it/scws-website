Riverside County Well Permit Requirements

In Riverside County, well permits are required for:

  • Drilling a new domestic or agricultural well
  • Deepening an existing well
  • Repairing or reconstructing well casing
  • Decommissioning (destroying) an unused well
  • Converting a well to a different use

The Permit Process

  1. Site Evaluation: We assess your property for optimal well location
  2. Application: Submit permit application with site plans
  3. Review: County reviews for setback requirements and regulations
  4. Approval: Permit issued (typically 2-4 weeks)
  5. Construction: Well work proceeds per permit specifications
  6. Inspection: County may inspect completed work
  7. Completion Report: We file required documentation

Setback Requirements

Wells must be located minimum distances from:

  • Septic tanks: 50-100 feet
  • Leach fields: 100-150 feet
  • Property lines: 5-10 feet
  • Buildings: 5 feet minimum
  • Other wells: varies

Exact requirements depend on Riverside County regulations and local conditions.

If you own property in Eastvale and you are thinking about drilling a new water well — or deepening, repairing, or retiring an old one — the work starts with a permit. Getting the permit right protects your investment and your neighbors' wells alike.

Who Needs a Well Permit in Eastvale

In Riverside County, you must obtain a permit from the Riverside County Department of Environmental Health (DEH) before you:

  • Drill a new well — domestic, agricultural, or commercial — anywhere in Eastvale.
  • Deepen or modify an existing well, including reconditioning the casing or changing the well's use.
  • Destroy or abandon a well that is no longer in use, which must be sealed to protect the aquifer.
  • Install a new pump in a way that alters the wellhead or conduct certain pump and yield tests.

Routine pump swaps that don't disturb the seal usually don't need a permit, but it is always worth a quick call before you start. We tell Eastvale property owners up front whether their job triggers a permit — at no charge.

The Riverside County Permit Process, Step by Step

Here is how a Eastvale well permit moves from idea to issued, working with Riverside County DEH:

  1. Site evaluation. We visit your Eastvale property, locate the best drilling spot for water and access, and confirm it can clear every required setback.
  2. Application. We complete the well permit application with your parcel information, a plot plan showing the proposed well relative to septic systems, structures, and property lines, and the owner's signature.
  3. Submittal and fees. The packet goes to the county's online permitting portal (administered through the Transportation and Land Management Agency), with permit fees paid on your behalf.
  4. County review. Riverside County DEH checks the plan against California Department of Water Resources Bulletins 74-81 and 74-90 and local groundwater rules.
  5. Permit issued. Once approved — usually in two to four weeks — the permit is released to us as your licensed driller.
  6. Construction. We drill and case the well to the approved specifications, with the permit on site.
  7. Inspection. The county may inspect the completed wellhead and seal.
  8. Completion report. We file the Well Completion Report (the state driller's log) filed with both the county and the Department of Water Resources, closing out the permit and creating the official record of your well.

Setback and Construction Requirements

California's well standards — California Department of Water Resources Bulletins 74-81 and 74-90 — set minimum distances between your well and anything that could contaminate it. In Eastvale, the key setbacks Riverside County DEH enforces are:

  • 50 feet from a septic tank and 100 feet from a leach field or seepage pit.
  • 50 feet from property lines and sewer lines.
  • 100 feet from any other source of contamination.
  • Adequate annular seal depth — typically a grouted sanitary seal at least 20 feet deep to keep surface water out of the aquifer.

On tighter Eastvale lots, meeting every setback at once can be a puzzle. If your parcel simply can't accommodate the standard distances, Riverside County DEH allows a variance request supported by engineering justification — something we prepare and submit routinely.

Inspections and Filing the Well Log

Once drilling is complete, the wellhead, casing, and sanitary seal may be inspected before the well is placed into service. The final — and legally required — step is filing the Well Completion Report (the state driller's log) filed with both the county and the Department of Water Resources. This document records the depth, geology, casing details, and water levels of your Eastvale well. It is filed with the Riverside County agency and the state, and it becomes the permanent record buyers, lenders, and future drillers rely on. We handle this filing for you so nothing is left undone.

Permit Fees and Timeline

Plan on county permit fees of roughly $500 to $1,200 for a Eastvale well, with additional charges if environmental review or a variance is required. A standard residential permit is typically issued in two to four weeks. For budgeting the whole project, a complete turnkey well — permit, drilling, casing, pump, and pressure system — generally runs $18,000 to $42,000 depending on depth and geology.
