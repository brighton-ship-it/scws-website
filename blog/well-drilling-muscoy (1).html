Muscoy's Groundwater: The Bunker Hill Basin

Muscoy lies within the Bunker Hill Subbasin of the Upper Santa Ana Valley Groundwater Basin — one of the largest and most reliable aquifer systems in the Inland Empire, with millions of acre-feet of storage capacity. Unlike the hard granite country up in the mountains, the ground under Muscoy is a deep accumulation of alluvial fan deposits washed down from the San Bernardino Mountains: sand, gravel, and cobbles that make excellent water-bearing formations. The principal aquifer is the older alluvium of Pleistocene age, which supplies water to more than a thousand wells across the basin.

In parts of the basin, groundwater is confined beneath less-permeable clay layers, which historically produced strong artesian pressure in the San Bernardino valley. Because the basin fill is thick and productive, Muscoy wells can often reach a dependable supply without needing to penetrate hard bedrock. Actual depth depends on your exact location and the water table at the time of drilling, so we review California DWR well completion records and neighboring logs before setting a target. This is coarse, permeable alluvial ground — generally favorable for both drilling efficiency and long-term well yield.

One characteristic worth understanding about the Bunker Hill basin is how much its water levels have moved over the decades. Historic pumping once drew the water table down significantly across the San Bernardino valley, and in wetter years managed recharge and natural runoff from the mountains have brought levels back up — in some low-lying areas even close enough to the surface to create liquefaction and high-groundwater concerns. For a well owner, the practical takeaway is that a properly designed well accounts for seasonal and long-term fluctuation: the pump has to be set deep enough to keep producing when levels drop, and the screen has to be placed to draw from the most productive water-bearing zones. This is exactly the kind of local judgment that a general contractor cannot provide but three decades of drilling in the region can.

Muscoy's setting at the mouth of the mountains also means the alluvial fan grows coarser and more cobbly closer to the range front and finer with more clay farther out onto the valley floor. Where your parcel sits along that gradient influences how the ground drills, how the well is screened, and how much sediment the completed well will produce. We factor all of it into the site assessment so the finished well delivers clean, steady water rather than sand and cycling problems down the road.

How We Drill a New Well

Every Muscoy well we complete follows a proven, code-compliant sequence from first assessment to running water.

Site Assessment & Siting

We evaluate your parcel, the local water table, and San Bernardino County setback rules — keeping the wellhead a safe distance from septic systems, animal enclosures, and property lines. Correct siting protects both water quality and permit approval.

Permitting

Water well permits in Muscoy are issued through the San Bernardino County Department of Public Health, Division of Environmental Health Services. We prepare the application, schedule required inspections, and file the well completion report, handling the full permitting process for Muscoy property owners.

Drilling the Borehole

Our Gefco rotary rig can reach 1,000+ feet. Through Muscoy's alluvial fan deposits, the rig advances efficiently, circulating drilling fluid to lift cuttings and keep the borehole stable. Occasional cobble and clay layers call for an experienced operator, but the coarse valley fill is generally cooperative ground. Most residential wells reach target depth in one to three days.

Casing, Gravel Pack & Development

We install casing — PVC or steel depending on depth — and set a gravel pack around the screened interval to filter fine sand while letting clean water flow in. A sanitary grout seal locks out surface runoff. We then develop the well, surging and pumping it to clear fines and confirm sustained yield.

Pump Installation

Finally we install a properly sized submersible pump, pressure tank, pressure switch, and controls, then connect the system to your plumbing. The complete turnkey process typically takes three to five business days from first cut to water at the tap.

Permits, Depth & Cost

Muscoy's productive, relatively accessible aquifer often keeps new-well projects reasonable compared with deep-drilling in hard-rock country. A full turnkey well — drilling, casing, gravel pack, development, pump, tank, and controls — typically runs $18,000 to $42,000 depending on final depth, casing, and pump requirements. Every project is quoted after a site assessment, with a free written estimate and no hidden fees.

Common Muscoy Scenarios

Muscoy calls tend to follow a few patterns. A homeowner on an older, shallow well finds it no longer keeps up with a growing household or livestock and wants a new, deeper well into the productive older alluvium. A buyer on an unincorporated lot needs a first well before building. And established well owners frequently want water quality upgrades — a water softener ($1,500–$3,500) for the hard, mineral-rich groundwater common in the Bunker Hill basin, sediment filtration ($300–$900), UV disinfection ($800–$1,800), or a reverse-osmosis drinking system ($300–$1,200). We test first and recommend only what your water actually calls for.

What to Check & When to Call a Pro

Falling pressure, gritty or discolored water, a pump that short-cycles or runs nonstop, rising power bills, or a total loss of water are all reasons to call before a minor issue turns into an outage. New drilling, below-water pump service, and control wiring must be handled by a licensed C-57 contractor for safety and San Bernardino County compliance. And when a Muscoy household loses water entirely, our same-day emergency service is ready to respond.

Do I need a permit to drill a well in Muscoy?
