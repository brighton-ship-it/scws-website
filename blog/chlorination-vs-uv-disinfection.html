Chlorination vs UV Disinfection: Well Water Treatment Comparison

Why Well Water Disinfection Matters

Well water can contain bacteria, viruses, and other pathogens—especially coliform bacteria, E. coli, and Giardia. Even wells that test clean can become contaminated over time. Disinfection provides ongoing protection for your family's health. Both chlorination and UV treatment effectively kill pathogens, but they work very differently.

Chlorination Treatment Explained

Chlorination adds chlorine to water, killing pathogens through chemical oxidation. Shock chlorination is a one-time treatment for contaminated wells. Continuous chlorinators inject small amounts of chlorine for ongoing protection. Chlorine provides residual protection throughout your plumbing. However, it affects taste, creates byproducts, and requires chemical handling.

UV Disinfection Explained

UV disinfection passes water through a chamber with ultraviolet light, which destroys pathogen DNA without chemicals. Treatment is instant with no taste impact. UV systems require pre-filtration for sediment and iron. They provide no residual protection—water could be recontaminated after treatment. Systems cost $500-$2,000 with annual bulb replacement.

Choosing Your Disinfection Method

Choose chlorination if you want residual protection through your pipes, have a known bacteria problem, or need to address other water quality issues (iron bacteria, sulfur). Choose UV if you want chemical-free treatment, have good pre-existing water quality, or are sensitive to chlorine taste. Many systems use both—chlorination for problem wells, UV as final treatment for taste-free water.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does UV treatment remove chlorine taste?

No. UV only disinfects—it doesn't remove tastes or chemicals. If you chlorinate and want to remove chlorine taste, you need an activated carbon filter after the UV unit (or instead of UV if chlorination provides adequate disinfection).

Is UV disinfection effective against all bacteria?

UV effectively kills bacteria, viruses, and parasites like Giardia and Cryptosporidium when properly sized and maintained. However, water must be clear—sediment or iron can shield pathogens from UV light. Pre-filtration is essential.

Can I use both chlorination and UV together?

Yes, this combination is common for problem wells. Chlorination handles heavy contamination and provides residual protection, while UV provides final disinfection. A carbon filter between them removes chlorine taste before UV treatment.
