Private Wells in La Mesa

La Mesa's elevated position and hilly terrain mean that some properties, especially older ones and those in outlying areas, were developed with private well systems. These wells tap into groundwater beneath the city's mesa and hillsides, providing an independent water source that many owners value.

Characteristics of La Mesa well systems include:

  • Hillside locations: Many wells serve properties on slopes where municipal water wasn't originally available.
  • Variable depths: Well depths vary based on local geology and water table levels.
  • Older systems: Many La Mesa wells have been in service for decades.
  • Supplemental use: Some properties use well water for irrigation while using municipal water indoors.
  • Hard water: Groundwater typically contains dissolved minerals.

Types of Well Pumps We Service

SCWS technicians service all pump types found in La Mesa:

  • Submersible pumps: Installed inside the well casing, the most common type for La Mesa's deeper wells.
  • Jet pumps: Above-ground pumps for shallower well applications.
  • Booster pumps: Secondary pumps that increase pressure for uphill distribution.
  • Irrigation pumps: Dedicated systems for landscape and garden watering.
  • Constant pressure systems: Modern systems that maintain steady water pressure.

Common La Mesa Well Pump Problems

Our experience serving La Mesa has shown us the most frequent pump issues:

  • Aging equipment: Many La Mesa well systems are older and experience more frequent issues.
  • Motor failures: Pump motors eventually wear out and require replacement.
  • Electrical problems: Control boxes, capacitors, and wiring degrade over time.
  • Pressure tank issues: Bladder failures cause short cycling and pump stress.
  • Scale buildup: Mineral deposits accumulate on pump components.
  • Low water levels: Drought conditions can lower water tables in some areas.
  • Corroded pipes: Older metal pipes may develop leaks.

Warning Signs of Pump Problems

La Mesa well owners should watch for these symptoms:

  • Gradual or sudden drop in water pressure
  • Pump running more frequently or continuously
  • Air in the water lines
  • Strange sounds from pump or pressure equipment
  • Increased electricity usage
  • Dirty or discolored water
  • Complete loss of water
  • Breakers tripping when pump runs
