Understanding Menifee's Groundwater

Menifee sits within the broader Perris Basin groundwater system, with local conditions influenced by the area's position between the Lakeview Mountains to the east and Gavilan Hills to the west. This geography creates distinct zones with varying water availability and quality.

  • Perris Basin Aquifer: The primary groundwater source for much of Menifee, this alluvial basin can provide excellent yields when properly developed.
  • Menifee Valley Deposits: Properties in the valley floor often access water from productive sand and gravel formations.
  • Hillside Areas: Properties in Quail Valley and the surrounding hills may encounter fractured bedrock requiring different drilling approaches.
  • Variable Depth Requirements: Well depths in Menifee typically range from 200 to 500 feet depending on location and local conditions.

Our Well Drilling Process

At SCWS, we follow a comprehensive approach to well drilling that maximizes your chances of success while minimizing environmental impact and cost. Our process begins long before any drilling equipment arrives on your property.

First, we conduct a thorough site evaluation, reviewing geological maps, neighboring well logs, and conducting an on-site assessment to determine the optimal drilling location. We consider factors like proximity to potential contamination sources, accessibility for drilling equipment, and the property's topography.

Once we've identified the best location, our experienced drilling crews arrive with state-of-the-art equipment designed to handle Menifee's geology.   • Water Testing: We collect samples for water quality analysis to ensure your water is safe.

Solutions for Existing Well Problems

Many Menifee properties have wells drilled decades ago that may be showing signs of age or declining production. Before committing to a new well, it's worth exploring options to restore your existing system.

  • Well Cleaning: Removing mineral deposits and biological growth that restrict water flow.
  • Hydrofracturing: Using high-pressure water to open new fractures and improve production.
  • Well Deepening: Drilling deeper to access additional water-bearing zones.
  • Pump Upgrades: Installing more efficient modern pumping equipment.
  • Casing Repair: Addressing corrosion or damage to well casing that may affect water quality.

Water Quality Considerations

Water quality in the Menifee area can vary significantly depending on well location and depth. Some common considerations include:

Hardness: Many wells in the area produce moderately hard water that may benefit from softening for household use while being perfectly suitable for irrigation.

Minerals: Depending on the formations encountered, wells may have elevated levels of certain minerals that require treatment for drinking water use.

We always recommend comprehensive water testing for new wells and can help you understand your results and treatment options if needed.
