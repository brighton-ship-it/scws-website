Agricultural Well Permits vs. Domestic Wells

Agricultural wells are defined by water use for farming, livestock, or irrigation. The permit process is generally similar to domestic wells but with different fee structures. No potability testing is required since water isn't for human consumption. Larger casing diameters and higher pumping rates are typical. SGMA reporting requirements may apply in critically overdrafted basins.

Water Rights and SGMA Considerations

Groundwater Sustainability Agencies regulate pumping in many California basins. New agricultural wells may require GSA approval in addition to the county permit. Some basins have pumping allocations or fees. Metering requirements are increasingly common for agricultural wells, and all wells must be registered with the State Water Resources Control Board.

Permit Application Requirements

Submit a standard well permit application through your county along with proof of agricultural use such as a farm plan or crop documentation. Estimated water demand calculations are often required. Larger parcels may allow multiple agricultural wells. Environmental review (CEQA) may apply for large-scale projects.

Inspection and Compliance

Construction standards are the same as domestic wells. A sanitary seal is required even for agricultural use. Flow meter installation is increasingly required in SGMA basins. Annual reporting to the GSA may be mandatory, and compliance with basin sustainability plans is required for continued operation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a permit for an agricultural well on my farm in California?

Yes, all wells in California require a permit from your county, including agricultural wells. Additionally, if your property is in a groundwater basin with an active Groundwater Sustainability Agency (GSA) under SGMA, you may need separate approval or registration. Always check with both your county and local GSA before drilling.

How much does an agricultural well permit cost in California?

County permit fees typically range from $400-$1,000 for agricultural wells. However, total costs depend on your location. GSA fees, if applicable, can add $500-$5,000+ depending on the basin. Some critically overdrafted basins charge per-acre-foot pumping fees. Contact your county and GSA for accurate fee estimates.

Can I use an agricultural well for my house if I live on the farm?

Technically, an agricultural well permit is for non-potable agricultural use only. If you want to use well water for household purposes, you typically need a domestic well permit, which includes water quality testing requirements. Some counties allow dual-purpose wells with appropriate permitting and testing for the domestic portion.
