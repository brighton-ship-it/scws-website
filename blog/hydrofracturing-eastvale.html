What is Hydrofracturing?

Hydrofracturing is a well rehabilitation technique that uses high-pressure water to clean and expand existing fractures in the bedrock surrounding your well. This process:

  • Clears mineral deposits and sediment blocking water flow
  • Opens new pathways for groundwater to reach your well
  • Can increase well yield by 2-10 times in many cases
  • Extends the life of your existing well

Unlike oil and gas fracking, well hydrofracturing uses only clean water—no chemicals are involved.

Signs Your Eastvale Well Needs Hydrofracturing

  • Declining water production over time
  • Well runs dry during peak usage
  • Slow recovery rate after pumping
  • Pump cycling on and off frequently
  • Lower water pressure than previous years

The Hydrofracturing Process

  1. Initial Assessment: We evaluate your well's current yield and condition
  2. Equipment Setup: Specialized high-pressure equipment is positioned at your well
  3. Isolation: Packers seal off sections of the well for targeted treatment
  4. Pressurization: Water is pumped at 500-3,000 PSI to fracture bedrock
  5. Development: Well is cleaned and tested for improved yield

What Hydrofracturing Does and Does Not Do

Hydrofracturing injects clean water under high pressure into the formation surrounding your well bore. In wells completed in or near fractured rock, the pressure widens and reconnects natural fissures, flushes out accumulated silt and mineral scale, and opens new pathways for groundwater to reach the well. The result is usually a measurable jump in both the rate water enters the well and how quickly the well recovers after pumping.

This is not the oil-and-gas "fracking" you read about in the news. Water-well hydrofracturing uses nothing but clean water, with no chemicals and no proppants. It has been performed safely on residential and agricultural wells for decades. That said, Eastvale sits on the Santa Ana River alluvial plain, and not every well here is a candidate. We are upfront about this below.

One advantage worth highlighting for Eastvale property owners is speed and disruption. Because hydrofracturing works through your existing borehole, there is no new drilling rig parked on your land for days, no large mud pit, and no permitting process for a new well. For a working ranch or a rural homestead that cannot afford to be without water, getting an existing well back online in a single day is often the difference between a minor interruption and a major one.

How the Process Works on an Eastvale Well

We start by pulling the pump and running a flow test to establish your well's true starting yield in gallons per minute. An inflatable packer (often two, to bracket a target zone) is lowered into the borehole to seal off the section we want to treat. Clean water is then injected through that isolated interval at pressures typically between 1,000 and 3,000 PSI. The pressure pulse opens the existing fracture network, after which we redevelop and re-test the well to confirm the gain. Most jobs are finished in a single day, usually four to eight hours on site.

Why Wells Lose Yield in the Eastvale Area

Low production around western Riverside County usually comes down to a handful of causes:

  • Declining groundwater levels. The Chino Basin has been heavily pumped and managed for decades, and seasonal drawdown can leave a well's perforated zone partly above water during peak summer demand.
  • Sediment and fines plugging the screen. Wells finished in mixed sand, gravel, and silt gradually clog as fine material packs around the screen and perforations.
  • Mineral scale. Hard groundwater leaves calcium and iron deposits that constrict the openings feeding the well.
  • A well completed in fractured rock that has tightened. Wells reaching the harder granite of the Chino Hills margin depend on cracks for flow, and those cracks fill in over time.

Hydrofracturing is most effective on the last category, where fractured bedrock is involved. For wells drawing purely from loose alluvial sand and gravel, other methods such as rehabilitation, screen cleaning, or a deeper replacement may be the better answer, and we will tell you which situation yours falls into.

Signs Your Well May Need Attention

  • Production has dropped noticeably compared with previous years.
  • The well runs low or dry during summer irrigation or when several fixtures run at once.
  • Recovery is slow, with the well taking hours to refill after heavy use.
  • Your pump short-cycles, turning on and off every few minutes.
  • A newly drilled well came in below the yield you were expecting.

What to Check Before You Call

A little observation up front helps us pinpoint the problem and can spare you a service call if the issue is simpler than yield:

  1. Rule out the equipment. A waterlogged pressure tank, a failing pressure switch, or a worn pump can imitate low yield. A pressure switch is a $150-$350 part and a tank typically runs $600-$1,500, both far cheaper than well work.
  2. Time the symptoms. Trouble only under heavy simultaneous demand points toward yield; an all-day pressure drop usually points toward equipment.
  3. Find your well log. The total depth, static water level, and the geologic materials recorded by the driller tell us whether your well sits in fractured rock that responds to hydrofracturing. Many wells in the Eastvale and Jurupa Valley area fall in the 150-to-500-foot range.
  4. Watch the water. Sand, cloudiness, or air spitting at the faucets are all useful clues to mention when you call.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will hydrofracturing work on my Eastvale well?

It depends on your well's geology. Hydrofracturing works best where fractured rock feeds the well. Wells drawing purely from loose sand and gravel are less predictable, which is why we assess each well before recommending it.

Is hydrofracturing safe?

Yes. It uses only clean water, no chemicals, and has been performed on residential and agricultural wells for decades. It does not contaminate the aquifer and does not damage a sound casing.

How much can it improve my yield?

In suitable wells, most owners see a two to five times increase in flow rate. The result depends on the formation, the well's depth, and the cause of the low yield.

How long does the work take?

Most residential treatments are completed in a single day, typically four to eight hours on site, with a verifying flow test usually within 24 to 48 hours.

Is it cheaper than drilling a new well?

Almost always. Hydrofracturing runs $3,000-$8,000 versus $18,000-$42,000 for a new turnkey well in this area, so trying it first can save you a great deal when the geology cooperates.
