Common Well Emergencies We Handle

Whether your well is in Point Loma proper or at a property you own elsewhere in San Diego County, here are the emergencies we respond to most frequently:

  • Complete water loss — Your pump has stopped producing water entirely. The cause could be a pump motor failure, electrical component failure, dry well condition, or a broken drop pipe. We diagnose the exact cause and repair it, usually in a single visit.
  • Sudden pressure drop — Water pressure was fine yesterday but is barely a trickle today. Common culprits include a failing pressure tank bladder, a leaking check valve, a partially clogged filter, or a pump that's losing capacity due to worn impellers.
  • Pump motor burnout — If the breaker keeps tripping or you detect a burning smell near the control box, the pump motor may be failing. Turn off the breaker immediately and call for service — continuing to reset a tripping breaker can cause additional damage and create a fire risk.
  • Electrical and control system failures — Blown capacitors, failed pressure switches, corroded wire connections, and control box malfunctions are among the most common causes of well failure. The good news: these are typically fast and affordable repairs, often completed in under an hour.
  • Water quality emergencies — Sudden changes in color, odor, or taste can indicate contamination from a damaged well cap, casing breach, or saltwater intrusion (a particular concern for coastal wells). We provide emergency disinfection and contamination source identification.
  • Storm and flood damage — San Diego's occasional heavy storms can damage wellheads, flood pump houses, and introduce contaminants into wells. We provide post-storm inspection, disinfection, and repair services.

What to Do Before We Arrive

These steps protect your equipment and help the repair go faster:

  1. Turn off the well pump breaker at your electrical panel. A pump running without water or with an electrical fault destroys itself within minutes.
  2. Conserve any remaining water in your pressure tank for essential needs only.
  3. Note the timeline and symptoms — when it started, whether it was sudden or gradual, any unusual sounds or smells, and what you were doing when it happened.
  4. Check for simple causes — a tripped breaker, a closed valve, or visible leaks near the wellhead or pressure tank.

Unique Challenges for Coastal San Diego Wells

Wells in coastal areas like Point Loma and surrounding San Diego communities face challenges that inland wells don't encounter. Understanding these issues helps property owners maintain their systems and recognize problems early:

Saltwater Intrusion

Coastal wells draw from aquifers that may be in contact with ocean water. When a well is over-pumped or the regional water table drops (from drought or increased development), saltwater can migrate inland and contaminate freshwater aquifers. Signs of saltwater intrusion include gradually increasing water salinity, a brackish or salty taste, and elevated chloride levels on water tests. Once saltwater enters an aquifer, it's very difficult to reverse. If you notice your well water becoming saltier over time, stop pumping immediately and have the water tested — continued pumping accelerates the intrusion.

Corrosion from Marine Environment

The salt air along the San Diego coast accelerates corrosion on exposed well components — electrical connections, pressure switches, wellhead fittings, and above-ground piping. Properties within a few miles of the ocean should schedule more frequent inspections of these components. Corroded electrical connections are a leading cause of intermittent well failures in coastal areas, where the connection works sometimes and fails at other times, making diagnosis frustrating for homeowners.

High Water Table Considerations

Some low-lying coastal areas have naturally high water tables, which means wells may be shallow and more susceptible to surface contamination. After heavy rains, the water table can rise to the point where surface runoff enters the well through an inadequate seal. Maintaining a proper sanitary well cap and ensuring the well casing extends above grade are essential protective measures for coastal properties.

Mineral Content from Marine Sediments

The sedimentary formations underlying coastal San Diego (including the Point Loma Formation) can impart higher mineral content to groundwater than the granite-based aquifers found inland. This may include elevated sodium, chloride, and sulfate levels that affect taste and can cause scaling in plumbing. Regular water testing helps you track mineral trends and determine whether treatment systems are needed.
