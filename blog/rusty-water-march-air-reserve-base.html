What Causes Rusty Well Water?

  • Dissolved Iron: Naturally occurring iron in Riverside County groundwater
  • Iron Bacteria: Bacteria that feed on iron, creating rusty slime

Is Rusty Water Safe to Drink?

Iron itself isn't a health hazard at typical well water concentrations. However, rusty water:

  • Tastes metallic and unpleasant
  • Stains sinks, toilets, and laundry
  • Can clog pipes and fixtures over time
  • May indicate other water quality issues

Rusty Well Water Near March Air Reserve Base: A Riverside County Guide

March Air Reserve Base sits in western Riverside County, ringed by the cities of Riverside, Moreno Valley, and Perris. The surrounding area is a mix of military, residential, and agricultural land set on the broad inland valley floor, where groundwater has long supplied homes, ranches, and irrigation. For property owners in this part of Riverside County who rely on a private well, one of the most common complaints is water that comes out rusty, orange, or reddish-brown. The cause is almost always iron, manganese, or corroding metal somewhere between the aquifer and the faucet.

The good news is that rusty water is one of the more solvable well problems once you trace it to its source. This guide explains how iron ends up in Riverside County groundwater, how to read the warning signs yourself, when to bring in a professional, and what the repairs typically cost.

How Iron and Manganese Get Into Riverside County Wells

The inland valleys around March Air Reserve Base are underlain by deep alluvial sediments — sand, gravel, silt, and clay eroded from the surrounding hills and mountains and deposited over thousands of years. Groundwater moving through that material dissolves trace minerals, including iron and manganese, out of the rock and soil. Both are common and naturally occurring, but once iron rises above roughly 0.3 mg/L or manganese above 0.05 mg/L, they begin staining fixtures, laundry, and appliances.

The orange color is the result of oxidation. Underground, iron is dissolved in a clear ferrous state. When the water surfaces and meets oxygen — in a toilet tank, pressure tank, or open glass — the iron converts to ferric particles, the rust we recognize. Manganese does the same but leaves a darker brown-black film. Water that pours clear and then turns orange as it sits is the classic signature of dissolved iron.

Common Causes in the March ARB Area

  • Dissolved aquifer iron: The most frequent cause, revealed when levels climb or filtration ages out.
  • Iron bacteria: Microbes that feed on iron and leave a rust-colored, slimy residue, often with a swampy odor. They flourish in low-use or seasonal wells.
  • Corroding steel casing: Older Riverside County wells cased in steel shed rust as the metal breaks down.
  • Galvanized plumbing: Aging galvanized iron pipe in older homes rusts internally and tints the supply.
  • Failing water heater: Rust limited to hot water usually means a corroding anode rod or tank, not the well.

Is Rusty Water Dangerous?

Iron and manganese are secondary contaminants — the EPA regulates them for taste, color, and staining rather than acute health risk. A slightly orange glass of water will not harm a healthy adult, but rusty water still causes real problems:

  • A metallic taste in drinking water and cooking.
  • Orange stains on sinks, tubs, toilets, and laundry.
  • Mineral scale that narrows pipes and shortens appliance life.
  • Iron-bacteria slime that clogs screens, pumps, and fixtures.
  • Sudden discoloration that can signal a casing failure or surface intrusion worth testing.

Manganese deserves a closer look because guidance for infants has grown stricter. A lab test settles the question inexpensively.

What to Check Before You Call

  1. Hot vs. cold: Rust only on the hot side points to the water heater.
  2. One fixture vs. whole house: Isolated rust suggests a corroded line or aerator.
  3. Clear-then-orange: Indicates dissolved iron oxidizing.
  4. Cloudy-orange from the tap: Indicates sediment, casing flakes, or pipe scale.
  5. Smell and slime: A musty odor with reddish sludge points to iron bacteria.

Collect a sample in a clean jar, note how fast it discolors, and save it for your technician. Test strips estimate; a certified lab test confirms the numbers that drive the right treatment.

Effective Solutions

Iron and Manganese Filtration

An oxidizing or catalytic media filter is the standard remedy for dissolved metals, converting them to a solid that backwashes away. A correctly sized softener can handle lighter iron with hard water, while heavier loads need air- or chemical-injection ahead of the filter. The right design depends on your water's pH and flow rate.

Shock Chlorination and Well Cleaning

For confirmed iron bacteria, a strong chlorine treatment disinfects the well and plumbing, often paired with mechanical cleaning of the casing and screen. Recurrent cases may need a maintenance program.

Casing and Plumbing Repairs

A corroded casing can be lined or replaced, and old galvanized house pipe can be swapped for PEX or copper to eliminate household rust at the source.

When to Call a Professional

Call a licensed well contractor if rusty water appears suddenly with no household cause, if both hot and cold water are discolored throughout the home, if you see sand or grit with the rust, or if a strong sulfur smell accompanies it. These signs can point to a failing casing, a pump pulling from a compromised zone, or an established bacterial colony. A professional can test the water, inspect the casing with a downhole camera, evaluate pump performance, and target the actual cause instead of masking the symptom.

Why Local Knowledge Matters in Riverside County

Groundwater conditions vary across Riverside County. A deep alluvial well on the valley floor near Moreno Valley or Perris behaves differently from a fractured-rock well in the surrounding hills, and the right iron treatment depends on depth, casing type, pH, hardness, and flow. A system that works well in one location can foul in another if it is not matched to the water. Crews who work this region regularly know where iron runs high, where iron bacteria are common, and which areas still have aging steel-cased wells, which leads to faster diagnosis and equipment sized to your actual water.

Keeping Your Water Clear Long Term

Once your water runs clear again, a modest maintenance routine keeps it that way. We recommend an annual water test, scheduled backwashing and media changes on any filtration system, and periodic checks of the pressure tank pre-charge and pressure switch. Wells that sit idle for long stretches benefit from regular use, since stagnation is what allows iron bacteria to establish themselves. For homes on older galvanized plumbing, a phased upgrade to PEX or copper prevents internal pipe rust from re-tinting clean water. These steps protect both your water quality and the equipment you have already invested in.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does my well water turn orange after it sits?

Dissolved ferrous iron is colorless until it meets air. As the water sits and oxidizes, it forms orange ferric rust. An oxidizing iron filter is the standard fix.

Is iron in my water a health risk?

Iron is an aesthetic contaminant, so it is generally not an acute health hazard, but the taste and staining are unpleasant. We recommend testing, especially to check manganese levels.

Why is only my hot water rusty?

That points to the water heater. A corroding anode rod or rusting tank discolors hot water while the well and cold water stay clear. Servicing or replacing the heater usually fixes it.

What is the slimy red buildup in my fixtures?

That is iron bacteria, which clogs screens and pumps over time. Shock chlorination kills the colony and clears the slime.

How much does a water test cost?

Our diagnostic visit is $125, credited toward any system or repair we perform, and it measures iron and manganese so we size treatment correctly.

Will a whole-house filter stop the staining permanently?

A properly sized iron-removal system stops staining as long as it is maintained with regular backwashing and periodic media replacement. We set you up with the right schedule.
