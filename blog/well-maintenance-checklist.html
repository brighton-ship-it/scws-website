Well Maintenance Checklist for Homeowners

Updated February 2026 • By SCWS

Regular maintenance extends your well's lifespan and prevents costly emergencies. Most well problems don't happen suddenly—they develop over time. Catching issues early through routine maintenance saves money and prevents the crisis of waking up to no water.

Use this comprehensive checklist to maintain your well system. Some tasks you can do yourself; others require professional service.

Monthly Maintenance Tasks (DIY)

These quick checks take just 5-10 minutes and can catch problems early:

  • Check pressure gauge: Normal range is 40-60 PSI. Consistently low pressure may indicate pump wear or tank problems.
  • Listen for pump cycling: The pump should run steadily when water is being used, not turn on and off rapidly (short-cycling).
  • Note water quality changes: Any sudden changes in taste, color, or smell warrant investigation.
  • Inspect visible components: Look for leaks around the pressure tank, fittings, and pipes.
  • Check wellhead area: Keep area clear of debris, chemicals, and potential contamination sources.

Annual Maintenance Tasks

Once a year, perform these more thorough checks:

Professional Inspection Recommended

  • Complete system inspection: Have a professional check all components including pump draw, pressure settings, and electrical connections.
  • Water quality testing: Test for bacteria (coliform), nitrates, and any contaminants common to your area. Cost: $50-$150.
  • Flow rate test: Measure GPM to track any decline in well production over time.
  • Pressure tank air charge: Verify the air pressure matches your pressure switch settings.

DIY Annual Tasks

  • Inspect wellhead: Check for cracks, damage, or settling. The cap should be secure and sanitary.
  • Clear the area: Remove any debris, vegetation, or items stored near the wellhead.
  • Check for surface water intrusion: Ground should slope away from wellhead. No pooling water nearby.
  • Inspect pressure tank exterior: Look for corrosion, damage, or moisture.
  • Test pressure switch operation: Verify pump turns on and off at correct pressures.

Every 3-5 Years

  • Comprehensive water chemistry: Full panel testing for minerals, pH, hardness, and potential contaminants.
  • Well rehabilitation: Consider preventive cleaning if production has declined.
  • Video inspection: Camera inspection of well casing to check for damage or deterioration.
  • Equipment assessment: Evaluate whether pump and tank are nearing end of life.

Warning Signs That Need Immediate Attention

Don't wait for your scheduled maintenance if you notice:

  • Sudden loss of water or pressure
  • Air spurts from faucets
  • Pump running constantly
  • Clicking sounds without pump running
  • Significant change in water color, taste, or smell
  • Visible damage to wellhead or equipment
  • Higher than normal electric bills

Maintenance Record Keeping

Keep records of all maintenance, repairs, and water tests. This history helps diagnose problems, track system performance, and provides valuable documentation if you sell your property.
