Commercial Well Drilling: Complete Guide for Businesses

Updated February 22, 2026 | 15 min read

Your business needs water—a lot of it. Whether you're running a farm, manufacturing facility, car wash, or large commercial property, municipal water bills can eat into your bottom line. Commercial well drilling offers water independence and potential long-term savings, but it's a significant investment that requires careful planning. This guide covers everything you need to know: costs, permits, capacity requirements, and whether a commercial well makes sense for your operation.

💼 Commercial vs. Residential Wells

Commercial wells differ from residential wells in several key ways: larger diameter casing (8-12" vs 6"), higher capacity pumps (25-500+ GPM vs 5-20 GPM), more complex permitting, and significantly higher costs. Planning is critical.

Cost Breakdown

Here's where your money goes in a commercial well project:

  • Drilling (40-50%): $50-$100+ per foot for commercial diameter wells
  • Casing & materials (15-20%): Larger diameter steel casing, screens, grout
  • Pump system (15-25%): High-capacity pumps, VFDs, controls
  • Electrical (5-10%): Higher voltage service, motor starters, panels
  • Permits & engineering (5-10%): Commercial permits, hydrogeological studies
  • Storage & distribution (variable): Tanks, booster pumps, piping

Commercial Well Permit Requirements

Commercial wells face stricter permitting than residential wells. In San Diego County, you'll need:

📋 Required Permits & Approvals

  • County well permit: $500-$1,500 (required for all wells)
  • Water rights review: Verify groundwater extraction rights
  • Environmental review: CEQA exemption or evaluation for larger projects
  • GSA notification: Groundwater Sustainability Agency approval in SGMA basins
  • Health department permit: If water serves employees or public
  • Building permit: For pump house or significant infrastructure

Permit timeline: 2-6 months depending on project complexity

SGMA and Groundwater Regulations

California's Sustainable Groundwater Management Act (SGMA) affects commercial wells in designated basins. You may need:

  • Registration with local Groundwater Sustainability Agency
  • Metering requirements for water extraction
  • Potential pumping fees or allocation limits
  • Reporting requirements for high-volume users

Check with your local GSA before planning a commercial well project. Learn more in our SGMA regulations guide.

Water Capacity Planning

The most critical factor in commercial well design is accurately estimating your water needs:

Industry Water Usage Guidelines

Livestock (per head) 15-30 gal/day cattle 2-5 gal/day small animals
Vineyard (per acre) 0.5-1.5 acre-feet/year 10-20 GPM per acre
Car wash (per bay) 15,000-40,000 gal/month 20-40 GPM
Restaurant (per seat) 25-50 gal/day Peak lunch/dinner
Office building (per employee) 15-25 gal/day Morning, restroom peaks

Sizing Your Well

Commercial wells are sized for peak demand, not average usage. Consider:

  • Peak hourly demand: Maximum water use in any single hour
  • Safety factor: Design for 1.25-1.5x expected peak demand
  • Future growth: Plan for 10-20 year expansion
  • Redundancy: Consider backup pumps or multiple wells

⚠️ Don't Under-Size

Under-sizing a commercial well is expensive to fix later. It's far better to install capacity you'll grow into than to need a second well or upgrades in a few years. Get a professional assessment before finalizing design.

Commercial Well Types

Drilled Wells (Most Common)

Standard rotary-drilled wells with larger casing for commercial applications:

  • 8-12" casing diameter (vs 6" residential)
  • Depths from 200-1,000+ feet
  • Yields of 25-500+ GPM
  • Best for: Most commercial applications

Gravel Pack Wells

Designed for sandy or unconsolidated formations:

  • Dual-casing with gravel between
  • Prevents sand infiltration
  • Higher yields in appropriate geology
  • Best for: Alluvial valleys, coastal areas

Multiple Well Systems

For very high demand or redundancy:

  • Two or more wells feeding common storage
  • Provides backup if one well fails
  • Can alternate pumping to extend well life
  • Best for: Critical operations, very high demand

Choosing a Commercial Well Driller

Commercial well projects require experienced contractors. Look for:

  • Commercial experience: Portfolio of similar projects
  • Proper licensing: C-57 Well Drilling Contractor license
  • Engineering capability: In-house or partner hydrogeologists
  • Equipment capacity: Rigs capable of commercial diameter drilling
  • Permit expertise: Experience navigating commercial permit process
  • Service capability: Ongoing maintenance and support

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does commercial well drilling cost?

Commercial well drilling typically costs $30,000-$150,000+ depending on depth, diameter, required flow rate (GPM), and pump systems. Small commercial wells cost $30,000-$60,000, agricultural wells average $50,000-$100,000, and industrial high-capacity wells can exceed $150,000.

What permits are required for commercial wells?

Commercial wells require County well permits, water rights assessment, potential environmental review (CEQA), GSA approval in SGMA basins, and health department permits if water serves the public. The permit process typically takes 2-6 months.

How much water can a commercial well produce?

Commercial wells produce 25-500+ GPM depending on design and aquifer. Small commercial needs 25-50 GPM, agricultural operations 50-200 GPM, and industrial facilities 100-500+ GPM. Proper yield testing is essential before committing.

How long does commercial well drilling take?

Commercial well projects take 3-6 months from planning to completion. Permit acquisition takes 1-4 months, drilling 1-3 weeks, pump installation 1-2 weeks, and testing 1-2 weeks. Complex projects can take longer.

Is a commercial well worth the investment?

For high water users, commercial wells often pay for themselves in 2-5 years. A business paying $3,000/month for municipal water saves $36,000/year—recovering a $100,000 well investment in under 3 years.
