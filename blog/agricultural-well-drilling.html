Types of Agricultural Wells

Irrigation Wells

The backbone of farm water supply, irrigation wells provide water for crops:

  • Drip irrigation: Lower GPM requirements, constant demand during growing season
  • Sprinkler systems: Higher instantaneous GPM, often run in zones
  • Flood irrigation: Highest GPM demand, less common in our region due to water costs

Livestock Wells

Cattle, horses, and other livestock need reliable daily water:

  • Pasture wells: Supply water troughs in grazing areas
  • Barn wells: Domestic-style wells for smaller livestock operations
  • Feedlot wells: High-capacity systems for concentrated animal operations

Multi-Purpose Farm Wells

Many farms need wells that serve multiple purposes:

  • Household use (domestic quality water)
  • Crop irrigation
  • Livestock watering
  • Equipment washing
  • Processing operations

Well Sizing for Agriculture

Calculating Required GPM

To size your agricultural well:

  1. Calculate peak daily demand — total gallons needed on hottest irrigation day
  2. Determine pumping hours — typically 8-16 hours per day
  3. Add storage buffer — storage reduces required GPM

Example: 10-acre avocado grove needs 40,000 gallons on peak day. Pumping 12 hours/day requires 55 GPM minimum. With 20,000-gallon storage tank, could operate with 35-40 GPM well.

Agricultural Well Construction

Well Design

Agricultural wells differ from residential wells in several ways:

  • Larger diameter: 8-12" casing common (vs. 6" residential)
  • Deeper setting: Pump set deeper to handle heavy pumping
  • Robust equipment: Industrial-grade pumps rated for continuous operation
  • Storage integration: Often paired with large storage tanks

Casing Options

Casing Diameter Max GPM Typical Use
6" 50-75 Small farms, livestock
8" 100-200 Medium farms, orchards
10" 200-400 Large farms, intensive irrigation
12"+ 400+ Large-scale agriculture

Pump Systems

Agricultural pumps must handle demanding duty cycles:

  • Submersible pumps: Most common, efficient, low maintenance
  • Line shaft turbines: For very high capacity wells, motor at surface
  • Booster pumps: For storage tank systems, pressurize distribution
  • VFD controllers: Variable speed drives reduce energy costs 20-40%

Crop Sensitivity

Some crops are more sensitive to water quality:

  • Highly sensitive: Avocados, citrus, strawberries, beans
  • Moderately sensitive: Tomatoes, peppers, most vegetables
  • Tolerant: Cotton, barley, sugarbeets (less common in our area)

Always test water quality before planting sensitive crops. See our water testing guide.

Storage Systems

Agricultural storage tanks provide operational flexibility:

Benefits of Storage

  • Reduced well size: Store during low-demand periods, use during peak
  • Pump protection: Continuous cycling reduces pump life
  • Emergency reserve: Buffer during pump repairs
  • Fire protection: Required for some rural properties

Sizing Agricultural Storage

  • Minimum: 1-2 hours of peak demand
  • Recommended: 1 day of average demand
  • Optimal: 2-3 days of average demand

Example: Farm using 50,000 gallons/day should have 50,000-150,000 gallon storage.

Regulatory Considerations

County Permits

Both San Diego and Riverside Counties require well permits:

  • Well permit application and fees
  • Site plan showing setbacks from septic, property lines
  • Compliance inspection after completion

SGMA (Sustainable Groundwater Management Act)

Large agricultural wells may face additional requirements under SGMA:

  • Well registration: Required in most basins
  • Metering: May be required for high-capacity wells
  • Extraction fees: Some basins impose pumping fees
  • Allocation limits: Critical basins may limit new extraction

We help navigate these requirements as part of your project.

ROI: Well vs. Municipal Water

Compare ongoing costs to determine payback:

Municipal Agricultural Water Rates

  • San Diego County Water Authority: $1,200-$1,800 per acre-foot
  • Fallbrook PUD ag rate: ~$1,000 per acre-foot
  • Rainbow MWD: ~$1,100 per acre-foot

Well Water Cost

  • Electricity: $100-$300 per acre-foot pumped
  • Maintenance: $50-$100 per acre-foot (amortized)
  • Total: $150-$400 per acre-foot

Savings: $800-$1,400 per acre-foot compared to municipal water.

A 20-acre avocado operation using 3 acre-feet per acre (60 AF/year) saves $48,000-$84,000 annually with well water. Typical well ROI: 2-5 years.

Well Service in Drilling

Drilling properties in San Diego County rely on private wells drilled through the Peninsular Ranges batholith, primarily granitic and metamorphic rock. Local geological conditions affect everything from drilling depth and cost to water quality and pump selection.

Agricultural Water Needs in Drilling

Drilling's San Diego County location means a Mediterranean climate with dry summers that put heavy demand on irrigation wells from May through October. Agricultural wells here must be sized for sustained high-volume pumping, often 15-50 GPM from alluvial or weathered rock aquifers.

Common agricultural well setups in Drilling include variable frequency drives (VFDs) to match pump output to demand, storage tanks for buffer capacity, and booster systems for pressurized irrigation lines. We size every agricultural pump to the well's tested yield — oversizing wastes energy and can damage the well by drawing the water level down too fast.
