Common Pump Problems & Solutions

Understanding common well pump issues can help you identify problems early and seek professional repair before minor issues escalate:

No Water or Poor Pressure

Whether it's a submersible or jet pump failure, a common issue is no water or significantly reduced water pressure. Causes might include motor or control box failures, stuck check valves, pressure tank problems, or a drop in water levels.

Solution: Our repair includes thorough diagnostics of electrical components, mechanical parts, and system pressures to pinpoint the exact cause and provide effective solutions.

Pump Overcycling

Overcycling can lead to increased wear and energy usage. Causes include pressure switch malfunctions, tank issues, or minor leaks in the system.

Solution: We offer adjustments or replacements of defective components to optimize your system's cycling and efficiency.

Unusual Noises

Rattling, humming, or grinding noises often indicate mechanical issues such as worn bearings, sediment build-up, or pump cavitation.

Solution: Our technicians are skilled in diagnosing and resolving the root cause of noises efficiently to prevent further damage and maintain system integrity.

Signs You Need Well Pump Repair

Be aware of these warning signs indicating that your well pump may need repair or maintenance:

  • Pulsating water flow or inconsistent water pressure.
  • Sudden spikes in electricity bills indicating pump inefficiencies.
  • Visible leaks or corrosion near the pump or system components.
  • Frequent pump cycling on and off, or the pump running continuously.

Recognizing these symptoms early allows for timely pump repair, minimizing damage and prolonging your pump’s lifespan.

Well Pump Repair Cost Guide

The cost of well pump repair varies depending on the complexity of the problem and the type of pump:

Standard Repair Costs

Basic Repairs: Minor issues like pressure switch adjustments and wiring repairs typically range between $150 and $400.

Pump Component Replacement: Replacing motors, impellers, or control boxes can range from $500 to $1,500 depending on the parts required.

Full Pump Replacement: For irreparable pumps, replacements can cost between $1,000 and $5,000+ depending on the specific system and complexity.

Repair vs Replacement Guide

Deciding between repairing or replacing a well pump can be challenging. We help guide you with expertise to make the best financial and functional decision:

When to Repair: Less than 7 years old with minimal wear, experiencing a single, simple issue such as a faulty pressure switch or minor leaks.

When to Replace: Older than 10 years or facing multiple or significant failures that would incur high repair costs. Upgrading to a more efficient or appropriately sized pump could also warrant a replacement.

Trust our knowledgeable recommendations based on an accurate assessment of your system to decide the most cost-effective approach.

Common Pump Problems in San Diego

The geological conditions in San Diego — the Peninsular Ranges batholith, primarily granitic and metamorphic rock — create specific challenges for well pumps. While moderate well depths are easier on pumps, local water chemistry and sediment conditions can still cause premature wear.

The most common pump repair calls we get from San Diego include: pumps running but producing low flow (often a failing impeller or dropped water level), circuit breakers tripping when the pump starts (bad capacitor or motor windings), and pressure tank waterlogging (failed bladder). We carry common parts on our trucks for same-day repair in most cases.

What should I do if my pump isn’t working?

Check the power supply and settings first. If the issue persists, it's best to call a professional to diagnose and repair the issue safely and effectively.

How often should well pumps be serviced?

Regular inspections are advised annually to ensure longevity and performance, with more frequent checks if experiencing problems.

What impacts the lifespan of a well pump?

Factors include water quality, usage patterns, maintenance regularity, and installation quality, all contributing to a well pump's lifespan.
