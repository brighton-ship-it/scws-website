Cost to Drill a 200-Foot Well in Southern California

Drilling a 200-foot well is a common choice for residential properties throughout Southern California, offering access to deeper, more reliable water sources than shallower alternatives. If you're planning to drill a well on your property, understanding the full scope of costs involved will help you budget effectively and make informed decisions. This comprehensive guide covers everything you need to know about 200-foot well drilling costs, from base pricing to additional factors that influence your final investment.

Average Cost to Drill a 200-Foot Well

The typical cost to drill a 200-foot well in Southern California ranges from $7,000 to $15,000, with most homeowners paying between $9,000 and $12,000 for a complete well installation. This estimate includes drilling, casing, well screen, grout sealing, and basic completion. However, your actual cost may vary based on several site-specific factors.

Per-foot drilling costs in our region typically range from $35 to $75, depending on soil conditions and drilling method required. For a 200-foot well, the drilling alone would cost between $7,000 and $15,000. Add in casing materials, permits, and completion work, and you can see how the total adds up.

It's worth noting that doubling the well depth doesn't necessarily double the cost. Certain fixed costs remain constant regardless of depth, such as mobilization, permits, and site setup. This means deeper wells often provide better value per foot of depth.

Factors That Influence 200-Foot Well Drilling Costs

Geological Conditions

Southern California's diverse geology significantly impacts drilling costs. The region features everything from soft alluvial soils in valleys to hard granite formations in mountainous areas. Drilling through decomposed granite, common in many foothill communities, costs more than drilling through sandy or clay soils. If your property sits on solid rock, specialized drilling equipment and techniques are required, increasing both time and cost.

At 200 feet, you're more likely to encounter varying geological layers than with shallower wells. Your drilling contractor may need to adapt techniques as they pass through different formations, which can affect the timeline and cost.

Casing Requirements

For a 200-foot well, casing costs become a significant portion of the overall expense. Standard wells use 4-inch to 6-inch diameter casing, with larger diameters costing more. Steel casing offers durability but costs more than PVC alternatives. The choice depends on your soil conditions, water chemistry, and long-term plans for the well.

At 200 feet of depth, you'll need approximately 200 feet of casing material, which can cost $1,500 to $4,000 or more depending on the type and diameter selected. Your contractor will recommend the appropriate casing based on your specific situation.

Location and Site Access

Getting drilling equipment to your well site affects costs. Large drilling rigs need adequate access roads and level ground to operate safely. If your property has challenging terrain, narrow access, or requires significant site preparation, additional costs apply. Rural and remote properties may also incur travel fees for the drilling crew and equipment transport.

Permits and Inspections

California requires permits for well drilling, with costs varying by county. In San Diego County, Riverside County, and other areas we serve, permit fees typically range from $200 to $600. Some jurisdictions also require inspections during or after drilling, which may involve additional fees. A reputable drilling contractor handles all permitting as part of their service.

Water Table Depth

The depth at which water is encountered affects certain aspects of the drilling process and well completion. If water is found at shallower depths, that's excellent news for your water supply. However, drilling typically continues to the planned depth to ensure adequate water storage capacity within the well bore and to access the most reliable water-bearing zones.

What's Included in Professional 200-Foot Well Drilling

Pre-Drilling Assessment

Before drilling begins, your contractor should evaluate the site, review any available well logs from nearby properties, and discuss expectations for depth, yield, and water quality. This assessment helps set realistic expectations and identifies potential challenges before equipment arrives.

Mobilization and Setup

Bringing a drilling rig to your property and setting it up requires significant logistics. The mobilization fee covers transportation of the rig, support equipment, and the drilling crew. For a 200-foot well, medium to large rigs are typically used, capable of efficient drilling to this depth and beyond.

Drilling Operations

The actual drilling of a 200-foot well typically takes two to four days, depending on conditions encountered. The drilling team works continuously during drilling hours, monitoring progress and making adjustments as needed. Drilling fluid or air is used to remove cuttings and stabilize the borehole.

Well Casing and Screen

Once drilling reaches the target depth, the well casing is installed from the surface down through the water-bearing zone. A well screen at the bottom allows water to enter while filtering out sand and sediment. Proper screen selection is crucial for well performance and longevity.

Grout Sealing

The annular space between the casing and the borehole is filled with cement grout or bentonite to prevent surface water from contaminating the aquifer. California regulations specify minimum sealing requirements, and proper grouting protects both your well and your neighbors' water supplies.

Development and Testing

After construction, the well is developed to remove drilling fluids and fine particles, maximizing water flow. Basic yield testing determines how much water the well can produce, which informs pump selection and system design.

Well Cap and Surface Completion

A sanitary well cap seals the top of the casing, preventing contamination. The surface around the well is graded to direct water away from the wellhead, and any required surface seals are installed per local codes.

Additional Costs Beyond Drilling

Pump System Installation

A 200-foot well requires a submersible pump capable of lifting water from depth. Expect to pay $1,500 to $3,500 for the pump, drop pipe, wiring, and installation. Pump sizing depends on your household's water demand and the well's yield. Higher-capacity pumps cost more but deliver greater flow rates.

Pressure Tank and Controls

Your water system needs a pressure tank to maintain consistent pressure and reduce pump cycling. A properly sized pressure tank with pressure switch and related controls typically costs $400 to $1,000 installed.

Electrical Connections

Submersible pumps require 240-volt electrical service. If your property needs a new circuit, subpanel, or electrical run to the well location, budget $300 to $800 for electrical work. This should be performed by a licensed electrician.

Water Quality Testing

Testing your new well's water is essential before drinking or household use. Basic coliform and nitrate testing costs $100 to $200, while comprehensive testing panels run $300 to $500 or more. Testing identifies any treatment needs before you invest in additional equipment.

Water Treatment Equipment

Depending on test results, you may need water treatment such as sediment filtration, water softening, iron removal, or disinfection. Treatment systems range from a few hundred dollars for basic filtration to several thousand for comprehensive treatment packages.

Benefits of a 200-Foot Well

Investing in a 200-foot well offers several advantages over shallower alternatives. Deeper wells typically access more reliable water sources that are less susceptible to seasonal fluctuations and drought conditions. The water quality from deeper aquifers is often better, having been naturally filtered through more soil and rock.

A 200-foot well also provides greater storage capacity within the well bore itself, acting as a reservoir that can sustain higher demand periods. This depth reaches many of Southern California's productive aquifer zones, providing adequate water for most residential needs.

Is 200 Feet Deep Enough?

Whether 200 feet is sufficient depends on local hydrogeology. In many parts of Southern California, 200 feet reaches excellent water-bearing formations. However, some areas require deeper drilling to access reliable water. Your contractor can provide guidance based on local well data and geological knowledge.
