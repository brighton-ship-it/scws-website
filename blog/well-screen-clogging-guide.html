Well Screen Clogging: Causes, Signs & Solutions

Updated February 2026 | By Southern California Well Service

Quick Answer: Well screens filter sediment and allow water into the well. Over time, they can clog with mineral deposits, iron bacteria, or fine particles—reducing yield. Signs include declining flow, pump running longer, and sand in water. Solutions range from chemical treatment ($500-$1,500) to full well rehabilitation ($3,000-$10,000).

What Is a Well Screen?

Purpose

A well screen is a filtering device installed at the water intake zone of the well. It allows water to flow into the well while blocking sand, sediment, and gravel from the aquifer.

Types of Screens

  • Slotted pipe: Simple slots cut into casing
  • Continuous slot (wire-wrapped): V-shaped openings, most efficient
  • Perforated pipe: Drilled holes
  • Bridge slot: Punched openings

Location

Screens are installed in the water-bearing zone, typically at the bottom of the well where the aquifer is accessed. There may be multiple screened sections in different water-bearing zones.

Causes of Screen Clogging

Iron Bacteria

  • Most common cause in our area
  • Bacteria oxidize iron, create slimy deposits
  • Reddish-brown buildup in well
  • Clogs screens and pump intakes

Mineral Encrustation

  • Calcium carbonate (scale)
  • Iron and manganese deposits
  • Builds up slowly over years
  • Common in hard water areas

Fine Sediment

  • Silt and clay particles
  • May pass through screen initially
  • Accumulates in slots over time

Gravel Pack Migration

  • Filter gravel around screen shifts
  • Can block screen openings
  • More common in poorly constructed wells

Biofilm

  • Microbial growth on screen surfaces
  • Combines with minerals to form deposits
  • Creates ideal environment for bacteria

Signs of Screen Clogging

Declining Yield

Primary indicator. Well produces less GPM than when new. Compare current pump test to original well log.

Pump Runs Longer

Takes more time to build pressure. Water enters well more slowly through clogged screen.

Sand or Sediment

May indicate screen failure or bypass around clogged screen openings.

Lower Static Water Level

Water can't recharge well as fast. Drawdown increases, recovery takes longer.

Pump Cycling

If yield drops enough, pump may cycle more frequently as tank drains faster than well refills.

Rusty or Discolored Water

Iron bacteria deposits in well can affect water color. May come in bursts.

Solutions for Clogged Screens

Chemical Treatment

Least invasive option. Acid or specialized chemicals dissolve mineral deposits.

  • Muriatic acid for calcium scale
  • Chlorine shock for bacteria
  • Specialty chemicals for iron bacteria
  • Cost: $500-$1,500
  • May need periodic retreatment

Mechanical Cleaning

Physical removal of deposits using brushes, scrapers, or surge blocks.

  • Wire brush cleaning
  • Surge block development
  • High-pressure jetting
  • Cost: $1,000-$3,000

Well Rehabilitation

Comprehensive approach combining chemical and mechanical methods.

  • Chemical treatment
  • Mechanical cleaning
  • Redevelopment (surging, airlifting)
  • Cost: $3,000-$10,000
  • Best results for serious clogging

Screen Replacement

If screen is damaged or corroded beyond cleaning.

  • Requires pulling casing or well reconstruction
  • Major undertaking
  • Cost: $5,000-$20,000+
  • May be time to consider new well

When to Choose Each Option

Situation Recommended Solution
Minor decline, recent Chemical treatment
Moderate decline Chemical + mechanical
Severe decline, old well Full rehabilitation
Screen damaged Screen replacement or new well

Prevention

Regular Shock Treatment

  • Annual chlorine shock for bacteria control
  • Especially important if iron bacteria present
  • Kills bacteria before major buildup

Proper Pump Operation

  • Don't overpump (exceeding yield creates turbulence)
  • Avoid frequent on/off cycling
  • Consistent, moderate pumping rates best

Water Treatment

  • Iron filters reduce iron reaching well
  • Softeners reduce scale-forming minerals
  • Reduces cycling of untreated water

Periodic Testing

  • Annual flow rate check
  • Water quality test
  • Catch problems before severe clogging

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if my well screen is clogged?

Declining yield, pump runs longer, sand in water, lower static water level. Compare current performance to well log records.

Can a clogged well screen be fixed?

Usually yes, through chemical treatment and/or mechanical cleaning. Severe cases may need full rehabilitation. Damaged screens may require replacement.

How long do well screens last?

20-50+ years depending on materials, water quality, and maintenance. Stainless steel lasts longest.

What causes iron bacteria?

Naturally occurring bacteria that feed on iron in groundwater. They're harmless to health but create troublesome deposits.

How much does well rehabilitation cost?

Chemical treatment: $500-$1,500. Mechanical cleaning: $1,000-$3,000. Full rehabilitation: $3,000-$10,000.
