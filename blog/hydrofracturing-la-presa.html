What is Hydrofracturing?

Hydrofracturing is a well rehabilitation technique that uses high-pressure water to clean and expand existing fractures in the bedrock surrounding your well. This process:

  • Clears mineral deposits and sediment blocking water flow
  • Opens new pathways for groundwater to reach your well
  • Can increase well yield by 2-10 times in many cases
  • Extends the life of your existing well

Unlike oil and gas fracking, well hydrofracturing uses only clean water—no chemicals are involved.

Signs Your La Presa Well Needs Hydrofracturing

  • Declining water production over time
  • Well runs dry during peak usage
  • Slow recovery rate after pumping
  • Pump cycling on and off frequently
  • Lower water pressure than previous years

The Hydrofracturing Process

  1. Initial Assessment: We evaluate your well's current yield and condition
  2. Equipment Setup: Specialized high-pressure equipment is positioned at your well
  3. Isolation: Packers seal off sections of the well for targeted treatment
  4. Pressurization: Water is pumped at 500-3,000 PSI to fracture bedrock
  5. Development: Well is cleaned and tested for improved yield

Reviving a Declining La Presa Well With Hydrofracturing

A weakening well in La Presa does not automatically mean it is time to drill a new one. La Presa lies in the inland hills of southern San Diego County, adjoining Spring Valley and the Sweetwater area, where wells commonly tap decomposed granite and fractured crystalline bedrock, and wells in that hard-rock setting are strong candidates for hydrofracturing. By cleaning and extending the natural fractures that supply the well, this technique can restore robust water production for a small fraction of what replacement would cost.

Hydrofracturing, also called hydrofracking, is purely a clean-water rehabilitation method. No chemicals are used, and it has no connection to oil-and-gas operations. Across the hillside parcels of La Presa and adjacent Spring Valley and Casa de Oro, it has brought tired wells back to life.

What Happens Underground

A bedrock well is fed by a system of fractures threading through the rock. Over years of use, mineral scale and sediment clog those fractures, and the well gradually loses its link to the moving groundwater. The pump and casing can be perfectly fine while the well still underperforms, because the real limit is the choked fracture network.

To correct it, we set inflatable packers to isolate a section of the borehole and inject clean water at high pressure. That pressure flushes the existing fractures and pushes them outward to meet additional water-bearing cracks, expanding the well's reach into the aquifer. The injection itself takes four to eight hours at the wellhead.

Signs Your La Presa Well Is a Candidate

  • Production that has dropped off over several years
  • The well running dry under peak household or irrigation demand
  • Slow recovery after the pump draws the water level down
  • A pump that short-cycles while battling a starved well
  • A newer rock well that never met its promised yield

When these appear on a well drilled into the fractured bedrock common around La Presa and Spring Valley, hydrofracturing is well worth evaluating.

Realistic Outcomes

We give honest numbers. In the decomposed granite and fractured crystalline rock of southern San Diego County, hydrofracturing improves yield in about 70 to 80 percent of treated wells, with typical gains of two to five times the prior flow. Success depends on connecting to productive fractures nearby. Hard-rock wells respond; wells in loose sand or gravel do not, which is why we confirm your formation before recommending the work. Measured flow tests before and after give you a documented result.

Comparing the Costs

Hydrofracturing a La Presa well typically costs $3,000 to $8,000. Drilling a new well in this part of the county runs $15,000 to $50,000 or more, and a full turnkey new well with pump and equipment generally falls between $18,000 and $42,000. Since hydrofracturing is a fraction of that and usually completes in a single day on site, it is the smart first option. A success keeps your existing well and saves you many thousands; even an underwhelming result leaves you better informed before you consider deepening or replacement.
