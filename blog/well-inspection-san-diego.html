Why Well Inspections Matter in San Diego

San Diego County has unique conditions that make regular well inspections particularly important:

  • Hard water effects: Our mineral-rich groundwater accelerates wear on pumps, tanks, and pipes
  • Drought impacts: Periodic droughts stress wells and can lower water tables significantly
  • Aging infrastructure: Many rural San Diego wells are 20-40+ years old
  • Contamination risks: Agricultural areas face nitrate concerns; flooding can introduce bacteria
  • Real estate transactions: Wells represent significant property value—problems must be disclosed

A professional inspection reveals what's working, what's wearing out, and what might fail next—allowing you to address problems on your terms rather than in an emergency.

What We Inspect

Our San Diego well inspections thoroughly evaluate every component of your water system:

Wellhead & Casing

  • Well cap/cover condition and security
  • Sanitary seal integrity (prevents surface contamination)
  • Visible casing condition above ground
  • Proper surface drainage away from well
  • Clearance from potential contamination sources

Well Pump System

  • Pump type, age, and condition
  • Motor amperage draw (indicates wear)
  • Flow rate measurement
  • Pumping water level vs. static level
  • Signs of pump failure

Pressure System

  • Pressure tank condition and air charge
  • Pressure switch operation and settings
  • System pressure range
  • Cycle timing (identifies tank problems)

Electrical Components

  • Control box condition
  • Wiring and connections
  • Proper grounding
  • Circuit protection

Water Quality

  • Coliform bacteria testing
  • Nitrate levels
  • Basic chemistry (pH, hardness, TDS)
  • Additional testing as needed (arsenic, iron, etc.)
  • See our water quality testing guide

Common Problems We Find During Inspections

Based on thousands of San Diego well inspections, here are the issues we most commonly discover:

Pump Issues (40%)

  • • Worn impellers reducing flow
  • • High amperage indicating wear
  • • Undersized pumps for home's needs
  • • Pump near end of life (10-15 years)

Pressure Tank Problems (35%)

  • • Waterlogged tanks
  • • Incorrect air pre-charge
  • • Undersized tanks causing cycling
  • • Corrosion and age

Water Quality (30%)

  • • Bacteria (often fixable with shock chlorination)
  • • High nitrates in ag areas
  • • Extreme hardness
  • • Iron/manganese staining

Wellhead Issues (25%)

  • • Improper or missing caps
  • • Failed sanitary seals
  • • Surface drainage toward well
  • • Contamination source proximity

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a well inspection cost in San Diego?

Well inspection costs in San Diego typically range from $250-$500 depending on the type and depth of inspection. Basic annual inspections run $200-$300. Real estate inspections with flow testing and water quality analysis cost $350-$500. Comprehensive inspections including video camera inspection of the well casing cost $400-$600+.

What does a well inspection include?

A standard well inspection includes: visual inspection of the wellhead and cap, pressure tank evaluation, pump performance testing, pressure switch operation check, electrical system inspection, flow rate measurement, water quality sampling (bacteria and nitrates at minimum), and a detailed written report. Comprehensive inspections may add video inspection of the well casing.

Do I need a well inspection when buying a home in San Diego?

Yes, absolutely. A well inspection is essential when buying a property with a private well. Unlike city water, wells can have hidden problems—failing pumps, low yields, contamination, or needed repairs that could cost thousands. A professional inspection reveals the well's condition, estimated remaining lifespan of components, and water quality before you commit.

How often should I have my well inspected?

We recommend annual well inspections for San Diego properties. This includes checking pump operation, pressure system function, and water quality testing for bacteria and nitrates. Annual inspections catch developing problems before they become emergencies and help extend equipment life. More frequent inspection may be needed for older systems or after unusual events.

How long does a well inspection take?

A standard well inspection takes 1-2 hours on site. This includes visual inspection, system testing, and water sampling. If video inspection of the well casing is needed, add another 1-2 hours. You'll typically receive a written report within 24-48 hours, or faster for time-sensitive real estate transactions.

What problems can a well inspection find?

Well inspections can identify: failing or undersized pumps, waterlogged pressure tanks, electrical problems, low water yield, dropping water table, casing damage or corrosion, sanitary seal issues allowing contamination, bacteria or chemical contamination, and aging components nearing end of life. Early detection prevents costly emergencies.

Do you provide reports for real estate transactions?

Yes, we provide detailed, professional inspection reports suitable for real estate transactions. Our reports include equipment condition, flow rate testing results, water quality analysis, estimated component lifespans, and any recommended repairs. Reports can be expedited for time-sensitive closings.

Can you inspect my well if I don't know how deep it is?

Yes. Many San Diego well owners don't have records of their well depth. During inspection, we can determine approximate depth through pump cable measurement and other methods. For precise measurements, we can perform a sounding or recommend pulling the pump. We can also help you obtain your well log from San Diego County records.
