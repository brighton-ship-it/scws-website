Well Casing Repair: Protecting Your Water Well Investment

Published February 2, 2025 | 16 min read

Your well casing is the protective barrier between your drinking water and the surrounding ground. When casing becomes damaged, corroded, or compromised, it can allow contaminants into your water supply and reduce well production. Well casing repair is essential for maintaining water quality and protecting your well investment. This guide covers everything San Diego and Riverside County homeowners need to know about diagnosing and repairing well casing problems.

What Is Well Casing and Why Does It Matter?

Well casing is the pipe that lines the inside of your drilled well. It serves several critical functions:

  • Structural support: Prevents the borehole from collapsing
  • Contamination barrier: Keeps surface water, bacteria, and pollutants out
  • Water conduit: Channels groundwater up to your pump
  • Aquifer isolation: Separates different water-bearing zones
  • Pump housing: Provides a smooth path for submersible pump installation

Most residential wells in San Diego and Riverside Counties use either steel casing (common in older wells) or PVC casing (standard in newer installations). The casing extends from above ground level down through unstable soils and typically into the bedrock where groundwater is found.

Casing Materials and Lifespan

Material Typical Lifespan Common Issues
Steel (Standard) 25-50 years Corrosion, rust-through, pitting
Galvanized Steel 30-50 years Corrosion (slower), scale buildup
Stainless Steel 50+ years Rarely fails; expensive
PVC (Schedule 40/80) 50+ years Cracking (rare), joint failure

Signs Your Well Casing Needs Repair

Casing damage often develops gradually, but some symptoms are unmistakable. Watch for these warning signs:

Water Quality Changes

  • Sand or sediment in water: Often the first sign of casing failure, as surrounding soil enters through holes
  • Sudden turbidity: Cloudy water after being clear indicates infiltration
  • Bacterial contamination: Positive coliform tests suggest surface water intrusion
  • Chemical changes: Nitrates, pesticides, or other surface contaminants appearing
  • Rust-colored water: May indicate steel casing corrosion
  • Taste or odor changes: Especially after rain events

Well Performance Issues

  • Declining production: Well produces less water than before
  • Water level changes: Static level drops without explanation
  • Pump damage: Sand or debris wearing out pump components
  • Short-cycling: Pump turns on and off frequently
  • Air in lines: Casing breach allowing air entry

Visible Damage

  • Rust on well cap area: Indicates corrosion below
  • Casing settling or tilting: Ground movement damage
  • Visible holes or cracks: At or above grade level
  • Deteriorated grout seal: Gap between casing and ground

⚠️ Important: If you notice sudden changes in water quality—especially after heavy rain—stop using the water and get it tested immediately. Compromised casing can allow dangerous bacteria and contaminants into your supply.

What Causes Well Casing Damage?

Understanding why casing fails helps you prevent problems and make informed repair decisions:

Corrosion (Most Common)

Steel casing corrodes over time, especially in aggressive water conditions:

  • Low pH (acidic) water: Common in some San Diego County areas, accelerates corrosion
  • High mineral content: Dissolved iron, manganese, and sulfates attack steel
  • Bacteria: Iron bacteria and sulfate-reducing bacteria cause bio-corrosion
  • Electrolysis: Electrical currents from dissimilar metals or stray current
  • Oxygen exposure: Air at the water line causes differential corrosion

Many older wells in the Ramona, Julian, and mountain areas have steel casing that's now 30-50+ years old and showing corrosion. Learn more about water quality factors in our iron in well water guide.

Ground Movement

  • Seismic activity: Earthquakes can crack or shear casing
  • Soil settling: Causes stress at casing joints
  • Landslide areas: Lateral movement damages casing
  • Subsidence: Groundwater depletion causing land to sink

Poor Original Installation

  • Inadequate grout seal: Allows surface water migration down casing exterior
  • Thin-wall casing: Some older wells used lightweight casing that fails sooner
  • Improper joint welding: Weak points that corrode first
  • Wrong material for conditions: Steel in highly corrosive environments

Physical Damage

  • Pump installation/removal: Equipment scraping casing walls
  • Dropped tools: Punctures from maintenance accidents
  • Vehicle impact: Surface casing struck by vehicles or equipment

Well Casing Repair Methods

The repair approach depends on the type, location, and extent of damage. Here are the main methods used:

1. Casing Liner Installation

The most common repair for corroded steel casing is installing a liner—a new, smaller-diameter pipe inside the existing casing.

How It Works:

  • Camera inspection identifies damage extent and location
  • Pump and drop pipe removed
  • Well cleaned and debris removed
  • New PVC or stainless liner lowered into well
  • Liner sealed at top and perforated at water zones
  • Annular space between liner and old casing grouted
  • Pump reinstalled (may need smaller diameter)

Cost: $3,000 - $8,000 depending on depth and liner material

Pros: Less disruptive than replacement, seals multiple problem areas

Cons: Reduces well diameter (may limit pump options)

2. Spot Repair/Patching

For isolated damage (single hole or small area), targeted repair may be possible:

  • Welded patches: Steel patches welded over holes (above water table only)
  • Casing clamps: External repair sleeves for accessible areas
  • Inflatable packers: Seal specific sections internally
  • Epoxy injection: Seal small cracks

Cost: $500 - $2,000

Pros: Least expensive, maintains full well diameter

Cons: Only works for limited damage; corrosion usually spreads

3. Upper Casing Replacement

When damage is concentrated in the upper portion of the casing (common), that section can be replaced:

  • Excavate around well head
  • Cut and remove damaged upper casing
  • Install new casing section (PVC or stainless)
  • Weld or couple to existing lower casing
  • Re-grout annular space
  • Install new sanitary well seal

Cost: $5,000 - $15,000

Pros: Full-diameter restoration, addresses surface contamination risks

Cons: Requires excavation, doesn't address deeper damage

4. Complete Well Rehabilitation

For severely damaged wells, comprehensive rehabilitation may include:

  • Full casing liner installation
  • Chemical treatment to remove scale and bacteria
  • Hydrofracturing to improve water production
  • New pump and pressure system
  • Water quality treatment installation

Cost: $8,000 - $20,000+

When needed: Old wells with multiple issues that are still producing adequate water

5. Well Replacement

Sometimes repair isn't practical, and drilling a new well is the better investment:

  • Casing damage too extensive for lining
  • Well has other problems (low production, poor water quality)
  • Cost of repair approaches new well cost
  • Well location problematic (too close to septic, etc.)

The Well Casing Inspection Process

Before recommending repairs, a professional inspection determines the casing's condition:

Downhole Camera Inspection

A waterproof video camera is lowered into the well to visually assess:

  • Corrosion extent and locations
  • Holes, cracks, or separations
  • Scale buildup or bacterial growth
  • Casing joint conditions
  • Debris or obstructions
  • Water entry points
  • Well screen condition

Cost: $300 - $500

Duration: 1-2 hours

Output: Video recording and professional assessment

Additional Testing

Depending on symptoms, additional tests may include:

  • Water quality testing: Bacteria, nitrates, other contaminants
  • Yield testing: Pumping test to assess production
  • Static water level measurement: Check for unusual changes

Preventing Casing Problems

While some casing deterioration is inevitable, these practices extend casing life:

Maintain Proper Well Head

Keep the sanitary seal intact, ensure proper drainage away from well, and maintain at least 12" of casing above grade.

Address Water Quality

Treat aggressive water (low pH, high minerals) to reduce corrosion. Consider acid neutralizers or whole-house treatment.

Annual Inspections

Have your well system checked yearly. Catch problems early before extensive damage occurs.

Protect from Damage

Install protective bollards around well head, keep equipment away, and mark the well location clearly.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does well casing repair cost?

Well casing repair costs vary from $500-$2,000 for spot repairs to $3,000-$10,000 for liner installation. Complete rehabilitation may cost $8,000-$20,000+. A camera inspection ($300-$500) is recommended first to assess damage extent.

What causes well casing damage?

The most common cause is corrosion from aggressive water chemistry, especially in steel casings. Other causes include ground movement, poor original installation, physical damage, and bacterial growth.

How do I know if my well casing is damaged?

Signs include sediment/sand in water, sudden water quality changes, bacterial contamination, decreased production, rust-colored water, and visible damage at the well head.

Can damaged well casing be repaired?

Yes, most casing damage can be repaired using liner installation, patching, or partial replacement. Complete replacement is only needed when damage is too extensive for other methods.

How long does well casing last?

PVC casing lasts 50+ years. Stainless steel lasts 50+ years. Standard steel casing lasts 25-50 years in neutral water, but may fail in 15-20 years in aggressive water.
