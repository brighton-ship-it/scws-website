What Is Well Casing?

Well casing is the pipe that lines your well borehole. It serves several critical functions:

  • Structural support: Prevents borehole collapse
  • Contamination barrier: Keeps surface water and shallow groundwater out
  • Pump housing: Provides a conduit for the pump and piping

Casing Materials

  • Steel: Most common, durable but corrodes over time
  • PVC: Won't corrode, common in modern wells
  • Galvanized steel: Older wells, zinc coating eventually fails

Typical Casing Lifespan

  • Steel casing: 25-50 years (depends on water chemistry)
  • PVC casing: 50+ years
  • Galvanized: 15-30 years

Signs of Casing Damage

Water Quality Changes

  • Brown or cloudy water after rain (surface infiltration)
  • Sand or sediment appearing in water
  • Sudden bacterial contamination
  • Change in taste or odor

Physical Signs

  • Visible rust or corrosion at wellhead
  • Casing sinking or settling
  • Gaps between casing and concrete pad

Performance Changes

  • Declining water production
  • Pump problems (sand damage)
  • More frequent pump cycling

Water Test Indicators

  • Coliform bacteria present (wasn't before)
  • Elevated nitrates (surface contamination)
  • High turbidity after rain

Causes of Casing Failure

1. Corrosion

Steel casing corrodes over time, especially with acidic water (low pH), high mineral content, or certain bacteria.

2. Age/Wear

All materials degrade eventually. Older wells (30+ years) are more likely to have casing issues.

3. Ground Movement

Earthquakes, subsidence, or soil shifting can crack or separate casing joints.

4. Poor Original Construction

Inadequate grouting, thin casing, or improper installation leads to early failure.

5. Electrolysis

Electrical currents in soil can accelerate steel corrosion, especially near utility lines.

Casing Repair Options

1. Casing Liner Installation

Most common repair method

  • Insert new PVC or steel pipe inside existing casing
  • Seal between liner and old casing
  • Cost: $2,000-$8,000
  • Pros: Extends life 20-30+ years, doesn't require new well
  • Cons: Reduces well diameter slightly

2. Patch Repair

For localized damage

  • Weld or seal specific holes or cracks
  • Only works for small, accessible damage
  • Cost: $500-$2,000
  • Pros: Less expensive if damage is minimal
  • Cons: May not address widespread corrosion

3. Re-Grouting

For grout seal failure

  • Pump cement grout around outside of casing
  • Seals gap between casing and borehole
  • Cost: $500-$2,500
  • Pros: Addresses surface contamination pathways
  • Cons: Doesn't fix casing holes

4. Casing Extension

If casing doesn't extend high enough

  • Weld or attach additional casing above ground
  • Cost: $300-$1,000
  • Required if casing is below grade (contamination risk)

Factors Affecting Cost

  • Well depth
  • Extent of damage
  • Casing diameter
  • Accessibility
  • Whether pump needs to be pulled

When to Drill a New Well

Repair May Not Be Possible If:

  • Casing has collapsed or severely deformed
  • Multiple sections of severe damage
  • Casing diameter too small for liner
  • Well is very old with other problems
  • Repair cost approaches new well cost

Consider New Well When:

  • Well is 40+ years old with multiple issues
  • Water production has significantly declined
  • Repair estimate exceeds $10,000
  • Contamination issues can't be resolved

Decision Framework

If repair cost > 30-40% of new well cost AND well is over 30 years old, a new well often makes more sense for long-term reliability.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can well casing be repaired?

Yes, usually. Liners, patches, and re-grouting can address most casing problems. A video inspection determines the best approach.

How do I know if my well casing is damaged?

Signs include: water quality changes after rain, sand in water, bacterial contamination, visible corrosion, and declining production.

How long does casing repair last?

A properly installed PVC liner can last 20-30+ years. The repair often outlasts the original casing.

Does homeowners insurance cover well casing repair?

Usually not for wear/corrosion (maintenance issue). May cover sudden damage from covered events. Check your policy.

How is casing damage diagnosed?

Video inspection (camera lowered into well) shows casing condition, holes, corrosion, and other problems. Costs $200-$500.
