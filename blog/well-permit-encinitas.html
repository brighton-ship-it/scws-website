San Diego County Well Permit Requirements

In San Diego County, well permits are required for:

  • Drilling a new domestic or agricultural well
  • Deepening an existing well
  • Repairing or reconstructing well casing
  • Decommissioning (destroying) an unused well
  • Converting a well to a different use

The Permit Process

  1. Site Evaluation: We assess your property for optimal well location
  2. Application: Submit permit application with site plans
  3. Review: County reviews for setback requirements and regulations
  4. Approval: Permit issued (typically 2-4 weeks)
  5. Construction: Well work proceeds per permit specifications
  6. Inspection: County may inspect completed work
  7. Completion Report: We file required documentation

Setback Requirements

Wells must be located minimum distances from:

  • Septic tanks: 50-100 feet
  • Leach fields: 100-150 feet
  • Property lines: 5-10 feet
  • Buildings: 5 feet minimum
  • Other wells: varies

Exact requirements depend on San Diego County regulations and local conditions.

Well Permits in Encinitas: What You Need to Know

Encinitas, on the San Diego County coast, blends dense beach neighborhoods with semi-rural inland pockets like Olivenhain where private wells remain common for irrigation and household use. Whether you are drilling a brand-new domestic well, deepening one that has stopped producing, or sealing an abandoned well on an inherited parcel, the work in Encinitas falls under California's statewide Water Well Standards (Department of Water Resources Bulletins 74-81 and 74-90) and is enforced locally by San Diego County. Southern California Well Service has been pulling these permits for property owners across the county since 2020, and we handle the entire process on a turnkey basis so you never have to stand in line at a county counter.

Who Issues Well Permits in Encinitas?

Because Encinitas is in San Diego County, well permits are issued by the San Diego County Department of Environmental Health and Quality (DEHQ), specifically its Land and Water Quality Division. This is the agency the State of California has designated to administer and enforce well construction, alteration, repair, and destruction standards in the unincorporated areas and within the incorporated cities of the county. The DEHQ well program can be reached at (858) 505-6688, and applications are processed through the Land and Water Quality Division rather than through a city building department. Even where a city issues building or grading permits, the water well permit itself still comes from the county DEHQ.

One thing that surprises many Encinitas property owners: the city you live in does not run its own well program. The county is the single authority. That is actually good news, because it means the rules are consistent whether your parcel is inside city limits or just outside them, and it means an experienced contractor who works countywide already knows the reviewers, the forms, and the local conditions.

Step-by-Step: The San Diego County Well Permit Process

Here is how a typical Encinitas well permit moves from application to final sign-off:

  1. Site evaluation. We walk your property, locate the best aquifer access, and confirm the proposed well can meet every required setback before a single form is filled out.
  2. Application and site map. DEHQ requires a completed Water Well Application accompanied by a detailed site map showing everything within a 250-foot radius of the proposed well: parcel boundaries, easements, existing and proposed septic systems and leach fields, structures, streets, water bodies, drainage patterns, other wells, sewer laterals, and any livestock or contamination sources.
  3. County review. The Land and Water Quality Division reviews the application against state standards and county ordinance, checking setbacks, depth, casing and sealing specifications.
  4. Permit issuance. Once approved, the permit is issued. Standard residential reviews in Encinitas typically take about 2 to 4 weeks.
  5. Construction. A licensed C-57 driller (that is us) constructs the well to the permitted specifications, including the required surface seal.
  6. Sealing and grouting inspection. The annular space is sealed with cement or bentonite grout to the required depth to protect the aquifer from surface contamination. The county may inspect this critical step.
  7. Well completion report. Within 60 days, the driller files the Well Completion Report (the state "Driller's Report") documenting depth, casing, perforations, and yield. We file this for you.

Permit Requirements and Construction Standards

San Diego County builds its requirements on top of the state minimums. For a Encinitas well you should expect to address the following:

  • Setbacks. Minimum separations of roughly 50 feet from septic tanks, 100 feet from leach fields and sewage disposal areas, and additional distances from property lines, streams, and drainage courses. Exact distances depend on soil and slope.
  • Surface seal. A continuous annular seal, normally a minimum of 20 feet deep (deeper in some formations), grouted to keep surface water and contaminants out of the borehole.
  • Casing and construction. Approved casing material, proper diameter, and sanitary surface completion with a watertight, vermin-proof cap.
  • Destruction standards. Abandoned wells must be destroyed by filling and sealing per Bulletin 74-90 so they cannot become a contamination pathway.
  • Completion reporting. The Well Completion Report must be submitted to the county and the Department of Water Resources.

Timeline and Costs in Encinitas

For a standard Encinitas residential well, plan on roughly 2 to 4 weeks for the county to review and issue the permit, assuming the application is complete and no variance is needed. Projects that trigger environmental review, sit near a protected waterway, or need a setback variance can take longer.

The county well permit fee in San Diego County generally falls in the $300 to $1,200 range for the basic permit, with additional charges possible for CEQA review, health clearance, or variance requests. When Do You Actually Need a Permit?

In Encinitas you need a San Diego County well permit to:

  • Drill a new well (domestic, agricultural, or commercial).
  • Deepen or modify an existing well to chase a dropping water table.
  • Repair or reconstruct casing in a way that alters the well.
  • Destroy or abandon a well that is no longer in use.

Routine pump replacement usually does not require a well construction permit, but anything that changes the borehole, casing, or seal does. When in doubt, ask us before you start.

Common Well Permitting Scenarios in Encinitas

Every Encinitas property is different, and the permitting path depends on what you are trying to do. Here are the situations we see most often and how each is handled under San Diego County rules:

  • Building a new home on raw land. If your Encinitas parcel has no water district connection, a domestic well permit is typically part of getting the property buildable. We coordinate the well permit alongside your septic and grading so the timelines line up.
  • An older well that has gone dry or lost yield. Deepening or replacing the well requires its own permit. The county treats a replacement well as new construction, so the full site map and setback review apply.
  • Buying a property with an abandoned well. An unsealed, unused well is a contamination liability and can hold up escrow. We pull a destruction permit and seal it to Bulletin 74-90 standards, then file the destruction report.
  • Agricultural irrigation expansion. Larger-yield ag wells may face additional review, especially near sensitive watersheds. We handle the heavier documentation these projects require.

The San Diego County Department of Environmental Health and Quality (DEHQ) Land and Water Quality Division, reachable at (858) 505-6688. The city does not run its own well program.

Wells must sit roughly 50 feet from septic tanks, 100 feet from leach fields and sewage disposal areas, and set distances from property lines, streams, and drainage. The site map must show everything within a 250-foot radius.
