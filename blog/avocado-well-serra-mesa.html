Chloride Sensitivity

Avocados are highly sensitive to chloride in irrigation water. If your Serra Mesa well has elevated chloride:

  • Blending with lower-chloride water source
  • Leaching irrigation to flush salts
  • Rootstock selection for salt tolerance
  • Regular soil and leaf testing

What Applies in a City-Water Neighborhood

In a neighborhood like Serra Mesa, the well is not the question — pressure and irrigation are. Homes on canyon rims and sloped lots, along with larger landscaped properties, sometimes need more pressure than the street provides to keep distant or elevated irrigation zones healthy. A booster or constant-pressure system raises municipal water pressure to reach those areas reliably. The pumps, pressure tanks, and controls in those systems wear out over time and need the same kind of service a well system does. For a homeowner with extensive landscaping or a few backyard fruit trees, the goal is simple: deliver enough water at adequate pressure during the hot afternoon hours when plants are working hardest.

What You Can Check Before You Call

  • If irrigation pressure is weak, note whether it is one zone or the whole system; one weak zone usually means a filter, valve, or emitter, not the pump.
  • On a booster system, check the pressure gauge and listen for rapid pump cycling, which suggests a waterlogged tank or failing switch.
  • Confirm breakers and controls before assuming the pump has failed.
  • For a rural well you are evaluating, collect any pump, water-test, or drilling records you can find.

How a Booster and Irrigation System Works

Understanding the hardware helps you spot trouble early, and the same parts appear whether water comes from a city main or a private well. A booster pump raises incoming pressure; a pressure tank stores a cushion of pressurized water so the pump does not restart on every demand; and a pressure switch or constant-pressure controller governs when the pump runs. A constant-pressure system uses a variable-speed drive to hold steady pressure as irrigation zones cycle, which suits large or sloped landscapes well. When a component fails, the symptoms mimic a failing well: surging and sagging pressure, rapid pump cycling, or zones starved of water. Pinpointing the faulty part instead of replacing everything is exactly where our experience saves you money.

Keeping a Landscape Healthy Through Summer

Serra Mesa summers are hot and dry, and slopes and large yards feel it most. A simple routine keeps irrigation reliable: inspect booster pumps and pressure tanks in spring, clean filters and check emitters for clogs, and verify that elevated zones receive full coverage before the heat arrives. If pressure has been slipping, fix it ahead of the hottest months. Homeowners with a few fruit trees do best with deep, consistent watering during fruit development rather than occasional heavy soakings that run off before they soak in.
