How Appraisers Evaluate Well Systems

Appraisers assess whether the water supply is adequate for the property's intended use. They look for documentation of water quantity (flow rate) and quality (test results). Visible problems like a damaged wellhead, outdated equipment, or lack of documentation raise concerns. In rural areas where wells are standard, a well in good condition is simply expected and doesn't affect value—problems do.

Flow Rate and Appraisal Value

Insufficient water supply can significantly impact appraisals. Most lenders require a minimum 3-5 GPM flow rate for residential properties. If flow testing shows inadequate supply, the appraiser may require remediation before loan approval, or reduce the appraised value to reflect the deficiency. Storage tanks can sometimes compensate for lower flow rates.

Water Quality Considerations

Clean water test results support full appraisal value. Contamination issues—bacteria, nitrates above limits, arsenic—require remediation and can delay or derail sales. Even treatable issues like hard water or iron may lead appraisers to note the cost of treatment systems. Having recent, clean test results available shows the well is a reliable water source.

Preparing for Appraisal

Gather all documentation before the appraisal: well completion report, recent water tests (within 6 months), equipment records, permits for any well work, and maintenance history. If you've invested in improvements—new pump, larger pressure tank, water treatment—have invoices available. Point out these upgrades to the appraiser. Well-documented, well-maintained systems appraise at full value.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do homes with wells appraise lower than city water homes?

Not necessarily. In areas where wells are common, properly maintained wells don't negatively impact value. However, wells with documented problems, insufficient flow, or contamination issues can lower appraisals significantly. A well in good condition with recent test results often appraises comparably to municipal water properties.

What do appraisers look for in well systems?

Appraisers evaluate: adequate flow rate (typically 3-5 GPM minimum), acceptable water quality test results, functioning equipment, proper permits and documentation, and compliance with lender requirements. For FHA/VA appraisals, specific testing and flow requirements must be met or the loan can't close.

How can I improve my well's impact on appraisal value?

Provide documentation: well completion report, recent water tests, equipment records, and any maintenance history. Address any obvious problems before appraisal. If you've made improvements (new pump, treatment system, increased storage), have documentation ready showing the investment.
