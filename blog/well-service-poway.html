Understanding Poway's Geology & Well Depths

Poway sits at the transition between San Diego's coastal lowlands and the Peninsular Ranges to the east. This location creates varied geological conditions that affect well drilling and water production across different areas of the city.

Typical Well Depths in Poway

  • Western Poway (near Poway Road): 350-450 feet
  • Central Poway & Old Poway: 400-500 feet
  • Eastern Poway (Green Valley, Espola): 450-550 feet
  • Hill properties (higher elevations): 500-600+ feet

*Actual depths vary by property; site evaluation provides accurate estimates

The predominant geology in Poway consists of decomposed granite and weathered crystalline rock, with pockets of harder granitic formations in the eastern hills. Water-bearing fractures typically occur between 300 and 500 feet, though productive zones can extend deeper in some areas. Common Well Problems in Poway

Poway's geology and climate create specific challenges for well owners. Understanding these common issues helps you recognize problems early and maintain reliable water production.

Mineral Buildup & Scale

Poway's groundwater contains dissolved minerals, particularly calcium and magnesium, that cause hard water and scale accumulation. Over time, mineral deposits can clog well screens, reduce pump efficiency, and damage household plumbing. Regular water testing and appropriate treatment prevents these issues.

Seasonal Water Level Fluctuations

During extended dry periods, water levels in Poway wells may drop, causing pumps to work harder or, in some cases, lose prime. Wells drilled to adequate depths with properly set pumps handle seasonal fluctuations better. If your well struggles during dry months, pump repositioning or well deepening may be needed.

Pump Wear from Sand & Sediment

Some Poway wells produce sand or fine sediment that accelerates pump wear. Worn impellers reduce water pressure and flow rate. If you notice declining pressure or sandy water, have your pump inspected before complete failure occurs.

Aging Well Infrastructure

Many Poway properties have wells dating to the 1970s and 1980s. Steel casings in older wells can corrode, and outdated pump systems may operate inefficiently. Upgrading aging components improves water quality, reduces energy costs, and prevents unexpected failures.
