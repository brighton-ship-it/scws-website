Signs You Need Pressure Tank Service

How do you know when your pressure tank needs attention? Watch for these warning signs:

  1. Pump short-cycling
    Your pump turns on and off rapidly (every few seconds to minutes). This is the #1 sign of a waterlogged tank.
  2. Low or inconsistent water pressure
    Pressure drops when multiple fixtures are used, or fluctuates unpredictably.
  3. Tank feels heavy at the top
    Tap the tank — the top should feel hollow (that's the air). If it feels solid/heavy, the tank is waterlogged.
  4. Water from the air valve
    Press the Schrader valve (like a tire valve) on top — if water sprays out, the bladder has failed.
  5. Visible rust or corrosion
    Rust on the outside often means worse corrosion inside. Time for replacement.
  6. Age (10-15+ years)
    Even if working fine, older tanks are at higher risk of failure.

Repair vs. Replace: Which Do You Need?

✅ Repair Makes Sense If:

  • • Tank is under 10 years old
  • • Only needs air charge adjustment
  • • Bladder is replaceable
  • • No visible corrosion
  • • Issue is with switch/fittings

❌ Replace Is Better If:

  • • Tank is 15+ years old
  • • Visible rust or corrosion
  • • Diaphragm tank (can't replace)
  • • Multiple problems at once
  • • Tank is undersized

Keep your pressure tank working properly

Why Your Service Pressure Tank Matters

The pressure tank is the heart of your well system — it stores pressurized water so your pump doesn't have to run every time you turn on a faucet. A properly sized and functioning pressure tank in your Service home means consistent water pressure, longer pump life, and lower electricity costs.

Signs Your Pressure Tank Needs Attention

  • Pump short cycling: Turns on and off every few seconds (the #1 sign of a waterlogged tank)
  • Fluctuating pressure: Strong pressure that suddenly drops, then recovers
  • Water hammer: Banging pipes when fixtures turn on or off
  • Visible rust or corrosion: On the tank exterior, especially at seams or the base
  • Water from the air valve: Press the Schrader valve — if water comes out, the bladder has failed

Sizing a Pressure Tank for Service Homes

Tank size depends on your pump's flow rate and how many fixtures you run simultaneously. For typical Service residences:

  • 1-2 bathroom homes: 20-32 gallon tank (adequate for 5-10 GPM pumps)
  • 3-4 bathroom homes: 44-86 gallon tank (handles 10-20 GPM pumps and higher simultaneous demand)
  • Estates/ranch properties: 86-120 gallon tank or multiple tanks (for high-flow systems with irrigation)

Undersized tanks cause short cycling, which destroys pumps. We always size based on your actual pump output and peak demand — not just the number of bathrooms.
