Sediment Filters for Well Water: Types & Sizing Guide

Updated February 2026 | By Southern California Well Service

Quick Answer: For well water, start with 20-50 micron filter for larger particles, add 5-10 micron for finer filtration. Whole-house filters go at point of entry. Change when pressure drops 8-10 PSI or every 3-6 months. Cost: $50-$300 for system, $10-$40 per filter.

When You Need Sediment Filtration

Signs You Need a Filter

  • Sand or grit in water
  • Cloudy or murky water
  • Particles visible in glass
  • Sediment in toilet tank
  • Clogged fixture aerators
  • Pump screen buildup

Causes of Sediment

  • Sand aquifer (common)
  • Well screen failure
  • New or disturbed well
  • Corroding pipes
  • Pump oversized for well

Benefits of Filtration

  • Protects fixtures and appliances
  • Cleaner water
  • Longer appliance life
  • Pre-treatment for other filters

Filter Types

Spun Polypropylene (Most Common)

  • Graduated density (tighter toward center)
  • Good dirt-holding capacity
  • Inexpensive ($5-$15)
  • Single-use, disposable
  • Available in many sizes/microns

Pleated Filters

  • More surface area
  • Often washable and reusable
  • More expensive initially ($20-$50)
  • Longer life between changes
  • Good for moderate sediment

String Wound

  • Wrapped string around core
  • Good depth filtration
  • Inexpensive
  • Single-use

Spin-Down/Disc Filters

  • Screen catches large particles
  • Flush to clean (no replacement)
  • Good for heavy sand
  • Pre-filter before cartridge filters
  • Cost: $50-$200

Multi-Media Filters

  • Tank with layered media
  • Backwash to clean
  • No cartridge replacement
  • Higher capacity
  • Cost: $500-$2,000+

Understanding Microns

What's a Micron?

1 micron = 1/1000 of a millimeter. Human hair is about 70 microns. Lower number = finer filtration.

Micron Guide

Micron Catches Use For
50-100 Visible sand Pre-filter, heavy sediment
20-30 Fine sand, silt First stage filtration
5-10 Fine particles Second stage, general use
1-5 Very fine particles Before carbon or RO
Below 1 Bacteria-sized Special applications

Multi-Stage Approach

Best practice: coarse first, then fine

  • Stage 1: 20-50 micron (catches big stuff)
  • Stage 2: 5-10 micron (catches fines)
  • This extends filter life and maintains flow

Sizing

Housing Size

Housing Flow Rate Application
10" x 2.5" 5-8 GPM Under sink, light use
10" x 4.5" (Big Blue) 10-15 GPM Small whole house
20" x 4.5" (Big Blue) 15-25 GPM Standard whole house

Match to Your System

  • Consider peak flow demand
  • Larger housing = less pressure drop
  • Larger = longer filter life

Pressure Considerations

  • New filter: minimal pressure drop
  • Clogged filter: significant pressure loss
  • Install pressure gauge to monitor

Installation

Location

  • Whole house: After pressure tank, before plumbing split
  • Point of use: Under sink or at specific fixture

Basic Setup

  1. Shut off water
  2. Install housing with bypass valve (optional but helpful)
  3. Use proper fittings for your pipe type
  4. Install pressure gauges before and after (optional)
  5. Insert filter cartridge
  6. Turn on water, check for leaks

Tips

  • Install shut-off valves before and after
  • Leave space for cartridge changes
  • Put drain or bucket catch underneath
  • Label filter micron and install date

Maintenance

When to Change

  • Pressure drop of 8-10 PSI across filter
  • Flow rate noticeably reduced
  • On schedule (3-6 months typical)
  • Visibly dirty when inspected

How to Change

  1. Turn off water supply
  2. Relieve pressure (open faucet downstream)
  3. Put bucket under housing
  4. Unscrew housing (wrench usually included)
  5. Remove old filter, discard
  6. Clean housing if needed
  7. Insert new filter (check orientation)
  8. Reseat housing, tighten
  9. Turn on water, check for leaks

Filter Costs

  • Standard cartridges: $5-$15
  • Pleated/washable: $20-$50
  • Buy in bulk for savings

Washable Filters

  • Rinse with hose or soak
  • Can reuse 5-10+ times
  • Replace when damaged or won't clean

Frequently Asked Questions

What micron filter should I use for well water?

Start with 20-50 micron for heavy sediment, add 5-10 micron as second stage. Multi-stage prevents clogging and maintains flow.

How often should I change my sediment filter?

Every 3-6 months for typical use, or when pressure drops 8-10 PSI. Heavy sediment may need monthly changes.

Will a sediment filter remove iron?

Only oxidized (particulate) iron. Dissolved iron passes through and needs dedicated iron filter or softener.

Do I need a whole house filter?

If sediment affects all fixtures, yes. If only concerned about drinking water, point-of-use may be enough.

Can filters make my pressure too low?

Clogged filters can. Proper sizing and regular changes prevent this. Install pressure gauges to monitor.
