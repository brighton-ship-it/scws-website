Sediment Filters for Well Water: Complete Guide

Updated February 2026 | By Southern California Well Service

Quick Answer: Most wells need 5-20 micron sediment filter. Use multi-stage (coarse to fine) for best results. Replace every 3-6 months or when flow drops. Filter protects fixtures, appliances, and downstream treatment equipment.

Why Filter Sediment?

What Sediment Does

  • Clogs faucet aerators
  • Damages appliances (water heater, dishwasher)
  • Shortens water softener life
  • Reduces flow rate
  • Leaves deposits in toilets/tubs

Common Well Sediment

  • Sand and silt
  • Rust particles (iron)
  • Clay and fine soil
  • Scale flakes
  • Organic debris

Protection For

  • Water softener (extends media life)
  • UV system (requires clear water)
  • Reverse osmosis (pre-treatment)
  • Fixtures and appliances

Filter Types

Spun Polypropylene (PP)

  • Most common type
  • Fibers trap particles
  • Depth filtration (captures throughout)
  • Inexpensive
  • Disposable

Pleated

  • More surface area (longer life)
  • Surface filtration
  • Can be washed and reused (some)
  • Better for finer particles
  • Higher cost per filter

String Wound

  • String wrapped around core
  • Good for high sediment
  • Depth filtration
  • Classic, reliable design

Spin-Down / Backwashing

  • Reusable screen filter
  • Flush to clean (no cartridge replacement)
  • Best as pre-filter for heavy sediment
  • Higher upfront cost, lower ongoing

Bag Filters

  • For high-flow applications
  • Large capacity
  • Commercial/agricultural use

Micron Ratings Explained

What Is a Micron?

A micron (micrometer) = 1/1000 of a millimeter. Human hair ≈ 70 microns. Lower number = finer filtration.

Common Ratings

Micron Removes Best For
50-100 Large sand, debris Pre-filter, heavy sediment
20-30 Fine sand, some silt General pre-filter
5-10 Silt, fine particles Final sediment filter
1-5 Very fine particles Pre-UV, pre-RO

Nominal vs Absolute

  • Nominal: Removes most particles of rated size (85-90%)
  • Absolute: Removes nearly all particles of rated size (99%+)
  • Nominal more common, absolute for critical applications

Multi-Stage Strategy

Best practice: coarse filter first, then fine filter.

  • Stage 1: 50 micron (catches big stuff)
  • Stage 2: 20 micron
  • Stage 3: 5 micron (final polish)

Extends life of finer filters, better overall filtration.

Sizing Your System

Flow Rate

  • Match filter housing to your flow needs
  • Undersized = pressure drop
  • Standard 10" x 4.5" handles most homes
  • High flow: 20" housings or multiple filters

Housing Sizes

Size Typical Flow Use
10" x 2.5" 2-5 GPM Point-of-use, low flow
10" x 4.5" 10-15 GPM Most residential
20" x 4.5" 15-25 GPM Larger homes, longer life

Installation Location

  • After pressure tank
  • Before water softener
  • Before other treatment
  • Accessible for maintenance

Maintenance

When to Replace

  • Scheduled: every 3-6 months
  • Pressure drop: noticeable flow reduction
  • Visual: filter discolored throughout
  • After well work: always replace

Signs Filter Needs Changing

  • Low water pressure
  • Filter very dark/discolored
  • Sediment appearing at fixtures
  • Pressure gauge shows high differential

How to Replace

  1. Shut off water supply (or bypass if available)
  2. Release pressure (open downstream faucet)
  3. Unscrew housing (filter wrench helps)
  4. Remove old filter, clean housing
  5. Install new filter
  6. Hand-tighten housing
  7. Turn water on slowly, check for leaks

Pro Tips

  • Keep spare filters on hand
  • Note replacement date on housing
  • O-ring may need occasional replacement/lubrication
  • Sanitize housing periodically with bleach solution

Frequently Asked Questions

What micron filter do I need for well water?

Most wells: 5-20 micron. Heavy sediment: add 50 micron pre-filter. Pre-RO/UV: use 5 micron or finer.

How often should I change my sediment filter?

Every 3-6 months on average. More often with heavy sediment. Check monthly and replace when discolored or flow drops.

Does a sediment filter remove bacteria?

No—bacteria are too small. Sediment filters are for particles. For bacteria, you need UV or chlorination.

Should I install filter before or after pressure tank?

After pressure tank. Protects your plumbing and filters at lower pressure (easier to change).

Why is my new filter clogging quickly?

Too fine for your water. Try coarser micron rating first, then step down. Or add pre-filter stage.
