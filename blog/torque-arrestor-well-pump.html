Understanding Pump Startup Torque

Every time your submersible pump starts, the motor generates powerful rotational force. Without a torque arrestor, this force transfers through the drop pipe, twisting connections and stressing the pump's electrical connections. Over years of cycling, this repeated stress causes premature failure of pipe joints and wire insulation.

How Torque Arrestors Work

A torque arrestor is a simple but effective device: rubber fins mounted on a collar that attaches to the pump or drop pipe. When torque occurs, these fins press against the well casing, transferring rotational force to the casing rather than through your piping. Quality arrestors use oil-resistant rubber rated for decades of use.

Installation Best Practices

Torque arrestors should be installed just above the pump motor. In deeper wells (200+ feet), additional centralizers help keep the pump centered and reduce pipe wear against the casing. Proper sizing is essential—the arrestor must match your casing diameter with enough grip to be effective without being so tight it's difficult to remove.

Signs of Torque Arrestor Failure

If your pump makes a loud thumping or banging sound at startup, the torque arrestor may be worn or missing. Other signs include visible pipe twist marks inside the casing, loose fittings where the drop pipe connects, or wire insulation damage near the pump. Catching these early prevents expensive motor or pipe replacement.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a torque arrestor do?

When a submersible pump starts, the motor generates significant rotational force (torque) that tries to spin the pump body. A torque arrestor grips the well casing, absorbing this force so it doesn't stress pipe connections, wiring, or the motor itself.

How often should torque arrestors be replaced?

Torque arrestors should be inspected whenever the pump is pulled for service—typically every 10-15 years. Replace them if the rubber fins are cracked, hardened, or worn down. A new arrestor costs $20-50 and prevents hundreds in potential damage.

Do all well pumps need torque arrestors?

All submersible pumps benefit from torque arrestors, but they're especially critical for larger motors (1 HP and above) and deeper wells where the long drop pipe creates more stress. Most manufacturers require them to maintain warranty coverage.
