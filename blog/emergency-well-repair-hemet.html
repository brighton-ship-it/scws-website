The Hemet-San Jacinto Valley's Water Heritage

The Hemet-San Jacinto Valley sits in a broad basin surrounded by mountains, creating one of the most productive groundwater regions in Riverside County. The valley's agricultural heritage – from early citrus and grain farming to modern diverse crops – has always depended on this groundwater.

Today, while municipal water serves much of the urban area, private wells remain vital throughout the valley. Agricultural operations depend on well water for irrigation. Rural properties beyond municipal service areas rely on wells exclusively. Even some properties within city limits maintain wells for irrigation or as primary water sources.

The valley's climate demands significant water during hot summers, when temperatures regularly exceed 100 degrees. This peak demand period coincides with maximum stress on both aquifers and pumping equipment, making summer well failures particularly common and urgent.

Warning Signs for Hemet Well Owners

Watch for these indicators of developing problems:

  • Declining pressure or flow
  • Pump running excessively
  • Rapid cycling
  • Air in lines
  • Sandy or discolored water
  • Higher electric bills
  • Equipment running hot
  • Unusual sounds

Early attention often prevents emergencies.
