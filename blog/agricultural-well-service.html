Agricultural Applications We Serve

San Diego County is home to thousands of acres of avocados, citrus, and stone fruit. We design irrigation wells matched to your acreage, tree density, and soil type. Most mature orchards need 20-50 GPM per 10 acres for drip irrigation.

🍇 Vineyards

🐴 Horse Properties & Ranches

🐄 Cattle & Livestock

Cattle need 10-20 gallons per head daily in hot weather. Large ranches require wells capable of supplying multiple troughs across pastures. We design systems with storage tanks to handle peak demand without overworking pumps.

Commercial nurseries have intensive water needs with strict quality requirements. We provide high-volume wells with appropriate filtration and treatment to prevent plant disease and optimize growth.

🌾 Row Crops & Field Agriculture

Large-scale irrigation requires wells capable of sustained high output—often 100-500+ GPM. We design systems with VFDs for efficiency, proper sizing for pressure requirements, and backup capacity for critical periods.

Agricultural Water Requirements Guide

Proper well sizing starts with understanding your water needs:

Application Typical GPM Needed
Small orchard (5-10 acres) 30-50 GPM
Large orchard (20-50 acres) 100-200 GPM
Vineyard (per 10 acres) 20-40 GPM
Horse property (10-20 horses) 15-30 GPM
Cattle ranch (50-100 head) 20-50 GPM
Commercial nursery (1 acre) 50-100 GPM
Large-scale irrigation (100+ acres) 200-500+ GPM

These are general guidelines. Actual requirements depend on soil type, climate zone, irrigation method (drip vs. sprinkler), and crop water needs. We provide detailed assessments to size your system correctly.

Well Service in Service

Service properties in San Diego County rely on private wells drilled through the Peninsular Ranges batholith, primarily granitic and metamorphic rock. Local geological conditions affect everything from drilling depth and cost to water quality and pump selection.

Agricultural Water Needs in Service

Service's San Diego County location means a Mediterranean climate with dry summers that put heavy demand on irrigation wells from May through October. Agricultural wells here must be sized for sustained high-volume pumping, often 15-50 GPM from alluvial or weathered rock aquifers.

Common agricultural well setups in Service include variable frequency drives (VFDs) to match pump output to demand, storage tanks for buffer capacity, and booster systems for pressurized irrigation lines. We size every agricultural pump to the well's tested yield — oversizing wastes energy and can damage the well by drawing the water level down too fast.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does an agricultural well cost in California?

Agricultural wells in Southern California typically cost $25,000 to $100,000+ depending on depth, diameter, and flow requirements. High-capacity irrigation wells (50+ GPM) require larger diameter drilling, industrial pumps, and more robust infrastructure. Factors include well depth (often 300-600 feet), casing diameter (6-12 inches vs 4-6 for residential), pump capacity, and electrical requirements.

How many gallons per minute does a farm well need?

Agricultural water needs vary dramatically by operation. Livestock operations typically need 10-30 GPM. Small orchards (5-10 acres) may need 30-50 GPM. Large-scale irrigation for row crops or vineyards often requires 100-500+ GPM. We help farmers calculate exact requirements based on acreage, crop type, irrigation method, and local evapotranspiration rates.

Do I need a permit for an agricultural well in San Diego County?

Yes, all new wells in California require permits. Agricultural wells must also comply with SGMA (Sustainable Groundwater Management Act) requirements in many basins. San Diego County permits are issued through the Department of Environmental Health. Agricultural wells may also require water rights documentation depending on the groundwater basin status.

What size well casing is needed for agricultural use?

Agricultural wells typically use 6-inch to 12-inch diameter casing, compared to 4-6 inches for residential wells. The larger diameter accommodates high-capacity pumps capable of delivering 50-500+ GPM. Casing size is determined by required flow rate, pump size, and total depth. We help design the optimal configuration for your specific agricultural needs.

How often should agricultural wells be serviced?

Agricultural wells should be inspected and serviced at least annually, with more frequent checks during peak irrigation season. High-use wells benefit from quarterly pump checks. Annual service should include flow rate testing, pump amp draw measurement, water quality testing, and inspection of irrigation infrastructure. Early detection of efficiency losses can save thousands in pump replacement costs.
