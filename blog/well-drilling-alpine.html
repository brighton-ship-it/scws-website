Alpine's Groundwater

Alpine sits on decomposed granite and fractured rock formations that generally provide favorable conditions for well development.

Decomposed Granite

Much of Alpine overlays weathered granite that stores groundwater in fractures and decomposed zones. This geology typically produces reliable wells with moderate to good production.

Variable Conditions

Conditions vary across Alpine's diverse terrain. Valley properties and lower elevations often have better water access than ridge-top locations, though most areas can support productive wells.

Good Quality

Alpine wells generally produce good quality water. The granite geology provides natural filtration, and most wells need minimal treatment.

FAQs

Is Alpine good for well drilling?

Generally yes. Alpine's decomposed granite provides favorable conditions, and most properties can develop productive wells. Conditions vary by location, which is why we provide free site evaluations.

Do I need a well if district water is available?

It depends on your property's needs. Large properties with irrigation, livestock, or high water use often benefit from wells. Many properties use wells for irrigation while maintaining district water for household use.

What about fire protection?

Critical for Alpine. We design systems with dedicated fire storage that can also benefit insurance rates. Storage tanks of 5,000-15,000+ gallons are common for rural properties.

Can you handle horse property needs?

Absolutely. We have extensive experience with equestrian properties—arena irrigation, pasture watering, barn supply, and the higher-capacity systems horses require.
