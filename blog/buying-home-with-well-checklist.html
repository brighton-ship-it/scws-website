Pre-Inspection Document Review

Before scheduling inspections, request the well completion report (well log) from the county or seller. This document shows well depth, casing material, flow rate at drilling, and aquifer information. Also ask for maintenance records, recent water test results, and any permits for well work. Missing documentation isn't necessarily a dealbreaker, but it affects what you'll need to verify through inspection.

Flow Rate and Recovery Testing

Your inspector should test the well's flow rate—typically by running water for an extended period while monitoring output. California homes need at least 3-5 gallons per minute for normal household use. More important is recovery rate: how quickly the well replenishes after drawdown. A well that runs dry during a 30-minute test may struggle during high-use periods like morning showers or irrigation.

Equipment and Infrastructure Assessment

The inspection should evaluate pump age and condition, pressure tank function, electrical connections and wire sizing, piping condition, and wellhead seal integrity. Ask about the pump's horsepower and whether it matches the well depth. Check for proper backflow prevention if the property has irrigation connections. Equipment typically lasts 10-20 years, so factor replacement costs into your offer.

Water Quality Testing Requirements

Beyond the basic bacteria and nitrate tests, consider testing for arsenic (common in California foothill regions), lead (from old plumbing), and any contaminants known in the area. If the property has agricultural history, test for pesticides. Hard water, iron, and sulfur aren't health hazards but affect fixtures and may require treatment systems. Get test results before removing contingencies.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I get a well inspection before buying a home?

Absolutely. A professional well inspection ($300-500) can reveal problems that cost thousands to fix—failing pumps, contamination, inadequate flow rates, or code violations. Most lenders require inspections for FHA/VA loans, but even for conventional purchases, it's essential due diligence.

What water tests are required when buying a home with a well?

At minimum, test for coliform bacteria, nitrates, and basic water quality (pH, hardness, iron). California also recommends testing for arsenic and other naturally occurring contaminants based on your region. FHA and VA loans have specific testing requirements that must be met before closing.

Can I back out of a purchase if the well has problems?

Yes, if your purchase contract includes a well inspection contingency. This is standard practice in California. If significant issues are found, you can negotiate repairs, a price reduction, or cancel the sale. Never waive inspection contingencies on well properties.
