San Diego County Groundwater Overview

San Diego County spans from coastal beaches to mountain peaks over 6,000 feet. This diverse geology creates very different groundwater conditions across the region:

  • 27 designated groundwater basins
  • Estimated 5+ million acre-feet of groundwater storage
  • ~25,000 registered water wells (residential, agricultural, industrial)
  • 2-3 critically overdrafted basins under SGMA management

Major Groundwater Basins

San Pasqual Valley Basin

  • Location: North of Escondido, including San Pasqual Valley Road area
  • Aquifer type: Alluvial (sand and gravel)
  • Typical depth: 100-300 feet
  • Typical yield: 20-100+ GPM
  • Water quality: Moderate hardness (150-400 mg/L), some iron
  • Status: Managed by San Pasqual Valley Basin Groundwater Sustainability Agency

Santee-El Monte Basin

  • Location: Santee, El Cajon, Lakeside areas
  • Aquifer type: Alluvial, following San Diego River
  • Typical depth: 50-200 feet
  • Typical yield: 10-50 GPM
  • Water quality: Variable; TDS 400-1000 mg/L, hardness common
  • Notes: Heavily urbanized, limited new well permits

San Diego River Valley Basin

  • Location: Along San Diego River from mountains to Mission Valley
  • Aquifer type: Alluvial
  • Typical depth: 100-400 feet
  • Typical yield: 15-75 GPM
  • Water quality: Varies by location; urban areas may have contamination concerns

Borrego Valley Basin

  • Location: Borrego Springs, desert areas
  • Aquifer type: Alluvial, very deep
  • Typical depth: 200-600+ feet
  • Typical yield: 50-500+ GPM (agricultural wells higher)
  • Water quality: Generally good, some areas have elevated fluoride
  • Status: CRITICALLY OVERDRAFTED under SGMA - groundwater sustainability plan requires pumping reductions

Fractured Rock Groundwater Regions

The mountains and foothills of San Diego County don't have traditional aquifers. Instead, groundwater exists in fractures within the underlying granite and metamorphic rock.

East County Mountains (Julian, Ramona Mountains, Cuyamaca)

  • Aquifer type: Fractured rock (granite)
  • Typical depth: 200-600 feet
  • Typical yield: 2-20 GPM (highly variable)
  • Water quality: Excellent—naturally soft, low TDS (100-300 mg/L)
  • Notes: Success depends on hitting fracture zones; professional siting critical

Ramona Valley & Backcountry

  • Aquifer type: Mix of fractured rock and small alluvial deposits
  • Typical depth: 150-400 feet
  • Typical yield: 5-30 GPM
  • Water quality: Generally good; some areas have iron or manganese
  • Notes: Our home base—we know this area well

Valley Center, Pauma Valley, Pala

  • Aquifer type: Mix of alluvial and fractured rock
  • Typical depth: 200-500 feet
  • Typical yield: 5-50 GPM
  • Water quality: Variable—test for hardness and iron

Coastal and Urban Areas

Coastal San Diego has limited fresh groundwater due to:

  • Saltwater intrusion: Seawater can contaminate coastal aquifers
  • Urbanization: Paved surfaces reduce recharge
  • Contamination: Historical industrial use in some areas
  • Permit restrictions: Many coastal areas have well drilling limitations

Wells in coastal areas are typically for irrigation only, not domestic supply. Contact the County for current permit status in your specific location.

Well Permits in San Diego County

San Diego County regulates well drilling through the Department of Environmental Health:

  • Permit required: All new wells, deepening, repairs affecting casing
  • Cost: $500-$1,500+ depending on type
  • Requirements: Site plan, setback verification, drilling contractor license
  • Inspection: Required before pump installation

We handle all permitting for our customers—included in our drilling services.

SGMA Impact on San Diego Wells

The Sustainable Groundwater Management Act (SGMA) affects some San Diego groundwater basins:

  • Borrego Valley: Critically overdrafted, pumping reductions required
  • San Pasqual: Under management plan
  • Other basins: Currently not subject to SGMA restrictions

If you're in an SGMA-managed basin, new wells may face additional requirements. Check with the local Groundwater Sustainability Agency.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are San Diego's main aquifers?

San Diego County has 27 designated groundwater basins. Major ones include San Pasqual Valley, Santee-El Monte, San Diego River Valley, and Borrego Valley. Mountain areas rely on fractured rock rather than traditional aquifers.

How does San Diego groundwater quality vary?

Quality varies significantly by location. Mountain areas (Julian, Cuyamaca) have excellent soft water. Valley aquifers often have hardness and iron. Coastal areas may have elevated TDS. Always test your specific well—our generalizations are starting points only.

Are San Diego aquifers being depleted?

Most San Diego basins are sustainable. Borrego Valley is critically overdrafted and subject to pumping restrictions under SGMA. Other basins are adequately managed. For typical residential use in most areas, long-term supply is not a concern.

How deep are wells in San Diego County?

Depth varies by location: Valley wells typically run 100-400 feet. Mountain/fractured rock wells often need 200-600 feet. Borrego desert wells can exceed 600 feet. Your specific location determines required depth.

What yield can I expect from a San Diego well?

Valley alluvial wells often produce 20-100+ GPM. Fractured rock wells (mountains) are less predictable: 2-20 GPM typical, but hitting good fractures can exceed 30 GPM. Minimum household need is 3-5 GPM.

Do I need a permit to drill a well in San Diego County?

Yes. San Diego County requires permits for all new wells, deepening, and repairs affecting casing. Contact the County Department of Environmental Health, or we handle permitting as part of our drilling services.
