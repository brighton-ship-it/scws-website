Few things are more frustrating than hearing your well pump running while no water comes from your faucets. This common problem can have several causes, ranging from simple fixes to issues requiring professional repair. Understanding what's happening underground can help you determine your next steps.

Common Causes

  • Low Water Table: Extended drought or seasonal changes can drop the water level below your pump's intake. This is increasingly common in Southern California.
  • Failed Foot Valve: The foot valve at the bottom of your well prevents water from draining back down. When it fails, the pump loses its prime.
  • Cracked Drop Pipe: The pipe connecting your pump to the surface can crack or separate, allowing air into the system and water to leak back down.
  • Worn Pump Impellers: Over time, the impellers that push water up can wear down, reducing the pump's ability to lift water to the surface.
  • Electrical Issues: The pump motor may be running but not at full capacity due to voltage problems or failing capacitors.
  • Clogged Intake Screen: Sand, sediment, or mineral buildup can block the pump's intake, starving it of water even when water is available.

Warning Signs to Watch For

  • Pump runs continuously without shutting off
  • Faucets sputter air before going dry
  • Pressure gauge shows zero or very low pressure
  • You hear the pump cycling but pressure never builds
  • Water pressure was gradually declining before stopping completely

How to Fix This Problem

1. Check the Pressure Tank

First, look at your pressure gauge. If it reads zero and the pump is running, the problem is likely in the well, not the tank. If the gauge shows pressure but no water flows, check for closed valves.

2. Listen to the Pump

Go to your wellhead and listen. A pump that's running but cavitating (pumping air) makes a different sound than one moving water. Loud, irregular sounds often indicate air in the system.

3. Check the Circuit Breaker

Ensure the pump is getting full power. A tripped or weak breaker can cause the pump to run at reduced capacity.

4. Wait and Retest

If the water table is temporarily low, turning off the pump for 30-60 minutes may allow water to recover. Then try again.

5. Call a Professional

If basic checks don't reveal the problem, you'll need a well technician to pull the pump and inspect the system. This requires specialized equipment.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a well pump run but not pump water?

Yes, a pump can run without pumping water if the impellers are worn, the water table has dropped, or there's a leak in the drop pipe. The motor operates but can't move water effectively.

How long can a well pump run dry before damage?

Running dry can damage a pump within minutes to hours depending on the pump type. Submersible pumps rely on water for cooling—running dry overheats the motor and can cause permanent damage.

Why is my well pump humming but not pumping?

A humming pump that won't start often indicates a seized motor, failed capacitor, or stuck impeller. This requires immediate attention to prevent motor burnout.

How much does it cost to fix a well pump not pumping?

Repair costs range from $300-500 for minor issues like a failed foot valve to $1,500-3,000+ for pump replacement. A diagnostic visit typically costs $150-250.
