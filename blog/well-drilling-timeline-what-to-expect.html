Well Drilling Timeline: What to Expect from Start to Finish

February 15, 2026 9 min read

"How long will it take?" is the first question most homeowners ask about well drilling. The answer depends on permits, geology, weather, and project complexity—but understanding the typical timeline helps you plan realistically and avoid frustration with what can feel like a slow process.

Quick Timeline Overview

For a standard residential well in Southern California:

  • Pre-drilling (permits, site prep): 2-6 weeks
  • Actual drilling: 1-3 days
  • Well development: 1-2 days
  • Pump installation & setup: 1-3 days
  • Testing & disinfection: 5-10 days
  • Final inspection: 1-2 weeks (scheduling dependent)

Total realistic timeline: 6-12 weeks from contract signing to using your water.

Complex projects, difficult geology, or permit complications can extend this to 3-6 months. Let's break down each phase.

Phase 1: Pre-Drilling Preparation (2-6 Weeks)

Initial Site Assessment (1-3 Days)

Before drilling begins, contractors evaluate your property:

  • Site accessibility: Can the drill rig reach the location? Will roads or fences need modification?
  • Utility clearance: Underground utilities marked (DigAlert required 2-3 days before)
  • Well location determination: Considering septic system setbacks, property lines, geology, and future access
  • Geological review: Checking neighboring well logs to estimate depth and yield

This phase typically takes 1-3 days but provides critical information for accurate planning and cost estimates.

Permitting Process (2-8 Weeks)

California counties require well permits before drilling. Timeline varies significantly:

San Diego County: 3-6 weeks typical, can expedite to 2 weeks with complete applications

Riverside County: 2-4 weeks for routine permits

Imperial County: 2-3 weeks average

San Bernardino County: 3-5 weeks depending on location

Permits require:

  • Site plan showing well location and setbacks
  • Proof of property ownership
  • Environmental review (some areas)
  • Hydrogeologic assessment (groundwater basins)
  • Septic system location verification
  • Application fees ($150-$500 typical)

What slows down permits:

  • Incomplete applications (most common—can delay weeks)
  • Properties in groundwater management areas
  • Environmental sensitive areas
  • Proximity to public water systems
  • County staff shortages or backlogs
  • Additional review requirements

Pro tip: Work with contractors experienced in your county. They know exactly what documents to submit and can navigate review processes efficiently.

Site Preparation (1-2 Days)

Once permits are approved and drilling is scheduled:

  • Access preparation: Clearing paths for the drill rig (30-40 feet long, 8-10 feet wide)
  • Pad construction: Level area for rig stability
  • Erosion control: Preventing mud and water runoff
  • Equipment staging: Space for casing, materials, and support vehicles

Drill rigs are massive—plan for temporary landscape disturbance in a 20x40 foot area minimum.

Phase 2: Drilling Operations (1-5 Days)

Rig Mobilization (0.5 Days)

The drill rig arrives and sets up—this alone takes 2-4 hours. Contractors verify final location, ensure level positioning, and confirm all safety measures.

Active Drilling (1-3 Days Typical)

Actual drilling time depends primarily on depth and geology:

Shallow wells (100-200 feet):

  • Soft formations (sand, clay): 4-8 hours
  • Mixed materials: 6-12 hours
  • Can often complete in one day

Medium depth (200-400 feet):

  • Typical residential range in Southern California
  • Sedimentary formations: 1-2 days
  • Granite or hard rock: 2-3 days

Deep wells (400-600 feet):

  • Usually require 2-3 days minimum
  • Hard rock can extend to 4-5 days
  • More common in mountain and desert areas

Very deep wells (600+ feet):

  • 3-7 days drilling time
  • Require specialized equipment and experienced crews
  • More expensive due to extended rig time

Drilling doesn't always proceed continuously. Crews may pause to:

  • Test water-bearing zones
  • Change drill bits or tools
  • Install casing sections
  • Address unexpected formations
  • Monitor for equipment issues

Casing Installation (Concurrent with Drilling)

Steel or PVC casing is installed as drilling progresses to prevent collapse and contamination. This is part of the drilling timeline but adds complexity to the process.

What Can Go Wrong During Drilling

Unexpected issues that extend drilling time:

  • Hitting bedrock earlier than expected: May require different drilling methods, adding 1-2 days
  • Lost circulation: Drilling fluid disappears into fractured rock, requiring special techniques
  • Equipment failure: Broken drill bits, hydraulic issues—can delay 1-3 days for repairs
  • Insufficient water yield: May need to drill deeper or try different location
  • Contaminated zones: If poor-quality water is encountered, drilling continues to better aquifer
  • Weather: Rain can halt operations for safety and access reasons

Experienced drillers anticipate many of these issues, but geology always holds surprises.

Demobilization (0.5 Days)

After reaching target depth and adequate water supply, the rig is dismantled and removed—typically 2-4 hours.

Phase 3: Well Development (1-2 Days)

Immediately after drilling, the well must be "developed" to maximize yield and water quality:

  • Removing drill cuttings and sediment
  • Opening up water-bearing fractures and pores
  • Testing sustained flow rates
  • Clearing fine materials that reduce permeability

Development involves surging, pumping, and sometimes air-lifting to clean the well. This takes 4-12 hours typically, but wells may be left to rest overnight between sessions.

Properly developed wells produce clearer water at higher flow rates. Skipping or rushing this step is a common mistake with long-term consequences.

Phase 4: Pump Installation & System Setup (2-5 Days)

Pump Selection and Procurement (Varies)

If the pump isn't already on-site, allow 1-3 days for delivery of the correct model based on well depth and yield.

Pump Installation (1 Day)

Installing a submersible pump involves:

  • Lowering pump on drop pipe to proper depth
  • Running electrical cable and safety rope
  • Installing pitless adapter or wellhead
  • Running supply line to house
  • Grounding and electrical connections

Straightforward installations take 4-6 hours. Complex setups or long runs to the house can take a full day or more.

Pressure System Setup (0.5-1 Day)

Installing and configuring:

  • Pressure tank
  • Pressure switch
  • Control box
  • Safety cutoffs
  • Plumbing connections

Electrical Connection (0.5-1 Day)

Licensed electricians connect:

  • Power supply from house or new service
  • Control wiring
  • Grounding system
  • Disconnect switch

Electrical work may require separate inspection, adding scheduling time.

Phase 5: Testing & Disinfection (5-10 Days)

Well Disinfection (1 Day Plus Wait Time)

California requires new wells to be disinfected before use:

  • Chlorination: High-concentration chlorine solution added to well
  • Contact time: Minimum 12-24 hours
  • Flushing: Chlorinated water pumped out until clear
  • Wait period: 48-72 hours before testing

Water Quality Testing (5-7 Days)

Samples sent to certified labs for bacterial analysis:

  • Sample collection: Specific protocols required
  • Lab processing: 2-5 days typical
  • Results review: Must show zero coliform bacteria

If bacteria are detected, the well is re-disinfected and re-tested, adding another 7-10 days.

Flow and Performance Testing (1 Day)

Verifying the system meets design specifications:

  • Flow rate verification
  • Pressure consistency testing
  • Recovery rate measurement
  • System cycling and controls

Phase 6: Final Inspections & Completion (1-3 Weeks)

County Well Inspection

Inspectors verify:

  • Well constructed per permit
  • Proper casing and seal
  • Adequate setbacks maintained
  • Wellhead above grade and protected
  • Water quality test results

Scheduling the inspection can take 1-2 weeks in busy periods. Failed inspections require corrections and re-inspection.

Electrical Inspection (If Required)

Separate electrical permit may require its own inspection. Schedule as early as possible to avoid delays.

Well Completion Report

Drillers must file completion reports with the state (DWR) documenting:

  • Total depth and casing details
  • Geological formations encountered
  • Water-bearing zones
  • Static and pumping water levels
  • Yield test results

This is filed within 60 days but doesn't delay system use once county inspections pass.

Special Circumstances That Extend Timelines

Shared Wells

Wells serving multiple properties require easement agreements and potentially more complex permitting—add 4-8 weeks.

Groundwater Management Areas

Properties in managed basins may need:

  • Additional hydrogeologic studies
  • Water rights documentation
  • Sustainability plan review
  • This can add 2-6 months to permitting

Environmental Review

Wells near sensitive habitats, tribal lands, or protected areas may trigger CEQA review—potentially adding 3-12 months.

Seasonal Factors

  • Winter (Dec-Feb): Rain delays common, especially in clay soils; may add 1-3 weeks
  • Spring (Mar-May): Peak season—longer contractor scheduling but better weather
  • Summer (Jun-Aug): Best weather, but high demand can mean 4-8 week wait for scheduling
  • Fall (Sep-Nov): Good weather, moderate demand—often ideal timing

How to Minimize Delays

  • Choose experienced local contractors: They know county processes and avoid application mistakes
  • Submit complete permit applications: Missing information is the #1 cause of permit delays
  • Schedule early: Book contractors 4-8 weeks ahead during peak season
  • Prepare access in advance: Clear paths and staging areas before rig arrival
  • Be flexible: Weather and equipment issues happen—rigid timelines cause frustration
  • Plan for contingencies: Budget extra time for re-testing or unexpected geology
  • Stay in communication: Responsive property owners keep projects moving

What You Should Be Doing During Each Phase

Pre-drilling: Secure financing, coordinate with contractors, prepare property access, notify neighbors

Drilling: Stay available for questions, keep pets/children clear, document progress with photos

Installation: Verify pump selection matches well conditions, plan interior plumbing connections

Testing: Coordinate lab access, review results with contractors, plan any needed filtration

Completion: Get all documentation (permits, test results, warranties), schedule maintenance training

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to drill a well from start to finish?

Total time ranges from 6-12 weeks for most residential wells in California. This includes 2-4 weeks for permits, 1-3 days actual drilling, 1-2 weeks for pump installation and testing, and final inspections. Complex projects or permit delays can extend this to 3-6 months.

How long does the actual drilling take?

Drilling itself typically takes 1-3 days for residential wells 200-500 feet deep. Shallow wells (under 200 feet) may finish in a single day, while deep wells (600+ feet) or hard rock formations can take 3-5 days of drilling time.

What causes delays in well drilling projects?

Common delays include: permit processing (2-8 weeks varies by county), weather (rain makes access difficult), equipment breakdowns, hitting unexpected rock formations, low water yield requiring deeper drilling, septic system conflicts, and utility clearances.

Can I use my well water immediately after drilling?

No. After drilling, the well must be developed, pump installed, disinfected, and water tested for bacteria. This process takes 1-2 weeks. California law requires passing bacterial tests before a well can be approved for domestic use.

What is the fastest a well can be completed?

With expedited permitting and no complications, minimum is 3-4 weeks: 1 week for permits, 1-2 days drilling, 3-5 days for development and pump installation, 5-7 days for testing and final inspection. This requires perfect conditions and experienced contractors.
