SGMA and Your Well: What Owners Need to Know | SCWS

California's Sustainable Groundwater Management Act affects some San Diego County well owners. Learn which areas are regulated, how SGMA may impact your water rights, and what to expect going forward.

What is SGMA?

The Sustainable Groundwater Management Act (2014) requires California to sustainably manage groundwater by 2040 or 2042 depending on basin priority. The state identified critically overdrafted basins requiring Groundwater Sustainability Plans. Local agencies implement these plans, which may include pumping restrictions, fees, and well metering to prevent continued depletion.

SGMA in San Diego County

San Diego County has several groundwater basins, but most aren't critically overdrafted. Borrego Valley is the primary basin under strict SGMA management—decades of agricultural pumping depleted the aquifer. San Pasqual Valley, while a medium-priority basin, has sustainable management already. Most private wells in mountains and foothills draw from fractured rock systems outside SGMA jurisdiction.

Impact on Borrego Valley Wells

Borrego Valley's Groundwater Sustainability Plan requires significant pumping reductions. Agricultural users face the largest cuts, but residential wells may also have allocations. The plan phases in restrictions through 2040. Property buyers should understand current allocations and future limitations. Existing wells may see property values affected by water rights limitations.

Planning for the Future

Even outside SGMA basins, groundwater sustainability matters. Climate change affects recharge patterns. Responsible well owners monitor their water levels and usage. If you're in or near a regulated basin, stay informed through your local Groundwater Sustainability Agency. For major property investments, understand water availability before committing—water rights increasingly affect property values throughout California.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does SGMA affect my well?

It depends on location. SGMA primarily affects wells in critically overdrafted basins—in San Diego County, that's mainly Borrego Valley. Most other county wells are in lower-priority basins or fractured rock areas not subject to SGMA adjudication.

Will SGMA limit how much water I can pump?

In affected basins, yes. Borrego Valley is implementing pumping reductions to achieve sustainability by 2040. This may include allocation limits for existing wells. De minimis users (under 2 acre-feet/year—about 650,000 gallons) may have different requirements.

Do I need to register my well under SGMA?

In SGMA-regulated basins, well registration may be required. Borrego Water District manages compliance for Borrego Valley. Outside regulated basins, county well permits serve as registration. Contact your local Groundwater Sustainability Agency for specific requirements.
