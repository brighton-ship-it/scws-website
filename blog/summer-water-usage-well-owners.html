Understanding Summer Well Demands

San Diego County summers push private wells to their limits. With temperatures regularly exceeding 90°F and minimal rainfall from May through October, your well faces increased demand precisely when aquifer recharge is lowest. Understanding this cycle helps you plan water usage that keeps your well healthy year-round.

Smart Irrigation Strategies

Outdoor watering accounts for up to 70% of summer water use. Switch to drip irrigation for gardens and consider smart timers that adjust based on weather conditions. Watering deeply but less frequently encourages deeper root growth and reduces overall consumption. Mulching around plants retains moisture and reduces watering needs by up to 25%.

Protecting Your Pump System

High summer demand stresses your pump motor and pressure system. Ensure your pressure tank is properly sized—undersized tanks cause rapid cycling that burns out pumps. Consider installing a cycle stop valve or variable frequency drive for wells with high usage. Regular summer maintenance checks catch problems before they become expensive failures.

Indoor Conservation Tips

Simple indoor changes make a big difference. Fix leaky faucets (one drip per second wastes 3,000 gallons yearly), install low-flow showerheads, and run dishwashers and washing machines only with full loads. These habits reduce well pump runtime and extend system life while keeping water available when you need it most.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much more water do wells use in summer?

Most San Diego County wells see 40-60% higher usage during summer months due to irrigation, pools, and increased household demand. Monitoring your usage helps prevent pump strain and aquifer depletion.

Can running my well pump too much damage it?

Yes. Continuous cycling during high-demand periods causes motor overheating and premature wear. Installing a pressure tank and cycling controls protects your pump and extends its lifespan.

Should I water my lawn with well water in summer?

You can, but be strategic. Water early morning (before 6 AM) to minimize evaporation, use drip irrigation where possible, and consider drought-tolerant landscaping to reduce overall demand.
