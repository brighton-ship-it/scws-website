Tank Basics

What a Pressure Tank Does

  • Stores water under pressure
  • Provides water between pump cycles
  • Maintains consistent pressure
  • Reduces pump cycling (extends pump life)

How It Works

  1. Pump fills tank until cut-off pressure (typically 50-60 PSI)
  2. Pump shuts off
  3. Tank supplies water as you use it
  4. Pressure drops to cut-in (30-40 PSI)
  5. Pump turns on, refills tank
  6. Cycle repeats

Key Terms

  • Tank size: Total capacity (gallons)
  • Drawdown: Usable water per cycle
  • Air charge: Pre-charged air pressure
  • Cut-in/Cut-off: Pressure switch settings

Understanding Drawdown

Drawdown vs Tank Size

Drawdown is NOT the same as tank size. Drawdown is the usable water delivered between pump cycles.

Why the Difference?

  • Tank contains air bladder/diaphragm
  • Air takes up space
  • Only fills to cut-off pressure
  • Doesn't empty completely (cut-in pressure)

Typical Drawdown Percentages

With 40/60 pressure switch (30/50 similar):

Tank Size Approximate Drawdown % of Capacity
20 gallon 5-6 gallons ~28%
32 gallon 8-10 gallons ~28%
52 gallon 14-16 gallons ~29%
85 gallon 23-26 gallons ~29%
119 gallon 32-36 gallons ~29%

Sizing Calculation

The Rule

1 gallon of drawdown per GPM of pump flow rate

This provides minimum 1 minute run time per cycle.

Example

  • Pump flow rate: 10 GPM
  • Minimum drawdown needed: 10 gallons
  • Tank size needed: 10 ÷ 0.28 = ~36 gallons
  • Recommendation: 52-gallon tank (provides buffer)

Quick Reference

Pump GPM Minimum Tank Recommended Tank
5 GPM 20 gallon 32 gallon
8 GPM 32 gallon 52 gallon
10 GPM 44 gallon 52-85 gallon
15 GPM 52 gallon 85 gallon
20 GPM 85 gallon 119 gallon

Other Factors

  • Peak demand: More fixtures = more drawdown helpful
  • Low-yield well: Larger tank provides buffer
  • Space constraints: May limit tank size
  • Constant pressure system: Different sizing rules

Common Tank Sizes

20 Gallon

  • Small homes, low flow pumps
  • ~5-6 gallon drawdown
  • Limited—pump cycles frequently
  • Cost: $150-$250

32-35 Gallon

  • Standard residential
  • ~8-10 gallon drawdown
  • Adequate for 5-8 GPM pumps
  • Cost: $200-$350

52-62 Gallon

  • Better residential choice
  • ~14-18 gallon drawdown
  • Good for 8-12 GPM pumps
  • Reduces cycling significantly
  • Cost: $350-$500

85-86 Gallon

  • Optimal for most homes
  • ~23-26 gallon drawdown
  • Excellent pump protection
  • Great for 10-15 GPM pumps
  • Cost: $500-$700

119+ Gallon

  • Large homes, high demand
  • 32+ gallon drawdown
  • Commercial/agricultural
  • Cost: $700-$1,000+

Why Bigger Is Better

Less Pump Cycling

  • Each start is hardest on motor
  • Frequent cycling = shorter pump life
  • Larger tank = longer between cycles

The Math

10 GPM pump, 5 gallon drawdown = pump cycles every 30 seconds under load

10 GPM pump, 25 gallon drawdown = pump cycles every 2.5 minutes under load

Tank Cost vs Pump Cost

  • 85-gallon tank upgrade: ~$200-$300 more than 32-gallon
  • Pump replacement: $1,500-$4,000
  • Longer pump life easily pays for bigger tank

Other Benefits

  • Better pressure stability
  • Buffer for low-yield wells
  • Handle higher peak demands
  • Less pressure fluctuation during use

Frequently Asked Questions

What size pressure tank do I need?

Match to your pump: 1 gallon drawdown per GPM. For a 10 GPM pump, need ~10 gallons drawdown = 35-52 gallon tank minimum. Bigger is better.

What's the difference between tank size and drawdown?

Tank size is total capacity. Drawdown is usable water per cycle (about 25-30% of tank size). A 52-gallon tank provides ~14-16 gallons of drawdown.

Can my tank be too big?

Not really. Bigger tanks are always better for the pump. Only limit is space and cost.

Why is my pump cycling so much?

Tank too small, lost air charge (waterlogged), failed bladder, or leak in system. Check air pressure in tank first.

How do I check tank drawdown?

Turn off pump. Open faucet until pump kicks on. Measure gallons that came out. That's your current drawdown.
