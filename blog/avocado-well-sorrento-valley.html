Avocado Water Demands

Avocados are thirsty trees:

  • Per acre: 4-6 acre-feet per year
  • Critical periods: Fruit set and sizing

Chloride Sensitivity

Avocados are highly sensitive to chloride in irrigation water. If your Sorrento Valley well has elevated chloride:

  • Blending with lower-chloride water source
  • Leaching irrigation to flush salts
  • Rootstock selection for salt tolerance
  • Regular soil and leaf testing

Canyon and Mesa Conditions That Shape Water Systems

Sorrento Valley's terrain of canyons, mesas and the adjacent lagoon means elevation changes and large landscaped footprints are the norm. Office parks and research campuses often irrigate substantial grounds, and properties spread across grade changes need booster pumps to deliver uniform pressure to every zone. For these reasons, commercial irrigation pumping, booster systems and pressure regulation - not drinking-water wells - are the heart of what we do here.

How Commercial Booster and Irrigation Systems Work

A commercial booster system raises and stabilizes municipal pressure for large or multi-level properties. It combines a pump (often a multi-stage or variable-speed unit), a pressure tank or constant-pressure controller, and a pressure switch or transducer. As irrigation zones cycle and demand shifts, the controller modulates the pump to hold a steady target pressure across the whole site. For a sprawling Sorrento Valley campus, a correctly engineered constant-pressure system means every zone - from the entrance landscaping to the back of the lot - gets consistent coverage, while protecting motors from the wear of constant on/off cycling.

Common Scenarios We See in Sorrento Valley

  • Irrigation controller and valve faults mistaken for supply failures.
  • Backflow and pressure-regulation issues on code-regulated commercial systems.

What to Check Before You Call

  • Power. Confirm the breaker and any fuse for the booster or irrigation pump.
  • Controller. A tripped central timer or stuck zone valve causes many "system down" calls.
  • Gauge. Note whether the booster cycles between two pressures or sits flat.
  • Tank. A short-cycling, solid-sounding pressure tank is typically waterlogged.
  • Scope. Determine whether the problem is one zone (valve) or the whole site (pump/supply).

Compliance, Backflow and Reliability for Facilities

We service these systems with that bigger picture in mind: correct backflow protection, properly set regulation, and pumps and controls sized for dependable, around-the-clock operation.
