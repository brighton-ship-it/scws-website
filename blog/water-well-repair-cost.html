Repair Cost Factors

Well Depth

For submersible pump issues, well depth is the biggest cost factor. Pulling a pump from a 150-foot well takes less time and equipment than a 400-foot well. Deep well pump replacements cost more for both labor and equipment.

Type of Problem

  • Electrical issues: Often the cheapest to fix—pressure switches, capacitors, and wiring are above-ground and accessible
  • Pressure tank problems: Mid-range cost—replacement is straightforward
  • Pump failure: Most expensive—requires pulling equipment from the well
  • Well itself: Rehabilitation, cleaning, or casing repairs can be complex and costly

Emergency vs. Scheduled

Emergency calls on nights, weekends, or holidays typically add $100-$200 to the service fee. If your situation allows, scheduling during business hours saves money.

How We Diagnose Well Problems

Our diagnostic process identifies exactly what's wrong before recommending repairs:

  1. Visual inspection: Check above-ground components for obvious issues
  2. Electrical testing: Verify power supply, switches, and controls
  3. Pressure testing: Check tank pressure and system pressures
  4. Amp draw test: Measure pump electrical draw to assess motor condition
  5. Flow testing: Measure actual water production

The diagnostic fee ($150-$250) is credited toward repairs if you proceed with us.

When Repair vs. Replace

Sometimes repair isn't the best option:

  • Pump over 15-20 years old: Replacement often makes more sense than repair
  • Multiple recent repairs: Pattern of failures suggests end-of-life
  • Well production declining: May need rehabilitation rather than pump work
  • Water quality issues: Could indicate well problems beyond the pump

We give honest recommendations—sometimes spending more upfront saves money long-term.
