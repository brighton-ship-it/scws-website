Water Testing Costs by Type

Bacteria testing (coliform/E. coli) costs $30-$50. Basic mineral panel runs $75-$150. Nitrate testing costs $25-$40. Standard homeowner panel ranges from $150-$250. Comprehensive testing with 50+ parameters costs $300-$500 or more. Real estate transaction panels typically run $200-$400.

Essential Tests for California Wells

Coliform bacteria testing is required annually at minimum. Nitrate testing is especially important near agriculture or septic systems. Arsenic occurs naturally in many California aquifers. Hardness and TDS affect fixtures and taste. pH impacts plumbing corrosion and should be monitored.

When to Test Your Well

Test annually for bacteria at minimum. Test after any flooding or well repairs, when buying or selling property, and if water taste, odor, or color changes. Also test after nearby construction or land use changes, and if anyone in the household has unexplained illness.

Understanding Your Test Results

Labs compare results to EPA and California MCLs (Maximum Contaminant Levels). Some contaminants have no health-based standard and are aesthetic only. Treatment recommendations should match specific contaminants found. Always retest to confirm positive results before making major treatment system purchases.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where do I get my well water tested?

Use a state-certified laboratory for reliable results. County health departments often offer low-cost bacteria testing ($20-$40). Private labs provide comprehensive panels. Your well contractor can recommend local certified labs and may offer testing services.

How do I collect a water sample properly?

For bacteria testing: sterilize the faucet, run water 2-3 minutes, fill the sterile container without touching the inside. Deliver to the lab within 24-48 hours (check lab requirements). Improper collection causes false positives—follow lab instructions exactly.

Do I need to test for everything?

Not necessarily. Annual bacteria testing is essential for all wells. Beyond that, test based on local risks: agricultural areas (nitrates, pesticides), old homes (lead), industrial areas (VOCs). Your county health department can advise on area-specific concerns.
