What is Hydrofracturing?

Hydrofracturing is a well rehabilitation technique that uses high-pressure water to clean and expand existing fractures in the bedrock surrounding your well. This process:

  • Clears mineral deposits and sediment blocking water flow
  • Opens new pathways for groundwater to reach your well
  • Can increase well yield by 2-10 times in many cases
  • Extends the life of your existing well

Unlike oil and gas fracking, well hydrofracturing uses only clean water—no chemicals are involved.

Signs Your Serra Mesa Well Needs Hydrofracturing

  • Declining water production over time
  • Well runs dry during peak usage
  • Slow recovery rate after pumping
  • Pump cycling on and off frequently
  • Lower water pressure than previous years

The Hydrofracturing Process

  1. Initial Assessment: We evaluate your well's current yield and condition

  3. Isolation: Packers seal off sections of the well for targeted treatment
  4. Pressurization: Water is pumped at 500-3,000 PSI to fracture bedrock
  5. Development: Well is cleaned and tested for improved yield

What Hydrofracturing Actually Is

Hydrofracturing, sometimes called hydrofracking in the water-well trade, is a rehabilitation method for low-yield wells drilled into hard, fractured crystalline bedrock, the decomposed granite and granitic rock that underlies much of inland San Diego County. A drilled bedrock well produces water only where the borehole happens to intersect natural fractures and seams in the rock. Over years, those fractures clog with fine sediment, iron, and mineral scale, and a well that once kept up with the household slowly falls behind. Hydrofracturing injects clean water at high pressure, typically 1,000 to 3,000 PSI, into isolated sections of the borehole. The pressure flushes the clogged fractures clean and nudges open hairline cracks, reconnecting the well to a larger network of water-bearing rock.

It uses only water. There are no chemicals, no proppant sand, and no relationship to oil-and-gas fracking beyond the shared root word. It is a one-day, surface-level procedure performed entirely inside the existing well casing.

Why It Rarely Applies in Serra Mesa Itself

Two facts make hydrofracturing a weak fit for Serra Mesa proper. First, the neighborhood is on reliable municipal water, so private wells are uncommon. Second, the geology directly beneath the mesa is not the same fractured granite that responds well to the treatment; the mesa surfaces are capped with older sedimentary and terrace materials. Hydrofracturing does essentially nothing in sand, gravel, or clay aquifers, because there are no rigid rock fractures to open, the pressure simply dissipates into loose material.

So if you live in Serra Mesa and your water pressure has dropped, the honest first step is not to think about your aquifer at all. It is to check whether the issue is in your home plumbing, a pressure regulator, or a municipal-side matter. A licensed plumber, or a call to the City, will resolve far more Serra Mesa water complaints than any well contractor could.

When Hydrofracturing Does Make Sense

If you own a property with an actual drilled well, particularly on the rural fringes of San Diego County toward Ramona, Lakeside, Alpine, or the backcountry, then hydrofracturing can be a strong, cost-effective option. It is worth a serious look when:

  • The well is drilled into granite or other hard bedrock, not a sand or alluvial basin.
  • Yield has declined over the years from what the original driller's report listed.
  • A newly drilled well came in at a disappointing flow rate and you want to improve it before giving up.
  • The well recovers slowly after the pump runs, or runs dry during summer demand.
  • You want to exhaust lower-cost options before committing to a brand-new well.

Hydrofracturing Versus the Other Options

One reason hydrofracturing gets so much attention is price. For a property owner with a tired bedrock well, the realistic alternatives are deepening the existing well, drilling a brand-new well, or living with the shortage. Drilling new is the surest path to more water, but in San Diego County it commonly runs $18,000 to $42,000 turnkey once you include drilling, casing, a pump, and electrical. Deepening can help if the water table has genuinely dropped, but it is unpredictable and you pay by the foot into rock that may or may not yield more. Against those numbers, a $3,000 to $8,000 hydrofracturing job that often doubles or triples flow is an easy first experiment: if it works you have spent a fraction of the new-well cost, and if it does not, you have lost comparatively little and learned something concrete about your aquifer.

For Serra Mesa readers, though, this comparison is largely academic, because you are almost certainly on city water. The more relevant comparison for an urban San Diego property is between a point-of-use water treatment system and simply accepting your municipal supply. If your concern is hardness, taste, or a specific contaminant rather than quantity, treatment, not any kind of well work, is the right category to explore.

How the Process Works, Step by Step

  1. Diagnostic visit. We measure your current yield, review the well log, and confirm the well is in suitable bedrock. Our diagnostic is $125 and is credited toward the work if you proceed.
  2. Pump removal. The pump and drop pipe are pulled so the borehole is open and accessible.
  3. Camera inspection. A downhole video survey checks casing integrity and locates the fracture zones worth targeting.
  4. Packer isolation. An inflatable packer seals off a section of the borehole below the casing so pressure is concentrated where it helps.
  5. High-pressure injection. Clean water is driven in at 1,000 to 3,000 PSI to clear and open fractures. Deeper or multiple zones are treated in sequence.
  6. Redevelopment and testing. The well is surged clean, the pump reinstalled, and a fresh flow test documents the before-and-after result.

Start to finish, a typical job runs four to eight hours on site.

Realistic Results and Expectations

In suitable San Diego County bedrock wells, hydrofracturing improves yield in roughly 70 to 80 percent of cases, with typical gains of two to five times the pre-treatment flow. It is a rehabilitation, not a miracle: a well sitting in genuinely dry rock cannot be made productive, and results vary with the specific fracture network. A reputable contractor will tell you honestly when the odds are poor rather than take your money on a long shot.

What to Check Before You Call

  • Are you on a well or city water? If you are on city water, you do not need us, you need a plumber or the utility.
  • What does your well log say? Depth, casing, and the formation drilled tell us whether the well is a hydrofrac candidate.
  • Has the pump been ruled out? A failing pump or pressure switch mimics low yield and is far cheaper to fix.
  • Is the casing sound? A cracked or corroded casing should be addressed before any pressure treatment.

Will it work in sandy or alluvial ground? No. The technique needs rigid fractured bedrock. In loose sand or gravel it has no meaningful effect.
