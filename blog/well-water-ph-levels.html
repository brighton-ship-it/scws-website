Understanding pH in Well Water

pH measures how acidic or alkaline your water is on a scale from 0 to 14, with 7 being neutral. Most well water falls between 6.0 and 8.5. The pH level affects taste, corrosiveness, and how well other treatment systems work. Acidic water (low pH) often has a metallic taste and leaves blue-green stains from copper pipe corrosion. Alkaline water (high pH) may taste bitter or soapy.

Effects of Unbalanced pH

Acidic water corrodes copper pipes, causing pinhole leaks and blue-green stains on fixtures. It also dissolves lead from older solder joints and brass fittings, creating serious health hazards. Highly alkaline water causes scale buildup similar to hard water and reduces the effectiveness of chlorine disinfection. Both extremes can damage water heaters, dishwashers, and other appliances.

Testing Your Well Water pH

Simple test strips give quick readings but aren't highly accurate. Digital pH meters provide better precision for about $15-50. For the most reliable results, have your water professionally tested by a certified lab. Test pH along with hardness, iron, and other parameters since these factors often interact and affect treatment choices.

pH Correction Systems

Acid neutralizing filters raise low pH by passing water through calcite (calcium carbite) or a calcite/corosex blend. These require periodic media replacement as the minerals dissolve. For very low pH, chemical injection systems add soda ash or sodium hydroxide solution. High pH is less common but can be treated with acid injection. Whole-house systems ensure all water is pH-balanced before reaching pipes.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the ideal pH for well water?

The ideal pH range for drinking water is 6.5 to 8.5, with 7.0 being neutral. Water below 6.5 is acidic and corrosive to pipes, while water above 8.5 may taste bitter and cause scale buildup.

What causes low pH in well water?

Low pH (acidic water) typically results from natural factors like decaying organic matter, certain rock formations, and dissolved carbon dioxide. Acid rain and mining activity can also lower groundwater pH in some regions.

Can acidic water make you sick?

Acidic water itself isn't directly harmful, but it corrodes copper and lead pipes, leaching these metals into your drinking water. Lead exposure is particularly dangerous, causing neurological damage especially in children.
