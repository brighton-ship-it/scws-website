Well Permits in California: Requirements & Process

Updated February 2026 | By Southern California Well Service

Quick Answer: California requires permits for new wells, deepening, modifications, and destruction/abandonment. Contact your county environmental health or water agency. Fees range $200-$2,000+ depending on county and well type. Licensed C-57 drillers typically handle permits as part of the drilling contract.

When Permits Are Required

Always Requires Permit

  • Drilling a new well
  • Deepening an existing well
  • Modifying well construction
  • Destroying or abandoning a well
  • Converting well use (domestic to agricultural)

Usually No Permit Needed

  • Replacing pump
  • Routine maintenance
  • Water treatment installation
  • Pressure tank replacement
  • Electrical repairs

Check with County

  • Well rehabilitation/cleaning
  • Casing repairs
  • Adding wellhead protection

Permit Types

New Well Permit

  • Required before drilling
  • Most involved process
  • May require environmental review
  • Site inspection often required

Modification Permit

  • For deepening or altering existing well
  • Typically less complex than new well
  • Requires existing well documentation

Destruction Permit

  • Required for abandoning wells
  • Prevents groundwater contamination
  • Specific sealing requirements
  • Important for property sales

Application Process

Step 1: Identify Permit Authority

  • Usually county environmental health
  • Some areas: water district or agency
  • Groundwater sustainability agencies (SGMA)

Step 2: Submit Application

  • Property information and ownership
  • Proposed well location (site plan)
  • Intended use (domestic, agricultural, etc.)
  • Licensed driller information
  • Proposed construction details

Step 3: Review

  • Setback requirements checked
  • Zoning compliance verified
  • Environmental review if applicable
  • CEQA compliance (larger projects)

Step 4: Site Inspection

  • May be required before approval
  • Verifies location meets requirements
  • Checks for contamination sources

Step 5: Permit Issuance

  • Typically valid 1-2 years
  • May include conditions
  • Inspection required before use

Step 6: Final Inspection

  • After drilling completion
  • Well log submitted
  • Water quality test may be required

Timeline

  • Simple permits: 1-4 weeks
  • Complex/CEQA review: 2-6 months
  • Plan ahead for delays

San Diego County

Permit Authority

San Diego County Department of Environmental Health (DEH)

Land & Water Quality Division

Requirements

  • Minimum setbacks from septic, property lines
  • Licensed C-57 driller required
  • Site inspection before and after
  • Water quality testing for domestic wells

Typical Fees (2026)

  • New well permit: $700-$1,200
  • Well destruction: $400-$600
  • Modification: $500-$800

Riverside County

Permit Authority

Riverside County Environmental Health

Requirements

  • Similar to San Diego
  • Additional groundwater agency requirements in some areas
  • Eastern Municipal Water District areas have specific rules

Typical Fees

  • New well: $600-$1,000
  • Destruction: $300-$500

Tips for Smooth Process

Use a Licensed Driller

  • C-57 license required in California
  • Most handle permits as part of service
  • Know local requirements and officials

Plan Ahead

  • Start permit process early
  • Don't wait until you need water urgently
  • Account for delays

Know Your Property

  • Survey showing property lines
  • Locations of septic, buildings, wells
  • Easements that may affect location

Keep Records

  • Well log copy
  • Permit documents
  • Water test results
  • Valuable for resale

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a permit to drill a well in California?

Yes. All new wells, modifications, and destructions require permits from your county environmental health department or water agency.

How much does a well permit cost?

Fees vary by county. San Diego: $700-$1,500 for new wells. Other counties: $200-$2,000+. Drillers typically include permit handling in their quotes.

How long does it take to get a permit?

Simple permits: 1-4 weeks. Complex cases with environmental review: 2-6 months. Start early.

Do I need a permit to replace my well pump?

Generally no. Routine maintenance and pump replacement don't require permits. Confirm with your county if unsure.

What happens if I drill without a permit?

Fines, required destruction of unpermitted well, legal issues. The well may also be unsafe or improperly constructed. Always get permits.
