What Causes Rusty Well Water?

  • Dissolved Iron: Naturally occurring iron in San Bernardino County groundwater
  • Iron Bacteria: Bacteria that feed on iron, creating rusty slime
  • Corroding Casing: Old steel well casing breaking down
  • Corroding Pipes: Galvanized or iron plumbing in your home
  • Failing Water Heater: Rusting anode rod or tank

Is Rusty Water Safe to Drink?

Iron itself isn't a health hazard at typical concentrations. However, rusty water:

  • Tastes metallic and unpleasant
  • Stains sinks, toilets, and laundry
  • Can clog pipes and fixtures over time
  • May indicate other water quality issues

We recommend water testing to identify the specific cause and concentration of iron in your Chino Hills water.

Rusty Water at a Chino Hills Property: What It Means and How to Fix It

Chino Hills sits in the southwestern corner of San Bernardino County, tucked into the rolling terrain between the Chino Valley, the Puente Hills, and the Orange County line. Most homes here are connected to municipal water drawn from the Chino Groundwater Basin, but a meaningful number of older equestrian properties, ranchettes along Carbon Canyon Road, and parcels on the rural fringe toward Sleepy Hollow and Los Serranos still rely on private wells or have legacy galvanized plumbing. When the water coming out of a Chino Hills tap turns orange, brown, or red, it is almost always an iron problem, a corrosion problem, or both. The first job is figuring out which one you have, because the fix for dissolved iron in groundwater is completely different from the fix for a rusting pipe in your own home.

How Iron and Rust Get Into Your Water

The Chino Basin beneath Chino Hills is made up of loose gravel, sand, and soil that hold and move groundwater. Water percolating through those sediments picks up dissolved minerals along the way, and iron is one of the most common. Underground, iron is usually in a clear, dissolved "ferrous" form you cannot see. The moment it reaches your faucet and hits oxygen, it oxidizes into the rust-colored "ferric" particles that stain everything. That is why a glass of water can look clear at first and then develop an orange tint or settle out reddish sediment after a few minutes.

The second source is metal. Steel well casings and galvanized-steel household pipes both corrode over decades. Many Chino Hills homes built before the mid-1970s were originally plumbed with galvanized pipe, and even on municipal water, that interior corrosion can deliver rusty water from a perfectly clean source. A simple test tells the two apart: if only the hot water is rusty, suspect the water heater; if rust appears only at certain fixtures, suspect the pipes serving those fixtures; if every tap runs rusty cold, the source is your well or service line.

How Treatment Actually Works

Removing iron is a two-step idea: oxidize the dissolved iron so it forms particles, then filter those particles out. Different equipment accomplishes this depending on concentration. Low levels of iron in fairly soft water can often be handled by a water softener, which swaps the iron out through ion exchange. Higher levels call for a dedicated oxidizing filter that uses air injection or a manganese-based media to convert and trap the iron. When iron bacteria are present, treatment also has to address the living organisms, usually through shock chlorination of the well followed by a filtration system. The right choice always follows a water test, never a guess.

Common Local Scenarios We See in Chino Hills

  • Aging galvanized plumbing in older Los Serranos and central Chino Hills homes: intermittent rusty water that is worst first thing in the morning after water has sat in the pipes overnight.
  • Private wells on Carbon Canyon and equestrian parcels: dissolved iron from basin sediments, sometimes paired with hard-water scaling that compounds the staining.
  • Iron bacteria after a well has sat idle: a slimy, reddish buildup in toilet tanks and a swampy or musty smell, common on seasonal or secondary wells.
  • A failing water heater anode rod: rust only on the hot side, often in homes where the heater is more than ten years old.
  • A corroding steel well casing: a gradual increase in sediment and rust over months or years, sometimes after the pump is pulled and inspected.

What to Check Before You Call

You can narrow the problem yourself in a few minutes. Fill a clear glass at the kitchen tap and let it sit; note whether color appears immediately or only after the water sits. Run only the cold water at several fixtures and compare to the hot. Look inside a toilet tank for orange staining or slimy buildup, which points toward iron bacteria. Check whether the rust is constant or comes and goes, and whether it spiked after recent plumbing work, a pump replacement, or a long vacancy. If you are on a private well, note the well's age and whether it has steel or PVC casing. Writing these observations down gives our technician a major head start.

When to Call a Professional

If rusty water is constant, affecting drinking and bathing, staining fixtures and laundry, or accompanied by a foul odor, it is time for a professional diagnosis. The same is true if you have a private well and have never had the water tested, or if you notice a sudden drop in pressure along with the discoloration, which can signal a corroding casing or a failing pump. Iron bacteria in particular rarely clear up on their own and tend to worsen, so early intervention saves money. Frequently Asked Questions

Is the rusty water in my Chino Hills home dangerous to drink?

Iron at typical concentrations is not a recognized health hazard, but rusty water tastes metallic, stains fixtures, and can harbor iron bacteria. If you are unsure of the source, especially on a private well, have it tested before relying on it for drinking.

Why is only my hot water rusty?

Rust isolated to the hot side almost always points to the water heater, typically a corroded anode rod or a rusting tank. Flushing the heater may help temporarily, but a heater more than ten years old often needs replacement.

I'm on city water in Chino Hills, so why is my water rusty?

Municipal water leaves the system clean, but older galvanized-steel household plumbing corrodes from the inside and releases rust. Replacing aging pipe runs with PEX or copper usually solves it.

What is iron bacteria and how do I know if I have it?

Iron bacteria are harmless-to-health organisms that feed on iron and leave a reddish, slimy buildup in toilet tanks and a musty odor. They are common in wells that have sat idle and require shock chlorination plus filtration to clear.

Will a standard water softener fix rusty well water?

A softener can handle low iron levels through ion exchange, but moderate to high iron, or any iron bacteria, needs a dedicated oxidizing or chemical-injection system. A water test tells us which approach your well requires.
