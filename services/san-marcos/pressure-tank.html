Pressure Tanks for San Marcos Wells

Your pressure tank is critical for protecting your well pump and maintaining steady water pressure. In San Marcos, where wells produce 5-15 GPM from sedimentary and volcanic formations, proper tank sizing and maintenance is essential.

How Pressure Tanks Work

A pressure tank stores pressurized water so your pump doesn't have to run every time you turn on a faucet. Key benefits:

  • Reduces pump cycling (extends pump life)
  • Maintains steady pressure throughout the house
  • Provides small reserve for power outages
  • Critical for San Marcos wells with yields of 5-15 GPM

Sizing Pressure Tanks for San Marcos Wells

Tank size depends on your well's flow rate and household demand:

  • Small households (1-3 people): 20-30 gallon tank for San Marcos wells producing 5
  • Medium households (4-5 people): 40-60 gallon tank
  • Large households or farms: 80-120 gallon tank, especially if well yield is on lower end (5-15 GPM)

Pressure Tank Considerations for San Marcos

San Marcos wells in sedimentary and volcanic formations typically produce 5-15 GPM. This affects tank sizing:

  • Lower yields need larger tanks to reduce pump cycling
  • Higher yields can use smaller tanks but still need adequate capacity
  • Water quality (moderate hardness (8-14 grains), limited wells - mostly municipal) affects tank lifespan

Signs Your San Marcos Pressure Tank is Failing

  • Waterlogging: Pump cycles on/off rapidly (short cycling)
  • Low pressure: Water pressure drops suddenly, especially upstairs or at far fixtures
  • Tank leaking: Water pooling around base, rust spots
  • No air cushion: Tank sounds "full" when tapped (should sound hollow at top)
  • Pump running constantly: Tank can't hold pressure

Common Pressure Tank Problems in San Marcos

Based on local conditions and sedimentary and volcanic formations:

  • Very few private wells, municipal water preferred, older wells needing updates, sedimentary/volcanic geology can have variable yields, limited drilling opportunities
  • Hard water (8-14 grains) causes sediment buildup inside tank
  • Water quality issues (moderate hardness (8-14 grains), limited wells - mostly municipal) can corrode bladder or tank interior

Pressure Tank Maintenance

  • Annual inspection: Check pre-charge pressure (should be 2 PSI below cut-in)
  • Drain sediment: Especially important with San Marcos water (moderate hardness (8-14 grains), limited wells - mostly municipal)
  • Check bladder: Bladder tanks last 5-10 years depending on water quality
  • Monitor pressure switch: Should cut in around 40 PSI, cut out around 60 PSI
