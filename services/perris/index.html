Understanding Perris Well Systems and Local Aquifer Conditions

Perris wells typically draw from the Perris Valley groundwater basin, a critical water source for western Riverside County. Most wells in the area range from 200 to 600 feet deep, depending on location and when the well was drilled. Older wells from the 1960s-1980s tend to be shallower (200-350 feet), while newer wells drilled in the last 20 years often go deeper (400-600+ feet) to access more reliable water tables and better quality water.

The groundwater in Perris tends to be hard with elevated total dissolved solids (TDS), nitrates from decades of agricultural use, and sometimes naturally occurring arsenic and uranium in certain geological formations. Water quality can vary significantly even between neighboring wells, so professional water testing is essential for all Perris well owners.

Common Well Problems in Perris

Extreme Hard Water and Mineral Scaling: Perris is notorious for exceptionally hard water. Hardness levels of 300-600 ppm (parts per million) are common, compared to "moderately hard" at 120 ppm. This creates severe scale buildup throughout plumbing systems, reduces water heater efficiency by 40-50%, clogs shower heads and faucets, and dramatically shortens pump lifespan. We regularly pull pumps that are completely encased in white mineral deposits, reducing their efficiency to almost nothing.

Nitrate Contamination: Decades of agricultural fertilizer use have left elevated nitrate levels in many Perris wells. Nitrates above 10 ppm are unsafe for infants and pregnant women. We frequently test Perris wells with nitrate levels of 15-30 ppm, requiring reverse osmosis treatment or drilling a deeper well that accesses a less contaminated aquifer zone.

Heat-Related Pump Failures: Summer temperatures in Perris regularly exceed 100°F, and the heat stress on well pump motors is significant. Pumps that run frequently during hot weather for landscape irrigation, livestock watering, or household use can overheat and fail prematurely. Proper pump sizing and thermal protection is critical in this climate.

Declining Water Levels: Groundwater levels in the Perris Valley fluctuate based on rainfall and pumping. During drought years, some wells experience declining water levels that cause pumps to run dry or produce insufficient flow. If your well is producing less water than it used to, we can perform a flow test, evaluate static water level, and determine if the pump needs to be lowered or if the well needs to be deepened.

Sand and Sediment Pumping: Some Perris wells pump sand or grit, especially after periods of heavy use. This indicates deterioration of the well screen or improper well development. Sand damages pump impellers, clogs pressure tanks, and ruins plumbing fixtures. We can install specialized sand-separation systems or, if the well screen has failed, recommend well rehabilitation or replacement.

Our Complete Well Services for Perris Properties

Well Pump Repair and Replacement: We service all pump types used in Perris wells—submersible pumps (the most common), turbine pumps for high-volume agricultural use, and older jet pump systems. Our diagnostic process includes electrical testing, performance evaluation, and often pulling the pump for direct inspection. We stock replacement pumps rated for hot climates and hard water conditions, so most repairs can be completed the same day. For agricultural wells, we offer high-capacity pumps designed for continuous duty and heavy use.

Water Treatment Systems: Given the hard water and potential contaminants in Perris wells, water treatment isn't optional—it's essential. We install and service whole-house water softeners for hardness, reverse osmosis systems for nitrates and TDS, UV sterilizers for bacteria, iron filters, arsenic removal systems, and sediment filtration. Our treatment recommendations are always based on laboratory water testing, not guesswork.

Well Drilling and Deepening: If your existing well has failed, can't produce adequate water, or has water quality issues that can't be economically treated, drilling a new well may be the best solution. We handle all aspects of well drilling—permitting, site evaluation, drilling, pump installation, and testing. We also offer well deepening services to access deeper, more reliable water zones.

Pressure Tank Service: Pressure tanks in Perris typically last 12-20 years before the bladder fails or the tank develops leaks. We install properly sized tanks based on your pump capacity and household water usage. Undersized tanks cause excessive pump cycling that wastes electricity and shortens pump life. We calculate the correct tank size and install quality tanks with solid warranties.
