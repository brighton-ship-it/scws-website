Pressure Tanks for San Bernardino Wells

Your pressure tank is critical for protecting your well pump and maintaining steady water pressure. In San Bernardino, where wells produce 10-30 GPM from San Bernardino Valley alluvial deposits, proper tank sizing and maintenance is essential.

How Pressure Tanks Work

A pressure tank stores pressurized water so your pump doesn't have to run every time you turn on a faucet. Key benefits:

  • Reduces pump cycling (extends pump life)
  • Maintains steady pressure throughout the house
  • Provides small reserve for power outages
  • Critical for San Bernardino wells with yields of 10-30 GPM

Sizing Pressure Tanks for San Bernardino Wells

Tank size depends on your well's flow rate and household demand:

  • Small households (1-3 people): 20-30 gallon tank for San Bernardino wells producing 10
  • Medium households (4-5 people): 40-60 gallon tank
  • Large households or farms: 80-120 gallon tank, especially if well yield is on lower end (10-30 GPM)

Pressure Tank Considerations for San Bernardino

San Bernardino wells in San Bernardino Valley alluvial deposits typically produce 10-30 GPM. This affects tank sizing:

  • Lower yields need larger tanks to reduce pump cycling
  • Higher yields can use smaller tanks but still need adequate capacity
  • Water quality (hard water (12-20 grains), nitrates, industrial contamination concerns) affects tank lifespan

Signs Your San Bernardino Pressure Tank is Failing

  • Waterlogging: Pump cycles on/off rapidly (short cycling)
  • Low pressure: Water pressure drops suddenly, especially upstairs or at far fixtures
  • Tank leaking: Water pooling around base, rust spots
  • No air cushion: Tank sounds "full" when tapped (should sound hollow at top)
  • Pump running constantly: Tank can't hold pressure

Common Pressure Tank Problems in San Bernardino

Based on local conditions and San Bernardino Valley alluvial deposits:

  • Hard water, nitrates, industrial contamination in some areas, most areas municipal water (very limited well service), few remaining private wells
  • Hard water (12-20 grains) causes sediment buildup inside tank
  • Water quality issues (hard water (12-20 grains), nitrates, industrial contamination concerns) can corrode bladder or tank interior

Pressure Tank Maintenance

  • Annual inspection: Check pre-charge pressure (should be 2 PSI below cut-in)
  • Drain sediment: Especially important with San Bernardino water (hard water (12-20 grains), nitrates, industrial contamination concerns)
  • Check bladder: Bladder tanks last 5-10 years depending on water quality
  • Monitor pressure switch: Should cut in around 40 PSI, cut out around 60 PSI

Tank Replacement for San Bernardino Wells

When it's time to replace your pressure tank, we:

  2. Choose quality: Bladder tanks from top manufacturers (Well-X-Trol, Amtrol)
