Understanding Rialto's Well Water Systems

Rialto sits at the base of the San Bernardino Mountains where the foothills meet the valley floor. Wells in Rialto typically range from 200 to 600 feet deep, drawing from the Rialto-Colton groundwater basin. Properties closer to Lytle Creek and the northern foothills often have shallower wells (200-350 feet), while valley floor properties may require deeper wells (400-600+ feet) to access reliable water tables.

The groundwater in Rialto tends to be moderately to very hard with elevated total dissolved solids (TDS). Some areas show elevated nitrates from historical agricultural use and urban development. The proximity to I-10 and industrial areas means that professional water testing is essential for all Rialto well owners.

Common Well Problems in Rialto

Hard Water and Mineral Scaling: Rialto well water is notoriously hard, often measuring 300-600 ppm or higher. This creates severe mineral buildup throughout plumbing systems, dramatically reduces water heater efficiency, clogs fixtures and appliances, and shortens pump lifespan. We regularly pull pumps that are completely coated in white calcium deposits that restrict water flow and cause motor overheating. Whole-house water softening is essential for protecting your home and well investment.

Extreme Heat Stress on Pumps: Summer temperatures in Rialto regularly exceed 100-110°F, putting enormous stress on well pump motors. Pumps running frequently during hot weather for landscape irrigation or household use can overheat and fail prematurely. Proper pump sizing, thermal protection, and adequate electrical supply are critical in this climate.

Declining Water Levels: Groundwater levels in the Rialto-Colton basin fluctuate based on rainfall, mountain runoff, and regional pumping. During drought periods, some wells experience declining water levels that cause insufficient flow or pump failures. If your well is producing less water than it used to, we can perform a comprehensive flow test and determine if the pump needs to be lowered or the well deepened.

Pressure Tank and System Cycling Issues: Short cycling—where the pump turns on and off every few minutes—is extremely common in Rialto wells. This usually indicates a waterlogged pressure tank, failed bladder, malfunctioning pressure switch, or check valve problem. Left unchecked, short cycling dramatically increases electricity costs and can burn out the pump motor within months.
