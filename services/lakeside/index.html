Lakeside sits on the San Diego River Valley aquifer, and on bedrock beside it

This town is served from the Ramona shop at 1077 Main St, Unit B, Ramona, CA 92065. Voice (760) 440-8520. Text (760) 823-7963.

DWR Bulletin 118 basin 9-15, the San Diego River Valley Groundwater Basin, is alluvium deposited by the San Diego River and its tributaries. DWR surrounds it with the Eocene Poway Group, Cretaceous crystalline rock, and Santiago Peak volcanic rocks. Quaternary alluvium is the principal water-bearing unit. The most productive parts are well-sorted sands in buried river channels and a coarse gravel near the base of the aquifer. Alluvium thickness exceeds 200 feet near Lakeside and is typically about 70 feet. In the more productive parts, DWR says wells yield up to 2,000 gallons per minute. That figure is for the productive alluvium, not a backyard bedrock well. El Capitan Dam (1935) and San Vicente Dam (1943) changed recharge. DWR says present recharge includes dam releases and underflow past the dams.

DWR's sentence about basal gravel being thickest adjacent to Moreno Valley is about the river valley in this basin, the same Moreno Valley name already listed in the neighborhood list on this page. It is not the City of Moreno Valley in Riverside County.

California DWR Well Completion Reports compiled in the site well-depth file (generated January 30, 2026). Depth and yield figures below use the domestic water-supply reports tagged with that city. Lakeside domestic reports do not all match that thin river alluvium. There are 211 domestic reports, median depth 500 feet, middle half between 250 and 780 feet, median reported yield 12 gallons per minute. Irrigation reports tagged Lakeside are shallower, median depth 270 feet, with a median reported yield of 42 gallons per minute. The file is mixing river-valley wells and deeper bedrock wells.

How thick is the alluvium near Lakeside?

DWR Bulletin 118 basin 9-15 says alluvium thickness exceeds 200 feet near Lakeside and is typically about 70 feet. The productive zones are buried river-channel sands and basal gravel. Wells in the more productive alluvium are described as yielding up to 2,000 gallons per minute.

Domestic reports tagged Lakeside have a median depth of 500 feet (211 reports). Irrigation reports tagged Lakeside have a median depth of 270 feet. Many domestic wells in the file are bedrock wells off the river valley, not the shallow alluvial aquifer DWR describes.

Which Moreno Valley does the basin report mean?

In the San Diego River Valley basin description, Moreno Valley is the valley along the river in the Lakeside area. That Lakeside-area Moreno Valley is served from the Ramona shop. It is not the City of Moreno Valley in Riverside County.
