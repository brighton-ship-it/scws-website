Common Pump Issues in Alpine

Wells in Alpine typically range from 300-550ft with geology consisting of gneiss and decomposed granite. This means pumps here face unique challenges:

  • Pump depth settings: Most Alpine wells require pump settings between 300 and 550ft, which means your pump is working hard to lift water from deep underground.
  • Typical yields: Alpine wells produce 5-12 GPM, which affects pump sizing and horsepower requirements.
  • Local water quality: moderate hardness, iron from gneiss — these contaminants can cause premature pump wear and motor failure.

Pump Types for Alpine Geology

Because of the gneiss and decomposed granite in Alpine, we typically install:

  • Submersible pumps for depths over 250 feet (most common in Alpine)
  • Specific horsepower: Based on your well's yield (5-12 GPM) and total dynamic head
  • Stainless steel construction to handle moderate hardness, iron from gneiss

Signs Your Alpine Well Pump is Failing

  • Pressure drops below 40 PSI
  • Pump runs continuously or cycles frequently
  • Rusty or sandy water (common with gneiss and decomposed granite)
  • High electric bills (pump working too hard)
  • No water at all (complete failure)
