Temecula groundwater, from the basin report and our files

This town is served from the Anza shop at 57174 CA-371, Anza, CA 92539. Voice (760) 440-8520. Text (760) 823-7963.

California Department of Water Resources places Temecula in the Temecula Valley Groundwater Basin, Bulletin 118 basin 9-05. The same basin underlies Murrieta, Pauba, Long, and Lancaster Valleys in southwestern Riverside County and a portion of northern San Diego County. DWR bounds it with crystalline rocks of the Peninsular Ranges and drains it by Wilson, Temecula, Murrieta, Warm Springs, and Pechanga Creeks to the Santa Margarita River. The basin description calls Quaternary alluvium the water-bearing material, estimated at more than 2,500 feet thick, with Holocene gravel, sand, silt, and clay generally about 100 to 125 feet thick and locally about 200 feet. It describes the Pleistocene Temecula Arkose as at least 1,400 feet thick, and says groundwater is also taken from residuum and fractured rock. Groundwater is generally unconfined, and confined in Pauba Valley and near some faults. Published yields in that description generally range to 300 gallons per minute in the northwestern part of the basin and reach 1,750 gallons per minute for wells in Pauba Valley. Those are basin figures cited by DWR from older reports. They are not a house-well promise for a De Luz hillside.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if my well pump needs repair?

Common signs include: no water or low pressure, pump cycling on and off rapidly (short cycling), unusual noises, dirty or sandy water, and higher-than-normal electric bills. Any of these warrant a professional inspection.

How much does well pump repair cost?

Simple repairs like pressure switch replacement run

50-$300. Motor or pump replacement typically costs ,500-$4,500 depending on well depth and pump type. We diagnose the issue before recommending repairs.

Can I repair my well pump myself?

Surface-level issues like pressure switch adjustment or breaker resets are safe DIY tasks. However, anything involving pulling the pump from the well requires specialized equipment and should be handled by a licensed contractor to avoid damaging the well casing.
