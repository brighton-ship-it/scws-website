Well Inspections in Temecula

Wells in Temecula face unique challenges due to mixed granite, alluvial, and sedimentary formations and local conditions. A thorough inspection identifies problems before they become emergencies.

What We Inspect in Temecula Wells

Well Casing & Construction

  • Casing integrity: Check for cracks, corrosion, or damage (critical in mixed granite, alluvial, and sedimentary formations)
  • Well seal: Ensure sanitary seal is intact (prevents surface contamination)
  • Depth verification: Confirm well depth matches records (Temecula wells typically 200-500ft)
  • Screen condition: Check for sand infiltration (common in mixed granite, alluvial, and sedimentary formations)

Pump System

  • Pump performance: Flow rate test (should be 10-30 GPM)
  • Motor condition: Amp draw, voltage, running temperature
  • Pump depth: Verify setting is appropriate for Temecula water table
  • Drop pipe: Check for leaks, corrosion, or age-related failure

Pressure & Storage

  • Pressure tank: Check pre-charge, waterlogging, bladder condition
  • Pressure switch: Test cut-in/cut-out settings
  • Storage tanks: Inspect condition if applicable (common in Julian, Anza for low-yield wells)

Water Quality

  • Visual inspection: Check for sediment, rust, odor
  • Testing: Bacteria, hardness, TDS, specific contaminants (variable by location, moderate hardness, some iron)
  • Flow rate: Measure actual GPM vs. expected (10-30 GPM)

Temecula-Specific Inspection Focus

Because Temecula wells are in mixed granite, alluvial, and sedimentary formations, we pay special attention to:

When to Get a Temecula Well Inspection

  • Real estate transactions: Required for buying/selling homes in De Luz, Wine Country, Vail Ranch, Redhawk, French Valley
