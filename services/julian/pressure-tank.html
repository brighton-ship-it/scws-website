Pressure Tanks for Julian Wells

Your pressure tank is critical for protecting your well pump and maintaining steady water pressure. In Julian, where wells produce 2-5 GPM from decomposed granite, deep fractured bedrock, proper tank sizing and maintenance is essential.

How Pressure Tanks Work

A pressure tank stores pressurized water so your pump doesn't have to run every time you turn on a faucet. Key benefits:

  • Reduces pump cycling (extends pump life)
  • Maintains steady pressure throughout the house
  • Provides small reserve for power outages
  • Critical for Julian wells with yields of 2-5 GPM

Sizing Pressure Tanks for Julian Wells

Tank size depends on your well's flow rate and household demand:

  • Small households (1-3 people): 20-30 gallon tank for Julian wells producing 2
  • Medium households (4-5 people): 40-60 gallon tank
  • Large households or farms: 80-120 gallon tank, especially if well yield is on lower end (2-5 GPM)
  • Low-yield wells: Larger tank + storage tank recommended for Julian, Anza wells with yields under 5 GPM

Pressure Tank Considerations for Julian

Julian wells in decomposed granite, deep fractured bedrock typically produce 2-5 GPM. This affects tank sizing:

  • Lower yields need larger tanks to reduce pump cycling
  • Higher yields can use smaller tanks but still need adequate capacity
  • Water quality (low yield common, iron, moderate hardness) affects tank lifespan

Signs Your Julian Pressure Tank is Failing

  • Waterlogging: Pump cycles on/off rapidly (short cycling)
  • Low pressure: Water pressure drops suddenly, especially upstairs or at far fixtures
  • Tank leaking: Water pooling around base, rust spots
  • No air cushion: Tank sounds "full" when tapped (should sound hollow at top)
  • Pump running constantly: Tank can't hold pressure

Common Pressure Tank Problems in Julian

Based on local conditions and decomposed granite, deep fractured bedrock:

  • Freeze damage to above-ground components, low flow rates, deep pump settings, power outages in storms
  • Hard water (6-12 grains) causes sediment buildup inside tank
  • Water quality issues (low yield common, iron, moderate hardness) can corrode bladder or tank interior
  • Freeze damage in winter (elevation 4,200ft means cold temps)

Pressure Tank Maintenance

  • Annual inspection: Check pre-charge pressure (should be 2 PSI below cut-in)
  • Drain sediment: Especially important with Julian water (low yield common, iron, moderate hardness)
  • Check bladder: Bladder tanks last 5-10 years depending on water quality
  • Monitor pressure switch: Should cut in around 40 PSI, cut out around 60 PSI
