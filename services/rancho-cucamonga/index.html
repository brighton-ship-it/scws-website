Rancho Cucamonga Wells & Geology

Rancho Cucamonga's foothill location creates variable well conditions. Wells range from 150 feet in lower elevations to 800+ feet in mountain properties. Most draw from the Cucamonga alluvial fan and Chino groundwater basin. The water is moderately to very hard with elevated TDS. Alta Loma and Etiwanda's wine country areas often have wells originally drilled for vineyard irrigation.

Common Well Issues

Hard Water & Scaling: Rancho Cucamonga wells produce hard water (300-700 ppm) creating severe mineral buildup, reducing appliance efficiency, and damaging pumps. Softening is essential for long-term system health.

Agricultural Well Conversion: Many foothill wells were drilled for vineyard or agricultural irrigation. Converting to residential use requires pump resizing, pressure system upgrades, and water treatment.

Heat & Equipment Stress: Summer temperatures exceeding 100°F stress pump motors and electrical components, especially during heavy irrigation use.
